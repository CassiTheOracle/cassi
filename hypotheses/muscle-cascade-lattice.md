## 2. Muscle as the Most Legible Cascade Ladder

### 2.1 Why Muscle, Not Other Tissues

Skeletal muscle's structural hierarchy is uniquely suited to reveal the bubble lattice for four reasons:

1. **Discrete, explicitly hierarchical levels.** Muscle organization is not a continuum. It has cleanly separated structural tiers—filament, sarcomere, myofibril, fiber, fascicle, belly, group—each separated from the next by a clear anatomical boundary. This is the cascade ladder made visible.

2. **Scale range spans the human window.** The muscle hierarchy spans from ~5 nm (actin/myosin filament diameter) to ~30–50 cm (muscle group width)—approximately 7.8–8 orders of magnitude, corresponding to roughly 127–165 $\varphi$-rungs. The placements are scale matches across a broad interval, not evidence that every adjacent anatomical level occupies one $\varphi$-scaled step.

3. **Regular spatial periodicity at the sarcomere level.** The sarcomere is intrinsically periodic: Z-disc to Z-disc spacing defines a 1D lattice along the muscle fiber axis. This is the most literal anatomical analogue of the condensation field's along-string periodicity.

4. **Fascial boundaries are geometrically well-defined.** The connective tissue planes that separate individual muscles (epimysium, perimysium, endomysium) form a continuous 3D network of surfaces. In the conditional bubble-lattice picture, these surfaces are the $B=\theta_{\text{cond}}$ level-set boundaries between condensate and non-condensate regions; $B=-1$ is the void-center extremum.

### 2.2 Comparison with Other Candidate Systems

- **Neural hierarchy** (`hypotheses/neural-criticality.md`): the 8-level neural hierarchy (synapse → spine → soma → microcolumn → column → area → network → whole brain) is a valid cascade instantiation, but its spatial scales overlap more densely, and the boundaries between levels are less anatomically discrete.

- **Chakra system** (`consciousness/chakras-as-cascade-bubbles.md`): the 13 chakra nodes along the spine are the along-string bubble maxima, but their anatomical correlates (nerve plexuses, endocrine glands) are not as cleanly hierarchical as muscle's nested levels.

- **Vascular tree, skeletal system, lymphatic network**: each has hierarchical structure, but none exhibits the same combination of (a) regular internal periodicity, (b) cleanly separated structural tiers, and (c) well-defined boundary surfaces as skeletal muscle.

Muscle is anatomically the most legible cascade ladder in the body.

### 3.2 Observations from the Table

**Non-monotonicity at the finest scales.** Levels 0, 1, and 2 do not increase monotonically in rung number because they represent different spatial dimensions of the same contractile apparatus: the filament diameter (level 0, ~5–10 nm) is smaller than the myofibril diameter (level 2, ~1 $\mu$m), but the sarcomere length (level 1, ~2.5 $\mu$m) is longer than both. The sarcomere's characteristic scale is its *along-fiber* period (Z-disc spacing), while the myofibril's scale is its *cross-sectional* diameter. In the bubble lattice, the string axis ($z$, along the fiber) and the Yang-Yin plane ($x$-$y$, transverse) carry different periodicities, so a structure's dominant scale depends on which axis is being measured. This is itself a signature of the triaxial lattice geometry ($\Lambda_Y \neq \Lambda_I \neq P_\parallel$): at these fine rungs, the three axes resolve into distinct anatomical features rather than being averaged into a single morphological scale.

**Scaling from fiber to whole muscle.** Levels 3 through 6 form a clean monotonic ladder: fiber (~50 $\mu$m, 145.9) → fascicle (~1–5 mm, 152.1–155.5) → muscle belly (~15–30 cm, 162.5–164.0) → muscle group (~30–50 cm, 164.0–165.0). The span from fiber to belly covers ~16.6 rungs (145.9 → 162.5); fiber to group ~19 rungs. The muscle group sits ~3 rungs below the organism scale (1.7 m ↔ 167.5), not at step ~168. These placements make the ladder *not* a clean consecutive-rung sequence at the finest scales: the myofibril (137.8) falls between the sarcomere (139.2–139.7) and the filament (126.8–128.2), because levels 0–2 mix axial and transverse dimensions (the document's non-monotonicity discussion)—a legibility argument for muscle as a ladder, not a derivation.

## 4. Geometric Predictions

### 4.1 Fascial Planes as $B=\theta_{\text{cond}}$ Level-Set Boundaries

The checkerboard count of four axial neighbors and four diagonal neighbors is a 2D Yang–Yin cross-section count, not a full 3D neighbor count. **Prediction M1:** Fascial planes—the connective tissue sheaths that separate individual muscles (epimysium between bellies, perimysium between fascicles, endomysium between fibers)—are the $B=\theta_{\text{cond}}$ level-set boundaries of the conditional bubble lattice. At the fascicle scale, each fascicle is a bubble condensate, surrounded by perimysial connective tissue across the threshold level set; at the fiber scale, each muscle fiber is a bubble, with endomysial connective tissue across the corresponding threshold boundary.

**Testable consequence:** The fascial plane between two adjacent muscles should be tested for the level-dependent edge-slope proxy $R(\theta_{\mathrm{cond}})$. At an independently selected $\theta_{\mathrm{cond}}=0.45$ boundary, the geometric proxy benchmark is $R=1.7072$; it varies with the boundary level, and no $C=0.45$ edge survives the fixed-step PDE endpoint. Approaching the fascial boundary along the Yin direction (perpendicular to the muscle fiber's long axis, along the body's left-right or anterior-posterior axis) should produce a steeper drop in tissue coherence—measurable by ultrasound shear-wave elastography—than approaching along the diagonal direction (oblique to the fascial plane). The physical boundary and proxy-to-observable map must be independently identified.

**Anatomical corollary:** Individual muscles are not arbitrarily bounded. Their shapes and the planes between them are constrained by the staggered checkerboard: each muscle belly occupies a bubble site, with threshold level-set boundaries on four axial sides in the transverse cross-section. This predicts a characteristic nearest-neighbor organization of muscles in cross-section—a staggered arrangement consistent with the checkerboard, not a random tiling.

### 4.2 Sarcomere Banding as 1D Bubble Lattice

The sarcomere—the fundamental contractile unit of striated muscle—is defined by the Z-discs at its ends, with the A-band (myosin-containing, dark) in the center and I-bands (actin-only, light) on either side. The repeating pattern Z → I → A → I → Z along the muscle fiber is a 1D periodic structure.

**Prediction M2:** The sarcomere banding pattern is the condensation field's along-string (1D) bubble lattice, restricted to the muscle fiber axis $z$. In this phase assignment, the Z-discs are $B=-1$ void-center extrema at period boundaries, providing low-$q_{\rm proxy}$ interfaces; the boundary between high- and low-region assignments remains the $B=\theta_{\text{cond}}$ level set. The I-bands rise from those boundaries toward the central bubble. The A-band is the central high-$q_{\rm proxy}$ condensate spanning the bubble interior, and the M-line at its center is the $B=+1$ bubble maximum. This phase assignment is a geometric hypothesis; the mapping to protein density remains testable.

This mapping gives specific structural consequences:

- **Z-disc spacing** ($L_Z$) is the candidate along-string bubble period $P_\parallel \cdot \ell_n$. The canonical ladder ($\ell_n = \ell_{\text{Pl}}\varphi^n$; `foundations/dimensionful-cascade.md`) places the observed resting range $L_Z \approx 2.0$–$2.5\ \mu$m at $n \approx 139.2$–$139.7$—residuals of 0.17–0.29 rungs from the nearest special point (integer or half-integer). At rung 135, a $P_\parallel=1$ period is $\ell_{135}=0.26\ \mu$m and a $P_\parallel=2$ period is $2\ell_{135}=0.53\ \mu$m, both below the observed spacing; the latter is a factor of 4–5 smaller. The current bookkeeping convention takes $P_\parallel = 1$ at the sarcomere's own rung, $L_Z = \ell_{139.4}$, with residuals within the framework's uniform-null expectation for placements (`particles/matter-organization.md` §2.2). A $P_\parallel = 2$ convention would place the period two rungs below the sarcomere rung ($2\ell_{138} = 2.24\ \mu$m); with $P_\parallel(n)$ not derived (`foundations/bubble-lattice-fabric.md` §8), anatomical data must determine which coordinate convention is supported. The sarcomere period becomes a prediction only when an independent $P_\parallel(n)$ law fixes $L_Z$ at the sarcomere rung.

- **A-band / I-band ratio.** The A-band occupies the central portion of the bubble where $B > \theta_{\text{cond}}$; the I-bands occupy the edges where $B$ falls below threshold. For the phase convention $B(z)=-\cos(2\pi z/L_Z)$, the central region selected by $B>\theta_{\text{cond}}$ has exact fraction $f_A=\arccos(\theta_{\text{cond}})/\pi\approx0.351$ at $\theta_{\text{cond}}=0.45$, while the complementary below-threshold fraction is $1-f_A=1-\arccos(0.45)/\pi\approx0.649$. The observed $\sim 1.5\ \mu$m A-band in a $\sim 2.5\ \mu$m sarcomere (ratio $\sim 0.6$) is therefore a separate anatomical measurement; inferring a scale-specific $\theta_{\text{cond}}$ requires the protein-density map.

### 4.3 A-Band as High-$q_{\rm proxy}$ Condensate, I-Band as Low-$q_{\rm proxy}$ Void

profile remains a separate test of the geometric mapping.

This phase assignment makes the A-band's high $q_{\rm proxy}$ correspond to the
dense, coherent myosin filament overlap region, while the I-band's low

## 6. Open Questions

1. **What sets $\theta_{\text{cond}}$ at the sarcomere scale?** The condensation threshold $\theta_{\text{cond}}$ is fixed only after the effective diffusion, asserted gate, bounded proxy-to-solver map, and balance parameter are supplied (`foundations/bubble-edge-geometry.md` §1.2). The value $\theta_{\text{cond}}=0.45$ is a phenomenological selection used for a directional geometric benchmark; the fixed-step PDE diagnostic does not retain that edge. At the sarcomere scale ($n \approx 139.5$), $D_{\text{eff}}$ and the map from $q_{\rm proxy}$ to protein density are unknown. Once those inputs are independently established, the A-band/I-band profile can test rather than set the boundary model.

2. **How does contraction modify the lattice?** Muscle contraction shortens the sarcomere from ~2.5 $\mu$m to ~1.8 $\mu$m (in human skeletal muscle). In the bubble lattice picture, this is a compression of the along-string period. Does the lattice remain coherent under compression, or does it undergo a phase transition (bubble collapse, void widening)? The PDE at the sarcomere rungs ($n \approx 139$–$140$) with a time-varying boundary condition (simulating contraction) could answer this.

3. **Is the 1D sarcomere lattice coupled to the 3D fascial lattice?** The sarcomere is contained within the myofibril, which is within the fiber, which is within the fascicle. The bubble lattice at each scale is nested within the lattice at the next. How does the along-string periodicity at the sarcomere level (Z-disc spacing under the current bookkeeping convention $P_\parallel = 1$) relate to the transverse checkerboard at the fascicle level? Do anatomical data support this convention, or the alternative $P_\parallel = 2$ placement two rungs below the sarcomere? Is the coupling between scales merely geometric (the nesting of `foundations/bubble-lattice-fabric.md` §3.2) or are there active PDE-driven interactions?

4. **Does smooth muscle show the same conditional mapping?** Smooth muscle lacks the regular sarcomere banding of skeletal and cardiac muscle. If the conditional lattice mapping extends to smooth muscle, it should still show periodic structure—perhaps at a coarser scale (dense bodies as Z-disc analogues, caveolae as void-center features) or in a less regular arrangement (the checkerboard's off-lattice configurations at low $q_{\rm proxy}$). The absence of regular striations may itself be informative: it could indicate that the conditional mapping at the smooth-muscle scale lies below the geometric $B$ threshold $B<-\theta_{\text{cond}}$ or uses a different $P_\parallel$. This is a comparative test, not an assertion of universality.

5. **Can cardiac muscle reveal a fivefold ventricular organization?** Cardiac muscle shares skeletal muscle's sarcomere structure but differs in its branching syncytial organization. A fivefold ventricular-wall arrangement is a speculative imaging target, not derived from Fibonacci or octave structure and not derived from the Torrent–Guasp ventricular-myocardial-band model. Diffusion-tensor MRI could test whether such a target is present; no current result is claimed.
