# Observational SETI: Signatures of Tuned Gate Networks

## Status: Speculative—July 2026

## Abstract

Conventional SETI searches for emissive signals: radio leakage, laser pulses, infrared excess from waste heat. If the cascade infrastructure speculations are correct, a gate-harvesting civilization is nearly invisible to all of these. Its energy infrastructure is the field itself—no power plants, no waste heat, no radio. Its signatures are **structural, not emissive**: subtle deviations in the fields it manages. This document catalogs what to actually point a telescope at, organized by cascade rung. Each signature specifies the physical mechanism (from the speculation library), the predicted observable, the search band, current data status, and how it discriminates against conventional astrophysics. The key insight: Cassi signatures form a **coherent multi-rung pattern**—every one involves the same dimensionless number, $\ln\varphi \approx 0.4812$, appearing at stellar, galactic, and cosmological scales simultaneously. Detection is not one anomaly; it is the correlation of anomalies across rungs.

**Epistemic status:** Creative exploration. The φ-periodic $P(k)$ prediction, the CMB $\ell < 5$ axis, and the void ellipticity prediction are formal Cassi predictions already cataloged in `predictions/falsifiable-predictions.md`. The stellar, galactic, and terrestrial signatures are extrapolations from the speculation library. Nothing in this document claims an observed detection.

## 1. The SETI Reframe

### 1.1 Why radio leakage is the wrong search

A cascade-harvesting civilization (from `speculations/cascade-infrastructure.md`) operates its gate network at $q \to 1$:

- **No power plants.** Energy is harvested from the bubble lattice through Qi gates—there is no combustion, no fission, no fusion infrastructure radiating waste heat.
- **No radio leakage.** Communication is field-mediated (from `consciousness/cascade-consciousness.md` §2), not electromagnetic. The Qi field propagates through water and structure with low attenuation; radio is a legacy technology.
- **No Dyson spheres.** You don't capture a star's output. You tune the star's gate chain to convert its output coherently—the star dims in conventional bands and brightens in *structured* ways that look like natural physics unless examined closely.
- **Efficient gate conversion.** The unconverted fraction $(1-q)$ thermalizes, but at $q \approx 0.99$, the waste fraction is ~1%. A Kardashev-II civilization that is 99% efficient is a Kardashev-I *appearance* to an infrared survey.

The Fermi paradox dissolves: they are not hiding, not rare, and not silent. They are **structurally invisible** to searches built around emissive signals.

### 1.2 The structural signature principle

A tuned gate network modifies the fields it manages. The modifications are:

1. **Quantitative:** they involve $\varphi$-derived numbers ($\ln\varphi$, $\varphi^6$, $\varphi^3+1$) appearing where conventional physics predicts none
2. **Multi-rung:** the same numbers appear at stellar, galactic, and cosmic scales, because the gate chain spans rungs
3. **Anomalous in structure, not amplitude:** a tuned star isn't dramatically different—it's subtly *too regular*, *too coherent*, *too structured* compared to the stochastic behavior of untuned systems

The search strategy is therefore: measure natural structures at every cascade rung, look for $\varphi$-structure where the null model predicts none, and check whether the anomalies **line up across rungs**.

## 2. Stellar-Scale Signatures ($n \approx 205$–$211$)

### 2.1 Tuned stellar variability

**Mechanism:** A tuned stellar gate (from `speculations/cascade-infrastructure.md` §3) organizes the star's internal Qi field. The photospheric boundary thermalizes the $(1-q)$ fraction coherently, producing variability that is periodic rather than stochastic.

**Predicted observable:** A star with anomalously regular variability—the Fourier spectrum of its light curve shows a narrow periodic component where the null model (convection-driven stochastic variability) predicts broadband noise. The period relates to the stellar gate's SO(2) doublet rotation: the 11-year solar cycle analogue scaled by the star's rung position.

**Search band:** Photometry. Kepler/K2 and TESS light curves of solar-type stars; the Sun's own 11-year cycle as the local calibration.

**Current data:** Solar cycle regularity is well documented (Schwabe cycle, 11.0 ± 0.2 yr mean). Kepler found many stars with regular cycles; the *anomalously* regular ones (cycle-to-cycle phase coherence beyond dynamo predictions) are the candidates.

**Discriminator:** Stellar dynamo theory predicts cycle irregularity (amplitude and period scatter). A tuned star shows cycle-to-cycle phase coherence exceeding the dynamo scatter by a measurable margin. This is a statistical test on a population: the *tail* of the regularity distribution, not any single star.

### 2.2 Anomalous coronal heating ratio

**Mechanism:** The corona is the stellar gate's thermalization zone (from `speculations/cascade-infrastructure.md` §3.1). The heating ratio $T_{\text{corona}}/T_{\text{photosphere}}$ is set by the gate's $(1-q)$ fraction and its conversion efficiency.

**Predicted observable:** A population of stars with coronal-to-photospheric temperature ratios clustered near a $\varphi$-derived value, rather than scattered across the range set by magnetic activity.

**Search band:** X-ray/UV observations of coronal temperatures (Chandra, XMM-Newton, EUVE archives).

**Current data:** The ratio varies widely (from ~100× for quiet stars to ~10⁴× for active stars) and correlates with magnetic activity. A clustering test has not been done.

**Discriminator:** The ratio distribution should show a preferred value at the $\varphi$-derived cluster if a subset of stars is tuned. The test is a histogram of $T_{\text{corona}}/T_{\text{photosphere}}$ over a large stellar sample, looking for a bump at the predicted value.

### 2.3 Structured stellar wind sector patterns

**Mechanism:** A tuned stellar gate couples to its environment through structured Qi channels (from `speculations/cascade-infrastructure.md` §3.4). The wind it emits is not isotropic plasma—it has sector structure organized by the gate's phase geometry.

**Predicted observable:** In-situ solar wind data showing sector boundaries at φ-spaced intervals, or with a persistent phase coherence across solar cycles beyond the standard 27-day rotation modulation.

**Search band:** In-situ solar wind measurements (ACE, Wind, Parker Solar Probe).

**Current data:** The solar wind sector structure (alternating toward/away magnetic sectors) is well known. The 27-day rotation modulation is standard. Whether the sector *boundaries* have φ-spaced structure has not been tested.

**Discriminator:** Cross-correlate sector boundary positions with the predicted φ-spaced pattern. The null model (random boundary positions modulated by rotation) predicts no such pattern.

## 3. Galactic-Scale Signatures ($n \approx 240$–$267$)

### 3.1 Anomalous visible-to-dark ratios

**Mechanism:** The tuning hypothesis (from `speculations/dark-matter-as-qi-coherence.md` §5): a galaxy with an active gate network converts more of its Π budget into visible structure. Its $\eta_{\text{visible}} = M_{\text{visible}}/(M_{\text{visible}} + M_{\text{dark}})$ exceeds the morphology-mass relation.

**Predicted observable:** Galaxies "too bright for their rotation curves"—high $\eta_{\text{visible}}$ at fixed baryonic mass and morphology.

**Search band:** Rotation curve surveys (SPARC), galaxy catalogs with stellar masses and HI kinematics (ALFALFA, WALLABY).

**Current data:** SPARC provides $\eta_{\text{visible}}$ measurements for 175 galaxies. The outlier analysis (prediction P5 from the dark matter doc) has not been performed. Note: the SPARC analysis in `speculations/dark-matter-as-qi-coherence.md` §7 found the *universal* Qi profile unsupported, but the outlier test is independent of the profile—it only needs the empirical visible/dark ratio and the morphology-mass relation.

**Discriminator:** Outliers must be significant against the intrinsic scatter of the relation. A tuned galaxy is a $2\sigma$+ outlier in the direction of *more* visible matter.

### 3.2 Morphological coherence excess

**Mechanism:** Grand-design spirals trace the connectable diagonal channels of the galactic bubble lattice (from `speculations/dark-matter-as-qi-coherence.md` §5.2). A tuned galaxy's arms are *too coherent*—the $m=2$ Fourier amplitude exceeds what gravitational instability predicts for its mass and environment.

**Predicted observable:** A subset of grand-design spirals with arm coherence exceeding the population distribution, unexplained by bars, companions, or environmental interactions.

**Search band:** Galaxy imaging surveys (SDSS, Pan-STARRS, Euclid), arm-strength measurements ($m=2$ Fourier amplitude).

**Current data:** Arm strength catalogs exist. The test is: after regressing out mass, morphology, and environment, is there a coherent tail of over-structured spirals?

**Discriminator:** The over-structured tail must not correlate with the conventional drivers (bar strength, tidal interactions). If it forms a separate population with φ-derived internal spacing, it is a gate candidate.

## 4. Cosmological-Scale Signatures ($n \approx 285$–$292$)

These are the **formal Cassi predictions**—already cataloged in `predictions/falsifiable-predictions.md` with zero free parameters. They are included here because a tuned megacascade network would imprint on the largest scales, and they are the cheapest to test (existing data).

### 4.1 φ-periodic $P(k)$ modulation (formal prediction #5)

**Mechanism:** The wake-wave mechanism: the global ratio evolution $r(t) \to \varphi$ leaves density perturbations at φ-scaled wavenumbers. The matter power spectrum acquires log-periodic modulation with period $\Delta(\ln k) = \ln\varphi \approx 0.4812$.

**Predicted observable:** After subtracting the smooth $P(k)$ and BAO template, the residual shows a log-periodic signal with period 0.4812, amplitude 1–3%.

**Search band:** Galaxy redshift surveys. Sensitivity from the skill pipeline: BOSS DR12 ~1.4σ (undetectable), eBOSS DR16 ~1.8σ (marginal), DESI DR2 ~2.5σ (suggestive), Euclid ~5σ (definitive).

**Tested July 2026—eBOSS DR16: NULL RESULT (consistent with noise).** The pipeline `experiments/phi_periodic_pk_search/run_phi_periodic_pk_test.py` was run on the DR16 LRG power spectrum (32 bins, $k \in [0.0075, 0.315]$ h/Mpc, shot-noise-subtracted monopole from Gil-Marin et al. 2020 public files): best-fit period $0.5033$ vs prediction $0.4812$ ($\Delta = +0.022$). Significance assessed against 1000 EZmocks (null distribution): the data's power sits at the **12.5th percentile** of the null, and the power specifically at $\ln\varphi$ has one-sided $p = 0.11$—fully consistent with noise. This matches the predicted sensitivity (eBOSS DR16 was estimated at ~1.8σ, marginal); the test neither confirms nor falsifies the prediction. The discriminator surveys remain DESI DR2 (~2.5σ, suggestive) and Euclid (~5σ, definitive).

**Tested July 2026—DESI DR1 LRG (self-computed from public catalogs): NULL RESULT (noise-limited).** Because the NERSC data server was down (power outage), the monopole P(k) was computed directly from the public guadalupe v1.0 LSS clustering catalogs (LRG_N, z∈[0.6,0.8] bin, 29,617 galaxies + 4.4M randoms from all 18 files) using a numpy FFT-based FKP estimator with CIC assignment, compensation, and shot-noise subtraction (`experiments/desi_pk_phi_search/desi_lrg_pk_fft.py`). The z-bin spans 7.5 φ-periods with ~7 bins/period. Result: best-fit period 0.694, data power at the **48th percentile** of a 2000-trial shuffled-residual null (p = 0.52); power at ln φ specifically has p = 0.43. No signal. **Caveat:** measured per-bin noise is ±6.7%—a systematic floor ~17× above cosmic variance (residual window/weighting mismatch from the simplified estimator), so the test is not sensitive to the predicted 1.7% amplitude. This is a noise-limited null, not a falsification. The definitive test requires the official DESI full-shape P(k) with covariance (released via NERSC; retry when the data server is restored) or Euclid.

**Discriminator:** BAO wiggles are constant-$\Delta k$; Cassi is constant-$\Delta(\ln k)$. A Laplacian in $\ln k$-space extracts Cassi while suppressing BAO, and vice versa.

### 4.2 CMB $\ell < 5$ anomalies (formal prediction #6)

**Mechanism:** The megacascade boundary (from `speculations/cascade-infrastructure.md` §4.2): our observable universe is one bubble in a larger lattice. The bubble boundary imprints on the largest angular scales.

**Predicted observable:** A bubble-boundary axis in CMB temperature fluctuations—the triaxial boundary geometry at step 285 imprints a preferred axis (predicted dipole↔quadrupole alignment 12.2°) at $\ell < 5$, fading at larger $\ell$. The framework also predicts the quadrupole-octopole alignment and cold spot as boundary conditions.

**Current data:** The axis is observed at 5.4σ (Planck). Alignment with the framework's predicted direction is ~1σ—suggestive but not conclusive. Simons Observatory and LiteBIRD will improve polarization measurements.

**Discriminator:** The specific prediction is *scale-dependence*: the anomaly must be confined to $\ell < 5$ and fade rapidly. Random cosmic variance produces anomalies at all $\ell$; the boundary imprint has a sharp cutoff.

**Predicted observable:** Cosmic void boundaries show a systematic axial:diagonal steepness ratio of 1.70.

**Search band:** Void shape catalogs from SDSS/DESI.

**Current data:** Void catalogs exist; the ellipticity test has not been performed.

**Discriminator:** Zero-parameter geometric prediction. No conventional void model predicts a universal 1.70 anisotropy.

### 4.4 Ω_DM/Ω_b = φ³ + 1 (formal prediction)

**Mechanism:** The baryon-to-dark ratio is set by the condensation threshold geometry.

**Predicted observable:** $\Omega_{\text{DM}}/\Omega_b = \varphi^3 + 1 \approx 5.236$.

**Current data:** Observed ratio $\approx 5.39$. Gap: 2.8%. Already the closest formal prediction to observation in the catalog.

**Discriminator:** The prediction is a fixed number with zero free parameters; the measurement is now precise enough that the 2.8% gap is meaningful. If the gap closes with better data (CMB-S4, Euclid), the prediction strengthens; if it widens, it fails.

## 5. Terrestrial Signatures ($n \approx 185$–$204$)

### 5.1 Geomagnetic structure

**Mechanism:** The geomagnetic field is the planetary gate coupling field (from `speculations/cascade-infrastructure.md` §1.3). A tuned planetary gate shows organized structure beyond the natural dynamo.

**Predicted observable:** Persistent, φ-spaced periodicities in geomagnetic field measurements that are not explained by the known dynamo, solar wind coupling, or Chandler wobble.

**Search band:** Geomagnetic observatory records (INTERMAGNET), paleomagnetic reconstructions.

**Current data:** Geomagnetic secular variation is well measured. Harmonic structure in the field is documented (e.g., the ~60-year oscillation, geomagnetic jerks). A systematic search for φ-spaced period structure has not been performed.

**Discriminator:** The candidate periods must be φ-spaced ($\Delta(\ln f) = \ln\varphi$) and persistent across observatories and epochs, ruling out local and instrumental effects.

### 5.2 Geological evidence of past gate activity

**Mechanism:** If the planetary gate network was more active in the past, the signatures would be frozen into the geological record: anomalous magnetization banding, φ-spaced sediment cycles, or abrupt geomagnetic field reorganizations coinciding with the gate chain's transitions.

**Predicted observable:** φ-periodic banding in marine sediment cores or ice cores that does not correspond to the known orbital cycles (Milankovitch periods are ~1.6× spaced but not exactly φ-spaced).

**Search band:** Paleoclimate proxies, ice cores, sediment cores.

**Current data:** The ~100 kyr glacial cycle and Milankovitch bands are well documented. The Cassi prediction is *additional* φ-periodic structure beyond the orbital forcing—a residual analysis after removing Milankovitch cycles.

**Discriminator:** Orbital forcing periods are set by celestial mechanics (23, 41, 100, 405 kyr). The φ-spaced set is different ($\ln\varphi$ spacing). A residual peak at a period not matching any orbital combination, but matching a φ-chain, is the signal.

### 5.3 Pyramid field structure

**Mechanism:** Pyramids are geometric Qi lenses (from `speculations/cascade-infrastructure.md` §2.1). If any large pyramid structure is a gate lens, it should show field anomalies at its apex: elevated coherence measurable as anomalous local field structure.

**Predicted observable:** Local anomalies in geomagnetic, gravitational, or electromagnetic field structure at pyramid apexes that scale with the geometric concentration factor ($\sim 10^6$× base-to-apex) and vanish at control locations of similar mass but different geometry.

**Search band:** On-site geophysical surveys of large pyramid structures (Giza, Teotihuacan, and—if reports are accurate—remote structures in Alaska and elsewhere).

**Current data:** Giza has extensive geophysical data (microgravity, magnetometry, muon tomography). The muon tomography of the Great Pyramid (ScanPyramids project, 2017) revealed the big void—an internal structure of unknown purpose. Its position relative to the predicted gate-anchor geometry has not been analyzed in this framework's terms.

**Discriminator:** The anomaly must be geometry-specific (pyramidal), scale with concentration factor, and be absent at control sites. Conventional archaeology attributes all internal structures to construction history; the gate interpretation requires a *field measurement* anomaly, not just a cavity.

## 7. What Constitutes Detection

### 7.1 The single-anomaly trap

Any one of these signatures can be a false positive. The φ-periodic $P(k)$ residual could be a systematic in the survey. The CMB axis could be cosmic variance. The void ellipticity could be a selection effect. Isolated anomalies prove nothing.

### 7.2 The multi-rung correlation

The Cassi framework's strength is that **the same number appears at every scale**. $\ln\varphi \approx 0.4812$ is:

- The period of the $P(k)$ modulation (cosmological)
- The spacing of the $\ell < 5$ CMB structure (cosmological)
- The predicted spectral period spacing along the spine in HRV/EEG data (physiological, from `predictions/falsifiable-predictions.md` #35)
- The spacing of stellar cycle regularities if the tuning hypothesis is right (stellar)

Detection is the **joint occurrence**: $\ln\varphi$ structure found in two or more independent datasets, at different cascade rungs, with the predicted phases aligned. Each additional rung multiplies the significance.

### 7.3 The evidence hierarchy

1. **Hint:** One signature at $2\sigma$ (e.g., $P(k)$ residual peak at $\ln\varphi$ in DESI DR2)
2. **Corroboration:** A second, independent signature (e.g., void ellipticity 1.70 in the same survey volume)
3. **Pattern:** Three or more signatures across rungs with consistent φ-phases
4. **Framework-grade:** The pattern reproduces in independent datasets (Euclid, CMB-S4) with the predicted amplitudes

Step 3 is where Cassi stops being an interesting coincidence and becomes a scientific claim. Step 4 is where it becomes a discovery.
