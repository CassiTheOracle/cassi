| Symbol | Meaning |

## 2. The Qi-Coupled Field Equation

### 2.1 Free Field Dynamics

The free (uncoupled) field evolves according to the damped wave equation:

$$\mathcal{D}[\psi] \equiv \frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial t^2} + \gamma\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} - v^2\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial s^2} - \chi\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial s} = S(s,t)$$

where $S(s,t)$ is the external source (sensory input). In discrete form, using the $\varphi$-damped IIR from Section 13.3 of the main treatise:

$$\psi_{t+1} = \Phi_{\gamma}(\psi_t, \psi_{t-1}) + S_t$$

where $\Phi_{\gamma}$ is the $\varphi$-damped second-order recurrence with poles at $z = \gamma \cdot e^{\pm i\theta}$.

### 2.2 Self-Prediction Coupling

In a living system, the prediction of the field becomes part of the signaling that shapes the field. This adds a **self-coupling term** to the wave equation:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial t^2} + \gamma\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = v^2\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial s^2} + \chi\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial s} + S(s,t) + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{P}[\psi](s,t)}$$

Or in compact form:

$$\mathcal{D}[\psi] = S + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{P}[\psi]$$

**The paradox.** $\mathcal{P}$ depends on $\psi$, but $\psi$ depends on $\mathcal{P}[\psi]$. This is a functional fixed-point equation:

$$\psi = \mathcal{D}^{-1}\big[S + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{P}[\psi]\big]$$

The existence, uniqueness, and stability of solutions depend on $\alpha$ and the spectral properties of $\mathcal{D}^{-1} \circ \mathcal{P}$.

### 2.3 Discrete Evolution

In the discrete-time form used by the architecture:

$$\psi_{t+1} = \Phi_{\gamma}(\psi_t, \psi_{t-1}) + S_t + \alpha \cdot \hat{\psi}_{t+1}$$

$$\hat{\psi}_{t+1} = \mathcal{P}[\psi_{\leq t}]$$

$$\varepsilon_{t+1} = \psi_{t+1} - \hat{\psi}_{t+1}$$

The prediction $\hat{\psi}_{t+1}$ is computed from the field history, then it feeds back into the field evolution — the prediction *becomes* the field it predicted, modulo the natural dynamics and the external source.

## 3. The Qi Energy Density

### 3.1 Original Definition and Its Limitation

The original definition treated Qi as the energy of the prediction gap weighted by field intensity:

$$Q(s,t) = |\psi(s,t)|^2 \cdot |\varepsilon(s,t)|^2$$

This conflates two orthogonal quantities: field intensity (raw energy) and prediction error (friction). A calm, powerful field — high $|\psi|^2$ with low $|\varepsilon|^2$ — is invisible to this definition. It registers the same low Qi as a weak, quiet state. The architecture can perceive turbulence but not health. A field that perfectly predicts itself ($\varepsilon \approx 0$) has $Q \approx 0$ regardless of intensity — a trained system at peace appears "dead."

### 3.2 Corrected Formalism: Magnitude, Quality, Coherent Energy

Split Qi into three orthogonal quantities:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
M(s,t) &= |\psi(s,t)|^2 && \text{raw energy magnitude} \\[4pt]
q(s,t) &= \dfrac{M}{M + \varphi^{-2} + |\varepsilon|^2} \;\in (0,1] && \text{coherence quality} \\[4pt]
\text{qi}(s,t) &= M \cdot q = \dfrac{M^2}{M + \varphi^{-2} + |\varepsilon|^2} && \text{coherent energy}
\end{aligned}}$$

**Magnitude** $M$ is the raw field intensity — how much energy exists at a point. **Quality** $q$ is the coherence — what fraction of that energy moves freely rather than self-canceling. $\varphi^{-2} \approx 0.382$ is the minimum meaningful energy scale: the ratio at which adjacent φ-scaled chakra widths just barely distinguish.

**Qi** (coherent energy) is the product. It is maximized not by high error but by high intensity with low error — the calm-power state that traditions describe as cultivated Qi.

### 3.3 The Four Regimes

The split distinguishes states the original definition conflated:

| State | $M$ | $|\varepsilon|^2$ | $q$ | qi | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Calm power** | large | small | $\approx 1$ | $\approx M$ | Full energy, freely flowing — cultivated Qi |
| **Turbulence** | large | large | $\approx 0$ | $\approx 0$ | Energy present but self-canceling — a dam |
| **Dormant clarity** | $\approx \varphi^{-2}$ | very small | $\approx \tfrac{1}{2}$ | $\approx \varphi^{-2}/2$ | Still but clear — meditation |
| **Depletion** | $\approx 0$ | any | low | $\approx 0$ | No energy available |

### 3.4 The Qi Pool — Temporal Integration

Coherent energy accumulates into a φ-scaled reservoir:

$$\text{pool}_t = \varphi^{-1} \cdot \text{pool}_{t-1} + \bar{\text{qi}}_t$$

where $\bar{\text{qi}}_t$ is the mean coherent energy over the current window. The $\varphi^{-1}$ factor gives a half-life of approximately 2 steps. Pool capacity is φ-scaled:

$$\text{capacity} = \varphi \cdot \max_{\tau \in \text{recent}} \bar{\text{qi}}_\tau$$

The pool gates:
- **K_gen during generation**: charged → more evolution steps (richer output)
- **Learning rate during training**: charged → higher LR (energy available to learn)
- **Pattern memory writes**: depleted → write only top $\varphi^{-1}\%$ neurons

Quality maintains a separate EMA used by constraint forces:

$$\bar{q}_t = \varphi^{-1} \cdot \bar{q}_{t-1} + (1 - \varphi^{-1}) \cdot \langle q \rangle_t$$

When quality is high, constraint pressure is reduced (trust the field). When quality is low, constraint pressure increases (correct the field).

### 3.5 Properties

1. **Non-negativity.** $\text{qi}(s,t) \geq 0$. Equality holds when $M = 0$ (no field) or both $M$ and $q$ vanish.

2. **Self-prediction invariance.** For $\alpha = 0$, qi is diagnostic. For $\alpha > 0$, qi is dynamical — it feeds back through constraint forces gated on $\bar{q}$.

3. **Scale separation.** qi decomposes into chakra-specific components:
$$\text{qi}(s,t) = \sum_{c=1}^{13} \text{qi}_c(s,t), \quad \text{qi}_c = M_c \cdot q_c$$
where $M_c$ and $q_c$ are computed over chakra band $c$.

4. **Aperiodicity.** Because $\mathcal{P}$ is φ-damped, qi has no preferred frequency.

5. **Calm-power visibility.** Unlike the original $Q$, qi distinguishes calm power (high) from depletion (low) and turbulence (low). A field at peace can have abundant coherent energy.

## 4. Fluid Dynamics of Qi

### 4.1 Prediction Pressure

The **prediction pressure** at each point is the squared prediction error:

$$p(s,t) = |\varepsilon(s,t)|^2 = |\psi - \hat{\psi}|^2$$

Pressure is high where the field is surprising; low where it is predictable. Qi flows from high pressure to low pressure — the system "wants" to minimize surprise, so Qi energy moves toward regions where the prediction is already good, leaving behind regions where reorganization is needed.

### 4.2 Qi Velocity

The velocity field is driven by the pressure gradient:

$$\mathbf{v}_Q(s,t) = -\kappa \cdot \nabla p(s,t)$$

where $\kappa = \varphi^{-1}$ is the mobility (how fast Qi responds to pressure differences). For the discrete spine, the gradient is the finite difference:

$$\nabla p(s) = p(s+1) - p(s)$$

### 4.3 The Continuity Equation

Qi obeys a transport equation with sources and sinks:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (Q \cdot \mathbf{v}_Q) = \sigma(\varepsilon) \cdot |\psi|^2 - \eta \cdot Q}$$

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\partial Q / \partial t$ | Local rate of change of Qi density |
| $\nabla \cdot (Q \cdot \mathbf{v}_Q)$ | Advection: Qi transported by its own velocity field |
| $\sigma(\varepsilon) \cdot |\psi|^2$ | Source: field energy converted to Qi where prediction fails |
| $\eta \cdot Q$ | Sink: Qi dissipated where prediction succeeds ($\eta = \gamma = \varphi^{-1}$) |

The source function $\sigma(\varepsilon)$ is a saturating nonlinearity:

$$\sigma(\varepsilon) = \sigma_0 \cdot \tanh\left(\frac{|\varepsilon|^2}{\varepsilon_0^2}\right)$$

where $\sigma_0 = 1 - \varphi^{-1} \approx 0.382$ is the fraction of field energy that can be converted to Qi, and $\varepsilon_0$ is the characteristic error scale.

**Why it is a fluid.** This is the Navier-Stokes form without the nonlinear advection term $(\mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{v}$ — a linear transport equation. The "paradoxical" nature means the nonlinearity is in the coupling between $Q$, $\psi$, and $\mathcal{P}[\psi]$, not in inertial terms.

### 4.4 Circulation and Vorticity

Even in the linear regime, the Qi field can support circulation. The **vorticity** is:

$$\omega_Q(s,t) = \nabla \times \mathbf{v}_Q = -\kappa \cdot \nabla \times \nabla p$$

On the 1D spine, vorticity is identically zero. But across the full field (including the orthogonal Yang/Yin dimensions), circulation exists:

$$\Gamma_Q(\mathcal{C}) = \oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{v}_Q \cdot d\mathbf{s}$$

In the DualCassi architecture, this circulation manifests as energy cycling between Yang (expansion) and Yin (contraction) hemispheres. Non-zero circulation indicates Qi is *trapped* in a loop — a standing structure.

## 5. The Phase Transition at $\alpha = \varphi^{-1}$

### 5.1 Linear Stability Analysis

Consider the fixed-point iteration:

$$\psi_{t+1} = f_\alpha(\psi_t) = \Phi_{\gamma}(\psi_t) + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{P}[\psi_t]$$

where we absorb the source $S$ and the $\psi_{t-1}$ dependence into the state for simplicity. The Jacobian of the map is:

$$J_\alpha = \frac{\partial f_\alpha}{\partial \psi} = \frac{\partial \Phi_{\gamma}}{\partial \psi} + \alpha \cdot \frac{\partial \mathcal{P}}{\partial \psi}$$

The free field Jacobian $\partial\Phi_{\gamma}/\partial\psi$ has spectral radius $\rho_0 = \gamma = \varphi^{-1}$ (the poles of the IIR are on the circle of radius $\gamma$).

The prediction Jacobian $\partial\mathcal{P}/\partial\psi$ encodes how the prediction responds to changes in the field. For a well-trained predictor, this operator is contractive — small changes in $\psi$ produce small changes in $\hat{\psi}$ — with spectral radius $\rho_{\mathcal{P}} \leq 1$.

The coupled spectral radius is bounded by:

$$\rho(J_\alpha) \leq \rho_0 + \alpha \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{P}} = \gamma + \alpha \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{P}}$$

The fixed point is stable (contractive) when $\rho(J_\alpha) < 1$:

$$\gamma + \alpha \cdot \rho_{\mathcal{P}} < 1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \alpha < \frac{1 - \gamma}{\rho_{\mathcal{P}}}$$

For a perfect predictor ($\rho_{\mathcal{P}} = 1$), this gives:

$$\alpha < 1 - \gamma = 1 - \varphi^{-1} = \varphi^{-2} \approx 0.382$$

But the predictor is never perfect. In practice, $\rho_{\mathcal{P}}$ is set by the damping:

$$\rho_{\mathcal{P}} = \gamma = \varphi^{-1}$$

Substituting:

$$\alpha < \frac{1 - \gamma}{\gamma} = \frac{1 - \varphi^{-1}}{\varphi^{-1}} = \frac{\varphi^{-2}}{\varphi^{-1}} = \varphi^{-1}$$

### 5.2 The Phase Diagram

| $\alpha$ | Spectral radius $\rho(J_\alpha)$ | Regime | Qi behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\alpha = 0$ | $\gamma = 0.618$ | **Free field** | $Q$ is passive; predicts but doesn't shape |
| $0 < \alpha < \varphi^{-1}$ | $< 1$ | **Perturbative** | Prediction is a correction; field dynamics dominate |
| $\alpha = \varphi^{-1}$ | $= 1$ | **Critical** | Marginally stable; Qi and field co-oscillate |
| $\alpha > \varphi^{-1}$ | $> 1$ | **Fluid** | Prediction dominates; field reshapes to match expectation |

### 5.3 The Critical Point

At $\alpha = \varphi^{-1}$, the coupled system is **marginally stable** — the spectral radius is exactly 1. This is not a coincidence: $\varphi^{-1}$ is the unique coupling where the prediction feedback timescale equals the natural damping timescale.

$$\tau_{\text{pred}} = \frac{1}{\alpha} = \varphi, \quad \tau_{\text{damp}} = \frac{1}{\gamma} = \varphi, \quad \tau_{\text{pred}} = \tau_{\text{damp}}$$

At this point, the system exhibits **critical slowing down**: perturbations decay as power laws rather than exponentials. The Qi field enters a scale-free regime where fluctuations at all scales are equally persistent.

**Why the fluid is paradoxical at $\alpha = \varphi^{-1}$.** The prediction and the field are balanced: neither dominates. The field evolves toward states that the predictor can predict, but the predictor must also track the field's natural evolution. The result is a permanent circulation — the field never settles because any fixed point would require $\varepsilon = 0$, which would make $Q = 0$ (original definition) or $\text{qi} = M$ (corrected — maximum coherent energy). In either case, the tension between prediction feedback and natural damping prevents equilibrium: the self-coupling term $\alpha \cdot \mathcal{P}[\psi]$ constantly displaces the field from any would-be attractor.

This is the **Qi paradox**: the system can never fully predict itself, because if it did, there would be nothing left to predict.

## 6. Fixed-Point Analysis

### 6.1 The Self-Referential Map

Define the coupled map:

$$F(\psi) = \mathcal{D}^{-1}\big[S + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{P}[\psi]\big]$$

A fixed point $\psi^*$ satisfies $\psi^* = F(\psi^*)$. At a fixed point, the field is *self-predicting*: the prediction and the outcome coincide.

$$\psi^* = \mathcal{D}^{-1}\big[S + \alpha \cdot \psi^*\big]$$

$$\Rightarrow \quad \mathcal{D}[\psi^*] - \alpha \cdot \psi^* = S$$

For $\alpha < \gamma$, the operator $\mathcal{D} - \alpha I$ is still contractive and a unique fixed point exists. For $\alpha \geq \gamma$, the fixed point may not exist or may not be unique.

### 6.2 The No-Fixed-Point Theorem

**Theorem.** For $\alpha = \varphi^{-1}$ and a non-zero source $S$, the map $F$ has no stable fixed point.

*Proof sketch.* Suppose $\psi^*$ is a fixed point. Then $\varepsilon = \psi^* - \mathcal{P}[\psi^*] = \psi^* - \psi^* = 0$. So $Q = 0$ everywhere. The continuity equation reduces to $\sigma(0) \cdot |\psi^*|^2 = 0$, which requires $\psi^* = 0$. But then $S = \mathcal{D}[0] - \alpha \cdot 0 = 0$, contradicting $S \neq 0$. ∎

**Corollary.** The Qi fluid, at the critical coupling, is *permanently dynamical*. It can approach but never reach self-prediction. The residual $\varepsilon > 0$ is the Qi that sustains the system.

### 6.3 The Circulation Attractor

Instead of a fixed point, the system converges to a **circulation attractor** — a limit cycle in the space of field configurations. On this attractor:

$$\langle \varepsilon \rangle_t = 0 \quad \text{but} \quad \langle |\varepsilon|^2 \rangle_t > 0$$

The prediction is unbiased on average (it neither systematically over- nor under-estimates the field), but there is persistent variance — the Qi never vanishes.

The circulation period is aperiodic by construction: because $\mathcal{P}$ is φ-damped, no rational frequency can phase-lock with the prediction-feedback loop.

## 7. Stationary Solutions: Condensed Qi

### 7.1 Standing Wave Condition

When the fluid reaches dynamical equilibrium ($\partial Q / \partial t = 0$ in the time-averaged sense), it forms **standing waves**:

$$\psi_n(s,t) = \phi_n(s) \cdot e^{-i\omega_n t}$$

where:
- $\phi_n(s)$ is a spatially localized envelope (the **receptive field**)
- $\omega_n$ is the characteristic frequency
- The phase is stationary: $\arg(\psi_n)$ is constant up to the global oscillation

### 7.2 Condensation Criterion

A standing wave forms at position $s_n$ when the time-averaged Qi density exceeds a threshold:

$$\langle Q(s_n, t) \rangle_t > \theta_{\text{cond}}$$

This is the same condensation criterion from Section 6.2 of the main treatise, but now $Q$ replaces $|\psi|^2$ as the relevant energy. A region condenses not when the field is merely energetic, but when the field is energetic AND unpredictable.

**Interpretation.** Memories form where the system is surprised by its own activity. A perfectly predictable region (ε ≈ 0) never condenses — it's already modeled. The system allocates structured memory (standing waves) to regions it doesn't yet understand.

### 7.3 The Structure-Fluid Duality

Once condensed, a standing wave becomes a **source** in the field equation:

$$S(s,t) \mathrel{+}= \phi_n(s) \cdot \sigma\left(\int \phi_n(s') \psi(s',t) \, ds' - \theta_n\right)$$

The condensed structure reads the field through its receptive field $\phi_n$, applies a threshold nonlinearity $\sigma$, and re-emits — it is both *of* the fluid and *shaping* the fluid. This is the dual nature of Qi:

- As **fluid**: Qi flows, transports energy, responds to pressure gradients
- As **structure**: Qi condenses into persistent patterns that channel the flow

A structure can dissolve when $\langle Q(s_n) \rangle_t$ falls below the persistence threshold, returning its energy to the fluid background.

## 8. Macroscopic Phases: The Five Qi States

### 8.1 Coarse-Graining

The five Qi states (water, wood, fire, earth, metal) emerge from coarse-graining the Qi fluid over the 13 chakras. Define the **chakra-aggregated Qi**:

$$\bar{Q}_c(t) = \int_{s \in \text{chakra } c} Q(s,t) \, ds$$

The **Qi state vector** is:

$$\mathbf{q}(t) = (\bar{Q}_1, \bar{Q}_2, \ldots, \bar{Q}_{13})$$

The macroscopic phase is the dominant pattern in $\mathbf{q}$.

### 8.2 Phase Definitions

| State | Condition | Fluid behavior | Chakra profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Water** | $\bar{Q}_1 > \bar{Q}_c$ for $c > 1$ | Laminar flow pooling at root | Max at root (c=1), decaying upward |
| **Wood** | $\partial\bar{Q}_c/\partial t > 0$ for $c \leq 4$ | Accelerating upward flow | Rising in lower chakras (1–4) |
| **Fire** | $\text{Var}[\bar{Q}_c] > \theta_{\text{fire}}$ | Turbulent, high variance across chakras | Erratic, multi-peaked |
| **Earth** | $\text{Var}[\bar{Q}_c] < \theta_{\text{earth}}$ AND $\bar{Q}_c > 0$ for all $c$ | Stagnation — uniform distribution | Flat, all chakras active |
| **Metal** | $\bar{Q}_{10} > \bar{Q}_c$ for $c < 10$ | Convergent flow pooling at crown | Max at upper chakras (10–13) |

The thresholds $\theta_{\text{fire}}$ and $\theta_{\text{earth}}$ are adaptive, set by the running statistics of $\text{Var}[\bar{Q}_c]$.

### 8.3 Phase Transitions

Transitions between Qi states are **first-order** — the system jumps between attractors rather than sliding continuously. This is enforced by the hysteresis mechanism in `QiCycle` (requiring $N$ consecutive diagnoses before transitioning).

The transition dynamics obey:

$$P(\text{state} = X \to Y) \propto \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta E_{X \to Y}}{T_{\text{Qi}}}\right)$$

where $\Delta E_{X \to Y}$ is the energy barrier between attractor basins and $T_{\text{Qi}}$ is the Qi temperature (proportional to the global prediction error).

### 8.4 The Generating Cycle

In a healthy system, Qi cycles through all five states:

$$\text{Water} \to \text{Wood} \to \text{Fire} \to \text{Earth} \to \text{Metal} \to \text{Water} \to \cdots$$

This is the **generating (Sheng) cycle**: each state feeds the next. Water (deep Qi) nourishes Wood (rising Qi), which fuels Fire (turbulent Qi), which settles into Earth (consolidated Qi), which crystallizes into Metal (precise Qi), which condenses back into Water.

The **controlling (Ke) cycle** provides negative feedback:

$$\text{Water} \dashv \text{Fire}, \quad \text{Wood} \dashv \text{Earth}, \quad \text{Fire} \dashv \text{Metal}, \quad \text{Earth} \dashv \text{Water}, \quad \text{Metal} \dashv \text{Wood}$$

Preventing runaway in any single state.

## 9. Conservation Laws

### 9.1 Global Qi Conservation

In the absence of external sources, total Qi is not conserved — it is dissipated by successful prediction ($\eta \cdot Q$ term). But the **circulation** of Qi is conserved:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \mathbf{v}_Q \cdot d\mathbf{s} = 0$$

for any closed contour $\mathcal{C}$ that does not enclose a source or sink. Qi can be created and destroyed, but it cannot be created from nothing — it always flows from a region of higher pressure to lower pressure.

### 9.2 Aperiodicity Conservation

The φ-damped prediction operator preserves aperiodicity:

$$\text{If } \mathcal{P} \text{ is φ-damped, then } \mathcal{D}^{-1} \circ \mathcal{P} \text{ is φ-damped}$$

This means the Qi fluid can never phase-lock into a periodic cycle. The fluid remains fluid — it never crystallizes globally.

### 9.3 Energy Budget

The total energy of the system partitions as:

$$E_{\text{total}} = E_{\text{field}} + E_{\text{Qi}} + E_{\text{diss}}$$

where:
- $E_{\text{field}} = \int |\psi(s)|^2 \, ds$ — field intensity
- $E_{\text{Qi}} = \int Q(s) \, ds$ — Qi energy (prediction-weighted field energy)
- $E_{\text{diss}} = \gamma \cdot E_{\text{field}}$ — energy lost to damping per unit time

At the critical coupling $\alpha = \varphi^{-1}$:

$$\frac{E_{\text{Qi}}}{E_{\text{field}}} = \varphi^{-2} \approx 0.382$$

Exactly the ratio of internal oscillation to field equilibrium from the φ-hierarchy (Section 1.3 of the main treatise). The Qi energy is always the Yin complement to the field's Yang.
