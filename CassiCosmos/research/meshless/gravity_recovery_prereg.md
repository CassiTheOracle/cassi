# Gravity recovery preregistration

Date frozen: 2026-08-31  
Status: frozen before recovery diagnostics or new GPU measurements  
Predecessor: `research/meshless/site_target_gravity_prereg.md`

## 1. Purpose

G61 showed that evaluating the tree at 8,192 sites is cheap but interpolating
the complete force back to particles is inaccurate. G63 showed that retaining
topology preserves the final particle force but a direct range-scan moment
refresh remains expensive and raw node IDs/ranges from a fresh atomic build are
not byte-identical.

This recovery wave tests two new mechanisms rather than rerunning either
rejected algorithm:

1. a standard far/near split: share the smooth far field at a particle's owner
   site and restore its rapidly varying local field by exact direct correction;
2. a hierarchical bottom-up moment reduction over a retained tree, measured in
   the queued regime in which the production engine actually runs.

Both mechanisms remain probe-only and default-off until every applicable gate
passes. No helix-dependent force or opening rule is admitted by this document.

## 3. Why the previous mechanisms can fail

The site field samples a softened inverse-square force generated by discrete
site masses. That field has its largest curvature inside the same Voronoi
neighborhood in which particles are being reconstructed. Nearest, inverse-
distance, and unconstrained affine interpolation all mix this local curvature
with the smooth far field.

The direct refit still executes `moments_main`, where every node scans its full
source interval twice. It therefore retains an $O(N\log N)$ range-scan even
after sort and split dispatches are removed. Its independently synchronized
measurement also contains a fixed submit/sync floor. Fresh node IDs are
allocated through atomic child reservations, so byte comparison by node slot is
not a valid semantic comparison unless node allocation itself is canonical.

These explanations are hypotheses until G67 and G70 measure them.

## 4. Arm H — owner-site far/near correction

For source site $j$ with chord weight $w_j$ and position $r_j$, define the
leaf-law contribution at target $x$ exactly as the production tree leaf:

$$
F_j(x)=-w_j\,\frac{x-r_j}
{\left(\lVert x-r_j\rVert^2+\epsilon^2+w_j^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}}.
$$

For each site $o$, $N_K(o)$ is its deterministic Euclidean nearest-site set,
including itself, with ties broken by site index. For particle $p$, $o(p)$ is
its nearest site under the same tie rule.

### H1 — owner-residual correction

$$
A^{H1}_K(p)=A_{site}(o)+
\sum_{j\in N_K(o)}\left[F_j(p)-F_j(r_o)\right].
$$

`A_site(o)` is the measured site-target tree force and already excludes source
$o$ at $r_o$; $F_o(r_o)=0$, so the formula needs no special case.

Frozen ladder: $K\in\{1,8,16,32,64\}$. The smallest passing K wins. Every K
is reported; no unregistered K may be substituted.

### H2 — local-corrected affine far field

H2 runs only if every H1 candidate fails. For each particle and each of its 16
nearest sample sites $s$, subtract the same owner-local source set:

$$
R_s=A_{site}(s)-\sum_{j\in N_K(o)}F_j(r_s).
$$

Fit the same four-column affine model `[1, dx, dy, dz]` used by Amendment A to
$R_s$, evaluate it at $p$, and add $\sum_{j\in N_K(o)}F_j(p)$. Frozen ladder:
$K\in\{8,16,32,64\}$; the smallest passing K wins.

- one site-target tree walk;

The control is the existing per-particle tree walk on the same targets. The

Otherwise Arm H is `REJECT` even if G68 passes.

## 5. Arm R — hierarchical retained moments

Arm R is independent of Arm H.

The retained tree's leaves are refreshed directly from `srcorder`. Internal
moments are then reduced from children, deepest level to root, using:

$$
W=\sum_c W_c,\qquad r=\frac{\sum_c W_c r_c}{W},
$$

$$
Q=\sum_c\left[Q_c+W_c\left(3d_cd_c^T-\lVert d_c\rVert^2I\right)\right],
\qquad d_c=r_c-r.
$$

Mean coherence is reduced as $q=\sum_cW_cq_c/W$. Leaf ranges with more than
one source retain the existing direct range scan. Depth is derived from node
half-width relative to the root; no host readback or parallel learned state is
introduced.

### G70 — retained-structure and force identity

After changing live mass and Yang/Yin values while holding source positions
fixed:

1. retained `srcorder`, `nr`, and `ncf` bytes before versus after refresh must
   be identical;
2. active node count must be unchanged;
3. hierarchical-refit per-particle gradient must be finite;
4. hierarchical-refit versus a fresh production build must have median relative
   difference `<= 1e-6`, p99 `<= 1e-5`, maximum `<= 1e-4`, and opposite-force
   fraction `0`.

Raw fresh-build node-slot identity is reported but is not a gate: the existing
atomic allocator is allowed to assign equivalent child groups different node
IDs. This changes the invalid internal-layout assertion, not the force
acceptance threshold.

### G71 — queued preparation cost

Measure GPU queue work without the independent submit/sync floor that dominated
G63. Record 32 consecutive preparations inside one compute list, then submit
and synchronize once. Alternate fresh and refit batches for three warmups and
eleven measured repetitions. Divide each batch wall time by 32.

The refit/fresh median per-preparation ratio must remain `<= 0.25`; the refit
must also be faster in every measured pair. Dispatch count, wall samples, and
all shader modes are recorded. Failure rejects Arm R.

### G72 — engine stability and default-off identity

Only after G70 and G71 pass may a default-off engine toggle be wired. Then:

- toggle off: the established focused gravity bytes and G30/G31 verdict remain
  unchanged;
- toggle on: at least 32 engine steps and one topology-generation transition;
- before the transition at least one refit occurs;
- the first gravity preparation after the transition is a full build;
- all readback positions, velocities, and gradients are finite.

Failure removes the production toggle and dispatch branch while retaining the
focused probe.

### G65 acquisition amendment — frozen 2026-08-31 before observation

The accepted Arm R engine runs on a windowed local RenderingDevice with
`tree_hierarchical_refit=true`, `meshless_mode=true`,
`meshless_gravity=true`, the regular two-fluid grid retained for the existing
spatial-phase detector, `grid_N=64`, 512 particles, seed 72, and `dt=0.001`.
The frozen field initialization is `field_attractor_init=true`,
`source_strength=0`, and unit box aspect; all other physics values use the
engine defaults.
Run 31 one-step engine calls, then record the complete live `EY`/`EI` grids
after steps 32, 33, and 34. These are the only registered snapshots; there is
no burn-in extension or seed search.
For each snapshot, calculate wrapped phase circulation on every oriented
elementary plaquette using the predecessor topology-observatory's open-domain
convention. Round circulation divided by `2π` to integer winding. A ring is a
closed connected component of nonzero dual plaquette segments under the same
deterministic component reconstruction. The registered quantities are:

- `rings`: number of closed nonzero-winding components;
- `winding_fraction`: nonzero plaquettes divided by `3 * grid_N^3`;
- `closure_residual`: the maximum absolute difference between a plaquette's
  unrounded circulation divided by `2π` and its rounded integer.

All `EY`/`EI` values and all three quantities must be finite. G65 passes only
if every snapshot has at least 3 rings, winding fraction at least `0.05`, and
closure residual at most `1e-5`, exactly as frozen in the predecessor
document. Any failed snapshot makes G65 `FAIL` and stops G66 without a helix
shader branch.

## 7. Decision vocabulary

- Gate result: `PASS` or `FAIL`.
- Arm result: `ADOPT` only after all applicable gates pass; otherwise `REJECT`.
- A negative result is final for the registered mechanism. A materially new
  mechanism requires a new frozen preregistration rather than an amendment made
  after inspecting its result.
