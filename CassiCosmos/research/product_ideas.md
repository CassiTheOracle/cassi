# Cassi Side-Project Ideas

## Status: Concept—planning only

## 1. Purpose

This document records small, sellable products that can grow out of the Cassi field work without turning every idea into a new platform. The intended pattern is:

- keep the foundational solver, protocols, and reusable code open source once licensing and provenance are settled;
- sell polished official builds, authored scenarios, export workflows, support, hosting, and commissioned work;
- use small products to develop reusable interaction, rendering, audio, and replay infrastructure for Cassi Cosmos Studio;
- label generative art and game mechanics as artistic or fictional when they are not validated physical models.

This is an idea portfolio, not a committed implementation roadmap. The separate cooperative multiplayer game specification is in [`cassi_coop_game_design.md`](cassi_coop_game_design.md).

## 2. Evidence boundary

The current Interactive Field Workbench supplies a credible foundation for bounded interactive products:

- paused `deposit`, `align`, and `impulse` operations;
- ordered command accounting and selected-region measurements;
- deterministic scenario save/replay;
- checkpoint and branch support in the controlled inline mode;
- viewport placement and deterministic field recipes in the next frontier;
- separate views for field intensity, bounded coherence, disequilibrium, density, and flow.

See [`interactivity_report.md`](interactivity/interactivity_report.md), [`next_frontier_report.md`](interactivity/next_frontier_report.md), [`interactivity_design.md`](interactivity/interactivity_design.md), and [`next_frontier_design.md`](interactivity/next_frontier_design.md).

The current evidence does **not** establish:

- a mobile deployment or mobile performance envelope;
- continuous live brush mutation in the production decoupled/site-native scene;
- cross-device deterministic multiplayer;
- a naturally persistent finite-separation double helix;
- realistic combustion, soot, radiative transfer, or a production fire solver;
- therapeutic, psychological, biological, consciousness, or human-energy effects.

The products below must therefore be framed as generative art, fictional worlds, games, creator tools, or reproducible software—not as validated physical observations or human mechanisms.

## 3. Shared product principles

Every small Cassi product should satisfy most of these conditions:

1. **Immediate hook:** the first interaction produces a legible change within one or two seconds.
2. **One primary verb:** the user should understand the main action without reading the theory.
3. **Causal organization:** the field state, paired channels, phase, balance, recovery, or actual scale readout changes the result. A golden-ratio palette or a decorative phi slider is not enough.
4. **Deterministic seeds:** a saved seed or scenario should reproduce the same result under the supported configuration.
5. **Exportable result:** a still, short loop, replay card, audio take, or standard asset is available quickly.
6. **Local-first operation:** no account, cloud rendering, or data collection is required for the core experience.
7. **Open foundation, paid convenience:** the official build, curation, support, and export workflow—not secret solver access—are the commercial layer.
8. **Bounded scope:** no product begins as an MMO, marketplace, plugin ecosystem, or always-on service.

## 4. Cassi Instruments

These are small tactile audiovisual applications that make organization felt rather than explained. The first products should share a field canvas, operation ledger, seed format, replay path, and export surface.

### 4.1 Cassi Field Loops

**Promise:** Make a living audiovisual loop in under three minutes.

**Loop:** choose a seed → place a `Deposit`, `Align`, or `Impulse` gesture → let the field evolve → switch between intensity, phase, balance, and flow views → save a replay or export a short loop.

**Minimum sellable scope:** one fixed 2D canvas, three tools, six authored seeds, two visual lenses, deterministic reset/replay, a poster export, and one fixed-duration video-with-audio export.

**Commercial shape:** a signed Windows build at approximately $15–$25, with the source and seed format public. A creator-oriented renderer can follow as a higher-priced product.

**Classification:** native paired-field/coherence-budget interaction plus artistic presentation. It is not a cascade-speedup claim and does not require a scientific interpretation.

### 4.2 Emberloom

**Promise:** A fingertip fire-and-smoke canvas where the smoke remembers the curl that created its flame.

**First ten seconds:** tap to kindle → drag a flame ribbon → cross it with a cool stroke → release → watch a smoke hook preserve the gesture.

**Minimum sellable scope:** one 2D canvas, three art-directed materials, warm/cool channel deposits, mouse/touch input, saved seeds, still export, and a six-to-eight-second loop export.

**Commercial shape:** approximately $6–$10 as a focused visual toy, or the hero material inside Cassi Field Loops.

**Boundary:** this is an art-directed field-fire effect, not a validated combustion, soot, radiative-transfer, or realistic fire simulator. A future realistic-fire research product would require a conventional fluid/combustion baseline and separate validation.

### 4.3 Paper Tide

**Promise:** A digital ink stone where two mineral pigments bloom, resist, and preserve the path of the hand.

**Loop:** press to bloom → draw a contrasting pigment through it → pause as the edge resolves → lock a postcard or save the settling loop.

**Minimum sellable scope:** four paper surfaces, four pigment pairs, press/drag/clear, deterministic seeds, and portrait/square/landscape export.

**Commercial shape:** approximately $5–$9. Its success criterion is that an exported artifact remains recognizable as Paper Tide outside the app rather than looking like generic marbling.

### 4.4 Inksea

**Promise:** Maintain an ink pool inside a surrounding fluid without freezing it or letting it dissolve.

**Loop:** strengthen a boundary → allow exchange → release accumulated imbalance → decide whether to remain distinct, merge, or divide.

**Game/art direction:** a gentle field study or survival-like composition where over-control produces a static knot and under-control produces washout.

**Boundary:** “balance” describes a local numerical game state, never a player, relationship, body, identity, morality, or mental-health condition.

### 4.5 Rungwell

**Promise:** A living infinite zoom where each luminous well contains a child field shaped by what the viewer did in its parent.

**Loop:** draw a small thread → select a bright well → dive → see the parent thread reappear as a large child-world ridge → bookmark or export the descent.

**Minimum sellable scope:** a 2D GPU projection, one gesture seed, prewarmed parent/child look-ahead, three materials, finite deterministic depth, saved seed cards, and portrait export.

**Authenticity requirement:** a child field must be independently evolved and seeded from parent state. A crossfade, random texture swap, ordinary Mandelbrot zoom, phi-colored palette, or repeated texture is not a Cassi cascade application.

**Commercial shape:** approximately $9–$15 once seam-free parent/child transitions are demonstrated.

### 4.6 Wisp Loom

**Promise:** Two anchors stretch luminous motes into a thread that tightens, tangles, and unravels into a constellation.

**Minimum sellable scope:** one 2D scene, two anchors, three behaviors, deterministic seeds, and still/loop export.

**Boundary:** the anchor-to-ridge behavior is a field-driven visual mechanism, not a claim about a stable physical two-strand attractor.

### 4.7 Knot Garden

**Promise:** Two people—or two thumbs—make a temporary luminous knot and save its release as a braid portrait.

**Minimum sellable scope:** two bounded anchors, a braid renderer, three palettes, and local capture.

**Boundary:** the braid is a guided artistic game state. It must not be sold as evidence that the canonical field naturally forms a persistent double helix.

### 4.8 Chime Current

**Promise:** A visual musical puddle where each touch creates a field pulse and one element of a short audiovisual phrase.

**Minimum sellable scope:** one field canvas, four bounded field-derived voices, four sound materials, event quantization, and video-with-audio export.

**Boundary:** the field supplies measured activity for the sound mapping; frequency anchors, timbre, and musical tuning remain artistic choices.

### 4.9 Windowfield

**Promise:** A quiet desktop field where cursor motion leaves slow local weather on an uncluttered screen.

**Minimum sellable scope:** one-screen mode, cursor deposits, three materials, auto-pause, local recording, and a safe idle mode.

**Commercial risk:** free screensavers and wallpaper products create a high polish bar. It should follow a successful exportable instrument rather than lead the portfolio.

### 4.10 Smoke Letters

**Promise:** Write one short word, ignite it, steer it once, and export an ephemeral smoke-and-ember seal.

**Minimum sellable scope:** short text, three type treatments, three materials, deterministic seed, and capture.

**Commercial risk:** generative typography is crowded. Treat this as a feature or preset before considering a standalone product.

## 5. Small playable products

These concepts turn the same field operations into short, replayable games rather than open-ended canvases.

| Concept | Player verb | Core tension | Approximate price |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Merge Window** | Deposit, align, and step two condensates toward a recoverable merge | More intensity can make the approach dramatic while making recovery worse | $6–$8 |
| **Rungweaver** | Allocate limited interventions across four visible scale bands | Fine detail can starve the larger organization | $8–$10 |
| **Threadkeeper** | Apply alternating pulses to guide two ridges through a corridor | Coupling risks coalescence; separation risks phase loss | $7–$9 |
| **Counterflow** | Decide when to correct, redirect, or do nothing | Too much correction can stall the current | $5–$7 |
| **Held Note** | Intervene once per unstable breath | Late or excessive help flattens the pattern | $3–$5 |
| **The Quiet Dissolve** | Compose sparse stamps, then decide when to stop touching | Control can destroy the later transformation | $5–$8 |
| **Mote Assembly** | Preserve a target number of clusters through alternating tides | Consolidation helps one phase and harms the next | $8–$10 |
| **Scale Courier** | Split, align, or advance a packet through four gates | Broad structure survives while fine detail washes out | $6–$8 |
| **Duet Field** | Coordinate complementary local interventions | Neither player can sustain the pattern alone | $7–$9 |
| **Afterimage** | Inspect a deterministic case and choose one intervention | Intensity, balance, density, and flow can disagree | $4–$6 |

The most promising first game is the cooperative **Tideweavers: Confluence Relay**, documented separately in [`cassi_coop_game_design.md`](cassi_coop_game_design.md).

## 6. Creator tools

These products have smaller audiences but stronger willingness to pay because the output is useful in someone else's work.

### 6.1 Cassi Loop Studio

A deterministic local renderer for motion designers, music-video editors, labels, science communicators, and technical YouTubers.

**Workflow:** choose a seed and visual lens → choose a camera rig and duration → preview → queue local renders → export video plus a JSON seed/settings receipt.

**Minimum sellable scope:** eight polished scenes, three camera rigs, four visual modes, 1080p/4K offline rendering, documented local transcode, and local render history.

**Commercial shape:** approximately $59–$99. The export is the product.

### 6.2 Cassi VFX Atlas

Turn evolving field states into standard game-art assets: flipbooks, glow bursts, density masks, distortion maps, and flow maps.

**Minimum sellable scope:** four effect families, fixed frame counts, an atlas packer, an alpha/mask mode, one reference material, and a JSON manifest.

**Commercial shape:** approximately $79–$149. Do not promise every engine or seamless temporal loops in v1.

### 6.3 Cassi Texture Forge

Create connected tileable material-map sets from one periodic field state.

**Minimum sellable scope:** 1024/2048 orthographic slices, six periodic presets, a tile-edge validator, color/scalar/normal-from-gradient outputs, and a contact sheet.

**Commercial shape:** approximately $49–$99. Keep v1 orthographic and standard-file based.

### 6.4 Cassi Audio-to-Field Video

Drop in user-owned audio; bounded audio features trigger field operations; the field creates the synchronized motion.

**Minimum sellable scope:** offline 1080p rendering, three fixed mappings, waveform/beat preview, MP4 output, and a mapping/seed receipt.

**Boundary:** this is an offline audiovisual renderer, not a live-performance host until latency and driver support are measured.

### 6.5 Cassi Sonifier

Render the existing broad multi-band field activity into local stems, cues, and optional matching visuals.

**Boundary:** the field-derived activity is real input to the mapping, while frequency conversion and musical tuning are artistic decisions.

## 7. Relationship to Cassi Cosmos Studio

The recommended portfolio sequence is:

1. Build a shared field-canvas, seed, replay, and export spine.
2. Release one small instrument, with **Emberloom** or **Paper Tide** as the first visual identity test.
3. Add **Rungwell** only after parent-to-child field transfer is independently demonstrated.
4. Build **Cassi Loop Studio** from the proven export path.
5. Fold the successful instruments, scenarios, and creator tools into Cassi Cosmos Studio.
6. Prototype the cooperative game as a bounded mode rather than making the Studio depend on a full online world.

A small product should be killed or folded into another product if its only differentiation is the Cassi name, phi decoration, a pretty screenshot, or an unmeasured physical claim.

## 8. Open-source-compatible monetization

Potential paid layers are:

- official signed builds;
- curated seeds, scenarios, palettes, sound materials, and replay treatments;
- reliable standard-file exports;
- creator workflows and batch rendering;
- bounded private hosting;
- installation, workshops, support, and commissioned scenes.

Avoid pay-to-win mechanics, paid influence over shared fields, private solver advantages, subscriptions before usage economics are measured, and any monetization that implies a buyer owns the foundational theory or code.

Before distribution, each active repository needs an explicit OSI-compatible software license, asset/model/audio provenance, dependency notices, and a trademark policy. The current source-visible state is not by itself a complete open-source release.

## 9. Shared kill criteria

- A new user cannot produce a recognizable result in the first ten seconds.
- The output is indistinguishable from a generic fluid or particle toy when Cassi labels are removed.
- Seeds cannot reproduce results under the supported configuration.
- Exported files require substantial manual cleanup.
- The product needs accounts, feeds, cloud infrastructure, or a marketplace to become interesting.
- A proposed physical or human-mechanism claim is doing more commercial work than the demonstrated software behavior.
- Support, mobile, or GPU scope expands beyond the one primary interaction.
