# Fine-Scale Transfer Dynamics and the Matter-Binding Comparison

## Status: Derived filtered identities and instantaneous obstructions / Conditional continuation estimate—September 2026

## Abstract

A Gaussian strain split isolates a part of the Navier–Stokes critical-energy transfer that ordinary kinetic energy controls. The remaining part has an exact ordered-triad representation and a differentiated response containing all generated Fourier modes. For the smooth periodic datum $(\sin y,\sin z,\sin x)$, the initial spatially averaged transfer vanishes at every filter width, while its fine-scale nonlinear response is strictly positive. A planar two-shell control supplies a different response whose sign depends on the declared split length. These statements constrain instantaneous relaxation laws; a time-integrated bound after viscous absorption remains open. The latest Cassi matter calculations give a useful comparison: self-consistent loading can admit an energy-lowering modulation, whereas unwound binding follows from a positive shifted gradient/potential energy at fixed carrier population. The ingredients behind that compactness argument have no established counterpart in the original Navier–Stokes dynamics.

## 1. The quantity that must remain controlled

Concentration can grow temporarily without causing a singularity. The required result is a bound that survives the full evolution at arbitrarily small scales. Use the original unforced equations on $(\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^3$, normalized spatial mean, real mean-zero velocity and viscosity $\nu>0$:
$$
\partial_tu=B(u,u)+\nu\Delta u,
\qquad B(u,u)=-\mathbb P[(u\cdot\nabla)u].
$$
With $\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{1/2}$, set
$$
\mathcal C=\sum_{k\ne0}|k||u_k|^2,
\qquad Y=\sum_{k\ne0}|k|^3|u_k|^2,
\qquad F=\operatorname{Re}\langle\Lambda u,B(u,u)\rangle.
$$
The exact budget from `turbulence/navier-stokes-transfer-boundary.md` is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal C'+2\nu Y=2F.}
$$
For $G_\ell$ with multiplier $e^{-\ell^2|k|^2/2}$,
$$
\tau_\ell=G_\ell(u\otimes u)-G_\ell u\otimes G_\ell u\succeq0,
\quad S_\ell=\operatorname{sym}\nabla G_\ell u,
\quad \Pi_\ell=-S_\ell:\tau_\ell.
$$
Smoothness gives convergent representations
$$
\mathcal C=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty\ell^{-2}\langle\operatorname{tr}\tau_\ell\rangle\,d\ell,
\qquad
F=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty\ell^{-2}\langle\Pi_\ell\rangle\,d\ell.
$$
The Gaussian is a positive spatial averaging operator. Covariance positivity is essential to the bounds below.

## 2. An exact strain split with a controlled coarse contribution

Large-scale strain can be bounded without a geometric assumption. Fix $L>0$ and put
$$
u_c=G_Lu,\qquad u_f=u-u_c,\qquad
S_{\ell,c}=\operatorname{sym}\nabla G_\ell u_c,
\qquad S_{\ell,f}=S_\ell-S_{\ell,c}.
$$
Both contractions use the full covariance:
$$
F_c=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty\ell^{-2}\langle-S_{\ell,c}:\tau_\ell\rangle\,d\ell,
\qquad
F_f=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty\ell^{-2}\langle-S_{\ell,f}:\tau_\ell\rangle\,d\ell.
$$
Thus $F=F_c+F_f$. The split changes neither the equation nor its mode content. In particular, $F_f$ includes interactions of fine strain with the complete velocity stress.

Define the explicit torus constant
$$
K_L=\left(\sum_{k\ne0}|k|^2e^{-L^2|k|^2}\right)^{1/2}<\infty.
$$
Fourier Cauchy–Schwarz and $G_\ell G_L=G_{\sqrt{\ell^2+L^2}}$ give
$$
\|S_{\ell,c}\|_{L^\infty,F}\le K_L\|u(t)\|_2.
$$
For a symmetric matrix $S$ and positive covariance $\tau$,
$|S:\tau|\le\|S\|_F\operatorname{tr}\tau$. The positive representation of $\mathcal C$ and kinetic-energy inequality therefore yield
$$
\boxed{|F_c(t)|\le K_L\|u_0\|_2\mathcal C(t).}
$$
This conservative constant uses the gradient norm; incompressibility permits a sharper Fourier estimate. Its finiteness at fixed $L$ is the relevant property here. On $\mathbb R^3$, the corresponding Gaussian calculation is
$$
\|\nabla G_L\|_2^2=\frac3{16\pi^{3/2}L^5},
\qquad
|F_c|\le\frac{\sqrt3}{4\pi^{3/4}}L^{-5/2}\|u_0\|_2\mathcal C.
$$
Under Euclidean Navier–Stokes rescaling, $L\mapsto L/\lambda$ and $\|u_0\|_2\mapsto\lambda^{-1/2}\|u_0\|_2$, so this rate scales as $\lambda^2$. A decreasing choice of $L(t)$ requires an additional integral bound on its growing coefficient.

## 3. The resolved cubic product remains in the fine-scale dynamics

The exact triad formula retains a term that a coarse-output estimate alone would miss. For $a=p+q$, write
$$
T_{a;p,q}=\operatorname{Re}\left[u_a^*\cdot\{-i\mathbb P_a(q\cdot u_p)u_q\}\right],
\quad h_a=e^{-L^2|a|^2/2},
\quad \mu_{a,p,q}=\sqrt{(|a|^2+|p|^2+|q|^2)/2}.
$$
Integration by parts in the spatial mean gives
$$
\langle\Pi_{\ell,c}\rangle
=\sum_{a=p+q}h_a
\left[e^{-\ell^2\mu_{a,p,q}^2}-e^{-\ell^2|a|^2}\right]T_{a;p,q}.
$$
The second covariance term contributes the first exponential in the brackets. It cannot be discarded when the strain is generated by a differently filtered velocity. The Gaussian integral gives
$$
\boxed{F_c=\sum_{a=p+q}h_a(|a|-\mu_{a,p,q})T_{a;p,q}.}
$$
At $L=0$, the symmetric triad factor $\mu$ cancels on summing all velocity legs by the energy-conserving trilinear identity. Thus $F_c=F$ in that limit. At nonzero $L$, the output-dependent $h_a$ prevents that cancellation.

The fine functional has weight
$$
W^L_{a,p,q}=|a|-h_a(|a|-\mu_{a,p,q}).
$$
For a direction $v$, its derivative is
$$
\begin{aligned}
D F_f[u][v]=\sum_{a=p+q}W^L_{a,p,q}\operatorname{Re}\bigl[
&v_a^*\cdot\{-i\mathbb P_a(q\cdot u_p)u_q\}\\
+&u_a^*\cdot\{-i\mathbb P_a[(q\cdot v_p)u_q+(q\cdot u_p)v_q]\}\bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
Along Navier–Stokes, $v=B(u,u)+\nu\Delta u$. Every slot includes modes generated by the full Leray-projected convolution. The nonlinear derivative is quartic in velocity amplitude; the viscous derivative is cubic.

At fixed small output $a$ with $p\simeq-q$ large, $\mu_{a,p,q}$ is large. Output smoothing alone therefore gives no bound on the individual fine weights. The summed covariance estimate in §2 handles the coarse term without this invalid modewise step. A useful fine estimate must retain the angular and polarization cancellations of the original interaction, or obtain equivalent control from its evolution.

## 4. Positive averaged transfer develops from a zero-transfer field

A fully admissible initial datum has a positive fine-scale response without any initial pressure gradient. Take
$$
u_0=(\sin y,\sin z,\sin x).
$$
Its nonlinear acceleration is
$$
B(u_0,u_0)=-(\sin z\cos y,\sin x\cos z,\sin y\cos x).
$$
This vector is divergence-free, so its Leray projection is unchanged. The initial pressure is constant because $\operatorname{tr}[(\nabla u_0)^2]=0$. The nonlinear acceleration remains nonzero.

The velocity occupies $|k|=1$, whereas its nonlinear acceleration occupies $|k|=\sqrt2$. Hence
$$
\mathcal C(0)=Y(0)=\frac32,\qquad
\|B(u_0,u_0)\|_2^2=\frac34,
\qquad F(0)=F_c(0)=F_f(0)=0.
$$
Differentiating the spatially averaged transfer gives
$$
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\langle\Pi_\ell\rangle\right|_0
=\frac34\left(e^{-\ell^2}-e^{-2\ell^2}\right)>0.
$$
The generated-output slot in the coarse derivative has $|a|=\mu=\sqrt2$, so its coarse weight vanishes. The remaining slots have $|a|=1$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{
F'(0)=\frac34(\sqrt2-1),\qquad
F_f'(0)=(1-e^{-L^2/2})\frac34(\sqrt2-1)>0.
}
$$
The viscous contribution to these first derivatives is zero by shell orthogonality. This is a global spatial-average calculation, separate from the pointwise isotropy witness in `turbulence/navier-stokes-stress-geometry.md` §7.

For fixed $L>0$, smooth local existence gives
$$
\int_0^tF_f(s)\,ds
=\frac38(1-e^{-L^2/2})(\sqrt2-1)t^2+O(t^3).
$$
The signed accumulated fine transfer is positive for sufficiently small positive time. The critical norm nevertheless initially decreases:
$$
\mathcal C'(0)=-3\nu,\qquad
\mathcal C''(0)=\frac32(\sqrt2-1)+6\nu^2.
$$
Viscosity and nonlinear transfer have separate roles in this expansion. Positive accumulated transfer alone neither violates a viscous absorption estimate nor produces a singularity.

## 5. The instantaneous response also admits negative signs

A second exact control distinguishes nonlinear response from a universal restoring law. Use the real planar field specified by positive modes
$$
p=(1,0,0),\quad u_p=i(0,1,0),\qquad
q=(0,2,0),\quad u_q=i(1,0,0).
$$
Its initial transfer is zero, and the generated modes lie on $|k|=\sqrt5$. Two-dimensional Euler energy and enstrophy cancellations, or direct differentiation of all cubic slots, give
$$
D F[B]=\|B\|_2^2(\sqrt5-7/3)<0.
$$
For the Gaussian split,
$$
\boxed{
D F_f[B]=\frac{\|B\|_2^2}{3}
\left[(1-e^{-L^2/2})(1-\sqrt5)
-4(1-e^{-2L^2})(2-\sqrt5)\right].
}
$$
The separate contributions change with $L$. Their signs concern the declared transfer decomposition, while the full velocity obeys the same equation. The cyclic and planar controls retain both generated modes and the original incompressibility projection.

## 6. The cumulative estimate that remains open

A proof can permit positive episodes if their total effect is bounded. One sufficient target is
$$
F_f\le\theta\nu Y+a_f(t)\mathcal C,\qquad
0\le\theta<1,\qquad a_f\ge0,
$$
with $\int_0^T a_f(t)\,dt$ bounded using admissible initial data, viscosity and finite time. Let $a=K_L\|u_0\|_2+a_f$. Then
$$
\mathcal C'+2(1-\theta)\nu Y\le2a\mathcal C,
\qquad
\mathcal C(t)\le\mathcal C(0)e^{2\int_0^t a(s)ds}.
$$
The integrating factor also controls $\int_0^T Y$. Interpolation gives $u\in L^4(0,T;\dot H^1)\subset L^4(0,T;L^6)$, a Serrin continuation class.

The standard trilinear estimate supplies only
$$
F_f\le K\mathcal C^{1/2}Y+K_L\|u_0\|_2\mathcal C.
$$
For large critical data, the coefficient $K\mathcal C^{1/2}$ cannot be absorbed by a fixed fraction of $\nu$. It leaves an uncontrolled multiple of $Y$. The exact differentiated functional in §3 introduces quartic interactions rather than a sign-definite replacement for this term.

The fixed multiscale control also tests absorption without a growth term. For eight times the three-coordinate fixture, $L=2$ and $\nu=0.01$, the qualified value is
$$
\frac{F_f-\nu Y/2}{\mathcal C}
=0.029379280637034728>0.
$$
Thus $F_f\le\nu Y/2$ fails on this admissible initial datum. A positive data-controlled growth term remains allowed in the sufficient estimate above. The fixture, amplitude, viscosity and split length belong to the fixed schedule; no coefficient is fitted to this result.

The full stress equation retains third moments, pressure covariance and dissipative gradient covariance; the strain equation retains the pressure Hessian and stress-divergence derivatives. Pressure redistributes the dynamics and does not supply a separately signed global dissipation. These terms must remain in any feedback argument. Tao's averaged-equation construction retains energy cancellation and similar scaling estimates while permitting blow-up. A complete proof therefore needs an additional property of the original interaction beyond those generic estimates. The coarse estimate by itself supplies no such property.

The anisotropy envelope also remains conditional. Define $R_\ell=\|\tau_\ell-(\operatorname{tr}\tau_\ell)I/3\|_F/\operatorname{tr}\tau_\ell$ when the trace is positive, and $R_\ell=0$ otherwise; set $G(t)=\sup_{x,\ell}\|S_\ell(x,t)\|_F R_\ell(x,t)$ and $S=\operatorname{sym}\nabla u$. Positivity gives $R_\ell\le\sqrt{2/3}$ and
$$
G(t)\le\sqrt{2/3}\|S(t)\|_{L^\infty,F}
\le\sqrt{2/3}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^\infty,F}.
$$
The cyclic field has $R_\ell=0$ at maximum-strain points at its initial instant. This excludes a universal positive pointwise lower bound on $R_\ell$ there. It establishes no strict improvement of the corresponding time-integrability criterion.

### 7.2 Unwound binding uses a coercive constrained energy

The unwound scalar class has a stronger variational result. With $u=\sqrt\rho$ in this subsection only, carrier amplitude $c$ and fixed population $N=\int c^2$, its energy is
$$
\mathcal E=\int\left[\frac{K_x}{2}|\nabla u|^2+\frac{K_{Cx}}{2}|\nabla c|^2+W(u^2,c^2)\right]dx.
$$
A fixed-population shift gives
$$
\boxed{
\mathcal E+\eta_C\rho_0N
=G_{\rm mat}+\int\left[
\frac{\lambda_\rho}{4}(u^2-\rho_0)^2+
\eta_Cu^2c^2+\frac{\lambda_C}{2}c^4\right]dx.
}
$$
Every displayed term on the right is nonnegative. Together with the stated boundary and coefficient assumptions, this controls the $H^1$ norms of $u-\sqrt{\rho_0}$ and $c$. Here $G_{\rm mat}$ is the sum of the two gradient-energy integrals. Rearrangement, compactness and the population dilation
$$
\mathcal E_s=s\mathcal E-(s-s^{1/3})G_{\rm mat}
$$
give strict fixed-total-population subadditivity when binding occurs and an attained minimizer. The witness excludes binding below the sufficient bound $N_{\rm low}=15.2918967$ and supplies a negative admissible trial at population approximately $15497.13$. Its exact critical population remains uncomputed.

This result establishes conditional binding and an energetic preference for coalescence. It supplies no merger trajectory or selected elementary population; energy per carrier continues to decrease toward its bulk limit. The unwound vacuum boundary and the fixed-winding periodic cylinder describe different variational classes.

For original Navier–Stokes, kinetic energy supplies no corresponding positive potential or fixed carrier population. On $\mathbb R^3$, a nonzero smooth compactly supported solenoidal velocity $v$ gives the family of initial data $v_\lambda(x)=\lambda^{3/2}v(\lambda x)$. Its kinetic energy remains fixed while $\|v_\lambda\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}^2=\lambda\|v\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}^2$. This energy-preserving reconcentration differs from the Navier–Stokes evolution rescaling. The matter compactness proof therefore requires structural ingredients beyond the NS energy law. A transferable construction would have to derive a coercive functional and its production bound from NS itself.
