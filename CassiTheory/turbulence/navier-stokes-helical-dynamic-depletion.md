# Helical Geometry and the Dynamical Depletion of Vortex Stretching

## Status: Tested finite helical families / Universal sign depletion contradicted / Open scale-uniform direction-strain estimate—September 2026

## Abstract

Coherent spiral or helical vortex geometry does not by itself make the
vortex-stretching production negative in the original three-dimensional
incompressible Navier–Stokes equation. A pre-registered Fourier–Galerkin
matrix evolves eight initial-data families at two cutoffs with a timestep
refinement and measures the signed production

$$
P(t)=\int_{\mathbb T^3}\omega\cdot S\omega\,dx
$$

at every accepted state. The exact Beltrami control holds $P$ at roundoff, as
its closed-form heat-flow solution requires. The homochiral-mode and
opposite-helicity-mode families stay nonpositive throughout the finite
horizon. The wide, narrow, tight-pitch, two-scale and opposite-handed tube
families all develop positive $P$ by the first nonzero checkpoint $t=1/8$;
the opposite-handed family does so while its normalized helicity stays near
$10^{-18}$.

The finite matrix therefore classifies the universal sign statement for the
declared helical families as **CONTRADICTS**. Cancellation of signed helicity
and visual coherence leave the strain-frame alignment uncontrolled. A
scale-uniform initial-data-controlled estimate for vorticity-direction
coherence together with the nonlocal strain remains **UNRESOLVED**, and no
continuation theorem, singularity construction or regularity statement is
produced here.

## 1. Stretching production and direction geometry

Vortex stretching enters the enstrophy budget through the cubic production

$$
E=\frac12\int|\omega|^2dx,\qquad
G=\frac12\int|\nabla\omega|^2dx,\qquad
P(t)=\int\omega\cdot S\omega\,dx,\qquad
E'(t)+2\nu G(t)=P(t),
\tag{HDD1}
$$

with $\omega=\nabla\times u$ and $S=\tfrac12(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\mathsf T})$.
Writing $\xi=\omega/|\omega|$ where $\omega\ne0$ isolates the geometric factor,

$$
P(t)=\int|\omega|^2\,\xi^{\mathsf T}S\xi\,dx
=\int|\omega|^2\sum_{j=1}^3\lambda_jc_j^2\,dx,
\qquad \sum_j\lambda_j=0,
\tag{HDD2}
$$

where $\lambda_j$ are strain eigenvalues and $c_j$ the components of $\xi$ in
a strain eigenframe. The sign of $P$ is decided by the weighted longitudinal
strain $\xi^{\mathsf T}S\xi$, not by how ordered the vortex lines look.

The direction field obeys

$$
(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla)\xi
=(I-\xi\otimes\xi)S\xi
+\nu\left[\Delta\xi+2(\nabla\log|\omega|\cdot\nabla)\xi+|\nabla\xi|^2\xi\right]
\tag{HDD3}
$$

on the nonzero-vorticity set. Transverse strain, vorticity-magnitude
gradients, the full three-dimensional direction gradient and the nonlocal
strain generated by every scale all act on $\xi$. A helical centerline
controls only selected one-dimensional geometry, and for a tube of core
radius $r$ and circulation $\Gamma$ the amplitude scales as
$|\omega|\sim\Gamma r^{-2}$ while transverse gradients of $|\omega|$ and of
$\xi$ scale as $r^{-1}$. Shrinking that geometry supplies no
cutoff-independent coherence constant.

## 2. Declared families and run matrix

The frozen schedule lists eight families, each rescaled after Fourier
projection to $K(0)=\tfrac12\|u\|_2^2=1$ on the $2\pi$-periodic torus with
$\nu=1/10$ and horizon $T=1/2$:

1. the exact Beltrami control $u_B=(\sin z,\cos z,0)$ with $\omega_B=u_B$;
2. four interacting homochiral modes with a common curl sign at
   $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$, $(1,1,0)$ and $(2,1,0)$;
3. opposite-helicity equal-energy modes at $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0)$ with two
   further fixed modes;
4. a wide helical tube with $a_c=3/4$, $m=1$, $r=1/2$;
5. the same centerline at $r=1/4$;
6. a tight-pitch tube with $m=4$, pitch $\pi/2$ per turn, $r=1/4$;
7. two separated tubes of common handedness at scales $(3/4,1)$ and
   $(3/8,4)$;
8. two separated tubes with windings $m=+3$ and $m=-3$ and near-cancelling
   signed helicity.

Each family runs at $N=16$ and $N=32$ on the odd product grid $M=6N+1$ with
1024 equal RK4 steps in float64, plus a 2048-step $N=32$ refinement: 16
primary and 8 refined trajectories, and checkpoints at
$t/T\in\{0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1\}$. The receipt binds the protocol revision `A1`,
which fixes the mode list and the normalization convention that the
zero-helicity comparison value is the volume-normalized $P(0)=1/2$ of the
fixed-periodic control in `computations/navier-stokes-helical-spread-prereg.md`.

direction variation is the initial weighted direction-gradient RMS $D_\xi(0)$,
and the helicity column is the normalized $H/C$ at $t=0$.

The tube families start from production at machine level: the wide, narrow,
tight-pitch and two-scale families have $P(0)\lesssim10^{-16}$
($4.0\times10^{-17}$, $2.0\times10^{-16}$, $5.7\times10^{-16}$ and
$3.7\times10^{-17}$ respectively) and the opposite-handed family has
$P(0)=-7.4\times10^{-18}$. By the first nonzero checkpoint their signed
production is already positive,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{\text{wide}}(1/8)&=1.05\times10^{-3},&
P_{\text{narrow}}(1/8)&=3.34\times10^{-3},\\
P_{\text{tight}}(1/8)&=2.18\times10^{-2},&
P_{\text{two-scale}}(1/8)&=1.12\times10^{-3},\\
P_{\text{opposite-handed}}(1/8)&=1.24\times10^{-2},&&
\end{aligned}
\tag{HDD4}
$$

while the two mode families remain negative at that time
($-1.79\times10^{-3}$ and $-2.10\times10^{-2}$). The final signed values of
the tube families are the maxima reported above to three digits; the narrow
and tight-pitch families end at $2.74\times10^{-3}$ and
$7.16\times10^{-3}$, and the opposite-handed family at
$6.40\times10^{-3}$.

Signed helicity obeys

$$
H'=-2\nu\int\omega\cdot(\nabla\times\omega)\,dx,
\tag{HDD5}
$$

so its nonlinear contribution cancels from the helicity budget. The
opposite-handed family carries $H/C=-4.7\times10^{-18}$ initially and
$-9.7\times10^{-18}$ at the final checkpoint while producing positive
stretching throughout the measured window. The tight-pitch family shows the
same decoupling in the opposite direction: its normalized helicity is
$-2.4\times10^{-2}$ initially and $+4.5\times10^{-1}$ at the end, with
positive production at every checkpoint from $t=1/8$ on. $H=0$ or small $H/C$
supplies no sign for $P$.

## 4. Reading of the stressors

The wide-to-narrow radius change multiplies the local positive stretching by
five ($7.82\times10^{-2}$ to $3.94\times10^{-1}$) and raises the signed
maximum by about $63\%$. The tight-pitch family, which carries the largest
initial direction variation in the matrix ($D_\xi(0)=3.88$), produces the
largest signed maximum, $2.37\times10^{-2}$, roughly an order of magnitude
above the wide-tube value. The two-scale mixture lands close to the wide-tube
response, and the opposite-handed pair behaves like the single-scale tubes at
the same radius even though its total helicity is numerically zero.

Each stressor isolates a distinct uncontrolled channel of (HDD3): transverse
gradients under $r\to0$, rapid geometric variation under decreasing pitch,
cross-scale triadic contributions in the two-scale case, and the absence of
any sign inheritance from global handedness in the opposite-handed case. The
matrix uses one viscosity and one horizon; it identifies these channels but
does not quantify their growth in a cutoff-uniform way.

## 5. Exact Beltrami control

Every helicity eigenmode has $\omega=\lambda u$, so
$u\times\omega=0$ and the nonlinear term collapses to a pressure gradient:

$$
(u\cdot\nabla)u=\nabla\frac{|u|^2}{2}-u\times\omega,\qquad
\omega_B=u_B.
\tag{HDD6}
$$

The control trajectory is therefore the exact heat flow and $P(t)=0$
identically. The measured maximum absolute production over the 4099 recorded
state evaluations of its three resolutions is $1.04\times10^{-33}$, with
$H/C=1$ at both ends. This is the only
family in the matrix whose depletion is exact, and it is exact because the
field is a single curl eigenmode, not because its vortex lines are helical.
The homochiral modes share a curl sign but span several wavenumbers, so they
are not eigenmodes and inherit none of this structure.

## 7. Scope and the open estimate

The matrix measures one viscosity, one horizon, two cutoffs and eight
declared families in a finite Galerkin truncation. A positive production
episode in a coherent helical field shows that a shared curl sign or a
visually ordered tube does not supply a sign-definite depletion. It supplies
no cutoff-uniform estimate, no blow-up construction and no regularity
statement, and finite nonpositive trajectories in the two mode families
establish no general inequality either.

A conditional continuation route would need an initial-data-controlled,
cutoff-independent bound on the alignment factor together with the nonlocal
strain, strong enough to give
$\int_0^T\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^\infty}dt<\infty$ or a quantitatively equivalent
criterion. The $L^3$-in-time direction-coherence condition of
Constantin–Fefferman is conditional on a spatial coherence modulus, and its
propagation from arbitrary data remains a separate obligation. The present
result fixes the empirical constraints such an estimate must respect: shrinking
radius, shrinking pitch, multiscale interaction, opposite-handed
superposition and direction variation that visual organization does not
bound. The ledger record in `field-experience/probe-outcome-ledger.md`
carries the frozen classification.
