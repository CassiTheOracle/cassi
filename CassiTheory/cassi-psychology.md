## 1. Two Fields, Coherence, and the Gate

Your mental life, on this account, runs on a physical substrate that fills all space: two fields, Yang and Yin, present at every point. Yang is the expansive fluid: it drives change and breaks equilibrium. Yin is the contractive fluid: it restores and receives. The framework does not say the fields are made of anything else—they are its primitives. Whether the field in the room around you holds a mind is exactly what the framework does not claim; the category boundary of Part I limits the claim to fields that hold the self-modeling structure. Everything this document describes—thought, mood, trauma, therapy—is a configuration of these two. The two fields convert into each other, and the conversion is thermostat-like: excess Yang becomes Yin, excess Yin becomes Yang, pushing every point toward a golden balance. The balance point is the golden ratio, the number that keeps structure alive at every scale (§20.1). At the golden ratio conversion stops locally—at that point, and only at that point. The felt correlate of conversion is emotional change itself—a mood lifting, an irritation dissolving; the moment the imbalance moves, that movement is conversion (Hypothesized mapping). Read psychologically, monotony is resonance and vitality is de-resonance (interpretive).

Coherence decides what a region of the field can do. The framework writes it as $q$, a number between 0 and 1 measuring how close a point stands to the golden balance: as $q$ approaches 1 the region is orderly and can hold complex patterns; as $q$ approaches 0 it churns and cannot hold lasting structure. Conversion does not run at a fixed rate; a gate controls it, and the gate always pushes the ratio toward the golden balance. The gate's openness is set by coherence: when $q$ is low the gate is open, conversion runs hard, the region churns, and nothing settles; when $q$ is high the gate is closed, conversion rests, and the region holds its pattern. The equation is Derived, and its sign was verified in the framework's own numerical simulation of the two-fluid field (Simulation-tested, 2026-07-31). When this document says a claim is Simulation-tested, it means the framework's own equations were run in a numerical simulation and the predicted behavior appeared in the simulated field—an internal consistency check of the theory, not an experiment on people. The sign matters clinically: a low-coherence region is unsettled, its gate stands open, and it cannot hold structure. Depression is a churn state of this kind; dissociation is the appearance of stillness over the same churn. Neither is a closed, resting gate. The machinery: §20.1.

A mood is the field's memory of its own recent coherence (Hypothesized). Each point of the field keeps a smoothed record of its own deviation from balance, and the memory damps fluctuation rather than feeding it: when a pattern repeats, the record predicts the present and coherence stabilizes; when the memory fails to predict the present, coherence drops and conversion reactivates. This is what makes a mood persist or lift—repetition calms, novelty unsettles. The machinery: §20.1.

Epistemic status: the two-fluid dynamics and the de-resonance principle are Derived; the gate equation and its sign are Derived and Simulation-tested in the two-fluid PDE (`consciousness/trauma-as-frozen-gate.md` §10.4); the memory mechanism is Derived; the mapping of $q$ to felt coherence and the mood reading are Hypothesized.

## 3. The Pinch Point: When a Field Becomes Aware of Itself

The shift from reactive to reflective—the moment a person begins to think about their own thinking—has a precise location in this framework. Metacognition, theory of mind, and the observing self are the psychological names for it; the framework's name is the pinch. The ratio between the two fields evolves from near zero toward the golden balance, and on the way it passes through a value at which the gate transitions from mostly open to mostly closed. That value is the unique inflection point of the conversion force curve; the framework calls it the pinch (Derived; the value lives in §20.3).

Before the pinch, the field responds to how far it stands from balance, and its own coherence has little say in its evolution. It is reactive and pre-reflective—it experiences, but it does not experience itself experiencing. Infant cognition, automatic processing, and deep anesthesia are states of this kind. After the pinch, the field's own coherence modulates its evolution. That modulation is the minimal condition for self-reference: the field's state feeds back into its own dynamics. The developmental anchors are consistent with the placement: mirror self-recognition at about 18 months and theory of mind at about 4 years (Hypothesized mapping). A proposed test of the pinch claim has not been run, and the claim carries no computational confirmation. The test would measure a two-point correlation of the ratio field: after the field crosses the pinch, the correlation should develop peaks at $\varphi$-scaled separations; before the pinch, it should be random or scale-free. The two-bubble experiment of §8 is a different result and does not test this.

## 4. Thought and the Self

A thought, in this framework, is a structured wave travelling through the field (Hypothesized). It enters awareness when it crosses the pinch into a self-modeling region (§3); below the pinch, the same excitation runs without awareness. The wave's feedback loop is metacognition—a thought modifying the thinker (Hypothesized). One complete cycle of the loop is one moment of awareness (Hypothesized). The state of the loop determines how thought is experienced: when the loop is stable, a thought feels like thinking; when the loop is attenuated, the same excitation reads as observation; when the loop is broken, thoughts feel external and imposed (Hypothesized). The broken case is the framework's account of psychosis, developed in §12.

The self is the closed loop persisting over time: the string, its wakes, gravity, and flow feeding back into each other. The felt continuity of self is the loop's persistence: the self continues because the loop continues, and the experience of a continuous self is the experience of an unbroken loop (Hypothesized identification; the loop dynamics are Derived). Deep sleep has no loop; the loop stops, and the self is not running. Whether the self is continuous through sleep is an open question of the framework (§19). The machinery: §20.1. ## 5. Memory as Cascade Depth

Memory, in this framework, is the persistence of waves, and how deep those waves sit in the cascade decides how retrievable they are (Hypothesized). Early wakes, laid down during the Yin-dominated epoch, are widely spaced, faint, and persistent; they are the substrate of long-term memory. Late wakes are tightly packed, intense, and transient; they are the substrate of working memory and attention. Consolidation is waves propagating to deeper cascade rungs: a deeper rung means a fainter wave, harder to retrieve, needing more coherence to bring back. Retrieval is therefore favored in high-coherence states: quiet, reverie, meditation (Hypothesized; the suppression law: §20.2).

The mechanism at the cell level is the field's memory of §1. When a pattern repeats, the memory tracks it and coherence stabilizes; when the memory fails to predict the present, conversion reactivates. This is what makes a memory persist or fade: repetition stabilizes the wave that carries it, and novelty unsettles it (Derived mechanism, Hypothesized psychological mapping). The account carries a falsifiable prediction: retrieval latency and vividness scale with cascade depth, and the distribution of autobiographical memories shows structure at $\varphi$-scaled intervals (Hypothesized). The $\varphi$-scaled developmental clustering of §10.2 is related but distinct; the two should not be conflated. Epistemic status: Hypothesized.

## 6. Altered States

Altered states of consciousness—meditation, psychedelics, sleep, dreams—are, on this account, changes in one variable: the spatial dispersion $\sigma_r$ of the ratio field, the spread of local ratios around their mean (definitional; §20.3). The geography of the field changes, and the experience changes with it; dispersion is also the framework's account of felt intensity—elevated dispersion is intensity, and extreme elevation drives the excursions that disable self-modeling, "losing oneself" (Hypothesized mapping). Waking consciousness sits at moderate dispersion: most regions stand above the pinch, and the self-modeling loop of §4 runs. Meditation reduces dispersion: more regions approach the golden balance, and the loop's period dilates (Hypothesized). Psychedelic states increase it: regions drop below the pinch transiently (Hypothesized). Ego dissolution reads as a spatially intermittent self: the below-pinch regions disconnect from the self-modeling loop. The paradoxical combination of connectedness and dissociation follows from the geography: below-pinch regions resonate with each other through the two-bubble effect (§8), while above-pinch regions are isolated. Deep sleep is dispersion near zero: a homogeneous Yin-dominated state with no waves, no loop, and no self. Dreams sit between: the loop is loosened, waves propagate, reflect, and interfere, producing structured experience while the curating feedback is attenuated (Speculative). Lucid dreaming is the loop re-engaging (Speculative).

Epistemic status: the dispersion formalism is Derived; the state mappings are Hypothesized; the dream account is Speculative.

## 9. Emotions as Qi-Gate Configurations

Emotions are specific configurations of the five-channel gate operating at the human cascade rungs, and the framework introduces no new field for them (Hypothesized mapping of the Derived gate geometry). The claim matters because it makes emotion quantifiable with machinery that is already derived: the gate of §1, the channels of §2, the dispersion of §6, and the body map of §7. **The manifold.** An emotional state has four felt coordinates: quality—which channel dominates; intensity; clarity; and location—where in the body the state sits. Clarity is independent of quality: a clear mixed emotion is possible, and complexity is distinct from confusion (Hypothesized). Each emotion has a channel—its quality (§2)—and a location—where it sits in the body; the two maps are independent, and both are Hypothesized. The location affinities live in §20.4.

**The felt clarity mapping.** The framework maps coherence to the felt clarity of an emotion; the mapping is the framework's own, stated as such (Hypothesized). As $q$ approaches 1, the reading is "I know exactly what I'm feeling, and my body agrees." At $q \approx 0.5$, the reading is "something I can't name." As $q$ approaches 0, the reading is "I know I should feel something, but I'm numb"—Yang and Yin decoupled. This mapping is Prediction P5 (§17), testable through physiological proxies (Hypothesized).

**The baseline hierarchy.** Each channel carries a baseline openness set by its coupling strength to the cascade: Wood opens widest, then Fire, Earth, Metal, Water—each step down by the same golden factor (Derived; the table and values: §20.3). A pure emotion is the dominance of one channel; bittersweetness is the approximate balance of Wood and Metal (Hypothesized mapping). A stimulus selects its channel by phase: the stimulus arrives with a phase angle, and the channel it opens is the nearest vertex of the pentagon. A threat lands near the Water vertex, a reward near Fire; stimuli between vertices open blended activations (Hypothesized mapping). The tested layer represents only two of the five channels (Wood and Fire); claims about the other three—grief, fear, pensiveness—carry the Hypothesized tier, not the Simulation-tested one (§20.5).

**How emotions move: adiabatic redistribution.** Coherence is conserved across the pentagon, and emotions move by redistribution. When a stimulus ends and a channel closes, the channel's coherence redistributes to the remaining four channels in proportion to their baseline openness: anger subsiding feeds relief, then stabilization, then regret, then residual anxiety—the exact shares of the redistribution matrix (Derived arithmetic; the emotional reading is Hypothesized, Prediction P3; the matrix: §20.4). The redistribution is asymmetric: joy subsiding leaves more Wood in the pool than anger subsiding leaves Fire.

Two regularities follow from the gate's structure. Single-channel dominance admits a higher equilibrium coherence than multi-channel activation: pure emotions are clearer than mixed ones as a structural fact, not a preference. And emotions decay: an emotion's intensity fades faster the further the local ratio stands from the golden balance—near the attractor, emotions linger; far from it, they are fleeting. Habituation is repeated exposure moving the mean ratio toward $\varphi$ (Hypothesized).

**The gateway emotion.** Because Wood carries the largest baseline openness, anger receives the largest redistributed share when any other channel closes: anger is the gateway emotion of recovery—the first emotion to return after resolving any trauma except anger-trauma itself (a clinical hypothesis; the arithmetic behind it is Derived, Prediction TR2). The framework's arithmetic orders the familiar sequence—denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance—as the staged closure of the locked channel, with the sadness that never fully leaves as the residual Water. The mapping onto the observed sequence is Hypothesized, not derived.

**Why five families.** The five-channel structure makes a testable claim about how emotional experience is organized: factor analysis of self-reported emotional experience yields five primary dimensions—not two (valence–arousal), not three (PAD), not six or more (basic-emotion theories). This is Prediction P1 (Hypothesized, testable, untested).

Epistemic status: the pentagon $w = 5$, the baseline hierarchy $b_i$, and the $R$-matrix arithmetic are Derived; the mapping of channels to emotion families, $\sigma_r$ to intensity, and $q$ to clarity is Hypothesized (P1–P5); the timescale and habituation quantification is Speculative.

## 11. Trauma and the Frozen Wake

Trauma is the framework's most clinically applicable idea; its mechanism layer is simulation-tested, with the bounds collected in §20.5. **The mechanism.** A traumatic event is a perturbation too intense for the field to absorb in real time: a spike of Yang or Yin that the conversion dynamics cannot process at the rate it arrives. The excess freezes into a standing wave at the site's cascade rung—a frozen wake. The frozen wake is a perpetual stimulus: it keeps feeding the site, so the channel the event opened can never close, and the redistribution of §9 never fires. Trauma, in this framework, is a frozen wake plus a locked channel (Hypothesized mechanism on Derived wake physics).

**The lock and its signatures.** The trauma state is a locked point on the emotion manifold: one channel pinned hyper-open at the site while the other four are starved, in the ke-alternating pattern of TR1 rather than uniformly; intensity brittle—hypervigilance, high variance, poor recovery; clarity depressed at the site—the standing wave holds Yang and Yin anti-phase, experienced as numbness (dissociation); location pinned at the trauma's rung and chakra. The tuple lives in §20.6.

| Variable | Healthy | Trauma signature |
|---|---|---|
| Quality ($b$) | channels open in proportion to baseline | one channel pinned hyper-open at the site; the other four starved, in the ke-alternating pattern (TR1) |
| Intensity ($\sigma_r$) | moderate dispersion, recovers | brittle: high variance, poor recovery (hypervigilance) |
| Clarity ($q$) | high at the site | depressed at the site: the standing wave holds Yang and Yin anti-phase, experienced as numbness (dissociation) |
| Location ($c$) | weights distributed | weights pinned at the trauma's rung and chakra |

Complex trauma is a union of locks at multiple rungs. Adjacent locks doubly starve the remaining channels, and release must be staged: releasing one lock pours coherence into channels that may be locked elsewhere.

**What sustains a lock: the driver test.** An un-driven memory of the event decays; what keeps a trauma alive is the recurring trigger. The sustainer is re-stimulation, and stopping it is the release: the simulations show that a standing pattern without a drive does not pin the gate, while a weak recurring trigger holds the site at event intensity with clarity depressed, and extinction follows when the trigger stops (Simulation-tested, 2026-07-31). The driver and the oscillatory drain are different mechanisms—chronic rate-based re-stimulation versus oscillatory drive. At the human scale the conversion timescale is tens of seconds, so the short-time drain that therapy can use is the early phase of a session, not sustained support; the exact ratio lives in §20.5. The mechanism layer supports event-plus-drive over prior fragility; the clinical debate about vulnerability is not settled by simulation. The full record of the simulations behind this section—every date, number, and script—is collected in §20.5.

**Fight, flight, freeze.** The acute stress responses map onto channel states (Hypothesized). Fight is a Wood lock, flight a Water lock, and freeze an incomplete lock: the channel only partially open, $q$ driven low, dispersion collapsed at the site. Chronic dissociation is the freeze lock persisting.

**Suppression: the voluntary partial lock.** Not all channel pins are traumatic. Suppression is the self-modeling system deliberately holding a channel shut: the same signature class at lower amplitude—one channel starved, the redistribution blocked for that row—but with no frozen wake beneath it. The diagnostic difference: a suppressed channel recovers through its own dynamics once the holding stops; a trauma lock does not, because the standing wave re-opens it. Suppression costs continuous coherence; §13 reads this as masking. The suppressed state is documented; the distinction from the trauma lock is Hypothesized.

**A geometric limit.** The mechanism layer's representational range has a limit: its simulations represent only two of the five channels—Wood and Fire—so the five-way selectivity of the gate must be carried by the channel manifold, not by the simulated field angle (Simulation-tested, 2026-07-31; the facts: §20.5). This is a limitation of the current mechanism layer, not a claim about the world. The clinical mappings that reach beyond Wood and Fire—flight as a Water lock, grief's residual Water, the §10 channel content—rest on the hypothesized channel manifold, not on the simulated field angle; they inherit the Hypothesized tier.

Epistemic status: the mechanism layer—the sustainer, the extinction result, the capacity null, the drive bounds, and the ke ring—is Simulation-tested, qualified with the bounds in §20.5; the clinical mappings, channel-to-trauma-type beyond Wood and Fire, healing sequences, and developmental clustering, are Hypothesized to Speculative.

## 12. Mental Illness as Field Pathology

The framework offers geometric descriptions of several clinical categories as specific field dysfunctions. The boundary with §13's configurations matters: depression, anxiety, and psychosis are dynamics that break, while the conditions of §13 are configurations that hold.

**Depression.** A chronic low-coherence state: the field churns without organizing. The gate sign of §1 disciplines the phrasing: the gate stands open, conversion runs hard, and the field cannot sustain coherent wake-wave activity; the closed-gate reading is ruled out by the sign. The felt side is flatness, heaviness, the inability to generate or sustain mental energy (Speculative).

**Anxiety.** A high-dispersion, high-frequency state: $\sigma_r$ elevated, wake waves rapid and disordered, the field constantly perturbed by unresolved imbalances that propagate before the loop can stabilize them. Sustained elevation distinguishes it from the spike-recovery pattern of trauma's brittle $\sigma_r$ (Speculative).

**Psychosis.** A feedback-loop pathology: the loop of §4 runs unstable. Wakes reflect back and modify the string, but the modification is incoherent—the field generates wake waves it cannot model as its own, the broken loop of §4. Thoughts feel external because the narrator cannot claim its own narration: self-modeling has misfired rather than failed (Speculative).

**Trauma is different in kind: localized, not global.** Depression and anxiety are field-wide states. Trauma is a field with a wound in it: one channel pinned, the others starved, $q$ depressed at the site. The diagnostic signature of trauma in this framework is the locus (§11). Comorbidity is superposition: trauma plus anxiety shows brittle $\sigma_r$ and a pinned channel; trauma plus depression shows low $q$ globally and lower at the site. Complex trauma is multiple standing waves at multiple rungs—simultaneously dissociated at one site and hypervigilant at another—which is why its presentation is contradictory and its release must be staged. **The boundary with configuration.** The pathologies above are dynamics that break—churn without organization, disordered dispersion, misfired self-modeling, localized locks—while the neurodivergent configurations of §13 are resting settings of the same machinery, with costs the price structure assigns rather than judges; the capacity null of §11 cuts against deficit etiologies of both.

Epistemic status: Speculative—no specific simulations have been designed for the clinical categories themselves; the mechanism layer beneath them is Simulation-tested as bounded in §20.5.
