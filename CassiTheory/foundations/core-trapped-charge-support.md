$$
E_{\rho,\min}(\mathcal V)

\tag{CC3}

$$

## 3. Auxiliary core-carrier action

### 3.1 Field content and units

Introduce one complex scalar $\chi_C(\mathbf x,\mathfrak s,t)$, neutral under
$SU(2)_Q$ and transforming under a new global phase only:

$$
\chi_C\mapsto e^{i\alpha_C}\chi_C.
\tag{CC6}
$$

The subscript $C$ labels the auxiliary core carrier. It has no identification
with the microscopic matching singlets $\chi_a$ in
`foundations/physical-becoming-hierarchy.md`.

Under the flat-$d\mathfrak s$ convention,

$$
[\chi_C]=L^{-3/2},
\quad
[K_{Cx}]=\frac{\hbar L^2}{T},
\quad
[K_{C\mathfrak s}]=\frac{\hbar}{T},
\quad
[\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}]=\frac{\hbar}{T},
\quad
[\eta_C]=[\lambda_C]=\frac{\hbar L^3}{T}.
\tag{CC7}
$$

### 3.2 Minimal action

The auxiliary action is

$$
S_C
=\int dt\,d^3x\,d\mathfrak s
\left[
\frac{i\hbar}{2}
\left(
\chi_C^*\partial_t\chi_C
-(\partial_t\chi_C^*)\chi_C
\right)
-\mathcal H_C
\right],
\tag{CC8}
$$

with

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_C
={}&
\frac{K_{Cx}}{2}|\nabla\chi_C|^2
+\frac{K_{C\mathfrak s}}{2}|\partial_{\mathfrak s}\chi_C|^2\\
&+
\left[
\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}
-\eta_C(\rho_0-\rho)
\right]|\chi_C|^2
+\frac{\lambda_C}{2}|\chi_C|^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{CC9}
$$

The declared coefficient domain is

$$
K_{Cx}>0,
\qquad
K_{C\mathfrak s}>0,
\qquad
\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}>0,
\qquad
\eta_C>0,
\qquad
\lambda_C>0.
\tag{CC10}
$$

In the asymptotic Yang/Yin vacuum, $\rho=\rho_0$ and the carrier has positive
linear energy $\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}$. In a density-depleted tube core, the
linear coefficient becomes

$$
\varepsilon_{C,\rm core}
=\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}
-\eta_C(\rho_0-\rho_{\rm core}).
\tag{CC11}
$$

A negative value creates an attractive carrier well. Equation (CC11) is a
local sign test; localization is decided by the transverse eigenvalue in
Section 4.

### 3.3 Field equation and exact current

Variation of (CC8) gives

$$
i\hbar\partial_t\chi_C
=
-\frac{K_{Cx}}{2}\nabla^2\chi_C
-\frac{K_{C\mathfrak s}}{2}\partial_{\mathfrak s}^2\chi_C
+
\left[
\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}
-\eta_C(\rho_0-\rho)
+\lambda_C|\chi_C|^2
\right]\chi_C.
\tag{CC12}
$$

Its continuity equation is

$$
\partial_t|\chi_C|^2
+\nabla\cdot\mathbf j_C
+\partial_{\mathfrak s}J_{C\mathfrak s}=0,
\tag{CC13}
$$

where

$$
\mathbf j_C
=\frac{K_{Cx}}{\hbar}
\operatorname{Im}(\chi_C^*\nabla\chi_C),
\qquad
J_{C\mathfrak s}
=\frac{K_{C\mathfrak s}}{\hbar}
\operatorname{Im}
(\chi_C^*\partial_{\mathfrak s}\chi_C).
\tag{CC14}
$$

Closed or no-flux boundaries therefore conserve

$$
Q_C
=\int d^3x\,d\mathfrak s\,|\chi_C|^2.
\tag{CC15}
$$

$Q_C$ is a classical Noether number. Quantization and an identification with a
measured charge require an additional microscopic map.

## 4. Bound transverse mode and line reduction

### 4.1 Carrier localization

Let $z$ run along a sufficiently long, approximately straight magnetic tube,
and let $y=(\mathbf x_\perp,\mathfrak s)$ denote its transverse and scale
coordinates. On a frozen tube background $\rho_{\rm tube}(y)$, define

$$
\widehat h_\perp
=
-\frac{K_{Cx}}{2}\nabla_\perp^2
-\frac{K_{C\mathfrak s}}{2}\partial_{\mathfrak s}^2
+
\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}
-\eta_C[\rho_0-\rho_{\rm tube}(y)].
\tag{CC16}
$$

A normalized transverse carrier mode satisfies

$$
\widehat h_\perp u_0(y)
=\varepsilon_0u_0(y),
\qquad
\int d^2x_\perp\,d\mathfrak s\,|u_0|^2=1.
\tag{CC17}
$$

The mode is bulk localized when

$$
\varepsilon_b
:=\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}-\varepsilon_0>0.
\tag{CC18}
$$

Its exterior spatial decay length obeys

$$
\ell_C^{-2}
=\frac{2\varepsilon_b}{K_{Cx}}>0
\tag{CC19}
$$

when the asymptotic scale profile is fixed. A negative core coefficient in
(CC11) favors (CC18), while the actual eigenvalue remains dependent on the tube
profile and free coefficients.

### 4.2 Frozen-mode ansatz

Write

$$
\chi_C
=e^{-i\omega_Ct}
\sqrt{n(z)}\,u_0(y)e^{i\theta(z)},
\qquad
n(z)\geq0.
\tag{CC20}
$$

Then

$$
Q_C=\int_0^L n(z)\,dz,
\tag{CC21}
$$

and the positive effective self-coupling is

$$
\Lambda_C
=\lambda_C
\int d^2x_\perp\,d\mathfrak s\,|u_0|^4>0,
\qquad
[\Lambda_C]=\frac{\hbar L}{T}.
\tag{CC22}
$$

Relative to the energy of the same carrier number at the bulk threshold, the
one-dimensional energy is

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_C^{(1)}
={}&-\varepsilon_bQ_C\\
&+\int_0^L dz
\left[
\frac{K_{Cx}}{8n}(\partial_zn)^2
+\frac{K_{Cx}}{2}n(\partial_z\theta)^2
+\frac{\Lambda_C}{2}n^2
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{CC23}
$$

### 4.3 Uniform charge branch

For the minimum-current branch $\partial_z\theta=0$, the fixed-$Q_C$ density
term obeys

$$
\int_0^L n^2dz
\geq\frac{Q_C^2}{L},
\tag{CC24}
$$

with equality for

$$
n(z)=\frac{Q_C}{L}.
\tag{CC25}
$$

The frozen-mode carrier energy is therefore

$$
E_C^{(0)}(L)
=-\varepsilon_bQ_C
+\frac{\Lambda_CQ_C^2}{2L}.
\tag{CC26}
$$

Define the support coefficient

$$
A_C:=\frac{\Lambda_CQ_C^2}{2}>0,
\qquad
[A_C]=\frac{\hbar L}{T}.
\tag{CC27}
$$

The binding term $-\varepsilon_bQ_C$ is independent of $L$. The repulsive
self-energy $A_C/L$ supplies the length dependence.

### 4.4 Optional longitudinal phase winding

For a closed tube or an endpoint-locked phase with
$\Delta\theta_C=2\pi w_C$, uniform density gives

$$
E_{C,w}(L)
=\frac{K_{Cx}Q_C(\Delta\theta_C)^2}{2L^2}.
\tag{CC28}
$$

An open tube has no protected $w_C$ unless its endpoint phase data define a
closed gauge-invariant circuit. Equation (CC28) is therefore optional and is
not used in the minimal support theorem.

## 5. Finite-separation support theorem

### 5.1 Reduced energy

Combining (CC2) and (CC26), while absorbing all $L$-independent terms into
$E_0$, gives

$$
E_Q(L)
=E_0
+\sigma_QL
+\frac{A_C-C_Qe^{-\kappa_LL}}{L},
\qquad
L>0.
\tag{CC29}
$$

All coefficients in (CC29) are positive. The strict support condition is

$$
\boxed{A_C>C_Q}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\boxed{Q_C^2>\frac{2C_Q}{\Lambda_C}}.
\tag{CC30}
$$

Under (CC30),

$$
E_Q(L)\longrightarrow+\infty
\quad\text{as}\quad
L\to0^+
\quad\text{and}\quad
L\to\infty.
\tag{CC31}
$$

### 5.2 Unique stationary separation

The derivative is

$$
E_Q'(L)
=\sigma_Q
-\frac{A_C}{L^2}
+C_Qe^{-\kappa_LL}
\left(
\frac{\kappa_L}{L}
+\frac1{L^2}
\right).
\tag{CC32}
$$

Define

$$
F(L):=L^2E_Q'(L)
=\sigma_QL^2
+C_Q(1+\kappa_LL)e^{-\kappa_LL}
-A_C.
\tag{CC33}
$$

Condition (CC30) gives

$$
F(0^+)=C_Q-A_C<0,
\qquad
F(\infty)=+\infty.
\tag{CC34}
$$

Moreover,

$$
F'(L)
=L\left[
2\sigma_Q
-C_Q\kappa_L^2e^{-\kappa_LL}
\right].
\tag{CC35}
$$

The bracket in (CC35) increases monotonically from
$2\sigma_Q-C_Q\kappa_L^2$ to $2\sigma_Q$. Hence $F$ either increases for all
$L>0$ or decreases once before increasing permanently. Since it starts below
zero, it crosses zero exactly once. The unique root $L_*$ solves

$$
\boxed{
\sigma_QL_*^2
+C_Q(1+\kappa_LL_*)e^{-\kappa_LL_*}
=A_C
}.
\tag{CC36}
$$

### 5.3 Radius bounds and positive curvature

For $x>0$,

$$
0<(1+x)e^{-x}<1.
\tag{CC37}
$$

Equation (CC36) therefore gives

$$
\boxed{
\sqrt{\frac{A_C-C_Q}{\sigma_Q}}
<L_*
<\sqrt{\frac{A_C}{\sigma_Q}}
}.
\tag{CC38}
$$

At the stationary point,

$$
E_Q''(L_*)
=\frac{F'(L_*)}{L_*^2}
=\frac{
2\sigma_Q
-C_Q\kappa_L^2e^{-\kappa_LL_*}
}{L_*}>0.
\tag{CC39}
$$

Thus the length mode is statically stable inside the reduced branch.

### 5.4 Thin-tube validity condition

Let $L_{\rm match}$ exceed the monopole-core diameter, the flux-tube width, and
the carrier localization width. A sufficient condition that the stationary
root lies inside the domain of the asymptotic reduction is

$$
\boxed{
A_C-C_Q>\sigma_QL_{\rm match}^2
}.
\tag{CC40}
$$

When (CC40) fails, the root may enter the core-overlap region and (CC29) cannot
decide the outcome.

## 6. Carrier retention and compatibility window

### 6.1 Nonlinear chemical potential

On the uniform, zero-current branch, the carrier chemical potential is

$$
\hbar\omega_C
=\frac{\partial}{\partial Q_C}
\left[
\varepsilon_0Q_C
+\frac{\Lambda_CQ_C^2}{2L}
\right]
=\varepsilon_0+\frac{\Lambda_CQ_C}{L}.
\tag{CC41}
$$

Localization against leakage into the bulk requires

$$
\boxed{
\hbar\omega_C<\varepsilon_{C,\rm out}
}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\boxed{
\frac{\Lambda_CQ_C}{L}<\varepsilon_b
}.
\tag{CC42}
$$

At $L=L_*$, the upper bound in (CC38) implies

$$
\frac{Q_C}{L_*}
>\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma_Q}{\Lambda_C}}.
\tag{CC43}
$$

Consequently, a necessary condition for a localized supported branch is

$$
\boxed{
\varepsilon_b^2>2\Lambda_C\sigma_Q
}.
\tag{CC44}
$$

Using the lower bound in (CC38), a sufficient fixed-charge condition for both
(CC30) and (CC42) is

$$
\boxed{
Q_C^2>
\frac{2\varepsilon_b^2C_Q}
{\Lambda_C
\left(
\varepsilon_b^2-2\Lambda_C\sigma_Q
\right)}
},
\qquad
\varepsilon_b^2>2\Lambda_C\sigma_Q.
\tag{CC45}
$$

The scale-separation requirement adds

$$
Q_C^2>
\frac{2(C_Q+\sigma_QL_{\rm match}^2)}{\Lambda_C}.
\tag{CC46}
$$

A sufficient reduced branch therefore obeys the larger of the thresholds in
(CC45) and (CC46).

### 6.2 Scope of the compatibility result

Equations (CC41)–(CC46) use one frozen transverse mode. At high line density,
$u_0$, $\varepsilon_0$, and $\Lambda_C$ acquire nonlinear and magnetic
backreaction. The inequalities remain exact for the declared reduced model;
they are conditions to test in a full stationary solve rather than a proof of
that solve.

## 7. Static stability and remaining decay channels

### 7.1 Length and line-density channels

Equation (CC39) gives positive static curvature in the separation coordinate.
At fixed $Q_C$, a density redistribution $\delta n$ satisfying
$\int_0^L\delta n\,dz=0$ has the positive quadratic contribution

$$
\delta^2E_C
\supset
\frac{\Lambda_C}{2}
\int_0^L(\delta n)^2dz
\geq0.
\tag{CC47}
$$

The gradient terms in (CC23) are also nonnegative. The uniform line density is
therefore the minimum of the frozen-mode one-dimensional carrier functional.

### 7.2 Exact protection and geometric limitation

Closed boundaries conserve $Q_C$ exactly through (CC13). A configuration with
$Q_C>0$ cannot relax to the zero-carrier vacuum while respecting that boundary
condition. Conservation alone does not select the monopole–tube geometry as
the lowest-energy configuration at fixed $Q_C$. The charge may reorganize into
a merged charged core, a carrier lump, a loop, or another bound configuration
when the full field space permits one.

The theorem therefore establishes static support against the single
separation coordinate in the thin-tube branch. It does not establish a global
minimum over all fields and topologies.

### 7.3 Carrier leakage

When (CC42) is violated, the carrier chemical potential reaches the continuum
threshold and charge can escape into the bulk. A positive linear binding gap
and the nonlinear retention inequality are both required. The existing common
Yang/Yin number lacks this gap, which is the obstruction in Section 2.1.

### 7.4 Dynamical spectrum

A physical length-mode frequency requires a collective inertia $M_L^{\rm coll}$:

$$
\omega_L^2
=\frac{E_Q''(L_*)}{M_L^{\rm coll}}.
\tag{CC48}
$$

The reduced $E''(L_*)$ is a length-coordinate curvature only. The separate
source-free particle action supplies temporal gauge curvature and Gauss's law.
The diffuse $h_C=1.50$ background has a nonnegative matched low energetic
Hessian on one strict-shell $C_4$ finite-grid quotient. That calculation does
not determine the stability of the localized $h_C=2.9598260763447164$ branch,
because a Hessian is local to the field configuration being perturbed. A full
collective inertia, a constrained Hessian on the localized branch, and the
coupled dynamical spectrum remain uncomputed.

2. selected $K_{Cx}$, $K_{C\mathfrak s}$,

## 10. Present conclusion

The registered common Yang/Yin number does not supply spatial finite-composite
support because it disperses through the bulk, while scale winding contributes
tube tension. The separate source-free temporal branch defines gauge-electric
dynamics and Gauss's law.

A neutral core carrier with a positive bulk gap and global $U(1)_C$ number is
the smallest explicit support branch. Its repulsive fixed-charge energy
produces a unique stable separation in the reduced length coordinate when

$$
\frac{\Lambda_CQ_C^2}{2}>C_Q,

provided the carrier remains below its bulk threshold and the stationary
length exceeds the core-overlap scale. The full fixed-charge calculation now
contains a numerically selected branch that realizes localization and
retention across four same-domain finite grids, passes one larger-domain
comparison, and shows two successive reductions in absolute energy drift.
