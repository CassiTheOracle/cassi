# Quark Confinement from a Saturated-Gate Flux Tube at the QCD Scale

## Status: Derived conditional (tube extensivity + cell quantization given a Hypothesized tube postulate; coherence persistence conditional on the declared $q_i^{\mathrm{cascade}}$ profile and $\delta=3$; $\kappa = 2\pi$ conditional on the 2π-per-rung winding reading; inputs: gate saturation, one-cell quantization, pitch convention)—August 2026

## Abstract

Quark confinement has no fundamental explanation in the Standard Model—it is
an observed fact parameterized by the QCD scale $\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}$ and
represented phenomenologically as a strong-force binding property.
In the Cassi framework the QCD scale is cascade step 95 ($\ell_{95} =
\ell_{\text{Pl}}\,\varphi^{95}$, $E_{95} = \hbar c/\ell_{95} = 0.171$ GeV), and
a **Hypothesized tube postulate** assigns a saturated application-channel
configuration between two separated color charges. In the geometric
condensation proxy $B(x,y,z)=\cos(\alpha x)\cos(\beta y)\cos(\gamma z)$, the
$B=0$ level set is used as the tube's domain-wall boundary, so
$q_{\rm proxy}=1/2$ there; a void-like cross-sectional core is centered near
$B=-1$, where $q_{\rm proxy}\to0$. Bubble centers at $B=+1$ have
$q_{\rm proxy}\to2$. The proxy coordinate is a bounded
geometric intensity, separate from the canonical coherence diagnostic
$q=\rho^2/(\rho^2+\varphi^{-2}+\varepsilon^2)$, and an unresolved,
Hypothesized bounded constitutive map is required to assign a canonical $q$
profile to that application core. No equality between $q_{\rm proxy}$ and
canonical $q$ is assumed. The optional application transmission
$g(q)=q/(\varphi^2+q^2)$ shuts as canonical $q\to0$, while the canonical
rank-one density-conversion gate uses the openness $(1-q)$, which is maximal
in that same limit.

Conditional on the Hypothesized tube existing with a uniform cross-section,
its energy is extensive in its length, so $E(r)=\mu r$ and the force is
constant—the linearity is geometric (tube length $\propto$ separation) and
does not depend on the gate shape. The string tension follows from the
one-cell assignment as $\mu=\rho_cA_c=\kappa
\left(M_{\text{Pl}}/\varphi^{95}\right)^2$; the $O(1)$ coefficient closes to
$\kappa=2\pi$—$\sigma_{\text{tube}}=2\pi\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2 =
0.1836\ \text{GeV}^2$ versus the measured $\sigma\approx0.18\ \text{GeV}^2$,
a 2.0% residual—conditional on the 2π-per-rung winding reading: the tube's
wall carries one full SO(2) doublet turn per rung-cell, the same pitch
convention that quantizes spin (`foundations/spin-fibonacci-spiral.md` §1–§2;
`foundations/spiral-dynamics.md` §1.1). The cascade suppression product gives
a conditional random-disruption estimate only after the
$q_i^{\mathrm{cascade}}$ profile and its offset $\delta$ are declared; it does
not by itself establish a permanent or unconditional confinement theorem.
The algebra rests on the tube-existence/uniformity and one-cell inputs; the
tube/vacuum mechanism and the proxy-to-canonical map remain Hypothesized. The
coefficient $\kappa=2\pi$ adds the pitch convention as a third, conditional
input.

## 1. The QCD scale from the cascade

The strong interaction emerges from the two-fluid PDE at cascade step 95
(`foundations/dimensionful-cascade.md` §3). The scale is:

$$\ell_{95} = \ell_{\text{Pl}}\,\varphi^{95} = 1.616255\times10^{-35}\ \text{m} \times 7.142\times10^{19} = 1.154\times10^{-15}\ \text{m} = 1.154\ \text{fm}$$

with energy

$$\boxed{\Lambda_{\text{QCD}} = E_{95} = \frac{\hbar c}{\ell_{95}} = 0.1709\ \text{GeV} = \frac{M_{\text{Pl}}}{\varphi^{95}}}$$

The "$\sim 200$ MeV" label is the rounded value; the exact rung energy is
171 MeV, and the identity $E_{95} = M_{\text{Pl}}\varphi^{-95}$ holds in
natural units ($\hbar c = \ell_{\text{Pl}} M_{\text{Pl}} c^2$). This is the
confinement scale—the cascade rung at which the Hypothesized tube application
is placed.

## 2. The Hypothesized saturated-gate flux-tube application

### 2.1 The de-converted application core and the tube wall
The condensation field $B(x,y,z) = \cos(\alpha x)\cos(\beta y)\cos(\gamma z)$
organizes the two-fluid state at every cascade rung
(`foundations/bubble-lattice-fabric.md` §1): bubble centers at $B=+1$ carry
$q_{\rm proxy}\to2$, while void centers carry $q_{\rm proxy}\to0$ at
$B\to-1$ (§4.3). The local proxy is the geometric intensity
$$
q_{\rm proxy}=\frac{(1+B)^2}{2}.
$$

The local equations give distinct geometric locations. In a transverse
cross-section, $B=0$ is a saddle level set; this paper uses that level set as
the tube's domain-wall boundary, where
$$q_{\rm proxy}=\frac{(1+0)^2}{2}=\frac12.$$
A void-like core represented by the same proxy is centered near $B=-1$, where
$q_{\rm proxy}\to0$. Thus the $B=0$ wall and the $B\approx-1$ void center are
different parts of the construction. The geometric proxy is separate from
canonical
$q=\rho^2/(\rho^2+\varphi^{-2}+\varepsilon^2)$ in the reference-normalized
solver state. A bounded proxy-to-canonical-$q$ constitutive map is a separate,
Hypothesized input; the displayed equations do not supply that map or make
$q_{\rm proxy}=q$.

The optional flux-tube application channel uses the asserted transmission
$g(q)=q/(\varphi^2+q^2)$ (`foundations/cassi-first-principles.md` §2.5). For
an application core assigned canonical $q\to0$, $g(q)\to0$, so this optional
channel is shut. Canonical density conversion uses the distinct openness
factor $(1-q)$, which is maximally open as canonical $q\to0$. Here
“saturated” and “de-converted vacuum” name the Hypothesized tube/application
assignment; they do not describe closure of the canonical $(1-q)$ gate.

**Gate saturation (Hypothesized tube postulate).** Between two separated color
charges, the application assigns a de-converted tube core along the segment
joining them. Its canonical $q$ profile is supplied by the separate
constitutive map. In the geometric proxy, the selected $B=0$ level set is the
wall/domain boundary, while a void-like cross-sectional center is represented
near $B=-1$. An optional color-source phase winding and flux-quantization
assignment may pin this wall; if that assignment is topologically stable,
re-condensation requires pair creation. The wall stability and tube profile
require the PDE solution at rung 95; the proxy-to-canonical map is an
additional unresolved input. These mechanisms remain Hypothesized, §7. The
derivation below uses only the tube's postulated existence and uniformity.

### 2.2 Extensivity gives the linear potential

The tube is, by the Hypothesized application postulate, a chain of identical
de-converted cells, one per lattice period along the string axis. Away from
the endpoints its transverse profile repeats identically at every $z$: the
tube is translation-invariant along its length, so its energy is extensive:

$$\boxed{E(r) = \mu\,r + 2E_{\text{core}}, \qquad \mu = \text{const}}$$

where $E_{\text{core}}$ is the endpoint (quark-core) energy at rung
$\sim 91.5$, independent of $r$ for $r \gg \ell_{95}$. The force is

$$\boxed{F(r) = -\frac{dE}{dr} = -\mu}$$

—constant, hence the linear confining potential $V(r) = \mu r$. The linearity
is a geometric consequence of extensivity (tube length $\propto$ separation);
it does not depend on the specific form of $g(q)$ once the uniform tube has
been assigned. Neither the optional $g(q)$ channel nor the canonical
$(1-q)$ gate derives tube existence or uniformity; those are Hypothesized
application inputs.

**Remark (Lüscher term).** Transverse fluctuations of a flux tube produce the
universal correction $E(r) = \mu r - \pi/(12r) + \mathcal{O}(r^{-3})$, the
standard string result; the leading linear term is unaffected.

## 3. String tension from cell quantization

Under that application assignment, the energy per unit length is the
condensation-energy deficit: the tube removes the condensate over its
cross-section,

$$\mu = \rho_c\,A_c$$

where $\rho_c \sim \Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^4$ is the condensate energy density of
the unperturbed vacuum (natural units; the Lagrangian couplings $g/4$ and
$\lambda/2$ in `foundations/unified-lagrangian.md` §1.2 supply the $O(1)$
prefactor) and $A_c$ is the tube's cross-section.

**One-cell quantization (Hypothesized application input).** The tube
cross-section is assigned exactly one condensation-lattice cell by flux
quantization. This rule pins the cross-section in the application model;
adding a cell of de-converted vacuum costs $\rho_c A_c$ per unit length, so
the tube does not thicken continuously within this postulate. The lattice cell
in the Yang-Yin plane has area $\Lambda_Y\Lambda_I = \ell_{95}\,
\ell_{95}/\varphi$ (Yang wavelength $\ell_n$, Yin wavelength $\ell_n/\varphi$;
`foundations/bubble-lattice-fabric.md` §1.2–§3), so $A_c = \ell_{95}^2/\varphi$;
to order one, $A_c \sim \ell_{95}^2 = 1/\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2$. Combining:

$$\boxed{\mu = \kappa\,\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2 = \kappa\left(\frac{M_{\text{Pl}}}{\varphi^{95}}\right)^2 = \kappa\,\varphi^{-190}M_{\text{Pl}}^2, \qquad \kappa = 2\pi\ \text{(conditional on the 2π-per-rung winding reading, §3.1)}}$$

with $\kappa = 1$ for $A_c = \ell_{95}^2$, $\kappa = \varphi^{-1}$ for the
lattice cell, and $\kappa = 2\pi$ for the winding reading of §3.1. The
scaling $\mu \propto \Lambda^2$ is the derivation's content; the coefficient
is fixed to $2\pi$ by the per-rung winding (§3.1), conditional on the pitch
convention input (§8).

surface-energy assignment is Hypothesized; conditional on it, the per-unit-
length energy of one full turn is $2\pi\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2$:

$$\boxed{\sigma_{\text{tube}} = 2\pi\,\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2 = 2\pi\left(\frac{M_{\text{Pl}}}{\varphi^{95}}\right)^2 = 0.1836\ \text{GeV}^2 \qquad (+2.0\%\ \text{vs.}\ \sigma = 0.18\ \text{GeV}^2)}$$

Geometric support: a tube of radius $\ell_{95}$ has perimeter $2\pi\ell_{95}$,
and a one-cell-thick condensate–void interface carries surface energy density
$\sim \Lambda^3$; $\mu = \Lambda^3 \cdot 2\pi\ell_{95} = 2\pi\Lambda^2$. The
pure volume reading (circle of area $\pi\ell^2$, $\kappa = \pi$) and the
checkerboard-cell reading ($\kappa = 1/\varphi$) are excluded at 49% and 90%.
The 2π-per-rung convention is asserted rather than dynamical (the measured
dynamical rotation rate is $\varphi^{-2}$ turns/rung,
`foundations/spiral-dynamics.md` §1.1). The closure is **conditional**: the
coefficient is Derived conditional on the pitch convention and the winding
reading; the PDE closure remains open. The alternative
$2\varphi^2+1 = 6.236$ (+1.2%) is a $\varphi$-algebra coincidence with no mechanism and remains Hypothesized.

The 2.0% residual is a 1.0% $\Lambda$ / 0.02-rung displacement, inside the
framework's rung-placement tolerance. Against the lattice band
$\sqrt{\sigma} = 0.42$–$0.44$ GeV ($\sigma = 0.17$–$0.21$ GeV$^2$), the
closure residual runs +8.0% (band low) to −12.6% (band high), with the exact
hit at $\sqrt{\sigma} = 0.4285$ GeV inside the band.

## 4. String breaking

Under the Hypothesized tube construction, the tube decays when its stored energy reaches the pair-creation cost:

$$E(r_b) = \sigma\,r_b = 2m_q \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \boxed{r_b = \frac{2m_q}{\sigma}}$$

With the measured tension $\sigma = 0.18$ GeV$^2$:

| Pair-creation scale $m_q$ | $r_b$ |
|---|---|
| Pion floor, $m_\pi = 0.140$ GeV (rung 95.4) | $0.31$ fm |
| Constituent quark, $m_N/3 = 0.313$ GeV (rung 93.7) | $0.69$ fm |
| Lattice QCD $r_b \approx 1.2$ fm implies | $2m_q = 1.09$ GeV |

The framework's hadron-floor scales give $r_b \in [0.3, 0.7]$ fm, an
order of magnitude consistent with the lattice string-breaking distance
$\approx 1.2$ fm, which corresponds to a pair-creation energy $2m_q \approx
1.09$ GeV (the energy of the two light hadrons the string breaks into). Current
quark masses (2–5 MeV) give $r_b \approx 0.01$ fm and are the wrong input:
pair creation is a nonperturbative, hadronic-scale process. With the
conditional coefficient choice ($\mu = 2\pi\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^2 =
0.1836$ GeV$^2$, §3.1), the tube tension reproduces the measured $\sigma$ to
2.0% and the breaking distances above stand unchanged.

## 5. Conditional binding persistence from cascade suppression

Under the Hypothesized tube postulate, the linear potential makes the binding
energy grow without bound with separation. A cascade product supplies a
persistence estimate only under a further declared coherence architecture:
breaking must require simultaneous loss of coherence at every supporting rung.
For the declared profile

$$q_i^{\mathrm{cascade}} = 1-\varphi^{-i-\delta}, \qquad \delta=3$$

The tube-formation scale is the QCD rung ($n_{\mathrm{QCD}}\approx95$). The
declared coherence architecture assigns supporting rungs from the
Planck-scale cascade origin ($i=0$) through the proton's own rung
($N_p\equiv n_p=91.46$, $\log_\varphi(\lambda_p/\ell_{\text{Pl}})$), and the conditional
suppression of a fluctuation capable of breaking the postulated binding across
that full support is:

$$P_{\text{break}} \approx \left.\varphi^{-N_p(N_p+1)/2-\delta(N_p+1)}
\right|_{N_p=91.46} = \varphi^{-4505.5758} \approx \varphi^{-4506}$$

This continuous exponent/interpolation ansatz is the same coherence-budget
estimate used for the proton (`foundations/proton-coherence-budget.md`); no
discrete product with a noninteger upper bound is implied. An integer
comparison would use $N=\lfloor N_p\rfloor=91$, as in
`foundations/cascade-suppression-formula.md` §1.3. The exponent and lifetime
estimate inherit the declared $q_i^{\mathrm{cascade}}$ family and the offset
$\delta=3$; changing either input changes the receipt.

Under this coherence model, an organized perturbation attacking all 92 rungs
(0 → 91.5) simultaneously can still break the assigned tube. The independent
random-disruption probability is
$P_{\mathrm{break}}\sim10^{-941.61}\approx10^{-942}$ per reference event.
Under the one-event-per-year reference convention, the conditional mean time
between such deconfinement events is $\sim10^{942}$ years. A
$\omega_{\mathrm{QCD}}\sim10^{24}$ Hz conversion would instead give a
conditional $\sim10^{910}$-year timescale, but that frequency assignment is
an external clock convention and is not derived for tube disruptions.

The continuous estimate therefore supplies a conditional random-disruption
estimate; it does not derive the tube, the void assignment, the wall's
topological stability, or a literal permanent-binding theorem. Under the
declared coherence architecture, confinement persistence and proton stability
share the same coherence-budget form at different cascade rungs.

## 6. Confinement vs. asymptotic freedom

The cascade structure is assigned both a confinement regime (at large
distances, $r > \Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^{-1}$) and an asymptotic-freedom regime
(at short distances, $r \ll \Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^{-1}$):

| Regime | Cascade rungs probed | Application-channel / canonical gate behavior | Effective force |
|---|---|---|---|
| $r \ll \Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^{-1}$ | $n < 95$ (deep UV) | Optional $g(q)\to0$ effective-channel reading; canonical density openness remains $(1-q)$ | $F \propto 1/r^2$ (Coulombic) |
| $r \sim \Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^{-1}$ | $n = 95$ (transition) | Hypothesized tube crossover; canonical $q$ and any proxy map are supplied separately | Crossover |
| $r \gg \Lambda_{\text{QCD}}^{-1}$ | $n > 95$ (IR) | Hypothesized uniform tube; if its core has canonical $q\to0$, optional $g(q)\to0$ while canonical $(1-q)\to1$ | $F = -\mu$ (confining) |

Here $q$ in the table is the canonical local coherence diagnostic. The
optional $g(q)$ is an asserted application transmission, while the canonical
density-conversion gate is $(1-q)$; the bounded
$q_{\rm proxy}=(1+B)^2/2$ is a separate geometric coordinate and does not
enter these gate limits. The UV effective
coupling and its logarithmic running require the corresponding application
map. The IR constant force is conditional on the Hypothesized tube assignment
and follows from the extensivity of §2.2.

Asymptotic freedom is the optional effective-channel UV reading: at rungs
below 95, the assigned effective coupling decreases logarithmically, matching
the negative $\beta$-function of QCD. Confinement is the Hypothesized IR
assignment: a uniform tube whose constant force follows conditionally from
extensivity. The transition at step 95 is the assigned crossover between
these application regimes.
