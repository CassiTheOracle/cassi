# Yang–Mills RG endpoint gap-matching diagnostic

## Status: Pre-registered analytic diagnostic—September 2026

## Abstract

This protocol fixes a conditional renormalization-group matching statement for four-dimensional pure $SU(2)$ Wilson lattice gauge theory. The universal two-loop scale from `foundations/loop-to-bubble-projection-theorem.md` §9.30 determines how many isotropic blocking steps separate a weak bare coupling from a fixed coarse matching window. A bounded cumulative scale defect and a positive coarse correlation-rate window then imply the required microscopic scaling $\delta_0\asymp F_W(g_0)$. The same identity makes the coarse spatial volume diverge exactly when $N_0F_W(g_0)\to\infty$.

The executable work checks these identities on frozen synthetic schedules. It does not construct an RG trajectory, a block map, a coarse interacting gap, a complete family of retained observables, an infinite-volume measure or a continuum quantum field. Every conclusion about the Yang–Mills gap remains conditional on those analytic inputs.

## 1. Mathematical target

Set

$$
b_0=\frac{11}{24\pi^2},
\qquad
b_1=\frac{17}{96\pi^4},
\qquad
p=\frac{b_1}{2b_0^2}=\frac{51}{121},
$$

and

$$
F_W(g)=
\exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{2b_0g^2}\right]
(b_0g^2)^{-p}.
$$

Fix an isotropic integer blocking factor $B>1$. A candidate RG trajectory consists of couplings
$g_0,g_1,\ldots,g_n$ and one-step scale defects

$$
r_k:=\log F_W(g_{k+1})-\log F_W(g_k)-\log B,
\qquad
R_n:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}r_k.
$$

The protocol treats this trajectory as an assumption. In particular, the computation does not infer the $g_k$ from the Wilson measure or prove that an exact gauge-covariant block transformation produces them.

The scale identity telescopes exactly:

$$
\frac{B^{-n}}{F_W(g_0)}
=\frac{e^{R_n}}{F_W(g_n)}.
$$

Assume constants

$$
0<f_-\leq F_W(g_n)\leq f_+<\infty,
\qquad
|R_n|\leq C_{\rm RG}<\infty.
$$

Then

$$
\frac{e^{-C_{\rm RG}}}{f_+}
\leq
\frac{B^{-n}}{F_W(g_0)}
\leq
\frac{e^{C_{\rm RG}}}{f_-}.
$$

This is the scale-matching estimate. A per-step estimate $|r_k|\leq\eta$ is insufficient when its cumulative sum grows with $n$.

## 2. Correlation-rate bridge

Let $T_k=e^{-H_k}$ be a positive transfer operator at blocking level $k$,
let $\Omega_k$ be its vacuum and let $A_k$ be a centered retained
gauge-invariant observable. Its positive spectral autocorrelation is

$$
C_k^{A_k}(m)
:=\langle A_k\Omega_k,T_k^mA_k\Omega_k\rangle
=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-mE}\,d\mu_{k,A_k}(E).
$$

For a nonzero vector $A_k\Omega_k$, define

$$
\gamma_k(A_k):=
\inf\operatorname{supp}\mu_{k,A_k}
=\lim_{m\to\infty}-\frac1m\log C_k^{A_k}(m).
$$

The equality with the logarithmic rate follows from positivity of the
spectral measure. The bridge assumes that exact isotropic blocking maps a
family $\mathcal R_0$ of centered observables onto a retained family
$\mathcal R_n$ and gives, for each corresponding pair and a finite positive
normalization $Z_{n,A}$ independent of $m$,

$$
C_0^A(B^nm)=Z_{n,A}C_n^{A_n}(m).
$$

Consequently

$$
\gamma_0(A)=B^{-n}\gamma_n(A_n).
$$

Define the two family rates

$$
\delta_k:=
\inf_{\substack{A_k\in\mathcal R_k\\A_k\Omega_k\ne0}}
\gamma_k(A_k).
$$

Assume a uniform endpoint window

$$
0<d_-\leq\delta_n\leq d_+<\infty.
$$

The lower bound must hold for every retained nonzero channel; the upper
bound requires one channel with finite rate. Assume also that the
Osterwalder–Schrader vectors generated by $\mathcal R_0$ are complete in the
vacuum-orthogonal physical Hilbert space. The exact observable
correspondence gives $\delta_0=B^{-n}\delta_n$, and the scale estimate gives

$$
\frac{d_-e^{-C_{\rm RG}}}{f_+}
\leq
\frac{\delta_0}{F_W(g_0)}
\leq
\frac{d_+e^{C_{\rm RG}}}{f_-}.
$$

The lower inequality becomes a full vacuum-sector gap bound only with the
stated completeness and transfer reconstruction. The upper witness supplies
a nonzero finite-energy physical channel.

## 3. Volume bridge

For an exactly divisible spatial lattice with $N_0=B^nN_n$ sites per direction,

$$
N_n
=N_0B^{-n}
=(N_0F_W(g_0))
\frac{B^{-n}}{F_W(g_0)}.
$$

Under the endpoint and cumulative-defect bounds,

$$
N_n\longrightarrow\infty
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N_0F_W(g_0)\longrightarrow\infty.
$$

Every Euclidean direction requires the same scale accounting. Integer rounding changes $N_n$ by at most one and does not change the implication when the right-hand side diverges.

## 4. Blocking depth and perturbative flatness

When $F_W(g_n)$ stays in a fixed positive window and $R_n=O(1)$,

$$
n
=\frac{1}{2b_0\log B}\frac1{g_0^2}
+\frac{p}{\log B}\log(b_0g_0^2)
+O(1).
$$

Thus a weak bare coupling requires $O(g_0^{-2})$ blocking levels before it reaches a fixed matching window. The corresponding microscopic rate is nonperturbative because, for every real $q$,

$$
\lim_{g\downarrow0}\frac{F_W(g)}{g^q}=0.
$$

This flatness statement says that a finite power-series, Puiseux or power-log truncation around $g=0$ cannot determine the positive coefficient of a rate proportional to $F_W$. It does not rule out the nonperturbative rate of the full theory. The quadratic weak-field spectrum remains a diagnostic of the perturbative sector and supplies no no-go theorem for a glueball mass.

## 5. Conditional theorem boundary

The analytic implication requires all of the following:

1. a gauge-covariant, reflection-positive RG construction along the coupled weak-coupling and volume trajectory;
2. bounded cumulative scale defect $|R_n|\leq C_{\rm RG}$ through $n=O(g_0^{-2})$ levels;
3. exact or quantitatively controlled transfer-correlation matching under isotropic time blocking;
4. a fixed positive endpoint lower rate for every retained physical channel and a finite upper witness in one nonzero channel;
5. completeness of the retained Osterwalder–Schrader vectors in the vacuum-orthogonal physical Hilbert space;
6. nontrivial full-sequence Schwinger-function convergence, restored Euclidean covariance and the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms.

Balaban's four-dimensional lattice-gauge renormalization work supplies ultraviolet stability and effective-density control in its stated scope. It does not supply items 2–6 as a four-dimensional Yang–Mills mass-gap theorem. The diagnostic therefore isolates an RG matching target without asserting that the target has been met.

## 6. Frozen arithmetic schedule

The primary and independent implementations use exactly:

```text
B = 2
endpoint_lower = 1 / 64
endpoint_upper_exact = 1 / 32
bounded_step_defect = 0.025
bounded_cumulative_limit = 0.025
endpoint_upper_bounded = 2 * exp(0.025) / 64
drift_step_defect = 0.025 / 4
coarse_rate_lower = 0.5
coarse_rate_upper = 2.0
g0_squared = [0.8, 0.5, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05]
flatness_powers = [1, 2, 4, 8]
```

For each initial coupling, the exact schedule uses

$$
n_{\rm ex}
=\left\lceil
\frac{\log(f_-/F_W(g_0))}{\log B}
\right\rceil,
\qquad
F_{\rm ex}=B^{n_{\rm ex}}F_W(g_0).
$$

The endpoint therefore lies in $[f_-,Bf_-)$. Its synthetic endpoint rate is

$$
d_i=0.5+0.25i,
\qquad i=0,\ldots,5,
$$

which stays inside $[d_-,d_+]$. The fine rate is defined only for arithmetic testing by

$$
\delta_{0,i}=B^{-n_{\rm ex}}d_i.
$$

These $d_i$ are assumption witnesses. They are not measured Yang–Mills eigenvalues.

The bounded-defect schedule starts from $F_W(g_0)$ and applies

$$
\log F_{k+1}=\log F_k+\log B+(-1)^k\eta
$$

until $F_k\geq f_-$. Its cumulative defect stays in $\{0,\eta\}$ and its endpoint lies in
$[f_-,Be^\eta f_-)$. The drift control applies the constant defect $\eta/4$ at every step. Every frozen drift row has $|R_n|>\eta$ and must be rejected even though every individual defect is smaller than $\eta$.

The five volume schedules are classified in the log domain:

| schedule | $\log N_0$ | required class |
|---|---:|---|
| fixed sites | $\log 1024$ | collapse |
| polynomial | $-8\log g_0$ | collapse |
| fixed physical box | $-\log F_W+\log 3$ | fixed |
| inverse-$g^2$ enhanced | $-\log F_W-\log g_0^2$ | infinite |
| logarithmically enhanced | $-\log F_W+\log\log(1/F_W)$ | infinite |

The flatness rows evaluate $F_W(g_0)/g_0^q$ for each frozen $q$ and require strict decrease as the coupling schedule approaches zero.

## 7. Primary check inventory

The Python verifier must emit exactly **139** decisions:

- 15 global checks for coefficients, frozen constants, finite scales, monotonicity, row counts, endpoint windows, drift rejection, volume classes, source bindings and the claim boundary;
- seven checks on each of six exact rows: scale reconstruction, integer stopping depth, endpoint recurrence, telescoping identity, time-rate rescaling, renormalized-rate bounds and coarse-volume factorization;
- eight checks on each of six alternating-defect rows: scale reconstruction, positive stopping depth, recurrence replay, endpoint window, cumulative bound, telescoping identity, time-rate rescaling and renormalized-rate bounds;
- three checks on each of six drift rows: recurrence replay, cumulative-bound violation and rejection;
- five volume-schedule classification checks;
- four perturbative-flatness checks;
- seven mutation-firing checks.

The seven mutations are fixed:

1. omit the $e^{R_n}$ cumulative-defect factor;
2. reverse the sign of $R_n$;
3. accept the drift schedule from a per-step defect bound alone;
4. omit the $B^{-n}$ time-rate rescaling;
5. classify $N_0\asymp F_W^{-1}$ as infinite volume;
6. accept a zero endpoint rate as gapped;
7. infer a full gap after deleting retained-observable completeness.

Each mutation must make a false rule return true or make an exact identity fail. A mutation recorded without a firing witness makes the primary verdict `FAIL`.
