### 9.2 Consequence for neutral stationary matter

### 24.1 Spherical-average confinement theorem

Let $E_Y,E_I\ge0$ be smooth stationary populations on $\mathbb R^3$, with common constant $D>0$, finite constant mobilities, and currents
$$
\begin{aligned}
J_Y&=E_Yu+\chi_Y E_Y\nabla\Phi-D\nabla E_Y,\\
J_I&=E_Iu-\chi E_I\nabla\Phi-D\nabla E_I.
\end{aligned}
$$
The local conversion terms are equal and opposite. Assume the fields and currents are regular throughout every finite ball, with no point source or imposed population flux at the origin. The sum therefore obeys $\nabla\cdot J=0$, where
$$
J=\rho u+(\chi_YE_Y-\chi E_I)\nabla\Phi-D\nabla\rho,\qquad
\rho=E_Y+E_I.
$$
This statement uses conservative currents. In the canonical base equations, incompressibility makes the implemented advective form equal to this conservative form.

Define the spherical average and a radial drift envelope by
$$
f(r)=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}\rho(r\omega)\,d\omega,\qquad
g(r)=\sup_{S_r}|u_r|+\chi_{\max}\sup_{S_r}|\partial_r\Phi|,
\qquad \chi_{\max}=\max(|\chi_Y|,|\chi|).
$$
Regularity and the divergence theorem give zero integrated total current through every sphere. Consequently
$$
\boxed{Df'(r)=\left\langle\rho u_r+
(\chi_YE_Y-\chi E_I)\partial_r\Phi\right\rangle_{S_r},
\qquad |Df'(r)|\le g(r)f(r).}
$$
Positivity of the component populations supplies the inequality; no pointwise zero-current or radial-symmetry assumption is used. Angular circulation can be present. Local boundedness of $g$ propagates positive $f$ across every finite radial interval, so a nonzero continuous population gives $f(R)>0$ at the start of an exterior region.

If $\int_R^\infty g(s)\,ds<\infty$, the integrating-factor inequality yields
$$
\boxed{f(r)\ge f(R)\exp\left[-\frac1D\int_R^r g(s)\,ds\right]
\ge f(R)\exp\left[-\frac1D\int_R^\infty g(s)\,ds\right]>0.}
$$
Thus $4\pi\int_R^\infty r^2f(r)\,dr$ diverges. Under these assumptions, the only stationary solution with finite total nonnegative population is $\rho=0$. The proof also excludes a smooth compact-support stationary population, since a positive spherical average cannot reach zero at a finite radius.
