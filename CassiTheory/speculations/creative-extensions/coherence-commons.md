# The Coherence Commons: A Marxist Field-Materialist Theory of Production, Power, and Human Development

## Status: Creative—August 2026

## Abstract

The Coherence Commons is a socioeconomic theory of how embodied persons create, retain, govern, and reproduce collective causal power. Its unit is the person-world relation developed in `consciousness/field-materialism-and-human-development.md`: people change shared material conditions through labor, care, knowledge, refusal, invention, and organization; those conditions return through resources, institutions, ecological limits, and control over future possibilities. Coordinated activity can create a relational surplus that no isolated participant produces. Capital appropriates this surplus through ownership and control of production, infrastructure, institutional memory, allocation, and social reproduction.

The theory retains Marxian value categories. Commodity value remains regulated by socially necessary abstract labor; the profit rate remains $s/(c+v)$; accumulation remains a social relation of control. Cassi contributes a separate account of relational production, reproduction debt, person-world feedback, institutional memory, correction, and viable human development. Leninist organization becomes persistent collective causal capacity. Maoist practice and the mass line become a closed learning process from distributed experience through synthesis and action back to consequences and correction. Ostrom's institutional analysis, Sen's capability approach, social reproduction theory, Minsky's financial fragility, and ecological economics provide independent constructs and comparison points.

A coherence commons is an institution in which affected people govern shared productive resources, relational surplus, reproduction systems, and collective memory while preserving universal material viability, individual agency, ecological limits, dissent, and correction. The proposal is Creative. Its normative constitution is declared rather than derived from physics. Its socioeconomic mechanisms are candidates for comparative study and preregistered tests.

## 1. Theory Boundary and Method

### 1.1 Five layers

| Layer | Question | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Material ontology | What kinds of entities and interactions constitute persons and social systems? | Framework-grounded physical inheritance plus Creative personhood mapping |
| Political economy | How are labor, value, surplus, accumulation, reproduction, and class organized? | Marxian theory plus Hypothesized Cassi extensions |
| Institutional dynamics | How do organizations store, transmit, amplify, suppress, and correct collective activity? | Hypothesized mechanism |
| Normative constitution | How should persons, needs, power, and common surplus be treated? | Declared axioms |
| Empirical program | Which observations distinguish the theory from alternatives? | Candidate hypotheses requiring preregistration |

The canonical Qi coherence scalar $q$ belongs to a declared pair of physical density fields. Social solidarity, agreement, output, welfare, organizational stability, and equality are not observations of that $q$. This document uses ordinary socioeconomic variables and component ledgers. It makes no inference from a physical optimum to a moral optimum.

### 1.2 The central thesis

Let $G$ be a society or institution containing persons $i$, a shared material-social field $F_G$, and an ecological environment $E_G$. The central loop is

$$
\boxed{
\mathfrak{s}_i(t)
\longrightarrow
\text{activity }J_i(t)
\longrightarrow
\bigl(F_G,E_G\bigr)(t+\Delta t)
\xrightarrow{\ \mathcal B_{i,\Delta t}\ }
\mathfrak{s}_i(t+\Delta t).
}
$$

Political economy governs this loop. It determines who controls activity, who owns its product, how cooperation is organized, whose experience becomes institutional knowledge, who receives the return flow, which costs are displaced, and whose future trajectories expand or contract.

### 1.3 A multidimensional state

A socioeconomic system cannot be represented by one coherence score. Its minimum state includes

$$
\mathcal{X}_G=
(K,L,S,F^{\mathrm{fin}},R,N,P,E),
$$

where:

- $K$: productive assets and infrastructure;
- $L$: labor-power, skills, health, and available time;
- $S$: produced surplus and its allocation;
- $F^{\mathrm{fin}}$: money, credit, debt, and financial claims;
- $R$: household, community, public, and care reproduction reserves;
- $N$: institutional memory, knowledge, and communication networks;
- $P$: ownership, decision, information, sanction, and appeal rights;
- $E$: ecological stocks, throughput, sinks, and regenerative capacity.

A theory that improves one component by silently depleting another has moved a cost rather than solved it.

## 2. Persons and Social Metabolism

### 2.1 The material person

A person is an embodied, causally continuous, self-steering field organization. The state

$$
\mathfrak{s}_i=(B_i,M_i,\Theta_i,A_i,\mathcal{V}_i,R_i)
$$

Here $B_i$ is boundary integrity, $M_i$ retained history, $\Theta_i$ a revisable model, $A_i$ effective agency, $\mathcal{V}_i$ the set of viable future trajectories, and $R_i$ a vector of separately measured reproduction reserves. The continuity-conditioned trajectory $\Sigma_i[C_i]=\left.\gamma_i\right|_{I_i(C_i)}$ is the provisional material soul defined in `consciousness/field-materialism-and-human-development.md` §2.

Economic activity acts on this person-state. Work can develop skill or produce injury. Income can expand options while schedule control contracts them. Education can increase individual capacity and the capacity of everyone who later interacts with that person. Debt can transfer future activity into present ownership. Care can restore the possibility of continued action. Ecological damage can contract viable futures before it appears in prices.

### 2.2 Social metabolism

Society transforms materials, energy, labor, information, and care through a network of institutions. A complete metabolism records both production and reproduction:

$$
\text{nature and inherited infrastructure}
\rightarrow
\text{production and care}
\rightarrow
\text{use, learning, and waste}
\rightarrow
\text{reproduction or depletion}.
$$

Each flow has a physical quantity, a labor requirement, a control relation, a distribution, and a consequence for future capacity. Monetary accounts capture only part of this metabolism.

### 2.3 The commons as a social relation

The Coherence Commons names the ensemble of shared social and material conditions through which people can act together and develop:

1. land, water, energy, housing, tools, and productive infrastructure;
2. care systems, health, education, and public provision;
3. language, science, culture, software, records, and institutional memory;
4. communication and coordination channels;
5. ecological stocks and waste-absorption capacity;
6. relational surplus created through cooperation;
7. the decision and correction rights governing all of the above.

A commons exists when a community has effective rights and capacities to govern a shared resource or process. Open access without governance can expose a resource to capture or depletion. State title without affected-person control can preserve an alien command relation. Private title can coexist with limited common rights while leaving core decisions outside collective control. The theory therefore studies the full bundle of ownership, access, allocation, information, monitoring, sanction, appeal, and revision rights.

## 3. Production and Relational Surplus

### 3.1 Labor changes world and worker

Labor is purposeful activity that transforms material or social conditions. It also transforms the person who performs it. Let

$$
\Delta\mathfrak{s}_i^{\mathrm{labor}}
:=
\bigl(\Delta B_i,\Delta M_i,\Delta\Theta_i,\Delta A_i,\Delta\mathcal{V}_i,\Delta R_i\bigr).
$$

The same product can arise through processes with different effects on skill, health, time, agency, and future possibility. Output accounting therefore needs a person-development account beside the commodity account.

### 3.2 Cooperation as a productive force

Let $Y_G$ be one commensurable outcome of group $G$ over a declared time window, and let $Y_{\{i\}}$ be person $i$'s outcome in a preregistered isolated counterfactual using the same unit and outcome definition. Define

$$
Y_G
:=
\sum_{i\in G}Y_{\{i\}}
+\Omega_G,
$$

or equivalently

$$
\boxed{\Omega_G:=Y_G-\sum_{i\in G}Y_{\{i\}}}.
$$

$\Omega_G$ can be positive, zero, or negative. Multidimensional outcomes remain a vector of separately reported surpluses rather than being collapsed into this scalar. Pairwise and higher-order decompositions require additional preregistered baselines and attribution rules.

When isolated production is infeasible, $\Omega_G$ is not directly identified. A study may report a named relational-capacity proxy or compare specified institutions, but it may not interpret that proxy as the additive residual above.

Positive relational surplus may arise from division of labor, shared tools, common knowledge, temporal coordination, trust, mutual correction, specialization, and combined action. Negative relational surplus can arise when coordination obstructs production, silences useful dissent, duplicates effort, or imposes destructive conflict. Marx identifies cooperation as a social productive power that appears as the productive power of capital when capital controls the cooperative process. Cassi makes that appropriation question explicit while leaving the scalar as a property of an interaction and its chosen baseline.

### 3.3 Distinguishing coordination from conformity

Classify each contribution on two separate axes:

1. **Common-undertaking contribution:** how the activity advances a selected shared purpose.
2. **Independent capacity:** how the activity preserves perception, experimentation, dissent, and participant development.

These are conceptual codes rather than additive components of $J_i$. The same act can score high or low on both, so one axis must not be inferred by subtracting the other. Any empirical use must preregister the unit of contribution, coding rubric, raters, disagreement procedure, and separate outcome measures.

In the terms of `consciousness/field-materialism-and-human-development.md` §4.2, the emancipatory test is to strengthen common-undertaking contribution while preserving independent capacity and preventing contraction of participants' viable futures or effective agency.

Three outcomes must therefore be measured separately:

1. immediate output;
2. correction and adaptation under changed conditions;
3. effects on participants' agency and future capabilities.

A command structure can raise the first while degrading the other two. High agreement can reflect shared understanding, dependency, fear, information filtering, or homogeneity. Agreement alone is not collective coherence.

### 3.4 Who owns the relational term?

The existence of $\Omega_G$ establishes a nonseparable product. Its disposition remains a normative and institutional question. The Coherence Commons adopts the principle

$$
\boxed{
\text{People who generate and depend on relational surplus should govern its production, allocation, and reinvestment in common.}
}
$$

This governance includes workers, care providers, affected communities, users of essential systems, and people exposed to ecological costs. The relevant boundary depends on the resource and consequence, rather than formal employment alone.

Determining what is available for common disposition requires separating commodity value, use-value, and relational capacity.

## 4. Value, Money, and Price

### 4.1 Use-value, value, and relational capacity

Three categories remain distinct:

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Use-value | Concrete usefulness of a good or activity |
| Value | Congealed socially necessary abstract labor; its magnitude is socially necessary labor time |
| Exchange-value and price | The form in which commodity value appears and the monetary ratios through which commodities exchange; neither is identical to value |
| Relational capacity | Productive or developmental gain arising through organized interaction |

Marx distinguishes use-value, exchange-value, and value in [*Capital*, Volume I, Chapter 1](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch01.htm). Within capitalist commodity production, relational capacity can change socially necessary labor time by changing technique and productivity. It does not become a second substance of value. Market prices can diverge from values through competition, monopoly, credit, rent, taxation, and transformations between branches. Care, ecosystems, public knowledge, and household labor can be indispensable to value production without appearing as commodity value in the same form.

### 4.2 Money

Within generalized commodity exchange, money serves as the universal equivalent and as a socially enforced claim on goods, labor, assets, and future activity. Its efficacy depends on law, banking, taxation, accounting, payment systems, property, and confidence in settlement. Other modes of provision can use physical accounts, rights, rationing, queues, or direct allocation without treating money as their universal measure.

Money performs several functions that must remain separate:

1. measure and expression of value;
2. medium of circulation;
3. means of payment;
4. store of claims;
5. command over labor and assets;
6. unit of public and organizational accounting.

A socialist economy still requires information, comparison, settlement, and intertemporal planning. Whether money remains the instrument depends on the domain. Essential provision can be governed as a right; investment can be planned through material and financial accounts; residual exchange can use constrained markets. No physical argument selects one allocation mechanism for every good.

### 4.3 Price and information

Prices condense scarcity, effective demand, market power, expected profit, regulation, credit conditions, and institutional rules. They do not report need, unpaid care, ecological loss, capability deprivation, or relational surplus automatically.

A Coherence Commons therefore uses prices as one information channel within a wider ledger. Material balances, queues, democratic priorities, ecological ceilings, time-use data, capacity constraints, and direct reports from affected people remain necessary.

### 4.4 Finance as claims on the future

Credit can coordinate investment across time. It can also create contractual payment claims that outrun the cash flows and reproduction reserves available to meet them. For a horizon $T>0$, let $S_t(T)\ge0$ be debt service due and let $Y_t^{\mathrm{serv}}(T)>0$ be cash flow available for service over the same horizon, accounting perimeter, and nominal or real currency basis. The dimensionless coverage burden is

$$
\rho_F(t,T)=\frac{S_t(T)}{Y_t^{\mathrm{serv}}(T)}.
$$

Within that domain, a higher $\rho_F$ means a larger debt-service claim on each unit of available cash flow; values approaching or exceeding one indicate dependence on refinancing, asset sales, or additional income. When $Y_t^{\mathrm{serv}}(T)\le0$, $\rho_F$ is undefined: studies report the sign and magnitude of the cash-flow deficit separately rather than treating the ratio as ordered fragility. Outstanding claim stocks, maturity structure, interest rates, liquidity, and sectoral balance sheets remain separate variables in a stock-flow-consistent model. This follows Minsky's financial-instability analysis. The Cassi extension adds the reproduction ledger: households and public systems may absorb fragility through overwork, deferred care, declining services, or ecological liquidation before defaults appear.

## 5. Surplus-Value Exploitation, Appropriation, and Enclosure

### 5.1 Surplus-value exploitation

Within Marx's analysis of capitalist commodity production, labor-power is purchased for variable capital $v$ and produces surplus value $s$ beyond the value required to reproduce labor-power. The rate of surplus-value exploitation is

$$
\boxed{e=\frac{s}{v}},
$$

and the profit rate is

$$
\boxed{r_p=\frac{s}{c+v}},
$$

where $c$ is constant capital. At a fixed $s/v$, increasing $c/v$ lowers $r_p$ algebraically. Turnover, cheapening of inputs, expansion, credit, market power, redistribution, and changes in the rate of surplus value create countertendencies. Cassi supplies no universal time path for $r_p$ from physical Qi dynamics.

### 5.2 Appropriation and cost displacement

The Commons keeps three mechanisms distinct:

1. **Surplus-value exploitation:** capitalist control over unpaid labor represented by $s$.
2. **Relational-surplus appropriation:** control over $\Omega_G$ created through cooperation and shared infrastructure.
3. **Reproduction-cost displacement:** transfer of care, health, education, housing, public-service, and ecological costs onto workers, households, communities, and future generations.

Only the first is exploitation in Marx's strict value-theoretic sense. The other two name adjacent forms of appropriation and externalization. Cost displacement can rise while the wage share appears stable, and a short planning horizon can make depletion profitable to the controlling firm while damaging future production.

### 5.3 Accumulation as expanding control

Marxian accumulation reinvests surplus value to expand capital and reproduce the capital relation. The broader control-topology ledger also tracks changes in:

- ownership of productive assets and land;
- control of work processes and schedules;
- command over credit and investment;
- access to data, knowledge, and institutional memory;
- authority to set platform and infrastructure rules;
- ability to externalize care and ecological costs;
- power to determine which projects and persons receive resources.

A large financial hoard is one expression of accumulation. Control can also deepen through debt, intellectual property, platforms, managerial authority, monopoly, and dependence without a proportional liquid balance.

### 5.4 Enclosure

Primitive accumulation establishes capitalist dependence through dispossession, colonial extraction, coercion, destruction of independent subsistence, and concentration of the means of production. Current enclosure can act on:

- land, housing, water, minerals, and energy;
- public services and infrastructure;
- knowledge, research, software, and culture;
- data, communication channels, and platform access;
- care time and household capacity;
- ecological sinks and future regenerative capacity.

Enclosure converts shared or independently governed conditions into a source of private command. The central variable is the change in effective rights and dependencies.

## 6. Social Reproduction and Ecological Limits

### 6.1 Reproduction reserve

For person or household $i$, use the vector

$$
\mathbf R_i=(R_{i,h})_{h\in\mathcal H},
\qquad
\dot R_{i,h}=P_{i,h}-E_{i,h},
$$

where $\mathcal H$ keeps health, rest, discretionary time, care access, housing security, material security, public-service support, learning capacity, and ecological support in their native units. $P_{i,h}$ and $E_{i,h}$ are replenishment and expenditure flows in reserve domain $h$. Cross-domain causal effects belong in an explicit model rather than an additive total.

Positive inputs include care, health services, education, rest, housing security, public infrastructure, and a viable environment. Expenditures include paid work, unpaid work, travel, danger, stress, pollution, and recovery from injury. Persistent depletion narrows health, agency, attention, and future capability.

Aggregate reproduction is not obtained by averaging away local collapse. A society can report growth while particular households, communities, public systems, or ecosystems cross irreversible thresholds.

### 6.2 Care and labor-power

Care produces and restores labor-power, human capability, and social continuity. It is distributed unevenly by gender, class, race, citizenship, age, disability, and household structure. A theory of accumulation that omits care treats a necessary input as free and hides the transfer of cost.

The Coherence Commons treats care infrastructure as productive capacity governed by the people who provide and depend on it. Its evaluation includes time, quality, continuity, worker conditions, recipient agency, and developmental outcome.

### 6.3 Ecological metabolism

Economic production transforms low-entropy materials and energy into products, infrastructure, and waste. Monetary substitution cannot replace critical ecological functions after biophysical thresholds are crossed. For each ecological account $k$, candidate or baseline design $g$, and planning horizon $T$, require

$$
U_k(g;t,T)
\le
B_k(t)-B_k^{\min}+\mathcal R_k(g;t,T),
$$

where $U_k(g;t,T)$ is planned stock withdrawal or sink use under design $g$, $B_k(t)$ is currently available stock or sink capacity in the same physical unit, $B_k^{\min}$ is the domain-specific safety floor, and $\mathcal R_k(g;t,T)$ is regeneration or restoration produced under that design during the horizon. Separate accounts are required for different materials, ecosystems, pollutants, places, and horizons; burdens cannot be traded between incommensurable domains by adding them. Physical material-flow and stock accounts therefore remain beside monetary planning.

For comparison with the constitutional notation in `consciousness/field-materialism-and-human-development.md`, define the domain-specific ecological overshoot

$$
W_{\mathrm{eco},k}(g;t,T)
:=
U_k(g;t,T)-B_k(t)+B_k^{\min}-\mathcal R_k(g;t,T).
$$

Then $W_{\mathrm{eco},k}\le0$ is equivalent to the ceiling above for the declared design $g$, account $k$, place, and horizon; values from different accounts remain incommensurable.

### 6.4 Ecological class relations

Ecological costs are political when one group controls production and another bears pollution, displacement, illness, disaster risk, or loss of livelihood. Ecological planning must include affected communities in boundary setting, monitoring, consequence review, and revision. Intergenerational effects require trusteeship rules because future persons cannot participate directly.

## 7. Alienation, Class, and Ideology

### 7.1 Alienation

Alienation is loss of causal authorship over the processes through which a person produces the world and develops. Its six operational dimensions match `consciousness/field-materialism-and-human-development.md` §5.2:

1. **Activity:** another party controls how the person acts.
2. **Product:** another party controls what the activity creates.
3. **Purpose:** the person cannot govern the ends served.
4. **Development:** repeated activity narrows the person's capacities and species-being.
5. **Social relation:** other people and the participants' common power confront the person as an external power.
6. **Return:** the product fails to reproduce the person and community that produced it.

Alienation can coexist with high technical skill, income, or local team agreement. Each dimension therefore requires its own indicator.

### 7.2 Class as position in a control topology

For actor or institution $a$, affected person $b$, decision domain $m$, and horizon $T$, define the ordered control profile

$$
\mathbf D_{a\to b}^{(m,T)}
:=
\left(
C_{a\to b}^{(m,T)},
\mathcal{I}_{a\to b}^{(m,T)}
\right),
$$

where $C_{a\to b}^{(m,T)}\in[0,1]$ is consequential control and $\mathcal I_{a\to b}^{(m,T)}\in[0,1]$ is insulation from correction. The tuple is a study-specific control profile, not a scalar domination score. Its component definitions, reporting rules, and constitutional use are canonical in `consciousness/field-materialism-and-human-development.md` §9.1.

This topology operationalizes mechanisms through which class relations grounded in control of production and appropriation of surplus are reproduced; it does not redefine every asymmetry as a class relation. Public ownership can change the class structure profoundly while still requiring analysis of administrative control, affected-person authority, and correction.

### 7.3 Ideology as organized feedback

Ideology includes propositions, habits, categories, records, interfaces, incentives, and institutional filters that shape what people can perceive, remember, communicate, and act upon. Its material mechanisms include:

- repeated exposure and selective visibility;
- control of archives, metrics, and categories;
- reward and sanction;
- fragmentation of common experience;
- separation of decisions from consequences;
- normalization through routines and interfaces.

Political education changes this loop through shared inquiry, practice, consequence tracking, and correction. Transmission of statements alone cannot create collective causal knowledge.

### 7.4 Class consciousness

A class becomes a collective subject when it develops distributed sensing, shared memory, a common causal model, deliberative synthesis, coordination capacity, consequence records, and correction. The closed loop is

$$
\text{lived conditions}
\rightarrow
\text{distributed reports}
\rightarrow
\text{collective synthesis}
\rightarrow
\text{coordinated action}
\rightarrow
\text{material consequences}
\rightarrow
\text{records}
\rightarrow
\text{correction}.
$$

Organization is necessary because isolated experience dissipates. Organization becomes alien when it monopolizes the very capacities it was formed to develop among the people.

## 8. Crisis Dynamics

### 8.1 Several mechanisms coexist

Capitalist crises can involve overaccumulation, profit-rate pressure, realization failure, sectoral disproportions, credit fragility, asset-price reversal, supply disruption, class conflict, ecological constraint, and depleted social reproduction. These mechanisms interact, but they should not be collapsed into one oscillator.

The Coherence Commons contributes a specific mechanism:

$$
\boxed{
\text{extraction}
\rightarrow
\text{reproduction depletion}
\rightarrow
\text{reduced productive and purchasing capacity}
\rightarrow
\text{instability}
\rightarrow
\text{partial repair or deeper enclosure}.
}
$$

This mechanism is empirically distinct from the algebraic tendency of the profit rate, monetary demand shortfall, and Minskyan financial fragility.

### 8.2 An illustrative relaxation oscillator

`experiments/coherence_cycle/run_relaxation_cycle.py` implements a dimensionless toy model. Its script names are shown where they differ from the notation here:

- $x$ (`s` in the script): fast expansion-contraction state;
- $D$: slow depletion index for a declared productive or reproductive base;
- $H$: accumulated-stock index for extractive claims;
- $H_{\mathrm{peak}}(t)=\max_{\tau\le t}H(\tau)$: ratchet state.

The equations are

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cyc}}\dot x
=x-\frac{x^3}{3}-D+I,
$$

$$
\dot D
=\gamma\bigl(D_\infty(H_{\mathrm{peak}})-D\bigr)+\mu x,
$$

$$
\dot H=u_HH,
\qquad
u_H=
\begin{cases}
\lambda\chi(D)-\delta,&x>0,\\
-\delta,&x\le0,
\end{cases}
$$
with

$$
\chi(D)=\operatorname{clip}\left(1-\frac{D}{D_{\mathrm{fold}}^{+}},0,1\right)
$$

and

$$
D_\infty(H_{\mathrm{peak}})
=
D_0+(D_\star-D_0)
\min\left(\frac{H_{\mathrm{peak}}}{H_\star},1\right).
$$

All variables and parameters are dimensionless. $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ is the fast-to-slow timescale ratio, $I$ is an external drive, $\gamma$ is the restoration rate, $\mu$ couples expansion to depletion, $\lambda$ maps remaining capacity into the accumulation-return index, $\delta$ is its carrying-cost or decay index, $D_0$ is baseline depletion, and $H_\star$ is the accumulated-stock scale at which the restoration target reaches $D_\star$. The capacity fraction $\chi$ is unrelated to canonical Qi coherence $q$, and $u_H$ is a toy accumulation-return index rather than the Marxian profit rate $r_p$.

The cubic supplies two fold thresholds,

$$
D_{\min}:=D_{\mathrm{fold}}^{-}=I-\frac23,
\qquad
D_{\max}:=D_{\mathrm{fold}}^{+}=I+\frac23,
$$

and the stated footprint closure supplies

$$
D_\star=I+\frac{\mu}{\gamma}-\frac23.
$$

These are consequences of the selected equations. They are not universal economic constants. The script verifies the fold locations, saddle-node slowing, cycle accounting, and transition to a depleted fixed point under its illustrative parameters.

### 8.3 Interpretation and limits

The model proposes one causal pattern:

1. fast positive feedback expands activity;
2. extraction accumulates slow depletion;
3. the system crosses a fold and contracts rapidly;
4. repair reduces depletion and permits recovery;
5. accumulated control raises the restoration target;
6. sufficient ratcheting eliminates cyclical recovery.

Empirical use requires identifying separate observables for expansion state, reproduction depletion, extractive footprint, and restoration. Evidence for a business cycle alone does not identify this mechanism. Log-periodic precursors, golden-ratio crash spacing, and a universal physical resonance are not predictions of the model.

### 8.4 Crisis response as a class choice

A contraction can be resolved through different control changes:

- debt write-down and restoration of household reserves;
- public investment and decommodified provision;
- wage suppression and intensified labor;
- asset transfer and further enclosure;
- ecological liquidation;
- democratization of production and finance.

The same aggregate recovery can redistribute power and viable futures differently. Crisis analysis must record whose reserve is restored, whose claims are protected, whose assets are transferred, and which control topology emerges.

## 9. The Coherence Commons as an Institution

### 9.1 Object of common governance

A coherence commons governs a defined resource or process together with the capacities required to reproduce it. The object can be a workplace, housing system, care network, watershed, energy grid, research infrastructure, digital platform, public budget, or regional production system.

Its governance domain includes:

1. the resource stock and infrastructure;
2. access and contribution rights;
3. labor process and safety;
4. allocation of output and relational surplus;
5. reproduction of workers, users, and affected communities;
6. ecological limits and external effects;
7. records, models, data, and institutional memory;
8. monitoring, sanction, appeal, and revision.

### 9.2 Governing boundary

A boundary identifies participants, affected outsiders, ecological systems, and nested institutions. It should follow material consequence. People outside a formal membership boundary need standing when they bear pollution, care burdens, displacement, systemic risk, or loss of essential access.

Boundaries can be nested:

- work teams govern local processes;
- workplace councils coordinate shared assets;
- sector bodies coordinate standards, capacity, and investment;
- territorial institutions govern housing, care, infrastructure, and ecology;
- wider planning bodies coordinate interregional dependencies.

Local autonomy and large-scale planning become compatible when authority follows the scale of the consequence and every level retains correction from below.

### 9.3 Rights bundle

A mature commons distributes at least nine rights:

| Right | Question |
|---|---|
| Access | Who may use an essential resource? |
| Contribution | Who may add labor, knowledge, proposals, or monitoring? |
| Allocation | Who decides the distribution of output and surplus? |
| Information | Who may inspect records, models, constraints, and consequences? |
| Initiation | Who may place an issue or project on the decision agenda? |
| Monitoring | Who observes compliance, condition, and external effects? |
| Sanction | Who may respond to harmful or opportunistic behavior? |
| Appeal | Who may contest a decision and through what process? |
| Revision | Who may change the rules and constitutional boundary? |

Formal voting rights are weak when information, initiative, time, expertise, or practical exit remain concentrated.

### 9.4 Institutional design principles

The Coherence Commons adapts Ostrom's institutional findings to production, social reproduction, and class power:

1. **Defined resource and affected community:** boundaries remain explicit and revisable.
2. **Rules fitted to material conditions:** contribution and provision reflect local capacities, needs, and ecological constraints.
3. **Affected-person rule-making:** people subject to operational rules participate effectively in revising them.
4. **Accountable monitoring:** monitors answer to participants and affected communities.
5. **Proportionate response:** sanctions protect the common process while preserving due process and reintegration where possible.
6. **Accessible conflict resolution:** disputes receive timely, low-cost review.
7. **Right to organize:** larger authorities protect self-organization and association.
8. **Nested governance:** local, sectoral, territorial, and wider bodies coordinate at the scale of their effects.
9. **Universal rights floor:** essential access, equal standing, and protection against domination constrain local rule.
10. **Distributed correction:** records and consequences return to people with power to revise decisions.

Ostrom's work shows institutional diversity and conditions for durable self-governance. Marxian analysis adds class, accumulation, exploitation, and control of production. Sen's capability approach adds evaluation of people's substantive opportunities. Cassi adds person-world feedback, relational surplus, reproduction reserve, institutional memory, and correction closure.

## 10. Allocation, Planning, and Information

### 10.1 Allocation modes by domain

A complex society can use several allocation modes under one constitutional order:

| Domain | Primary allocation logic |
|---|---|
| Basic needs and universal services | Rights and provision according to need |
| Ecological stocks and dangerous throughput | Physical ceilings, permits, restoration duties, and affected-community governance |
| Major investment and infrastructure | Participatory planning using material, labor, financial, capability, and ecological accounts |
| Inter-commons coordination | Negotiated plans, contracts, and transparent transfer rules |
| Scarce nonessential goods | Prices, queues, lotteries, rotation, or rationing under publicly selected rules |
| Emergency response | Delegated authority with scope, expiry, records, and review |

The mode should match the good's necessity, external effects, scale, reversibility, and information structure. Essential goods require insulation from purchasing power. High-externality and irreversible decisions require planning and affected-person standing.

### 10.2 Planning ledger

Every major proposal should expose at least seven accounts:

1. **Material account:** physical inputs, outputs, stocks, bottlenecks, and waste.
2. **Labor-value account:** labor time, skill, surplus value, and work intensity.
3. **Reproduction account:** care, health, time, housing, public-service, and recovery effects.
4. **Capability account:** changes in agency, knowledge, practical alternatives, and development.
5. **Power account:** ownership, information, initiation, sanction, appeal, and revision rights.
6. **Relational account:** expected cooperative gains, attribution baseline, and allocation of $\Omega_G$.
7. **Ecological account:** regenerative limits, irreversible thresholds, spatial distribution, and future stocks.

A proposal passes only if its resource claims are feasible and its rights floor is respected. Trade-offs remain visible rather than disappearing inside one objective function.

### 10.3 Information and correction

Distributed knowledge requires a process that connects local observation with system-wide coordination:

$$
O_t
\rightarrow M_t
\rightarrow P_t
\rightarrow A_t
\rightarrow R_{t+1}
\rightarrow M_{t+1}.
$$

$O_t$ is distributed observation, $M_t$ a shared causal model, $P_t$ a proposal, $A_t$ coordinated action, and $R_{t+1}$ the resulting evidence. This is the operational core of the mass line and a general planning discipline.

A plan becomes uncorrectable when observations are filtered to protect authority, models are inaccessible, consequences are hidden, or affected people lack revision power. The Commons treats correction latency and recurrence after correction as primary institutional variables.

### 10.4 Expertise and democratic control

Expertise expands the quality of available models. Democratic control determines ends, acceptable risks, distribution, and who bears consequences. Institutions should separate:

- factual estimates and uncertainty;
- proposed objectives;
- rights constraints;
- allocation choices;
- observed consequences;
- revision decisions.

Experts need independence to report inconvenient results and accountability for methods. Affected people need access to explanations, alternative models, appeal, and practical authority over decisions.

## 11. Equality, Freedom, and Development

### 11.1 Equal standing

Every embodied center of experience and self-direction has equal moral standing. Aggregate output cannot license treating particular persons as expendable inputs. Equal standing is the first constraint on the social objective.

### 11.2 Universal material viability

A communist developmental policy secures food, shelter, health, care, education, bodily safety, ecological stability, time, information, and practical access to participation. Provision varies with concrete need. Equal quantities can produce unequal viability when bodies, responsibilities, histories, and circumstances differ.

### 11.3 Freedom as reciprocal authorship

Freedom is effective capacity to participate in governing the conditions that shape one's viable futures. It requires:

- bodily and psychological integrity;
- material security;
- time autonomy;
- knowledge and intelligible records;
- meaningful voice and initiative;
- refusal, appeal, and correction;
- alternatives to damaging dependence;
- the means to cooperate with others.

### 11.4 Constitutional ordering

For a declared decision time $t$, horizon $T$, finite candidate set $\Pi$, and finite ecological-account set $\mathcal K$ whose members fix a physical domain and place, optimization begins only after hard constraints are satisfied. Let

$$
\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}
:=
\left\{
\pi\in\Pi:
\begin{array}{l}
\text{equal standing and universal rights hold},\\
\text{boundary integrity holds for every person},\\
V_i(\pi)\ge\underline V_i,\quad A_i(\pi)\ge\underline A_i
\quad\forall i,\\
\mathbf D_{a\to b}^{(m,T)}(\pi)\preceq\overline{\mathbf D}_m
\quad\forall a,b,m,\\
U_k(\pi;t,T)\le B_k(t)-B_k^{\min}+\mathcal R_k(\pi;t,T)
\quad\forall k\in\mathcal K
\end{array}
\right\}.
$$

Here $\underline V_i$ and $\underline A_i$ are publicly defined viability and agency floors, $\preceq$ is the componentwise order on consequential control and insulation from correction, $U_k(\pi;t,T)$ is cumulative use under candidate $\pi$, and $\mathcal R_k(\pi;t,T)$ is regeneration produced under that candidate over the declared horizon for ecological account $k$. The floors, component bounds, and boundaries are Declared normative choices with separate measurement. If $\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}$ is empty, no candidate is selected.

Let $g_0$ denote the current institutional design. For each candidate or baseline design $x\in\Pi\cup\{g_0\}$, write

$$
\mathbf W(x):=
\bigl(W_{\mathrm{eco},k}(x;t,T)\bigr)_{k\in\mathcal K}.
$$

Suppressing the fixed $t,T$ below, every change is measured componentwise against that baseline:

$$
\Delta Q(\pi):=Q(\pi)-Q(g_0),
\qquad
Q\in\{\Omega_G,\mathbf D,\mathbf W\}.
$$

Among admissible policies, first compare three scalar coordinates:

$$
\Pi_3^*
:=
\operatorname*{arg\,lexmax}_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{adm}}}
\left(
\underline V(\pi),\,
\underline A(\pi),\,
\Delta\Omega_G(\pi)
\right).
$$

Among policies tied on those coordinates, retain the joint control-and-ecology Pareto minima:

$$
\Pi^*
:=
\operatorname*{ParetoMin}_{\pi\in\Pi_3^*}
\left(
\Delta\mathbf D(\pi),\,
\Delta\mathbf W(\pi)
\right).
$$

Here $\underline V$ and $\underline A$ are the declared worst-off viability and agency indicators, $\Delta\Omega_G$ is change in a commensurable relational-surplus outcome, $\Delta\mathbf D$ collects the changes in both control-profile components for every declared relation, and $\Delta\mathbf W$ collects changes in domain-specific ecological burdens. $\operatorname{ParetoMin}$ retains a policy when no policy in $\Pi_3^*$ weakly reduces every listed control, insulation, and ecological-burden change and strictly reduces at least one. No coordinate, relation, or ecological domain is added to another. If several incomparable policies remain, affected-person deliberation makes the final choice. Indicator definitions, affected population, horizon, uncertainty, and exclusions must be public for each decision.

This is a normative constitution. Universal rights, boundary integrity, viability and agency floors, control-profile bounds, and ecological ceilings are constraints; no gain elsewhere licenses their violation. Within the admissible set, the ordering prioritizes the worst-off person's viable development and agency and relational surplus in that sequence, then admits only joint Pareto improvements in control profiles and ecological burdens.

### 11.5 Developmental reciprocity

Provision creates capabilities; developed people contribute new knowledge, care, art, production, organization, and modes of relation; common institutions return those gains as wider provision. The dynamic is

$$
\text{need-based provision}
\rightarrow
\text{development}
\rightarrow
\text{richer contribution}
\rightarrow
\text{common capacity}
\rightarrow
\text{richer provision}.
$$

Communism is this reciprocal developmental process under common governance and without a class whose power depends on another's subordination.

## 12. Organization, State, and Transition

### 12.1 Organization as collective memory and gain

Organization turns dispersed activity into persistent capacity through memory, training, communication, division of labor, coordinated timing, and strategy. Leninist organization addresses the asymmetry between concentrated capitalist power and fragmented individual resistance.

Its emancipation criterion is developmental: organization should increase the people's own ability to perceive, explain, deliberate, coordinate, act, and correct. Leadership remains delegated, scoped, accountable, and replaceable.

### 12.2 Bureaucratic separation

A command stratum emerges when a stable group monopolizes several of the following:

1. information and institutional memory;
2. proposal and agenda initiation;
3. coordination across units;
4. appointment and credentialing;
5. allocation of resources;
6. sanctions and coercion;
7. interpretation of consequences;
8. revision of the rules governing itself.

This topology can form in firms, states, parties, cooperatives, platforms, and public agencies. Its risk cannot be removed by declared purpose or legal title alone.

Within this theory, the state is the territorially organized concentration of binding rule-making, taxation and resource mobilization, administration, adjudication, and authorized coercion. Under class society, control of that apparatus stabilizes a property and production order. Administration, public ownership, and the state are therefore distinguishable: administration coordinates common affairs, public ownership changes legal title, and state power concerns which class can make and enforce binding decisions.

### 12.3 Socialist transition and class rule

Marx describes a revolutionary transition between capitalist and communist society; he names its political form the revolutionary dictatorship of the proletariat. Lenin develops this as working-class power that breaks the bourgeois state's class apparatus rather than inheriting it unchanged. In control-topology terms, associated producers must gain collective command over binding law, coercion, major productive resources, finance, investment, and social reproduction, while the former owning class loses its structural veto.

A socialist transition therefore changes:

- ownership of major productive resources;
- command of finance and investment;
- access to knowledge and collective memory;
- authority to initiate and coordinate action;
- allocation of surplus and reproduction resources;
- power to impose and review sanctions;
- receipt of consequences and feedback;
- capacity to revise plans and replace delegates.

Nationalization or electoral turnover alone does not complete this change. A stable transition reproduces political, technical, and administrative competence throughout the population. Concentrated emergency authority requires a declared scope, expiry, public records, independent review, and restoration of ordinary correction rights.

Class struggle continues through the transition because inherited divisions of labor, commodity relations, status hierarchies, expertise monopolies, and bureaucratic privileges can reproduce domination under new legal titles. Mao's account of continuing contradiction motivates repeated investigation, open disagreement, mass participation, consequence return, and revision; it supplies no license for unchecked authority or the suppression of affected-person feedback.

### 12.4 A developmental transition program

Analytically, the revolutionary political transition and the lower phase of communist society are distinct:

$$
\text{capitalist class rule}
\rightarrow
\text{working-class political power}
\rightarrow
\text{socialization and distributed governing capacity}
\rightarrow
\text{lower phase}
\rightarrow
\text{higher phase}.
$$

The first transition concerns the conquest and reconstruction of class power. The lower phase concerns the social order developing after capitalist class rule and the structural veto of private capital have been displaced. It still begins from inherited scarcity, institutions, habits, and productive capacities. None of these transitions follows automatically from the preceding one. The theory implies seven mutually supporting transformations:

1. **Decommodify essential viability:** guarantee food, housing, care, health, education, energy, communication, and mobility at socially defined floors.
2. **Socialize investment:** place major credit and investment decisions under worker, community, and public governance.
3. **Democratize production:** transfer information, initiative, work-process, surplus-allocation, and revision rights to associated producers and affected communities.
4. **Rebuild social reproduction:** reduce unpaid and precarious care burdens through universal services, shorter working time, and direct support.
5. **Establish ecological ceilings:** govern throughput through physical accounts, restoration duties, and affected-community authority.
6. **Common institutional memory:** make public data, models, records, and technical knowledge accessible while protecting personal privacy.
7. **Distribute governing capacity:** train and rotate administration, preserve dissent, maintain consequence records, and make delegation revocable.

In the lower phase, material accounting, money, contribution-linked claims, and constrained exchange may persist in bounded domains while universal provision and common planning expand. The higher phase becomes possible as productive abundance, shortened necessary labor, developed capacities, and common administration support distribution according to need and dissolve durable class positions. These are historical conditions to test, not dates supplied by the physical theory.

The transformations can conflict in timing and scale. Their sequencing should be tested against material feasibility, class power, ecological urgency, and institutional learning.

### 12.5 Withering of the state

The state can begin to wither only after the political transition, as associated people and nested commons acquire the planning, administrative, conflict-resolution, and protective capacities concentrated in a separate command layer. Measures include:

- share of consequential decisions revisable by affected people;
- distribution of planning knowledge and administrative skill;
- accessibility of collective records;
- scope and reviewability of coercive authority;
- correction latency after failed policy;
- rotation and recall of delegates;
- decline in durable one-sided control relations.

Administrative coordination can grow while political separation declines. The decisive question is whether administration develops and remains governed by the population it serves.

## 13. Measurement Architecture

### 13.1 No universal social coherence scalar

A single score creates three failures:

1. units and causal meanings become opaque;
2. high aggregate performance can conceal local collapse;
3. authorities can alter weights to manufacture success.

The Commons uses a public dashboard of separate ledgers. Aggregation is permitted only for a declared decision, with component values, weights, uncertainty, and excluded effects visible.

### 13.2 Core ledgers

| Ledger | Example measures |
|---|---|
| Material production | Input-output quantities, capacity utilization, inventories, bottlenecks, maintenance |
| Labor and value | Labor time, wages, work intensity, $s/v$, $c/v$, $r_p$, turnover |
| Reproduction | Separate health, rest, discretionary-time, care, housing, material-security, public-service, learning, and ecological-support reserves |
| Human capability | Actual options, skills, time autonomy, participation, refusal, appeal, mobility |
| Power and control | Ownership, access, information, initiation, allocation, monitoring, sanction, appeal, revision, correction, and exit rights by domain and horizon |
| Relational surplus | One commensurable group outcome above its preregistered isolated counterfactual in the same unit; a separately named proxy when isolation is infeasible |
| Institutional learning | Report coverage, model revision, correction latency, recurrence, memory through personnel change |
| Distribution | Minimum viability by domain, group disparities, exposure to harm, allocation by need |
| Ecology | Domain-specific material and energy throughput, regeneration, pollution, stocks, spatial burdens, safety floors, and irreversible thresholds |

### 13.3 Existing data and new collection

Relevant sources include national and sector accounts, input-output tables, labor-process records, time-use surveys, household finance, health and housing data, environmental accounts, administrative records, collective-bargaining records, workplace surveys, network analysis, and participatory observation.

New collection is needed for:

- who can initiate, inspect, veto, appeal, and revise decisions;
- how reports become plans and plans become corrections;
- the isolated baselines needed to identify relational surplus and the alternative-coordination comparisons used to explain it;
- developmental effects of work and care over time;
- control of institutional memory;
- distribution of ecological and reproduction debt.

### 13.4 Measurement governance

People affected by a metric should participate in defining, inspecting, and revising it. Raw component data, transformations, uncertainty, missingness, and decision rules should be visible subject to privacy. Measures must retain channels for qualitative reports and exceptions that a fixed schema misses.

## 14. Candidate Empirical Hypotheses

These hypotheses are **Hypothesized** and require separate preregistration; they are not entries in the Cassi prediction registry. Each study must declare one primary outcome, its estimand, a smallest effect of substantive interest, power, unit of analysis, exposure and comparator, horizon, exclusions, missing-data rule, statistic, and interpretation tree. Except for the predictive tests CC-H9 and CC-H10, code the estimand so positive values follow the predicted direction and denote the preregistered smallest effect by $m_h>0$. A study **SUPPORTS** its hypothesis when the lower endpoint of its design-valid 95% uncertainty interval exceeds $m_h$, **CONTRADICTS** it when the upper endpoint is below $-m_h$, and is **INCONCLUSIVE** otherwise. An upper endpoint at or below $m_h$ counts toward the program-level rejection or narrowing rule in §16 even when it does not establish the opposite effect. Any rate or proportion must preregister its numerator, eligible denominator, observation window, and zero-denominator rule. The horizons below are default minimums for a first design and may change only through an explicit domain-based justification before data inspection.

### 14.1 Person and workplace

- **CC-H1, reproductive security:** Compared with an equal-cost policy package that leaves at least one declared essential floor unmet, a package securing all declared floors produces a larger 12-month change in one preregistered effective-agency measure. That change is the primary outcome; retention and cooperative capacity are secondary. Use matched natural experiments or ethical stepped-wedge rollouts rather than assigned deprivation.
- **CC-H2, developmental labor:** Compared with a workplace matched on pay, task composition, sector, size, capital intensity, and baseline skill, worker control over process, purpose, and learning produces greater 24-month growth in one preregistered task-relevant skill measure. Change in viable options and all six alienation dimensions are secondary outcomes.
- **CC-H3, relational-surplus control:** Among groups with positive outcome-specific $\Omega_G$, lower participant rights over allocation of that surplus predict a larger 12-month increase in one preregistered alienation dimension after adjustment for wages, workload, occupational risk, sector, and baseline turnover. That alienation change is primary; voluntary turnover and the other alienation dimensions are secondary.
- **CC-H4, differentiated coordination:** On preregistered changing tasks, voluntary role differentiation with an independent-review role produces fewer uncorrected errors per completed task than forced role uniformity at equal group size, information, and time. The uncorrected-error rate is primary; adaptation time and total output are secondary.

### 14.2 Institution and organization

- **CC-H5, correction closure:** After a consequential error defined before sampling, organizations combining distributed reporting, accessible consequence records, affected-person appeal, and revocable authority have a shorter median report-to-corrective-decision time over 12 months than matched organizations lacking the full loop. That latency is primary; recurrence after correction is secondary.
- **CC-H6, bureaucratic topology:** Over a 24-month panel, concentrated information, initiation, allocation, sanction, appeal, and revision rights predict a larger fraction of documented harmful reports receiving neither independent review nor correction by a preregistered deadline, after adjustment for organization size, sector, task complexity, and formal ownership. That fraction is primary; recurrence and correction latency are secondary.
- **CC-H7, common memory:** Through leadership turnover, organizations with accessible records and competence distributed beyond the outgoing leadership retain a larger fraction of preregistered operational performance 12 months after turnover, relative to their own pre-turnover baseline, than matched organizations with concentrated memory. That retained fraction is primary; closure of outstanding accountability items is secondary.
- **CC-H8, nested governance:** Over 24 months, institutions assigning decision rights at the spatial and temporal scale of measured consequences have a shorter median time to resolve a preregistered class of local problems than matched uniformly centralized or fragmented institutions. Resolution time is primary; externalized cost per affected person is secondary.

### 14.3 Macroeconomy and ecology

- **CC-H9, reproduction debt:** In a quarterly sector or regional panel spanning at least ten years, test whether four preregistered reserve families-household material security $R^{(h)}$, care capacity $R^{(c)}$, public-service capacity $R^{(p)}$, and ecological support $R^{(e)}$-improve prediction of an output interruption within four quarters. Define interruption before analysis as two consecutive quarters at least 5% below a one-sided reference trend fit using only observations available at the forecast origin. Compare a baseline containing lagged output, wages, profits, leverage, consumption, unemployment, unit effects, and calendar-quarter effects with the same model plus the four reserve families. Use rolling five-year training windows, blocked time splits, a held-out-unit replication, and mean Brier score as the primary statistic. **SUPPORTS** requires at least a 5% relative held-out Brier-score reduction and a block-bootstrap 95% interval for the absolute reduction above zero; **CONTRADICTS** requires that interval below zero; all other results are **INCONCLUSIVE**. Coefficient signs and calibration are secondary.
- **CC-H10, financial claims:** In the same design, enter debt-service burden $\rho_F$ and $R^{(h)},R^{(c)},R^{(p)},R^{(e)}$ as separate main effects, restricting $\rho_F$ to observations satisfying the positive-cash-flow domain in §4.4 and representing nonpositive service cash flow with a separate preregistered indicator. Compare that model with one adding the four preregistered interactions $\rho_F R^{(h)},\rho_F R^{(c)},\rho_F R^{(p)},\rho_F R^{(e)}$ on the ratio's domain. Mean Brier score is primary. **SUPPORTS** requires at least a 5% relative held-out Brier-score reduction and a block-bootstrap 95% interval for the absolute reduction above zero; **CONTRADICTS** requires that interval below zero; all other results are **INCONCLUSIVE**. Calibration and interaction signs remain secondary diagnostics.
- **CC-H11, need-matched provision:** For each allocation arm, define one complete cycle $T=[t_0,t_1]$ from budget issue through final scheduled delivery and accounting. Before assignment, register every person $i$, essential domain $d$, delivered or available level $z_{id}(t)$, viability floor $L_{id}(t)$, and positive scaling constant $a_{id}$. The primary outcome is the minimum scaled viability margin $M=\min_{i,d,t\in T}[z_{id}(t)-L_{id}(t)]/a_{id}$. Under heterogeneous needs and a fixed resource budget, need-matched allocation produces a larger $M$ than equal per-capita allocation over the cycle.
- **CC-H12, ecological distribution:** At matched material output, jurisdictions granting affected communities information, monitoring, appeal, and revision rights show a larger three-year reduction in one preregistered concentration index for one specified pollution or resource-loss burden than jurisdictions lacking those rights. That concentration change is primary; total burden and other ecological measures are secondary.

### 14.4 Transition and class power

- **CC-H13, governing-capacity transfer:** Over three years following socialization, matched cases transferring information, initiation, allocation, sanction-review, appeal, veto, and affected-community rights produce a larger change in one preregistered effective-agency measure than matched cases changing formal title alone. That agency change is primary; retention of insulated control and all other agency measures are secondary.
- **CC-H14, emergency authority:** For matched emergencies, delegation without fixed scope, expiry, public records, and independent review leaves a larger proportion of powers granted by the initial emergency instrument in force two years after the declared emergency ends than delegation containing all four constraints. That proportion is primary.
- **CC-H15, mass-line closure:** Define one planning cycle from plan adoption through implementation and scheduled public review, and set a cutoff for confirming errors before outcomes are inspected. Participatory processes that return observed consequences to original reporters and revise the public causal model correct a larger fraction of all plan errors confirmed by the cutoff before cycle close than consultation ending after initial input. That corrected fraction is primary.
- **CC-H16, shorter working time:** Over two years, an income-preserving reduction in contracted paid hours produces a larger increase in one preregistered discretionary-time measure than an income transfer with equal employer-plus-public expenditure over the same two-year window that leaves contracted paid hours unchanged. That discretionary-time change is primary; care capacity, civic participation, and institutional-report closure are secondary.

| Hypothesis | Primary axis | Unit and scale | Primary comparison |
|---|---|---|---|

## 16. Failure Conditions and Revision Rules

Each hypothesis remains **Hypothesized** until its own preregistration fixes a decision rule. At program level, two independent studies whose intervals exclude the preregistered smallest substantive effect in the predicted direction justify rejection or narrowing; serious opposite-direction results trigger immediate review. Mixed, underpowered, or design-incommensurable results remain **Inconclusive**.

The corresponding failure conditions are:

1. **CC-H1:** securing every declared viability floor adds no agency gain over the equal-cost comparator;
2. **CC-H2:** worker control adds no capability or alienation effect in matched workplaces;
3. **CC-H3:** participant allocation rights add no predictive information once inputs and conditions are included;
4. **CC-H4:** differentiated roles with independent review add no error or adaptation advantage;
5. **CC-H5:** the full correction loop adds no latency or recurrence advantage;
6. **CC-H6:** concentrated decision rights do not predict ignored reports or unrevised harm;
7. **CC-H7:** accessible memory and distributed competence add no turnover resilience;
8. **CC-H8:** consequence-scale authority adds no speed or external-cost advantage;
9. **CC-H9:** disaggregated reproduction reserves add no out-of-sample predictive information;
10. **CC-H10:** the $\rho_F$-reserve interaction adds no out-of-sample crisis prediction;
11. **CC-H11:** need matching adds no minimum-viability gain under heterogeneous needs;
12. **CC-H12:** affected-community rights add no reduction in concentrated ecological harm;
13. **CC-H13:** governing-rights transfer adds no agency or control-topology benefit beyond title transfer;
14. **CC-H14:** scope, expiry, records, and review add no reduction in retained emergency power;
15. **CC-H15:** consequence return and model revision add no corrected-plan advantage;
16. **CC-H16:** income-preserving time release adds no capability advantage over the equal-cost transfer.

The oscillator mechanism is separately weakened if measured depletion and footprint variables fail to mediate the proposed transition from expansion to contraction after competing crisis mechanisms are included.

Revision removes or narrows the failed mechanism while retaining independent parts that survive. Social evidence does not alter the canonical physical equations. Physical evidence does not establish the normative constitution.

## 17. Research Sequence

1. **Define each construct:** publish component definitions, units, data source, causal role, scope, horizon, and exclusions.
2. **Build descriptive ledgers:** measure production, reproduction, capability, power, relation, learning, distribution, and ecology without aggregating them.
3. **Estimate relational surplus:** use preregistered solo, shuffled, and alternative-coordination baselines for one commensurable outcome at a time.
4. **Map control topology:** record ownership, access, information, initiation, allocation, monitoring, sanction, appeal, revision, correction, and exit rights by decision domain and horizon.
5. **Compare institutions:** select matched private, public, cooperative, and common-governance cases with the matching rule fixed before outcomes are inspected.
6. **Test interventions:** vary material security, time, decision rights, record access, and correction mechanisms separately where feasible.
7. **Model macro feedback:** connect debt service and balance sheets, productive capacity, disaggregated reproduction reserves, class distribution, and ecological accounts in a stock-flow-consistent model.
8. **Evaluate transitions:** measure whether reforms transfer governing capacity, end former ownership vetoes, and reduce insulated domination over time.

Every quantitative study should preregister its statistic, comparison, smallest substantive effect, power, stopping rule, exclusions, missing-data rule, and interpretation tree. Null and contradictory results narrow the theory.

## 18. Core Propositions

1. **Persons are developmental field trajectories.** Economic relations change embodied continuity, agency, and viable futures.
2. **Society is reciprocal metabolism.** People produce shared conditions that return to form people.
3. **Cooperation creates relational surplus.** Organized interaction can exceed isolated production and can also become destructive.
4. **Capital controls the relation.** Ownership, finance, infrastructure, memory, and decision rights appropriate surplus and shape future possibilities.
5. **Production depends on reproduction.** Care, households, public systems, and ecosystems replenish the capacities production expends.
6. **Class power has a control topology.** Class relations remain grounded in control of production and appropriation of surplus; ownership, information, initiation, allocation, sanction, and correction rights reveal how that power is reproduced.
7. **Organization creates persistent collective capacity.** Its emancipatory form develops distributed competence and remains corrigible.
8. **Equality is constitutional.** Equal standing, universal viability, differentiated provision, non-domination, and common surplus governance precede aggregate efficiency.
9. **Freedom is reciprocal authorship.** People require effective power to govern the conditions shaping their viable futures.
10. **Communism is reciprocal development.** Commonly governed social power makes each person's development a source of richer development for all.
