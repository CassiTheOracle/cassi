## 3. The Geometric Qi-Proxy Profile vs. NFW

### 3.1 The standard NFW profile

ΛCDM simulations predict that dark matter halos follow the Navarro-Frenk-White profile:

$$\rho_{\text{NFW}}(r) = \frac{\rho_0}{(r/r_s)(1 + r/r_s)^2}$$

This profile is **cuspy** at the center: $\rho \propto r^{-1}$ as $r \to 0$. The density diverges.

Resolved-galaxy inferences are dataset- and method-dependent: some analyses
favor cored inner profiles, while others retain cusp-compatible slopes. The
SPARC comparison in §7.2 reports intermediate, nondiscriminating slopes, so
the cusp–core question remains open for this mapping.

### 3.2 The geometric proxy profile

Near a bubble center, the condensation field is quadratic:

$$C(x,y) \approx 1 - \frac{1}{2}(\alpha^2 x^2 + \beta^2 y^2)$$

for small displacements. The geometric condensation coordinate is therefore:

$$q_{\rm proxy}(x,y) \approx 2 - (\alpha^2 x^2 + \beta^2 y^2)$$

The effective gravitational mass density inferred from a rotation curve or
lensing measurement depends on both the coupling and the matter density:
$G_{\text{eff}}\rho_{\rm matter}$. The working **Hypothesized** matter-density
ansatz sets the analysis central value
$\rho_{\rm matter,0}=\rho_{\rm geom,0}=\rho_0$; physical matter content still
requires a separately supplied and measured matter map. At the center, the
bounded proxy map contributes
$q_{\rm center}=\mathcal{M}_{\rm proxy\to q}(2)\in[0,1]$, giving

$$G_{\text{eff}}\rho_{\rm matter}\approx
\pi\left[1+(\varphi^6-1)\mathcal{M}_{\rm proxy\to q}(2)\right]
\rho_{\rm matter,0}.$$

This finite central expression is conditional on the stated matter-density
normalization and constitutive map. The geometric proxy alone supplies a
coordinate; it does not determine a finite or cored effective halo profile.
The endpoint assignment $\mathcal{M}_{\rm proxy\to q}(2)=1$ gives the recorded
working value $\pi\varphi^6\rho_{\rm matter,0}$ only in the fixed-composition
high-density limit. A cored profile and the radius estimate below require a
smooth matter map in addition to the smooth $C(x,y)$ and proxy-to-canonical-$q$
map. The core scale is set by the bubble wavelength at galactic scale:

$$r_{\text{core}}\sim\frac{1}{\alpha}
=\frac{\Lambda_Y}{2\pi}\sim\frac{\ell_{267}}{2\pi}.$$

For the Milky Way, $\ell_{267}\approx9.3\times10^{20}$ m $\approx30$ kpc,
giving the selected geometric scale $r_{\text{core}}\sim5$ kpc. This is a
comparison with observed core radii of large spiral galaxies (~2–10 kpc), not
a profile prediction before the matter map and its calibration are supplied.

- Is **cored** at small $r$ only when the proxy-to-$q$ map and

## 4. Resolving the Small-Scale Crises

### 4.1 The cusp-core problem

The cored profile described in §3.2 is a consequence of the smooth geometric
field together with the Hypothesized proxy-to-canonical-$q$ constitutive ansatz.
Its observational status is tested through the model comparisons reported in
§7.

### 4.2 The missing satellites problem

ΛCDM predicts hundreds of dark matter subhalos around a Milky-Way-sized galaxy.
We observe only ~50 satellite galaxies. The discrepancy is a factor of ~5–10.

In the Qi picture, subhalos are modeled as small geometric bubble
condensates at lower $n$ (dwarf galaxy scale). Under the working
matter-condensation ansatz, a subhalo forms visible stars when its geometric
center reaches the condensation threshold, $C_{\text{max}} >
\theta_{\text{cond}}$; small bubbles have shorter wavelengths
($\Lambda_Y \propto \ell_n \propto \varphi^n$) and correspondingly smaller
$q_{\rm proxy}$ spans. A subhalo with peak
$C_{\text{max}} < \theta_{\text{cond}}$ remains a geometric dark Qi halo: its
gravitational signal is supplied by the Hypothesized
$q_{\rm proxy}\to q\to G_{\text{eff}}$ chain, while its visible star
formation assignment requires the separately measured matter map.

The visible-satellite count therefore tracks the number of subhalos with
$C_{\text{max}} > \theta_{\text{cond}}$, conditioned on both constitutive maps
and the star-formation model.

### 4.3 The too-big-to-fail problem

Some ΛCDM subhalos are massive enough that they should have formed stars—their
gravitational potential wells are deep enough to retain gas against supernova
feedback—yet they are dark. In the Qi picture, a subhalo's inferred mass from
$G_{\text{eff}}$ is separately controlled by the canonical $q$ supplied by the
proxy map, while its geometric condensation coordinate $C$ feeds the
matter-condensation map. A massive dark halo can therefore have deep
$G_{\text{eff}}$ amplification while $C_{\text{max}} < \theta_{\text{cond}}$.
The gravitational-depth and matter-condensation thresholds are independent
model inputs.

### 4.4 The Bullet Cluster

The Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-56) is often cited as direct evidence for particle
dark matter. Two galaxy clusters collided. The hot gas (visible in X-rays)
collided and slowed. The gravitational lensing signal (tracing "dark matter")
passed through unimpeded. The offset between gas and lensing mass is taken as
evidence for a collisionless dark component.

In the Qi picture, the X-ray gas is visible matter assigned to high-$q_{\rm
proxy}$ bubble centers by the matter-condensation map. When the clusters
collide, the gas interacts (shocks, ram pressure) and slows. The Qi field is a
field-mediated component whose canonical gravitational coupling is computed
after the proxy map supplies $q$. A Bullet Cluster comparison requires a
modified Poisson and lensing forward model that maps this coupling and the
matter-density field to convergence and shear; the product
$G_{\text{eff}}\rho$ alone is not a lensing prediction. No such forward model
is supplied here, so the observed offset is a target constraint on the
extension rather than a passed collision test.

The Bullet Cluster measurement supplies an offset between collisional gas and
collisionless gravitational mass. The Qi-field interpretation can be tested
against that offset once the $q_{\rm proxy}\to q\to G_{\text{eff}}$ chain and
the lensing forward model are specified.

## 5. The Tuning Hypothesis

The geometric condensate fraction can be summarized by a dimensionless
visible-structure index once a normalized matter weight is supplied. Let
$w_{\rm matter}(C)\ge0$ be a dimensionless weight derived from the measured
matter-density map. Define

$$\eta_{\text{visible}}^{\rm idx}\equiv
\frac{\int_{C>\theta_{\text{cond}}}w_{\rm matter}(C)\,dV}
{\int_{\rm all}w_{\rm matter}(C)\,dV}.$$

If coupling weighting is part of the model, use
$w_G(C)=[G_{\text{eff}}(C)/G]w_{\rm matter}(C)$ and report the analogous
$\eta_{\text{visible}}^{(G)}$ as a dimensionless model index. These indices
are organizational summaries; they are not visible-plus-dark mass fractions
until a complete matter and lensing forward model defines those masses.

A galaxy with a natural gate chain has a baseline index set by the
conversion-diffusion balance at the galactic rung. An **active gate network**—
stellar-scale analogues of the pyramids and ocean bases from
`speculations/cascade-infrastructure.md`—would have a higher index under that
matter map if the model's conversion closure supplies that effect. The gate
interpretation remains a Hypothesized extension.

A Creative/Hypothesized candidate correlation, pending operational definitions
and data, is to search for galaxies with an anomalously high visible-structure
index compared with galaxies of similar mass and morphology. A galaxy that is
"too bright for its rotation curve" is a candidate for a tuned galactic gate
network, not an established technosignature.

### 5.2 Morphology as coherence

Spiral galaxies may be more organized than irregulars in the optional geometric
reading. In that reading, spiral arms trace connectable diagonal channels of
the galactic-scale bubble lattice
(`foundations/bubble-edge-geometry.md` §4.1). A grand-design spiral may have a

An unregistered Creative/Hypothesized candidate correlation is that

of the same baryonic mass. Testing it requires an operational morphology

The small-$r$ dark matter velocity slope is intermediate between cusp ($p = 0.5$) and core ($p = 1.0$): median $p = 0.65$ from 19 galaxies with sufficient inner resolution. The SPARC data do not clearly discriminate cusps from cores.

### 7.3 Interpretation

The analysis keeps candidate checks P1 and P2 (both unregistered) at the
Hypothesized, model-dependent tier. The
specific radial profile tested in §7.1 uses the recorded 1D/2D
condensation-field analysis variable $a_{\rm rec}$ with fixed
$\xi = \varphi^6$; that run provenance is retained without identifying
$a_{\rm rec}$ with the squared geometric $q_{\rm proxy}$ or canonical $q$.
The oscillatory profile is ruled out as a universal galaxy-halo profile by the
reported SPARC comparison.

The existing Cassi result—that $\xi = \varphi^6$ matches the Milky Way
rotation curve—stands at its reported calibration tier. The SPARC result
isolates limitations in applying a fixed proxy geometry and constitutive map as
a universal radial profile. Possible explanations for the failure:

1. **Geometry**: A full 3D bubble-lattice solution with checkerboard
   connectivity may produce a radial profile different from the 2D angular
   average
2. **Coupling saturation**: $\xi = \varphi^6$ is the vacuum coupling; the
   effective $\xi$ in partially organized galaxy halos may be lower
3. **Scale dependence**: $\theta_{\text{cond}}$ and $n_{\text{cond}}$ may vary
   with cascade rung, rather than remaining fixed at the cosmological values
4. **The right test**: The Qi mechanism may require full two-fluid PDE
   simulations at galactic scale alongside static radial fits to rotation
   curves

- The constitutive map $q=\mathcal{M}_{\rm proxy\to q}(q_{\rm proxy})$, with the recorded fit assignment retained as run provenance rather than a canonical-$q$ measurement
- The full $q_{\rm proxy}\to q\to G_{\text{eff}}$ halo coupling and the resulting radial profile
- That $\theta_{\text{cond}}$ at galactic scale equals the cosmological value ($\approx 0.45$)—the conversion-diffusion balance is scale-invariant, but this has not been verified by multi-scale PDE simulation

  field may operate at galactic scale ($n \approx 267$) as at cosmological
  scale ($n \approx 285$); this remains Hypothesized, not Derived
- The condensation exponent $n_{\text{cond}}$ at galactic scale

  transport law linking local canonical-$q$ diagnostics across cascade scales,
