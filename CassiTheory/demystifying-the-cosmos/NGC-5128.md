## Status: Hypothesized—August 2026

## Abstract

Webb's fourth-anniversary images of Centaurus A resolve the galaxy star by star through its dust, revealing a warped, parallelogram-like dust band cutting across the center, an S-shaped ribbon structure, and the scars of a major merger two billion years ago. The band's shape is strikingly similar to the cascade r-field of the string-bubble-cascade PDE figure (`visual-explainers/string_bubble_cascade.py`, Panel E)—the ratio field $r = E_Y/E_I$ that a coherent bubble leaves behind after the cascade release. Cassi reads the morphology as the field's geometry showing through: the parallelogram is the projected trace of the condensation-field checkerboard, the S-shape is the wake wrap around the jet string, the merger is an anti-phase meeting that released a wake cascade, and the active nucleus is a coherence sink whose jet is a coherent channel. The resemblance is geometric, not a derivation; the testable content is quantitative—φ-spaced radial wake rings (the wake-envelope prediction, prediction 44) and the 1.70× edge anisotropy of the dust band (prediction 38), both zero-parameter and measurable in the MIRI image.

## 1. The observation

Centaurus A (NGC 5128), 11 million light-years away, is the nearest active galaxy with a feeding supermassive black hole and the nearest major merger remnant. Webb's MIRI and NIRCam views (July 2026, fourth science anniversary) cut through the dust that hides the center in visible light. The article's salient features:

1. **A warped, parallelogram-like dust band** cuts across the galaxy's center—gray/white in MIRI, mottled with patches and bright knots, "surprising and even perplexing" astronomers.
2. **An S-shaped feature** of loops and ribbons arcs above and below the center, flagged as unusual: "What created this shape? How does the black hole influence it?"
3. **A merger ~2 billion years ago**: a major collision whose aftermath is still visible in the galaxy's unusual structure and its ongoing star formation.
4. **An active nucleus**: the central supermassive black hole launches powerful jets and drives fast outward-flowing ionized gas; warm molecular hydrogen traces a **warped rotating disk** near the center (early spectroscopy, [A&A 2025, aa54823-25](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2025/07/aa54823-25/aa54823-25.html)).
5. **Star-by-star resolution**: the "grainy" texture is millions of individually resolved stars—galactic archaeology, a timeline of formation, collision, and aftermath.

A band of material that settles along a lattice plane, seen with the lattice tilted against the line of sight, projects as a **parallelogram**: the two lattice directions shear into the band's slanted sides, and the band's width is the lattice period along the third axis. The warp the article emphasizes is exactly what a tilted lattice trace does when the structure is not coplanar: the parallelogram's sides stay lattice-parallel while the band bends across them.

## 8. Falsifiable tests

**T1—φ-spaced wake rings (prediction 44, applied).** The merger's wake cascade should leave radial surface-brightness peaks in the MIRI/NIRCam image whose successive radii satisfy

$$r_{k+1}/r_k = \varphi, \qquad \Delta(\ln r) = \ln\varphi \approx 0.4812.$$

The test: azimuthally averaged radial profiles around the nucleus (and around the merger's secondary center, if separable), peaks found, consecutive ratios compared to $\varphi$ with the same periodicity machinery as the cosmological $P(k)$ search (prediction 5). The period is fixed by the stated $\varphi$ mapping; no independent scale parameter is fitted. Note the extrapolation: wake geometry is documented at cosmological rungs and verified at rung 285; applying it at galaxy scales ($n \approx 262$–277) assumes the envelope mechanism is rung-universal, which the framework claims (prediction 44 is scale-free) but has not tested at this rung.

**T2—Conditional directional edge proxy (prediction 38).** If an independently identified dust-band boundary is aligned with the proposed lattice directions, compare its axial and diagonal surface-brightness gradients using $R(\theta)=\frac{\sqrt{1+\varphi^2}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{1+\theta}{\theta}}$. The ratio is $1.7072\times$ only for the selected $\theta_{\mathrm{cond}}=0.45$ and varies with $\theta$; it is not universal, zero-parameter, canonical, or a PDE output. No $C=0.45$ edge survives the fixed-step PDE endpoint, and the cosmological boundary receipt is null, so this test supplies no observational support unless the boundary and direction are independently identified.

**What would falsify the mapping:** radial peaks with ratios inconsistent with $\varphi$ (T1 null at the catalog's significance machinery), or an independently identified edge comparison inconsistent with the stated $R(\theta)$ at the preregistered $\theta_{\mathrm{cond}}$ (T2 null). Neither existing morphology nor the cosmological boundary receipt supports the mapping.

## 9. What the framework cannot say (yet)

- The parallelogram shape itself is not derived—the resemblance to the r-field is geometric and suggestive; the testable claims are T1 and T2, not the shape.
- The warp's amplitude, the S-shape's exact formation, and the black hole's trigger/limit balance for this galaxy are not computed; each needs a galaxy-scale model that does not exist.
- The star-by-star archaeology is read as the condensate's micro-structure, but no prediction follows from it yet.
