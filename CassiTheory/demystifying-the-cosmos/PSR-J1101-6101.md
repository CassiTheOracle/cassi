# PSR J1101−6101: The Lighthouse Pulsar

## Status: Hypothesized—August 2026

## Abstract

The Lighthouse Nebula is the first pulsar wind nebula whose magnetic field has been measured directly by X-ray polarimetry. NASA's IXPE spent 18 days on PSR J1101−6101 and returned three results: the filament's X-ray polarization aligns with the particle flow at >99% confidence; the polarization degree is high enough to exclude the turbulent filament models; and the radio field points nearly perpendicular to the trail while the X-ray field points parallel to it. Cassi reads all three as the signature of a coherent condensate: the pulsar is a spinning soliton, the bow shock and its trail are the low-coherence wake of a moving coherent source, the filament is the coherent escape channel along the organized field, and the radio–X-ray divergence is energy stratification across cascade rungs, where the field orientation winds one full turn per rung. The analysis closes with a zero-parameter falsifiable test (prediction 48): the polarization angle of synchrotron emission should be log-periodic in photon energy, $\text{PA}(\nu\varphi^k) = \text{PA}(\nu)$ (mod $\pi$).

The mappings in this document are Hypothesized applications of Derived machinery; the rung placements are observations, not predictions (see §6). The observation itself: Dinsmore et al. 2026, ApJ, [10.3847/1538-4357/ae64f3](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ae64f3).

## 1. The observation

PSR J1101−6101 is a pulsar in the Lighthouse Nebula, spinning 16 times per second. Like all pulsars it is a neutron star—a stellar core with more mass than the Sun compressed to city size—moving supersonically through the interstellar medium. Its wind of near-light-speed particles piles up against the ambient gas in a bow shock, most particles trapping into a turbulent trail behind it while the highest-energy fraction escapes through the shock into interstellar space.

In June 2025 IXPE mapped the X-ray polarization of three features: the long filament, the short trail, and the pulsar's own emission. Three results carry the paper:

1. **Field ∥ flow (>99% CL).** The filament's polarization angle confirms the magnetic field points along the particle flow, settling a 2008 conjecture that the highest-energy particles escape along the galaxy's field lines.
2. **High polarization degree.** The field is more ordered than the turbulent filament models assume—"lower turbulence than such models require."
3. **Radio ⊥ vs X-ray ∥.** The X-ray-emitting field is parallel to the trail, but radio polarimetry sees the field almost exactly perpendicular—the first clear indication that different particle energies occupy distinct regions, with multiple acceleration mechanisms at work.

## 4. High polarization degree as high coherence $q$

The filament models' failure is instructive: they assume the field is turbulent and are surprised by order. Cassi's default is the opposite. The condensate's attractor is the $\varphi$-equilibrium configuration; random perturbation is what the coherence budget protects against (`foundations/proton-coherence-budget.md`, `foundations/quantum-measurement-derivation.md`). A high-$q$ region resists randomization and re-organizes toward the attractor, so a high polarization degree is what a high-coherence region looks like in synchrotron light.

The framework also carries a quantitative expectation in the other direction: the measured $(1-q) \approx 0.23$ floor is an irreducible disorganized fraction, so even a perfectly ordered field should not polarize to 100%—the polarization degree should saturate below unity at a floor set by $(1-q)$. The exact mapping from the PDE floor to synchrotron polarization degree requires a radiative-transfer model that does not exist yet; the sharpening is open. **Tier:** Hypothesized.

## 5. Radio ⊥ vs X-ray ∥: rung stratification

The striking divergence in field orientation between energy bands is the framework's most distinctive prediction showing up in data. The spiral geometry of §2 rotates the field orientation with scale: one full turn per cascade rung. Synchrotron emission at energy $E$ is produced by particles at a corresponding cascade depth, so the field orientation seen at photon energy $\nu$ winds with $\ln\nu$:

$$\boxed{\Theta(\nu) = \Theta_0 + \frac{2\pi}{\ln\varphi}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_0}\right) \quad (\text{mod }\pi)}$$

Radio and X-ray photons span roughly $10^9$ in frequency—$\Delta n = \log_\varphi(10^9) \approx 43$ rungs of frequency, about 21.5 rungs of emitting-particle Lorentz factor at fixed field (the true separation is uncertain by the field ratio, since $B$ itself differs between the regions). The framework says the two populations should sit at different spiral phases and see different field orientations—"multiple, potentially very different, acceleration mechanisms" is one mechanism acting at different rung depths. The 90° divergence between radio and X-ray is a real multi-band measurement; the P48 test (2026-08-06, `experiments/demystifying_cosmos/pa_logperiodic_test.py`) does not reproduce the spiral rate for it: over the full radio→X-ray span (~37 rungs) the formula demands ~67° of rotation and the data show 5–17° (cross-region pairs carry the B-ratio caveat—$\nu \propto \gamma^2 B$ differs between emission regions), and the Crab's intra-mm band (2.6 rungs, no B-ratio ambiguity) is constant to <1° where the formula demands ~40°. **Tier:** Hypothesized—tested 2026-08-06, null at face value (descends from the Hypothesized form-factor log-periodicity of `foundations/spin-fibonacci-spiral.md` §5).

## 7. Falsifiable tests

**Prediction 48 (log-periodic polarization orientation).** In pulsar wind nebulae and other synchrotron sources, the polarization position angle should be log-periodic in photon energy:

$$\text{PA}(\nu\,\varphi^k) = \text{PA}(\nu) \quad (\text{mod }\pi), \qquad \Delta(\ln\nu) = \ln\varphi \approx 0.4812$$

The test: measure PA in at least three bands spanning $\Delta(\ln\nu) \geq \ln\varphi$—radio (ATCA), X-ray (IXPE), and, once available, hard-X/γ-ray polarimetry (XL-Calibur, LEAP-class)—and check the angle returns to itself under the $\varphi$ period. The period is zero-parameter, the same $\ln\varphi$ as the cosmological $P(k)$ and form-factor predictions. A band pair at quarter-rung separation ($\Delta n = \tfrac14$, i.e. $\nu_2/\nu_1 = \varphi^{1/4}$) should show PA rotated by 90°; a half-rung pair ($\nu_2/\nu_1 = \sqrt\varphi$, $\Delta n = \tfrac12$) returns PA unchanged (mod $\pi$)—the winding gives $\Delta\Theta = \pi$ at half-rung, i.e. parallel. **Status:** tested 2026-08-06 on the Crab Nebula (`experiments/demystifying_cosmos/pa_logperiodic_test.py`, commit 5e5310f)—NULL at face value: PA is constant (~138–142°) across Planck 100/143/217/353 GHz + NIKA 150 GHz (Δ ln ν = 1.26 = 2.6 rungs ≥ ln φ), the constant-PA fit beats the log-periodic spiral fit by Δχ² = −110,943 (4 dof), 0/10 band pairs lie within 3σ (100→353 GHz predicts +43.6°, observed +0.8° ± 0.5°, z = 79; 100→143 GHz predicts +87.6°, observed +0.9° ± 0.3°, z = 328), and a search-corrected uniform-angle null confirms no spiral signal (p = 0.77). The Lighthouse radio–X-ray pair is a 2-band check, not a ≥3-band test.

**Polarization floor from $(1-q)$.** The irreducible disorganized fraction $\approx 0.23$ implies synchrotron polarization degree saturates below 100% even in the most ordered regions; the exact cap needs the radiative-transfer mapping, which is open. A measurement of PD consistent with 100% in any region would contradict the floor.

**What would falsify the mapping:** PA that varies aperiodically in $\ln\nu$, or a band pair at $\Delta n = \tfrac14$ showing parallel (not perpendicular) orientation. The Crab mm-band measurement (2026-08-06) shows the constant-PA pattern—the mapping's falsifier, observed.
