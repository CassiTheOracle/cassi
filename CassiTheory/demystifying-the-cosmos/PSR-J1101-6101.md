# PSR J1101−6101: The Lighthouse Pulsar

## Status: Hypothesized—August 2026

## Abstract

The Lighthouse Nebula is the first pulsar wind nebula whose magnetic field has been measured directly by X-ray polarimetry. NASA's IXPE spent 18 days on PSR J1101−6101 and found that the filament's X-ray polarization aligns with the particle flow at >99% confidence, that the polarization degree supports lower turbulence than some filament models assume, and that the radio field is nearly perpendicular to the trail while the X-ray field is parallel. These observations support or are consistent with conditional Cassi mappings of a spinning soliton, a low-coherence wake, and a coherent escape channel, but the physical mechanism remains open.

The mappings in this document are Hypothesized applications of Derived machinery; the added $\chi$ coordinate, selected one-turn-per-rung coordinate pitch, proposed half-angle spin label $s=\Delta n/2$, imposed rotation, and pulsar correspondence are optional Hypothesized coordinate or phenomenological constructions beyond canonical real-density conversion. The rung placements are observations, not predictions (see §6). The observation itself: Dinsmore et al. 2026, ApJ, [10.3847/1538-4357/ae64f3](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/ae64f3).

## 1. The observation

PSR J1101−6101 is a pulsar in the Lighthouse Nebula, observed spinning at 16 times per second. Like all pulsars it is a neutron star—a stellar core with more mass than the Sun compressed to city size—moving supersonically through the interstellar medium. Its wind of near-light-speed particles piles up against the ambient gas in a bow shock, most particles trapping into a turbulent trail behind it while the highest-energy fraction escapes through the shock into interstellar space.

In June 2025 IXPE mapped the X-ray polarization of three features: the long filament, the short trail, and the pulsar's own emission. Three results carry the paper:

1. **Field ∥ flow (>99% CL).** The filament's polarization angle confirms the magnetic field points along the particle flow, settling a 2008 conjecture that the highest-energy particles escape along the galaxy's field lines.
2. **High polarization degree.** The field supports lower turbulence than some turbulent filament models assume; it does not exclude all turbulence models.
3. **Radio ⊥ vs X-ray ∥.** The X-ray-emitting field is parallel to the trail, but radio polarimetry sees the field almost exactly perpendicular—the first clear indication that different particle energies occupy distinct regions, with multiple acceleration mechanisms at work.

## 5. Radio ⊥ vs X-ray ∥: rung stratification

The striking divergence in field orientation between energy bands motivates a conditional phenomenological test. If the optional spiral coordinate of §2 is adopted, its selected $\varphi$-scaled pitch assigns one full turn per cascade rung. This coordinate assignment is Hypothesized beyond canonical real-density conversion. Under that ansatz, synchrotron emission at energy $E$ is produced by particles at a corresponding cascade depth, so the field orientation seen at photon energy $\nu$ winds with $\ln\nu$:

$$\boxed{\Theta(\nu) = \Theta_0 + \frac{2\pi}{\ln\varphi}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_0}\right) \quad (\text{mod }\pi)}$$

The script/source pair uses $\nu_{\mathrm{radio}}=7.25\times10^9$ Hz and $\nu_{\mathrm{X}}=4.83\times10^{17}$ Hz, giving $\Delta n=\log_\varphi(\nu_{\mathrm{X}}/\nu_{\mathrm{radio}})=37.4358$ rungs. Under the optional ansatz, the formula therefore gives $\Delta\Theta=360^\circ\Delta n=13476.87^\circ$, which is $156.87^\circ$ modulo $180^\circ$ (acute separation $23.13^\circ$). The radio EVPA is inferred rather than tabulated, and the two-band check cannot test periodicity; its roughly $90^\circ$ observed separation is in tension with this optional ansatz but does not identify a mechanism or exclude other models. The cross-region pair also carries the caveat that $\nu\propto\gamma^2B$ and the magnetic field can differ between emitting regions. The unsupported earlier 67° and 43.6° expectations are not retained.

## 7. Falsifiable tests

**Prediction 48 (conditional log-periodic polarization orientation).** Under the optional Hypothesized $\chi$-coordinate and selected one-turn-per-rung coordinate ansatz, the polarization position angle in pulsar wind nebulae and other synchrotron sources is tested for log-periodicity in photon energy:

$$\text{PA}(\nu\,\varphi^k) = \text{PA}(\nu) \quad (\text{mod }\pi), \qquad \Delta(\ln\nu) = \ln\varphi \approx 0.4812$$

The test of this optional mapping: measure PA in at least three bands spanning $\Delta(\ln\nu) \geq \ln\varphi$—radio (ATCA), X-ray (IXPE), and, once available, hard-X/γ-ray polarimetry (XL-Calibur, LEAP-class)—and check the angle returns to itself under the $\varphi$ period. Within the ansatz, the period has no additional fit parameter, using the same $\ln\varphi$ as the cosmological $P(k)$ and form-factor predictions. Its quarter-rung implication is a 90° PA rotation at $\Delta n = \tfrac14$ ($\nu_2/\nu_1 = \varphi^{1/4}$); its half-rung implication is unchanged PA (mod $\pi$) at $\nu_2/\nu_1 = \sqrt\varphi$, $\Delta n = \tfrac12$, because the coordinate winding gives $\Delta\Theta = \pi$. **Status:** tested 2026-08-06 on the Crab Nebula (`experiments/demystifying_cosmos/pa_logperiodic_test.py`, commit 5e5310f)—NULL at face value: PA is constant (~138–142°) across Planck 100/143/217/353 GHz + NIKA 150 GHz (Δ ln ν = 1.26 = 2.6 rungs ≥ ln φ), the constant-PA fit beats the log-periodic spiral fit by Δχ² = −110,943 (4 dof), 0/10 band pairs lie within 3σ (100→353 GHz predicts +43.6°, observed +0.8° ± 0.5°, z = 79; 100→143 GHz predicts +87.6°, observed +0.9° ± 0.3°, z = 328), and a search-corrected uniform-angle null confirms no spiral signal (p = 0.77). The Lighthouse radio–X-ray pair is a 2-band check, not a ≥3-band test. X-ray/radio band pairs and hard-X/γ-ray polarimetry (XL-Calibur, LEAP-class) remain the open tests.

**Polarization floor from $(1-q)$.** The irreducible disorganized fraction $\approx 0.23$ implies synchrotron polarization degree saturates below 100% even in the most ordered regions; the exact cap needs the radiative-transfer mapping, which is open. A measurement of PD consistent with 100% in any region would contradict the floor.

**Falsification criteria:** PA that varies aperiodically in $\ln\nu$, a band
pair at $\Delta n=\tfrac14$ showing parallel orientation, or a band pair at
$\Delta n=\tfrac12$ showing perpendicular orientation would falsify the
optional mapping. The Crab mm-band measurement (2026-08-06) is a null result
