# The Clock: A Field Watch Reading the Local Tempo

**Question under design:** how the field is read when *what* is not the question —
when the player already knows the Weatherglass's `q` and `ε²`, and wants instead
**how fast the field is running where they stand**. The instruments read `q`
(the Weatherglass's lume), `ε²` (its climbing glow), the gradient (its lean) —
nothing reads the **tempo**. [`patient-field.md`](./patient-field.md) designed
local time as a real quantity — the mixing clock `T = 2π/[λ(1−q)]` (**theory**,
cited with its flag) and the regional `(1−q)` variation as the proposed reading —
but **no instrument renders it**. This document is that missing instrument.

> **The Clock is the second-order instrument for patient-time: a field watch
> that reads the local `(1−q)` tempo the way the Weatherglass reads `q`.** It
> shows not *what* the field is but *how fast it is running* where you stand.
> A heavy-coherence core reads slow/patient — time is kind there, and the watch
> turns calmly; a thin or de-cohering region reads rushed — the watch races,
> warning you that the vent and the Coda's accumulation run faster here. **The
> pocket-watch of a window**: the instrument a player wears to know *when they
> have* — the tempo in which their next deep op will fit — composing with the
> tide's big clock (the watch is local, the tide regional).

This is a **lens-on-a-lens**: the Weatherglass reads the field's *state*
(`q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)`); the Clock reads the field's *rate* — the `(1−q)` the
Weatherglass already presents as a waste tint, re-read as a **tempo**. It is a
pure consumer of the published channels with a presentation idiom, obeying the
instrument family rule ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1)
exactly: **never a new channel, never new physics.** The honest boundary — the
mixing clock is theory, the tempo reading is [design], the engine never "slows
time" — is drawn in §5 and never blurred.

## 1. The Clock, stated once

The instrument family reads the field's *state*: the reader (`coherence-magic.md`
§2) aims; the Weatherglass (`field-instruments.md` §1.2) glances; the sky
(`atmosphere-orbits-auroras.md`) reads at scale; the Life-Signal
(`life-signal.md`) classifies *what is breathing*. **None of them reads the
field's *rate*** — the tempo in which its coherence economics actually run at a
place.

`patient-field.md` named that tempo and never instrumented it. It designed local
time as a real quantity: the **mixing clock** `T = 2π/[λ(1−q)]` (the attractor's
relaxation cadence, `qi-as-time-clock.md`) and the openness gate
`λ_mix = λ(1−q)`. The key structural fact is that the clock is **local**: it
depends on the local `q` at each place — high where the field is organized
(the clock runs slow, the field is patient), suppressed toward fast where the
field is decoherent (`(1−q)` large, the clock races). Every presentation in
`patient-field.md` §4 is a *state* read wearing the tempo as a *meaning*. The
Clock is the instrument that makes the tempo itself the read.

> **The Clock, stated once.** It is the second-order instrument for
> patient-time: a field watch that reads the local `(1−q)` tempo the way the
> Weatherglass reads `q`. The Weatherglass answers *what the field is*; the
> Clock answers *how fast it is running where you stand*. It is the 
> pocket-watch of a window — the instrument a player wears to know *when they
> have*: the tempo in which their next deep op will fit.

The one-line distinction from every sibling read:

| Instrument | It asks | It reads |
|---|---|---|
| The reader (`coherence-magic.md` §2) | *what is that?* | the published `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)` aimed at a target |
| The Weatherglass (`field-instruments.md` §1.2) | *what is this, now, constantly?* | the same four channels at the player's position, glanceable |

| The tide (`tide-of-the-attractor.md`) | *when in the season am I?* | the regional-`q` trend over a long cycle |
| **The Clock (this doc)** | ***how fast is the field running here?*** | **the local `(1−q)` tempo** — the rate itself, at the player's position |

The Clock is the one instrument the corpus named (`patient-field.md` §4.1 calls
the Weatherglass "a wristwatch on the field's pulse") but never actually built as

## 3. What the watch shows

### 3.1 The tempo read — calm at a patient core, racing where it is thin

The Clock's face is a single read, derived from the published `(1−q)` at the
player's position and mapped (through the theory-cited `T = 2π/[λ(1−q)]`) to an
idiomatic hand. The mapping is faithful: it is the *same* `(1−q)` the Weatherglass
already renders as its waste tint (`field-instruments.md` §1.2), re-read as a rate.

| The field where you stand | `(1−q)` (the published waste fraction) | The Clock's hand |
|---|---|---|
| **A heavy-coherence core** — a Qi-bath's core, a coherent ridge, a Still Room's interior | small (`q` high, near the `q ≈ 0.947` attractor) | **turns calmly, slowly** — the watch is *patient*, time is kind there |
| **A living plain / an in-tune locality** | at the working floor (`energy-harnessing.md` §2's near-lossless region) | a moderate, steady pace — the everyday tempo of a healthy field |
| **A thin or de-cohering region** — a scar, a storm's leading edge, a thin-tide margin, a desert's approach | large (`(1−q)` toward `q ~ 1e-3…1e-1` regimes) | **the watch races** — the field is running fast, and it is rushing *everything that happens here* |

The **[design]** is the hand's mapping — which `(1−q)` swing reads as "clearly
slower vs. clearly faster," what a *felt* tempo swing is (the `patient-field.md`
open-Q "what feels like a different tempo in units"), and the hand's speed curve
over the `(1−q)` range. The **channel** it reads (`(1−q)`) is real. The **direction**
is the theory's own: `λ_mix = λ(1−q)` is high (fast) where the field is far from
φ-equilibrium and suppressed (slow) where it is coherent (`qi-as-time-clock.md`
§2.2). The Clock never invents the *direction*; it only gives it a face.

### 3.2 What the rush warns — the three things that run faster here

The watch racing is not a mood; it is a **warning about what accelerates where
the field is thin**, each grounded in a source doc:

| The warning | What runs faster | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|
| **The vent backs up.** | A fast tempo is a thin-region tempo: the field heals slowly (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2, magic row — "the vent backs up, resonance is hard to match, overdraw risk rises"). A channeler who does a deep op where the watch races pays closer to the overdraw line (`coherence-magic.md` §4.3) than the same op where it turns calmly. This is `the-burden.md` §2a's **interest**: the debt compounds the vent back-up in thin regions, and a fast-tempo locality is where that interest comes due. | `the-burden.md` §2a; `patient-field.md` §3.3 |
| **The Coda's accumulation runs quicker.** | The Coda's growth `R = ρ_signature·τ·M_stability` (`signature-predator.md` §2.2) counts **duration** `τ` against the field's relaxation. Where the watch races, the accumulated resonance of a trail does not fade into a patient heal the way it does in a calm core — the `τ` sits on the player through the fast tempo, and the Coda's approach (already `signature-predator.md`'s thin-tide season) runs quicker here. | `signature-predator.md` §2; `tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2 |
| **A fast-tempo bed is a dangerous bed.** | `sleep.md` §3 names the margin bed: a thin-tide, high-`(1−q)` place is a **sitting signature** through the night — the overnight `τ` keeps counting against a resting, un-moved-away body. The Clock at the bedside tells the player the bed's *tempo before they lie down in it*: a racing watch over the pillow is the sleep doc's danger frame made glanceable. | `sleep.md` §3/§2.2 |

The through-line is the corpus's own economics run faster where the field is thin:
**the same act that is cheap at a patient core — a deep op, a clean trail, a rest —
is dear where the watch races.** The Clock makes that *dearness* visible as a
rate, so the player reads *when they have* — when their next deep op will fit
inside the local tempo.

### 3.3 The composition with the tide — the pocket-watch of a window

The tide (`tide-of-the-attractor.md`) is the mixing clock given a face at the
**window/region** scale: the regional `q` breathes between harvest and thin over a
long season. The Clock is the **same clock at the player's position**: your
`(1−q)` right here. The two compose as the *local* hand inside the *regional*
season:

> **The watch is local; the tide is regional.** The tide tells you *when in the
> season you are* — a shared tempo the whole window breathes (`tide-of-the-attractor.md`
> §2). The Clock tells you *how fast the field is running right where you stand* —
> the pocket-watch you wear, not the tower-clock you look up at. Walking from a
> Still Room's patient interior (the watch nearly still) out to a desert margin
> (the watch racing) is the local `(1−q)` gradient made *felt as a rate*. **The
> pocket-watch of a window: worn to know *when you have* — when the local tempo
> will fit your next deep op, inside the season that frames it.**

The composition matters at the decision: a player at **harvest** reads a calm
watch at a coherent ridge — the deep op is cheap *here and now* (`tide-of-the-attractor.md`
§3.2). The same player at **thin** in a margin reads a racing watch — the op is
dear *here*, and the tide already made the season dear. The Clock turns
`patient-field.md` §3.3's "the same channeled act costs more patience at thin
tide" into a *positional* read: even within one season, *where* you do the act
changes its tempo-cost, and the watch is the instrument that shows the local part
of that cost.

**The accumulated twin (reverse pointer, two-way).** This §1/§3 speedometer has a
trip-meter: [`the-hourglass.md`](./the-hourglass.md) §1 is **the Clock's
accumulated twin** — it reads the same `(1−q)`-derived tempo this §1 maps, but
*integrated over a player-chosen interval* and presented as draining sand, so it
answers "how much time do I have" rather than "how fast is the field running
here" (`the-hourglass.md` §1 — "the Clock is the speedometer-at-a-glance; the
Hourglass is its trip-meter"). It reuses this Clock's tempo mapping whole and
inherits this doc's L1–L3 meaningful-hands probe; the two docs now cite each other
as read-and-answered (how-fast ↔ how-long).

## 4. The design surface — the family-rule forms

The Clock obeys the instrument family rule (`field-instruments.md` §2.1) exactly:
**every instrument is a consumer of the same publish with a presentation idiom,
never a new channel.** It adds no channel, no telemetry, no write — it reads the
published `q` (and its derived `(1−q)`), the *same* channel the Weatherglass's
waste tint already renders, and re-presents it as a *rate*. It is the Weatherglass's
idiom sibling: where the Weatherglass is the magnetometer-at-a-glance
(`field-instruments.md` §1), the Clock is the field's speedometer-at-a-glance.

| Form | Idiom | Channel it reads | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The watch face** | a worn pocket-watch / fob — the Weatherglass's sibling object, the hand turning at the tempo | the local `(1−q)` | **the tempo itself**: a calm, slow hand at a patient core; a racing hand where it is thin. The glanceable primary read. |
| **The lean** | the *same* gradient-lean as the Weatherglass's (the bauble's tilt toward `∇(g·Φ)`), kept because the tempo is only useful with a sense of direction | `∇(g·Φ)` (unchanged from the Weatherglass) | **where fast/slow runs** — the hand tells you the rate, the lean tells you which way the river (and its tempo) is going. Not a new form; the family's shared lean, inherited. |
| **The chime** | an *audible* form — field-music's accessibility rule held: sound is an enhancement, never the only channel (`field-music.md` §6). A slow patient tick at a calm core; a quickened, urgent tick where the watch races. | the same `(1−q)` | **the tempo heard** — the mute player is never diminished; the watch face is the glanceable guarantee, the chime the ambient sonification of the identical read. |

All three are consumers of the published `q`/`(1−q)` (plus the Weatherglass's
inherited `∇(g·Φ)` lean) — the ≈ 6 MiB publish unchanged
(`corpus-reconciliation.md`). **No new channel, no new physics.** The Clock is a
second-order instrument in exactly the corpus's sense: it does not read a new
quantity; it reads a *rate* out of a quantity the family already presents as a
state. Each later instrument in the family inherits the same freedom — a consumer
may re-present the published channels at a new *axis* (rate, position, scale) but
never at a new source.

**The field-music accessibility rule, held hard.** The chime is an enhancement,
never the only channel (`field-music.md` §6; `field-instruments.md` §1.3). The
tempo is always *also* readable, more laboriously, from the reader overlay and the
published `q`/`(1−q)` channels themselves (§5e). A player with sound off wears the
watch face and loses nothing; a player who never wears a watch can still read the
`(1−q)` readout and derive the tempo by hand. The Clock is a *layer* over the 
channels, structurally guaranteed by the shared-publish architecture — never a
hidden-only instrument.

## 5. Honest gates

### (a) The theory/[design]/engine-real line

Drawn in §2, restated once here: the mixing clock `T = 2π/[λ(1−q)]` and the
local-tempo *variation* are **theory** (`qi-as-time-clock.md` is a speculation;
the engine kernel's conversion term is q-free); the tick, the publish, and the
`q`/`(1−q)` channels are **engine-real** (canonical); the **Clock — the tempo
reading and its three forms — is [design]** over the published time-series. The
Clock never claims the engine "slows time" (§2).

### (b) PHASE-1-ABLE — a bounded read-only consumer

The Clock is **Phase-1-able** because it is the cleanest possible second-order
consumer: **a read of the published `q`/`(1−q)` time-series at the player's
locality, rendered as tempo.** It needs:

- **The published `q`** (and its derived `(1−q)`) at the player's position —
  already in the ≈ 6 MiB publish the sampler consumes (`corpus-reconciliation.md`);
  the Watch is one more trilinear sample at the player's position, inside the
  ≈ 1–6 ms/tick server sample budget, exactly as the Weatherglass is
  (`field-instruments.md` §5d). Nothing new to budget.
- **The patient-field q→time mapping** — the designed mapping from the `(1−q)`
  read to a *tempo* hand. This is the one prior it needs: `patient-field.md`
  §4.1 already specifies the four-form local-tempo reading, and §6b's probe sets
  whether the mapping is *felt* (meaningful hands) or *atmospheric* (presentation
  only). **Once that mapping is set — even as a pure presentation — the Clock is
  buildable.**

**The Phase-1 slice, stated:** **the watch's first face over the living-terrain
demo.** Wear the watch in the Phase-1 coupled box (`chunk-field-quantization.md`
§1.2), read the local `q`/`(1−q)` as the hand, and measure whether the watch's
tempo actually *moves* as the player walks ridge → scar → margin. This is a
bounded read-only consumer, shipable before the tide's season is measured and
before the Coda exists. It de-risks the whole time-bearing layer of the corpus the
way the Weatherglass de-risked the instrument family (`field-instruments.md` §6):
**ship a tempo read first, prove the tempo is felt or honest-atmosphere, then let
every later time-bearing system (the tide's season, the burden's interest, the
Coda's rush) read through the same idiom.**

### (c) Determinism — same locality, same field state → same tempo read

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `life-signal.md` §6d). Therefore:

> **Same locality, same field state → same tempo read.** The local `(1−q)` at a
> position — and its mapping to the watch's hand — is a **pure function of the
> published channels**. A player who stands in the same spot at the same field
> state twice gets the same tempo, every time.

This is a **hard gate**, inherited unchanged from the corpus's determinism
discipline (`patient-field.md` §5(c); `sleep.md` §6(c)): the Clock adds no
randomness. A player who internalizes "this ridge is patient, this scar is
racing" can rely on it, and can reproduce a locality and get the identical tempo
read. **[design]** The hand's speed curve over the `(1−q)` range is a designed
constant; the `(1−q)` it reads is deterministic.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — a watch informs, never mints

A watch reads a rate; it does not create one. `energy-harnessing.md` §6's
no-free-energy discipline holds:

1. **There is no "tempo harvest."** The Clock's reading is information and
   timing, never a source. Knowing that a ridge is patient does not convert the
   field's patience into coherence, ε², or power — `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`
   (`energy-harnessing.md` §6) bounds every conversion, and the Clock converts
   nothing. This is the same refusal that closes tide-farming
   (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §5d: "the tide's gift is information and timing,
   not energy") applied to the local tempo.
2. **The watch does not shift its own read.** A watched tempo is not a borrowed
   one: the Clock cannot be used to "slip" a fast locality into a slower one, any
   more than the Weatherglass can raise a local `q`. The `(1−q)` it reads is the
   field's; the watch only shows it. (The *Worn Field's* EY-shell, `worn-field.md`
   §2.1, is the *separate* designed coupling that changes the local `q` at the
   body — a [design] consumer+write over Q4, gated on regime constants landing.
   The Clock is not that; it is pure read.)
3. **The clock never accelerates the field to earn anything.** A player does not
   "spend" a fast tempo to gain energy; the rush is a *warning* (the vent backs

### (e) Accessibility — the tempo is never hidden-only

## Open questions

1. **The probe's verdict on the meaningful hand (§5b/§6).** If the L1–L3 probe
   (`patient-field.md` §6b) returns no meaningful `(1−q)` spread, the Clock
   collapses to presentation-only. Is that presentation *genuinely felt* (a watch
   whose hand moves a little, honestly, over the field's real `(1−q)` gradient) or
   does it ring hollow against a mechanically-flat time-series — the same
   presentation-vs-mechanic honesty question `patient-field.md` open-Q5 records?
   The doc commits to presentation-only being a legitimate finding; whether it
   *holds as a feel* is a Phase-1.5 design judgment.
2. **What "feels like a different tempo" is, in hand-units.** Even if `(1−q)`
   varies, what swing of `T = 2π/[λ(1−q)]` reads as a *different* watch-hand a
   player can distinguish from a flat read — the patient-field open-Q "units"
   question made a dial shape? Ties `tide-of-the-attractor.md` §5b's season-length
   dial and the tempo hand's speed curve. **[design]**
3. **The watch's tempo vs. the Worn Field's shell.** A player wearing an EY-shell
   (`worn-field.md` §2.1) raises local `q` *at the body* — which, through the
   tempo mapping, reads a calmer watch. Is the shell's steadied read a *designed,
   honest* coupling (the shell genuinely changes the tempo at the body) or a
   confusion the instrument must flag (the watch showing your own dress, not the
   field's)? The same honest-ambiguity discipline as `the-burden.md` §2c: the
   Clock must not misattribute the wearer's own coupling to the field's tempo.
   **[design]**
4. **The tempo's coupling to the mixel-scale q-gate.** Since the engine kernel is
   q-free (`qi-as-time-clock.md` §2.1), the Clock's tempo reading assumes the
   theory-solver's `(1−q)` gate governs the living-terrain field. The probe that
   measures the `(1−q)` spread answers this too — a spread *matching* the q-gate
   model supports the tempo; a q-free-constant spread pushes the Clock to
   presentation-only. Cross-owned with `tide-of-the-attractor.md` open-Q5 (the
   same mixel-scale q-gate probe, theory-owned in `qi-as-time-clock.md` §2.1).
5. **Local vs. regional tempo nesting.** Does the Clock's *local* `(1−q)` read
   nest cleanly inside the tide's *regional* `q` season (harvest at a ridge reads a
   calm watch *and* a harvest sky), or can the two disagree (a thin-tide ridge that
   still holds a locally-patient core, or vice-versa)? A disagreement is not a bug
   — it is the honest *positional* read of §3.3 — but how often it occurs, and what
   it means, is a design-probe question the tide's open-Q4 (regional vs. local
   tempo) already shares.
