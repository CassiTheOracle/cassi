slope** — no stepped stair of level beds that turns a mountainside's own lift into the

## 2. The stepped slope — the honest mechanics

The terrace's field-state is a composition of the corpus's landed slopes-and-beds
claims, read at the cultivated edge of the land's lift. Its parts are each grounded in
a source doc's real quantity; **the terrace's form — its steps' bound, its slope's
hold — is this doc's [design]** over those channels, probe-calibrated.

### (a) The farm's crop-band held on the steps — the crop as a shallow-rung regime, the farmer's bounded Q4 writes at the slope

**The landed piece.** The farm establishes the crop as a **shallow-rung regime** — the
`material-regimes.md` §2 table at crop scale — precipitating where the bed's `q` sits
in a band, its growth a *run* the field holds against the `ε²` drain
([`farm-that-feeds.md`](./farm-that-feeds.md) §2/§2.1). The farmer's acts — weeding,
husbanding, harvesting, fallowing — are **bounded Q4 writes**, `{op, worldPos, rung,
magnitude, sustain}`, amplitude-capped by the no-free-energy rule, or read-only
consumers of the publish (§3).

**The [design] over it — the terrace holds that band on a slope's steps:**

> **A terrace is the farm's crop-band held on level steps cut into the slope.** Each
> step is a bed — a level hold where the soil's `q` can sit in a crop's precipitation
> band the way a flat farm's bed holds it (`farm-that-feeds.md` §2). The farmer's
> weeding/husbanding/harvesting writes are the same bounded Q4 ops, done *at the
> slope*: each step's band is held exactly as a flat bed's is — weed the `ε²`, husband
> the bath, harvest at the tide-high — and the `(1−q)` waste law
> (`energy-harnessing.md` §2) taxes the slope's beds the way it taxes any bed, no
> discount for being cut. **The terrace is a stair of beds — the farm's regime, kept
> level against the land's lift.**

**[design]** — the *steps' form*. The farm lands that a crop precipitates where the
bed's `q` sits in a band and that the farmer's acts are bounded Q4 writes; the climb
lands the gradient. What is this doc's [design] is **that a mountainside is cut into
level *step-beds* that hold that band on a slope** — the step's bound (how wide/tall a
level bed), the slope's hold (how steep a face the steps can carry), and the farmer's
per-step cost. This is probe-calibrated against the climbing-terrain demo, exactly as
the farm's crop-band and harvest-peak are probed (`farm-that-feeds.md` §7b/§8). The
*crop-band mechanics* — does a regime precipitate where `q` sits in a band — are
landed; **which slope cut holds which crop is this doc's designed constant.**

### (b) The mountainside's beds fed by runoff — the spring's water drawn down the steps, the rain's water caught

**The landed pieces.** The spring establishes the **welling** — a fixed, bounded place
where the field's order rises of itself, the conservative re-precipitation at a
stationarity point, a named source a settlement draws from
([`the-spring.md`](./the-spring.md) §1/§2). The rain establishes the gentle fall — the
flood's gentle twin, a mild high-`q` fall that feeds a bed's `q` band, nourishing but
wet ([`the-rain.md`](./the-rain.md) §2/§3).

**The [design] over them — the terrace's beds are fed by that runoff, drawn down the
steps:**

> **A terrace's beds are fed by the spring's and the rain's own water, drawn down the
> steps.** The stepped slope catches the runoff the field's own sources provide: the
> spring's welling rising where a settlement draws (`the-spring.md` §2) and the rain's
> gentle fall wetting the slope from above (`the-rain.md` §3). The steps **hold** that
> water — each level bed catches what falls or flows onto it and holds its `q` in the
> crop's band, so a fall that would run straight off a bare slope instead *soaks* the
> terraced beds step by step. This is the farm's water made a slope's: a fall that
> feeds a flat bed's band (`the-rain.md` §2.2) also feeds a stepped bed's — the rain
> is the terrace's water, and the spring is its named source, never a minted
> irrigation.

**[design]** — the *runoff's draw*. The spring lands a fixed source and the rain lands
a gentle fall; what is this doc's [design] is **that the stepped slope channels that
water into its beds — that each level step catches the runoff its crops need**, and the
spring's draw books through the same Q4 lane a settlement's other draws do
(`the-spring.md` §4). It is never a *minted* irrigation — the water is the spring's and
the rain's own, drawn down the steps the field provides, never conjured by the cut

### (c) The built landform that turns a climb's face into fields — the ascent made a crop-holding slope

**The landed piece.** The climb establishes the ascent — the body working `∇(g·Φ)`

**[design]** — the *climb-made-to-feed frame*. The climb lands the gradient and its

**[design]** — the *naming of the worked slope*. The landform-name lands the anchor and

### 4.2 The terrace must be kept — an unmaintained terrace erodes and fails toward the raw slope

The farm lands that the beds need **husbanding** — weeding, husbanding the bath,

you cannot un-mine an ore* ([`the-fallow.md`](./the-fallow.md) §1); and the causeway
adds the **swallow** — a neglected structure gets reclaimed ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md)
§6; `house-that-steers.md` §3).

**The terrace folds all three into the stepped land's keep:**

> **The stepped hold must be kept — an unmaintained terrace erodes and fails toward
> the raw slope.** The terrace is not a standing gift: its steps' beds are the farm's
> held bands, and un-husbanded they dissolve toward the field's own state
> (`farm-that-feeds.md` §2.1). Its cut is a worked scar, and neglected it erodes — the
> level steps fail, the soil's `q` spends and the `ε²` rises, and the terraced slope
> reads back toward the raw mountainside it was cut from — **the fallow's one-way
> run-out at the landform's register, the causeway's swallow at the farm's**
> (the-fallow §2; the-causeway §6). A settlement that stops tending its terraces does
> not lose a farm's *harvest*; it loses the *landform* — the cut un-held is the name
> shed, the slope's return, never a standing reward.

### 4.3 The runoff is the spring's and the rain's own water — never a minted irrigation

The spring lands the welling — the field's own conservative re-precipitation, never a
mint ("a spring provides nothing beyond its own welling, the fixed source is the
field's own"). The rain lands the gentle fall — the water cycle's own return, "never a
mint" ([`the-spring.md`](./the-spring.md) §5d; [`the-rain.md`](./the-rain.md) §3.2).

> **The terrace's water is the spring's and the rain's own, drawn down the steps —
> never a minted irrigation.** The steps catch what the field provides: the spring's
> welling where a settlement draws, the rain's gentle fall wetting the slope. There is

> already gives, the same `q`-surfeit source and the same conservative welling, at a

### 4.4 The terrace is the landform-name's named land — the stepped hold named, held, never mints

The landform-name lands the honest decay: "the name holds what the land held, and when
the land changes, the name changes honestly," and a landform-name informs and holds,

> **The terrace is the landform-name's named land — the stepped hold named, held,
> never mints.** A worked, level-held stepped slope is a named landform — read at
