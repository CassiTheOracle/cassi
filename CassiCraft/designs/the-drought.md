# The Drought: The Receding — the Event of the Ground's Water Leaving, Distinct from the Dry Land Itself

**Question under design:** the corpus has the **desert** as the *standing place of
dry* — a window living at the attractor's thin edge, the thin regime sustained as a
place to be and endure (`the-desert.md` §2). It has the **rain** as the *nourishing
fall* — the wet that gives, the flood's gentle twin at a yield the field can take
(`the-rain.md` §1/§3). It has the **flood** as the *surplus* — the q-excess event,
the harvest-tide accumulation pushed past its enriching limit
(`the-flood.md` §2). It has the **fallow** as the *depletion of order* — the one-way
run-out, the window's generational exhaustion (`the-fallow.md` §1/§2). It has the
**spring** as the *living well* — the fixed, bounded place where the field's order
rises of itself (`the-spring.md` §2). It has the **cistern** as the *held water* —
the built store that makes a dry season survivable rather than merely mapped

drawn-out process of the ground's water *leaving* — the well slowing, the cistern
drained, the rain failing, the stores spent — distinct from the dry land that stands
after. This document designs that event: **the Drought is the receding — the event
of the ground's water leaving, distinct from the dry land itself; the flood's
slow-inverse; not the dry place but the process of going dry that a settlement must
weather.**

## 2. The receding — the honest mechanics

The drought's field-state is a composition of the corpus's landed water-receding
mechanics, read over the long window as a *process of going dry*. Each of the five
faces is grounded in its source doc's real quantity; **the drought's form — its
receding's pace, its window's length — is this doc's [design]** over those channels,
probe-calibrated against the tide's measured period and the spring's rate-calibration.

### (a) The well slowing — the rate-read at its thinning

**The landed piece.** The spring's welling is the field's own conservative
re-precipitation at a fixed point — a locality whose ambient `q` the field keeps
re-surfacing, held above its dissolution threshold tide after tide (`the-spring.md`
§2.1). Its *rate* — the ambient `q` a spring holds above its locality's baseline,
sustained — is the spring's rate-read, `[design]` over the welling (open-Q2 there).
The caretaker reads that rate over the long horizon: *is the well full, thinning, or
failing?* — the trend over the tide's `[t_k, t_k+T]` cycle, never a one-time glance
(`the-spring-caretaker.md` §2.1). And the drawn well's honesty is hard: a settlement
that draws a spring out beyond its replenishment spends the stationarity that made it
a spring, falling toward the fallow's read (`the-spring.md` §4).

**The [design] over it — the drought is the well slowing:**

> **[design] The drought is the well slowing — the welling's rate at its thinning,
> read over the long window.** Where a normal window's well holds full across the
> tide-cycles (the stationarity holds), a drought window's well *recedes*: the
> caretaker's rate-read shows the elevated `q` trending down toward the locality's
> baseline — full, then thinning, then failing — the welling's rate actually falling,
> the re-precipitation no longer keeping the point as full as it was
> (`the-spring-caretaker.md` §2.1's thinning/failing reads). The over-draw's honesty
> is the drought's own: a well slowed past its replenishment falls toward the fallow's
> one-way run-out (`the-spring.md` §4, `the-fallow.md` §2a) — the drought's draw
> shares the over-draw's bound, never compounds it. [design] The *well's receding
> pace* — how fast the welling's rate falls, over what window, before a thinning well
> reads as failing — is this doc's dial over the spring's rate-calibration and the
> tide's measured period, probe-calibrated exactly as the spring's own rate-read is.

### (b) The cistern drained — the built store's water spent against the long absence

**The landed pieces.** The cistern is the built water-hold — the rain caught and the
spring-drawn water kept against the dry, the water-twin of the granary's order-vault

never free, a real one-way spend the fallow reads honestly — *you can steward a
scarcity, you cannot un-mine an ore* (`the-cistern.md` §4, `the-fallow.md` §2a). And
it holds only what the field gave it — never a mint, never a hidden oasis
(`the-cistern.md` §4/§5d, `energy-harnessing.md` §6).

**The [design] over it — the drought is the cistern drained:**

> **[design] The drought is the cistern drained — the built store's water spent
> against the long absence.** The held water that made a dry season survivable
> (`the-cistern.md` §3) is drawn down as the rain fails and the well slows; the cistern
> that could not be refilled reads its level falling toward the marsh's patchiness if
> un-kept, its water shed as `(1−q)` bleed toward the attractor if held past its
> keeping (`the-cistern.md` §4, `energy-harnessing.md` §2). The full cistern is the
> survival store; the drought is the store reading toward empty — the honest buffer
> spent against the long absence, a real one-way draw never a re-precipitation the
> settlement can force. [design] The *drain's pace* — how fast a full cistern's held
> water spends against a receding field, over what window, before an empty cistern

### (c) The rain failing — the nourishing fall's absence

> never returns (`the-rain.md` §2.2, `the-fallow.md` §3). The failing fall is the

## 3. The flood's slow-inverse — the load-bearing design

This is the load-bearing design. The flood doc designs the **arrival** of the
water-surplus; it presupposes a receding but never names it. And the drought's whole
thesis is that the receding is the **same event's process read in reverse**:

> **The drought is the flood's slow-inverse: the flood is the q-surfeit's arrival
> (`the-flood.md` §2 — the surplus's event), the drought is the water's slow removal —
> the same event's process read in reverse, the field's abundance receding over the
> long window.** Where the flood is the harvest-tide accumulation pushed past its
> enriching limit — the *arrival* of the surfeit, the wet that drowns
> (`the-flood.md` §2/§1) — the drought is the same water-channels' *slow removal*: the
> spring's slowing well (`the-spring.md` §2), the rain's failing fall
> (`the-rain.md` §3), the cistern's drained store (`the-cistern.md` §2). The same
> channels read both, at a **rate**: a flood reads as the accumulation climbing past
> the enriching band's top; a drought reads as the water falling away from it, the
> abundance receding over the long window. The flood is the surplus's arrival; the
> drought is the water leaving.

**The reverse-read, stated against the flood's own season's arc.** The flood's season
has three named phases — the rise (the harvest becomes the flood when it overshoots),
the bloom's peak (the deafening), the shedding (the self-limit)
