# The Map: The Player-Drawn Chart as a Field Instrument

**Question under design:** every existing field-instrument reads the *now* — the
Weatherglass and the Far Mirror sample the present publish, the coherence reader
(Sense) is aimed at the present state. None is a **record you draw**. In a
building game -- the corpus's own domain (`house-that-steers.md`,
`farm-that-feeds.md`, `coherence-highway.md`) -- a player who has lived in one
place needs the instrument of a *continued* inhabitant: **a map in their own
hand, which is itself a field instrument.** This document is that design.

The core claim, stated once and kept:

> **A player-drawn map is a field instrument. Its ink reads q: when you draw a
> road, a ridge, a desert-margin, the drawn line binds to the published channel
> at that place and *ages as the field moves*. The chart is a living record of
> *what you once read*, and it keeps its honesty only if you re-read and redraw
> -- cartography as stewardship.**

## 1. A map is a field instrument — stated once

### 1.1 The gap: no instrument is a record you draw

The corpus's field-instruments are all readers of a present `now`. Sense
(`coherence-magic.md` §2) is *aimed* at the present state. The Weatherglass
(`field-instruments.md` §1) is *always-on* at the present location. The Far
Mirror (§3) resolves a *present* sparse far-window record. Sound (`field-music.md`)
and the sky (`atmosphere-orbits-auroras.md`) are ambient presents. **None keeps a
record.** None answers the question a player who has lived in one place actually
asks: *what did the field look like here when I first came, and what has it done
since?*

That question is what **the map** answers. The player's own hand-drawn chart is
the natural instrument of a continued inhabitant — the person who sits in one
window and wants to know not merely *where things are now* but *how the place
has moved under them*. Cartography is the undesigned instrument, and it is the
one the corpus has never built.

### 1.2 The map is a field instrument — ink that reads q

The design claim, stated once:

> **The drawn chart is a field instrument. When you draw a line — a road, a
> ridge, a desert-margin — the ink binds to the published channel at the drawn
> sites. The line records *the q (and ε²) the field had there when you drew it*,
> and it keeps reading the *live* channel beneath the page. The mismatch
> between what the ink records and what the field now says is the map's honest
> aging.**

Concretely: the player draws on a physical surface (a chart item, a wall-mounted
board). Each drawn stroke is **bound** to the world position it sits over. The
binding does not snapshot a picture; it *samples the published channels at that
position at draw time* and keeps a live handle to the same channels' current
values. The map's read is then the **difference between the drawn record and the
live field**:

- A **road** you drew along a coherence ridge records the line's `q` as high. If
  the ridge holds, the ink stays *live* — the road is where the field still is.
  If the desert reclaims it (`field-hazards.md` §3 — a regional `q` collapse
  creeps over the margin), the live `q` under that line falls, and **the drawn
  road goes stale on the page** — a line that records a road where the field no
  longer holds one.
- A **ridge** you drew at the field's organized edge records, at each point, the
  `q`/`ε²` across it. The field's organization *moves* (`field-emergent-ecology.md`
  §4.1 — coherence ridges migrate, scars close, the biome map reshapes). When
  the live ridge drifts off the line you drew, **the inked ridge no longer
  matches what the field now shows** — the map is a map of a moment, and this
  is the drift made visible in your hand.
- A **desert-margin** you drew at the boundary of a `q` collapse records where
  the edge was *when you drew it*. The margin creeps (§3.3 there). The ink's
  recorded edge and the field's real edge disagree, and the disagreement *is*
  the aging.

The map does not lie, and it does not pretend. It records what you once read —
**that is its only claim** — and it makes the field's movement since your drawing
legible as the page's own staleness. This is the honest inverse of every other
instrument: the Weatherglass tells you the present; the map tells you *the
present measured against your past hand*.

### 1.3 The family rule held — a consumer of the publish, never a new channel

The map obeys the family rule (`field-instruments.md` §2.1 — **every instrument
is a consumer of the same publish with a presentation idiom, never a new
channel**):

- **It reads only the published channels.** The drawing binds to the ≈ 6 MiB
  publish (`corpus-reconciliation.md` §2: `q` 1 + pot 1 + `∇(g·Φ)` vec3-trim 3 +
  `ρ` 1; the `ε² = (EY − φ·EI)²` decoherence is derived from the pot channel, as
  every field read does) at the *drawn sites* — a bounded set of trilinear
  samples, exactly the Weatherglass's "one sample at a position" pattern
  (`field-instruments.md` §1.4), repeated per drawn stroke.
- **It adds no channel.** The staleness read is a *difference* between two reads
  of the *same* channels (draw-time vs live). No new published quantity.
- **It is read-only.** It never touches physics state; it writes only the
  player's drawing record (a Q4 player-return op, per `async-field-domain.md` §7
  Q4 / `coherence-magic.md` §5.1's read-only-consumer discipline — a *record*,
  not a field mutation).

The map is the instrument family's **recording** member: the others present the
publish's present; the map records and re-reads the publish across the player's
own history. It is a presentation idiom — *a line that reads q* — over the
channels every family member already reads, which is what makes it a pure
consumer and not a new reader.

## 2. The honest aging — what goes stale, and why

The map's single distinguishing behavior is **aging**. This is not a flaw to be
smoothed away; it is the instrument's whole honesty. The map records what you
read; the field moves; the page falls out of step; **and the page showing its
own staleness is the map reporting the field's movement since you drew.**

Three load-bearing stale reads, each grounded in an existing corpus system:

|| Drawn thing | What goes stale | The field movement that stales it | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|---|
|| **A road** | the line records a road where the field no longer holds one | the desert reclaims it — a regional `q` collapse creeps its margin over the line (`q ≈ 1e-3…1e-1` at noise up to the attractor ≈ 0.947); a coherence drought is a *made*, creeping consequence, not a biome | `field-hazards.md` §3 |
|| **A ridge** | the inked ridge no longer matches what the field now shows | the field's organization *moves* — coherence ridges migrate, scars close, biome⇔regime reshapes (`field-emergent-ecology.md` §4.2: "the biome map moves with them") | `field-emergent-ecology.md` §4.1/§4.2 |
|| **A harvest-season chart** | a chart drawn at harvest reads thin at the trough | the tide — the regional `q` breathes between harvest (`q` high, near-lossless) and thin (`q` toward the noise floor, wasteful), and the field's states shift which basins, ores, and routes hold | `tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2 |

The through-line is the archaeology doc's honest determinism applied to the
chart (`field-archaeology.md` §1.2): **the map does not lie; it ages.** A
stale line is not a wrong line — it is an honest record of what you once read,
kept truthful only insofar as the page *shows* it has gone stale. A map that hid
its staleness would lie; a map that ages visibly is the honest record of the
field's movement.

### 2.1 The Life-Signal tie — a fresh line reads maintained; a stale line reads fallen

The map's staleness is the corpus's vitality classifier read on a page
(`life-signal.md` §3 — the maintenance axis: live things hold, static things
fall):

- **A fresh line reads `maintained`.** While the drawn record matches the live
  channel at the drawn site (the ink's `q` is still the field's `q`), the line
  is *live* — the map keeps the place in mind. This is the maintained-lock read:
  a line held by the player's re-reading and redrawing stays in step with the
  field.
- **A stale line reads `fallen`.** Once the live channel has moved off what the
  ink records, the line is a *fallen floor of ink* — the ordinary-scar class
  (`life-signal.md` §3): the field moved on without your redraw, and the page
  shows it. A neglected map is a page of fallen lines — exactly the
  house-that-steers ruin read (an unmaintained structure collapses toward the
  attractor, `house-that-steers.md` §3) taken from architecture to cartography.

The tie is structural, not a metaphor: both the map's staleness and the
Life-Signal read the *same publish* (the drawn-site channel vs the structure's
channel) against a maintenance expectation. The map is a maintained structure
at page scale, and its Life-Signal is **does the ink still hold?** — a line you
keep re-reading stays live; one you abandon goes stale and reads fallen.

### 2.2 The naming of the aging — a designed read

The *staleness read* — how a live `q` mismatch is presented (a line that dims, a
margin that greys, a road that "dries on the page") — is **[design]**, probe-
calibrated over the real channel mismatch. What is engine-real is the mismatch
itself: the published `q`/`ε²` at the drawn site is a real number, and its drift
is the field's real movement. The *presentation* of that drift as aging ink is
the designed surface (§6). The map is an instrument, not a telemetry screen: it
records what you *drew and re-read*, with all the honesty and fallibility of a
hand — it does not stream the field, it records your encounter with it.

## 3. Cartography as stewardship

### 3.1 Re-read and redraw, or the desert reclaims your chart

The map's honesty is the house/farm discipline at instrument scale
(`house-that-steers.md` §2, `farm-that-feeds.md`, `energy-harnessing.md` §5.4's
anti-corruption): a well-kept structure holds; an unmaintained one collapses
toward the attractor. The map is a maintained structure whose maintaining act is
**re-reading and redrawing**.

- **Re-reading** is the map's upkeep. You walk a charted road, aim the reader at
  the drawn sites, and the map *refreshes its live comparison* — the ink rebinds
  to the field's present. A line you re-read stays true; a ridge you confirm
  stays drawn.
- **Redrawing** is the honest correction. When the field has moved (the ridge
  drifted, the desert-margin crept), re-reading alone does not re-truth a map of
  the *present* — you **draw the new edge, the new ridge, the reclaimed road**.
  The old line does not get erased; it gets *superseded* (see the strata-map,
  §4.1 — the layers of old drawings are the map's own archaeology).

**Neglect has the same honest consequence as neglecting a house** (`house-that-steers.md`
§3.1): an unmaintained map's lines go stale, then fallen, then unreadable as a
guide to the present — a **fallen floor of ink** where a chart used to be. The
desert that reclaims a charted road also claims the chart: the margin's creep
(`field-hazards.md` §3.3 — "a desert visibly *spreads* from its cause") is, on
the page, the map's aging made legible. Cartography is stewardship because a map
of a living field is only ever as honest as your last re-read.

### 3.2 A well-kept map is a maintained structure; a neglected one a fallen floor

The Life-Signal tie of §2.1 is stewardship made literal: **a well-kept map reads
`maintained`** — its lines live, its edges match, it is a kept place in the
player's mind the way a hearth is a kept room. **A neglected one reads `fallen`**
— a page of scar-class ink the field has moved past. The player who stewards
their map is performing the corpus's maintenance act (`energy-harnessing.md`
§5.4 anti-corruption is "spend coherence to hold ground against decay"; the
map's form of that spend is *attention paid at the drawn sites* — cheap, honest,
and essential). The map is the house/farm discipline — keep what you hold, or
the field takes it back — at the instrument scale.

### 3.3 The site-map tie — the engine's map and the player's hand

The deepest tie is to the engine's own map:

> The meshless sites are "where the field is most organized" — 2·16³ = 8,192
> moving-Voronoi sites, Lloyd-relaxed weighted on coherence `q`, clustering
> where the field is coherent, projected to mark the active-chunk set
> (`chunk-field-quantization.md` §5). **This is the field's own map** — the
> engine's continuous, always-current record of where its organization is.

The player's chart is **that map in their own hand** — a drawn, discrete,
stale-able record of the same organization, made by a person rather than the
solver. And here is the honest power of the pairing:

> **The engine's site map and the player's chart can disagree, and the
> disagreement is the field's movement made legible.** The site map is always
> now; the player's chart is always *then-and-now*. Where the two agree, the
> page is true. Where they part — a site cluster the player drew as a ridge but
> the solver has since moved, a road the player charts across a region the site
> map no longer marks active — the player sees, in the gap between the engine's
> living map and their own hand's record, the field's actual movement since
> they drew. The site map is the *truth now*; the chart is *the truth you
> recorded*; the two-sheet comparison is the instrument.

This is what makes the map the instrument of a *continued* inhabitant: the
player who has lived in a place long enough to have drawn it is the one for whom
the site map's present and their chart's past both mean something, and whose
stewardship keeps them honest with each other.

## 4. What the map composes

The map is not a lone instrument; it is the **page** on which several already-
designed records layer. Each is a consumer of the publish drawn with an idiom —
the map's composition of them is their common surface.

### 4.1 The strata-map — a personal archaeology layered on the chart

`field-archaeology.md` §2.3 designs the strata as condensation history imprinted
as bands ("a cross-section of the world is a log-periodic stratigraphy"). The
map is the *player's own* stratigraphy:

> **The strata-map is the drawn history of the window's field, in your own
> hand** — a personal archaeology layered on the chart. Every redraw, every
> superseded edge, every old ridge you drew that the field has since wandered
> past is a *band* in the map's own record. The physical page accumulates layers
> of what you once read — a chart-drawn stratigraphy of your own encounters with
> the field, where each old line is the residue of a past state, exactly as the
> field's strata are the residue of past condensations.

Where the field's archaeology is the *world's* deep past read as residue
(`field-archaeology.md` §2.2), the map's strata-map is the player's *personal*
deep past — not what the field precipitated and kept, but what the player read
and drew. Redrawing does not erase the history; it *bands* it. A well-aged map
is a palimpsest of drafts, and reading its oldest visible lines is a personal
archaeology of the player's own stewardship — or neglect (a line never redrawn
is a fossil of a field state the player has forgotten).

### 4.2 The Far Mirror's remote chart — the map's distant counterpart

`field-instruments.md` §3 designs the Far Mirror as the Weatherglass's lens
*repointed outward* — resolving a distant window's sparse publish into a chart.
The map is the same instrument family's other range direction:

> The **Far Mirror** charts a *distant* place from its sparse far-window record
> (the ~KB sparse publish of `world-seams.md` gate (b)) — the map's distant
> counterpart, resolving "there" without a hand having been there. The **map**
> charts the *near* place from the player's own drawn-and-re-read samples — the
> only instrument that records "here" in the player's own hand. One reads
> another window's present sparse state; the other records this window's past in
> ink. Together they give a settler the two horizons of a charted life: the
> far chart of where one might go, and the near chart of where one has been.

The map is the **personal** far-mirror inversion: where the Far Mirror is a
*resolved chart of a present elsewhere*, the map is a *drawn record of a past
here*.

### 4.3 The vein-map — a charted road network is the window's circulation in ink

`coherence-highway.md` §2 designs the road network as the window's **living
vein-map** — a settlement's connective tissue is a map of its sustained
coherence ("read where the roads are strong and you read where the settlement
holds the field up"). The player's charted roads are that vein-map in ink:

> **A charted road network is the window's circulation in ink.** When the player
> draws a road, they record a line the field was holding (high `q` along the
> route). When the field stops holding it — a thin tide drains the line's `q`
> (`coherence-highway.md` §4.1), a Coda feeds on the marginal stretch (§4.2), or
> the desert reclaims it — the inked vein goes stale, and the map shows the
> window's circulation drying on the page. Charting the roads is drawing the
> settlement's own vein-map by hand; keeping them live is the stewardship of
> §3.

The vein-map tie is the map's most *physical* composition: a drawn road is not a
decoration but a recorded claim that "here the field was carrying a line" — and
the aging of that line is the circulation's honest, observed slow-down.

### 4.4 The storm-track — a charted provenance

`weather-not-storm.md` designs the provenance classifier over the danger layer's
extremes — weather (happens where the field is) vs aimed (happens where the
player is), read from the storm's published dynamics, pre-registered before any
weather-verdict. The map composes it as a **charted provenance**:

> **A charted storm-track is a record of *provenance observed*.** The player
> who has lived through a storm traces its path on the map — the `ε²` front's
> edge, where it passed, what it dissolved. Re-reading that track later is
> reading *the storm that was*, a drawn residue of the short event the tide
> framed (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2). A charted track is the player's own
> contribution to the provenance read: not the classifier's verdict, but the
> eyewitness record of where a `ε²` extreme was, which is exactly the raw the
> classifier's derived axes (`weather-not-storm.md` §2 — front shape, phase,
> tracking) are built from. The map holds the storm the player observed; the
> weather doc's classifier tells them what it *was*.

The composition is honest: the map does not classify; it *records the drawn
path*. Classification still belongs to `weather-not-storm.md`'s designed reader
(and its Phase-1.5 provenance probe, `field-hazards.md` open-Q2). The map adds
the settler's drawn memory of the event, so a charted storm-track is a *personal
provenance record* on top of the field's own channels — never a new classifier,
only a drawn trace the classifier could read.

### (c) Determinism (hard)

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `life-signal.md` §6d). The map's aging
inherits it as a **hard gate**:

> **Same drawing, same field state → same staleness.** The staleness read is a
> deterministic function of the drawn sites' published channels (draw-time vs
> live) — no hidden randomness, no player-relative variance. A player who redraws
> the same chart in the same field state, or two players who drew the same
> strokes, get the same aging read. This is what makes the map *learnable* and
> the strata a *truthful record* rather than a guess.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — a map records, never mints

The corpus's single hard rule (`energy-harnessing.md` §6: **no conversion yields
more than it sinks**, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` amplitude caps) applies to the map
as a hard bound:

> **A map records, it never mints.** The drawn chart reads channels; it changes
> nothing in the field. There is no "draw here to extract" — the ink is a read
> of the publish at the drawn sites, and drawing adds no tap, no convert, no
> stored coherence beyond the record itself. The no-free-energy cap therefore
> closes the *map-to-farm* exploit trivially: a map cannot create a harvest
> because it writes nothing the field could be farmed through — its only
> "yield" is the honest record, which is information, never energy (the tide's
> §5d already states the same for information: "the tide's gift is information
> and timing, not energy").

A map-to-farm exploit — "draw many points to somehow extract q" — is impossible
by construction: the map's draws are reads of existing channels, bounded by what
the field already provides at those sites, with no write-back that could mint
(`energy-harnessing.md` §6; `coherence-magic.md` §5.1's read-only consumer).

### (e) Accessibility — the map is a layer, never hidden-only

The map's read must remain a layer of the existing field-reading surfaces, per
`field-music.md` §6 (an enhancement, never the only channel) and the instrument
family rule (`field-instruments.md` §1.3 — the Weatherglass is the mute player's
guaranteed-equal form):

> **The map is a layer — the same information is readable, more laboriously,
> from the reader and the Weatherglass.** Everything a stale map shows — the
> `q`/`ε²` at a drawn site, the field's movement — is readable from the reader
> overlay (`coherence-magic.md` §2, the published channels) and the Weatherglass
> (`field-instruments.md` §1.2, the same channels glanceable). A player who never
> draws a map loses *nothing informational*; the map is the *drawn, remembered*
> framing of the same shared read, never a hidden channel. It is the recording
> idiom over the accessible publish, exactly as the strata-map is a layer over
> the residue read and the storm-track a layer over the weather read.

## Open questions

1. **The aging calibration (§6a).** Does the staleness read track the real
   published-channel movement at the drawn sites — a measurable relation between
   field drift and page aging — at a scale a player can read? The Phase-1 slice
   is the pre-registered probe that answers it; a negative (channel drift too
   weak at drawn-site resolution to be legible) would push the map toward
   coarse region-band aging rather than per-stroke. **[probe]**
2. **The ink-presentation tuning (§2.2).** How does a live match/mismatch read
   as *aging ink* rather than a mis-drawn line? The map must present a stale
   stroke as an honest, readable fact ("the field moved"), not as an error the
   player should feel they drew wrong. **[design]** — Phase-1, along with the
   Family's Weatherglass form tuning.
3. **The redraw-vs-strata balance (§3.1/§4.1).** A map that re-binds a line on
   re-read keeps it live; one that redraws supersedes it. Where does the
   "refresh" stop and the "strata band" begin — at what staleness (or how many
   redraws) does an old line read as a historical residue rather than a live
   chart? This is the map's analog of the residue-vs-live classifier
   (`field-archaeology.md` §7 open-Q3 / `life-signal.md` §3), applied to its own
   history. **[design]**, inheriting the classifier line.
4. **The site-map divergence reading (§3.3).** How much of the two-sheet
   comparison (engine site map vs player's chart) is *presented to the player*
   — an explicit "here the field moved off your drawing" overlay — vs left to be
   felt as the page's own staleness? The former is a stronger instrument, the
   latter truer to the "in your own hand" feel; both are [design], and the
   balance is open (the site-map/chunk-active read is engine-real,
   `chunk-field-quantization.md` §5; its presentation is the dial).
5. **The map-to-farm shadow (§6d).** The no-mint guard is enforced by
   construction (draws are reads). But does the *record itself* ever acquire
   economy — e.g. a well-kept chart as a tradable good, or a "survey" act
   charged like a deep read (`field-archaeology.md` §3.2's depth-charge)? Whether
   drawing/re-reading has a coherence *surplus* (attention as upkeep) or a *cost*
   (a powered survey) is open; the conservation stance favors free, the
   instrument-depth stance a bounded cost. **[design]**, Phase-1.5.
