## 1. The Sledge, stated once

The corpus designs the cart for the *road* and the carry for the *body*. The cart is the road's vehicle — a wheel-carriage that rides the highway's maintained `∇(g·Φ)` gradient, worthwhile only on a steered route (`the-cart.md` §2/§3). The carry is the body's pack — the deliberate, read, costed weight a walker holds (`the-carry.md` §2). The farm yields and the granary stores. **But nothing carries a load across deep winter** — nothing moves the settlement's freight once the cold's snow buries the road, the white erases the footing, and the wheels cannot turn. The cart's line is the highway's maintained filament; the sledge's is the cold's own snow. This is that design.

> **The Sledge, stated once.** The winter sled — the loaded carriage built for the white and the deep cold, the cart's frozen twin: a low, friction-drawn carrier that rides the cold's snow and drags the granary's goods where the wheels cannot turn. It is the cart's load-and-gradient trade made for the terrain the cart cannot serve — trading the road for the sliding surface, the maintained line for the cold's own — and it is honest to the same law as every vehicle: the load is real, the drag is real, and the sledge converts nothing.

The Sledge is deliberately **the cart's frozen twin — the freight-bearing complement of the road's vehicle on the road's buried season.** Where the cart rides the highway's held line (`coherence-highway.md` §1 — a maintained, high-`q` filament the field prefers to route through), the sledge rides the cold's own packed `q`-thin surface — the snow the field itself has laid, the deep cold's surface made a road by the winter rather than by any player. Where the cart is worthwhile only on a steered route (`the-cart.md` §3 — off the maintained line it is worse than a walk), the sledge is worth hitching **when the road is gone** — when the winter's surface is the only road, and the wheels would only dig. It is the carry's vehicle-form at the winter's scale: the pack a single body can hold (`the-carry.md` §2) becomes, on the sledge, a load the settlement itself can drag across the white.

**The honest flag, once.** The pieces a sledge composes are all landed — the cart's ride and its load-and-gradient trade (`the-cart.md` §2/§3), the carry's pack and its deliberate cost (`the-carry.md` §2), the cold's long-thin and the `(1−q)` waste at the thin (`the-cold.md` §2), the granary's store (`the-granary.md` §2), the highway's maintained filament (`coherence-highway.md` §1), the fallow's one-way depletion (`the-fallow.md` §2a), the toll's held doors (`the-toll.md` §4), and the no-free-energy cap (`energy-harnessing.md` §6). **The sledge's composition — that a low, friction-drawn carrier rides the cold's own `(1−q)`-spent snow to drag a real load, that its glide is the field's own spent rather than a minted winter-road, that its freight crosses honest doors rather than a hidden sled-path — is this document's [design]** over those landed pieces. Where the **walk** is the un-roaded primitive and the **cart** is the road's freight, the **sledge** is the winter's freight — the corpus's fourth transport face, the cart's frozen twin. ## 2. The frozen twin — the honest mechanics

The Sledge is the cart's frozen-form: the wheel-carriage's `∇(g·Φ)` conversion (`the-cart.md` §2 — the ride) made a low, friction-drawn carrier that trades the road for the sliding surface (`the-cart.md` §3 — a cart is only worthwhile on a steered route; the sledge rides the cold's own snow, the field's own packed `q`-thin surface, never the highway's maintained filament, coherence-highway §1), the body's pack made a vehicle (`the-carry.md` §2 — the deliberate, read, costed carry at the winter's scale), carrying the granary's goods across the white (the-granary §2 — the store the winter holds; the-blizzard §2/§3 — the white the sledge crosses where the walk's footing is buried). Each is grounded in its source doc's real quantities; the *sledge's form* (its runners' glide, its load's bound) is this doc's [design] over the cart/carry/cold landed mechanics, probe-calibrated.

### (a) The ride — the cart's `∇(g·Φ)` conversion, traded for the cold's surface

`the-cart.md` §2 establishes the cart's ride: a downhill cart carries its payload on the gradient — the wheel-carriage's held low-`q` chassis compounds the descent's work into a carried load, the field doing the descent-work the walk spends its stride on. The descent is real: `∇(g·Φ)` is engine-real field-hydro `a = −G_N·(π/ρ)·∇(g·Φ)` (energy-harnessing §1.1, ≈ 40 ns/entity), and the walk already rides it ungated at foot-pace (`the-walk.md` §2a). **The Sledge is the frozen twin of that conversion — the same carried-load principle, on the one surface the wheels cannot use:**

> **The sledge trades the road for the sliding surface.** Where a cart rides the highway's maintained high-`q` filament (`coherence-highway.md` §1) to compound the descent into a carried load, the sledge rides the cold's own packed `q`-thin surface — the snow the field itself has laid — and the same carried-load principle happens at the runners rather than the wheels. It neither needs the road's maintained line (the road is buried under the white, `the-blizzard.md` §3 "the footing buried") nor invents a winter-road of its own; it crosses the winter on the surface the winter itself provides.

**[design]** — the *frozen-form mapping*. The source docs land that the cart compounds `∇(g·Φ)` into a carried load along the highway's maintained line, and that the walk's footing is buried under the white. What is this doc's [design] is **that a low, friction-drawn runner-carriage is the same carried-load principle on the snow's surface** — the runners' glide for the wheels' roll, the field's own packed surface for the maintained filament. This is probe-calibrated: *how much* of a crossing a runner-bound freight converts on the field's own `(1−q)`-thin surface, and how a loaded sledge's drag reads vs. an empty one's, are measured on the Phase-1 living-winter demo (§5b), exactly as the cart's ride and the walk's stride-cost are probed (`the-cart.md` §5 open-Q1; `the-walk.md` §5). The surface is real; the drag is real; the sledge's *form* — its glide, its bound — is the designed surface over them.

**The honest contrast with the cart, stated once.** The cart and the sledge are both deliberate, costed carriers — neither is a free mover. But they are the two edges of the same freight: **the cart is worthwhile only on a steered route; the sledge is worthwhile when the route is gone.** Where the cart trades the walk's freedom for the road's cheap haul (`the-cart.md` §3 — off the maintained line it is worse than a walk), the sledge trades the cart's road for the cold's own surface — a carrier that crosses the white precisely because it asks nothing of the buried line. **The cart rides the road; the sledge rides the snow. Neither rides free.**

### (b) The return — the winter does the one-way hauling

The cart's ride is not one-way; freight goes *to* the market and the cart comes *back* on the maintained line (`the-cart.md` §2b — a loaded cart needs the bat-band's maintained line to climb back). The Sledge's honesty is sharper, because the winter surface is one-way:

> **The sledge drags down the cold's own surface, and the empty sledge is carried back.** The `(1−q)`-spent snow does not haul the load back against the grain — the winter is a one-way surface, and the return is the settlement's own act (the empty sled carried, the freight unloaded, the harness walked back). There is no maintained line to make the return cheap; the sledge's round trip is the winter itself: drag the load out on the snow's glide, carry the empty sled back. **The sledge's freight is the granary's order dragged one way; its return is the settlement's own labor, never a free ride back.**

**[design]** — the *one-way mapping*. The sources land that the cold is a season the settlement endures through husbandry (`the-cold.md` §3) and that the fallow's run-out is one-way (`the-fallow.md` §2a). What is this doc's [design] is **that the sledge's freight is the winter's one-way crossing** — the load dragged out, the empty carrier returned by hand, the return priced as the settlement's own act rather than a minted glide. This is what keeps the sledge honest against the cart's travel-mint (§4b of the-cart): the sledge cannot be farmed into a free round trip, because the return is not the snow's work but the settlement's.

### (c) The carry made a vehicle — the pack at the winter's scale

`the-carry.md` §2 designs the pack: the deliberate, read, costed weight a body carries and its field-cost — carried *matter*, never a debt, never a hidden meter, the load's feel on the stride. **The Sledge is the pack's vehicle-form at the winter's scale:**

> **The sledge is the carry made a carriage.** Where a walker's pack is what a single body can hold on the walk (`the-carry.md` §2 — a tool, meals, a little store, held at the cost of holding it), the sledge is the settlement's own pack — a low, friction-drawn carrier that drags what no single body could carry, at the winter's own weight. The load is carried *matter*, never a debt, never a hidden meter; the sledge's drag is the winter-scale version of the pack's load's feel — a real, legible, costed weight the field reads.

**[design]** — the *vehicle-form mapping*. The sources land that the pack is carried matter whose weight the field reads, and that a loaded body reads heavier on the maintenance axis (`the-carry.md` §3; `life-signal.md` §3). What is this doc's [design] is **that the sledge is that same carried-matter weight, made a carriage** — the pack's deliberate cost applied at a bounded, draggable load's scale, probe-calibrated against the same living-winter demo the carry's load-cost calibrates against. The load is the granary's real order (§4), never a winter-mint.

## 3. The glide — the load-bearing design

This is the Sledge's whole point: **the sledge's glide is the field's own `(1−q)`-lubricated surface, never a free ride.** The low friction a winter sled is *for* is not a designed gift — it is the cold's own spent register, the `(1−q)` waste at the thin read as a glide, and the no-free-energy cap holds it hard: the sledge converts nothing.

### (a) The low friction is the cold's own spent register

`the-cold.md` §2 establishes the long-thin: regional `q` held near the noise floor (`q ~ 1e-3…1e-1` vs. the attractor `q ≈ 0.947`, corpus-reconciliation), and at the floor **every op wastes more** — the `(1−q)` waste law `E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput` (energy-harnessing §2) makes the same machine that ran near-lossless at harvest bleed most of its throughput to glow. **The sledge's glide is that spent register made a surface:**

> **The sledge's low friction is the field's own `(1−q)` spent — the cold's waste at the thin, read as a glide.** A heavy, deep thing that would otherwise dig into the frozen ground rides *on* the cold's packed `q`-thin surface because the winter's own `(1−q)` — the spent fraction that the thin makes large — is what lubricates the crossing. The sledge does not cheapen the crossing out of nothing; it rides the same spent register that makes a thin field wasteful everywhere, at the one shape that turns that waste into a carried glide.

**[design]** — the *glide mapping*. The sources land that the thin's `(1−q)` is large (the cold §2's waste at the floor) and that every held thing wastes that fraction. What is this doc's [design] is **that a low, runner-bound carrier shapes that spent register into a glide** — that the same `(1−q)` a machine bleeds to glow is, under a sledge's bearing, the winter's own surface friction spent instead. This is probe-calibrated: whether a runner-bound freight *measurably* rides the cold's surface with less drag than the same load dragged on foot, and what the `(1−q)`-spent reads as, are the Phase-1 landing claims (§5b). A flat read (the snow's surface too weak, or the extra harness-drag washing out the glide) pushes the Sledge to pure presentation — the freight honest, dragged, but not a mechanical free-ride — exactly the honest outcome the cart and the walk pre-register for their calibrations.

### (b) The sledge converts nothing — no glide that yields

`energy-harnessing.md` §6 states the corpus's single hard rule: **no conversion yields more than it sinks**, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`, coded as amplitude caps on the write-back lane. The sledge inherits it as its most personal register:

> **The sledge converts nothing — there is no glide that yields.** The low friction is the field's own `(1−q)` spent register, and that spend is *spent* — the thin field wastes a larger fraction of everything forever, and the sledge rides that waste as a surface; it does not harvest it. A player cannot bank the glide, cannot store the winter's surface, cannot turn a cheaper snowy crossing into stored coherence. **There is no winter-mint: no sledge that "generates" a cheaper haul, no snow that yields, no cold crossing that pays the settlement coherence it did not already have in the field.**

Three concrete guards, all inherited from the no-free-energy discipline (`energy-harnessing.md` §6; `the-cart.md` §4b; `the-cold.md` §5.3):
1. **No harvesting the glide.** The sledge's low friction is free *movement through the field's own spent register* — never a stored yield, never a capacitor charged by sliding downhill. The cold's own "nothing in a cold mints" (`the-cold.md` §5.3) holds the sledge as the same honesty at the runners.
2. **No shedding into a harvestable drain.** The `(1−q)` the sledge rides is the winter's spent, never a waste a collector can tap — the same refusal that closes cart-wear harvesting (`the-cart.md` §4b #2) and pack-farming (`the-carry.md` §4b).
3. **The sledge is never a winter-mint.** Cheap winter freight is a *relation to the cold's surface*, not a free-transport exploit — the sledge neither speeds up forever nor pays nothing forever, because the load is real (§4) and the return is the settlement's own labor (§2b).

## 4. The honest freight — the load-bearing honesty

The Sledge drags a **real load**: the granary's order, drawn and dragged across the white, spent never free; the crossing honest at the settlement's doors; and the sledge the settlement's own act in the deep cold.

### (a) The freight is the granary's real order, drawn and spent

`the-granary.md` §2/§3 designs the store: the everyday harvest held at tide-high against the lean, a full store the settlement's weather against a thin tide, an empty store *is* the lean; the store's draw books the way any op does (§4) and is spent, never free. **The sledge's freight is that store drawn and dragged across the white:**

> **The sledge's freight is the granary's real order — the store's draw, dragged across the winter, spent never free.** Loading the sledge is a **draw from the granary's store** (`the-granary.md` §4 — the deposit/draw op of the settled record, the store's draw *spent*, never free), booked with the load's magnitude; the sledge drags that booked load across the white; and unloading at the far door is the freight's book carried to its destination — the same single-writer Q4 lane every op uses (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1; `the-cart.md` §4a). **The sledge's load is real depletion, never a winter-mint** — the fallow's one-way run-out read at the sledge's scale: you can steward a scarcity, you cannot un-mine an ore, and a sledge full of the granary's drawn order is the store's honest spend, not a new store of the winter's own (the fallow's honesty — the draw is real depletion, nothing is gained from a fallow, `the-fallow.md` §2a/§5d — applied to the crossing).

**[design]** — the *freight-book mapping*. The sources land that a draw from the granary's store and a cart's load both book the way any op does (`the-granary.md` §4; `the-cart.md` §4a), and that a worked/drawn thing is spent one-way (`the-fallow.md` §2a). What is this doc's [design] is **that the sledge's freight is that same bookable draw, dragged across the winter** — the granary's order booked as a `sustain`-flagged held record over the haul's `worldPos` (the settled record's §2.2 semantics), never a phantom "sled" record off the stream. The cell-ownership rule (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.3) bounds the load's locality; the freight's honesty is that it is the store's real order, never the winter's own.

### (b) The crossing is honest at the settlement's doors — never a hidden sled-path

`the-toll.md` §4 establishes the held-door honesty: a settlement can only collect a toll where the door is actually held — **a toll demanded without the held door is a false op the field will not book**; the freight that crosses into the settlement crosses a held door and books its contribution there. **The sledge's crossing is honest against that same door:**

> **The sledge's freight crosses honest doors, never a hidden sled-path.** A sledge that enters a settlement's territory by a *hidden* route — a sled-path that bypasses the held gate, the harbor's quay, or the maintained road's edge, slipping the freight past the door the settlement holds from the between (`the-toll.md` §2) — is a hidden crossing the honesty forbids. The freight the sledge drags in crosses the same held door any arrival crosses, and books its passage the same way (`the-toll.md` §4; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1). **There is no sled-path that is not the settlement's own, no freight that sneaks past the held bound.**

**[design]** — the *honest-door mapping*. The sources land that a toll is only collectible where the door is held, and that a toll demanded without the held door is a false op (`the-toll.md` §4). What is this doc's [design] is **that the sledge's winter crossing composes honestly into the settlement's held doors** — the freight dragged across the white enters through the same gate, quay, or threshold-arch any crossing enters, never a hidden sled-path behind the bound. The accessibility floor (§5e) is what makes this load-bearing: a sledge whose route were hidden would be exactly the hidden sled-path the design refuses.
