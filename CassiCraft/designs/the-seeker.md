## 1. The Seeker, stated once

The instrument family reads the field's state and rate, the self, the sky, the night — but all those reads are *present-tense at a place*: the reader aims at what is here, the Weatherglass glances at what is here, the Clock times what is here, the Mirror reads yourself, the aurora reads the far field, the Lantern reads the ground underfoot. None answers the question a player who has *named* things actually asks: **where is that named thing now?** The map records what you drew and re-read (`the-map.md` §1), but it is a *record you keep with you*; it does not resignal when the thing moves or when you are far from your own page. The Seeker closes that hole:

> **The Seeker is the finder instrument — a carried device that reads *one* bound persistent-Π pattern and points toward it: a homing anchor you carry.** You bind it to a name (a structure, a companion, a seed-order, a named ruin, your family's hearth) and it reads that anchor's direction and distance through the already-published channels. Because a name IS a persistent-Π anchor (`the-name.md` §1), **the Seeker is the name's instrument face** — the instrument-family reading of naming: the name is the carried identity's *flag* (`the-name.md` §3.4), and the Seeker is the *pointer* that holds that flag's direction.

The design claim, stated once and kept:

> **A name is a real, held structure — a persistent-Π anchor the field re-quantizes around (`the-name.md` §1; `qi-computation.md` §5.2). The Seeker aggregates the reader/glass's read of that anchor's coherent well at the player's position into a single pointing: one bound name at a time, one direction and distance, honest against what the field actually holds.**

The composition the design leans on is the name's anchor's coherent well being *already legible*: the reader/Sense already renders a name's held `q`-core (a true name's structure reads as a real coherent well, `the-name.md` §6e), the Weatherglass already shows the local `q`/`ε²` trend (`field-instruments.md` §1.2). The Seeker does **not** invent a ranged sense of the named thing's location out of nothing — **it aggregates the already-published read of that anchor's well into a pointing**, the way the Far Mirror aggregates the sparse far-window publish into a chart (`field-instruments.md` §3.2). The difference is scope: the Far Mirror reads *any* present far place from its sparse record; the Seeker reads *one bound named anchor* and resolves it as a homing direction.

The honest once-flag: the *well* a seek reads is theory-ratified persistent-Π structure (with `reason-field.md` §6a's flag) where the name holds; the *binding-and-pointing* — which names can be bound to a seek, how the seek resolves the anchor's well into a direction, the range and discrimination dials — is **this doc's [design]**, probe-calibrated. The two are never blurred (§5).

## 2. The seeking read — what the Seeker reads, and how

### 2.1 The named anchor's coherent well is the read's subject

A true name holds because it sits in a **real coherent well** — a persistent-Π structure the field keeps holding because it is attractor-consistent (`the-name.md` §2.1; `qi-computation.md` §5.2: "persists as long as the ambient q remains above the threshold"). That well is not a designer's invention; it is the same structure the reader/Sense renders as a held `q`-core and the Weatherglass shows as an elevated local `q`, flat-low `ε²` (`the-name.md` §6e; `house-that-steers.md` §5e). **The Seeker reads that well.** Where the glass shows it glanceable-now, the Seeker *locates* it — it resolves the anchor's read as "that way, that far" from the player.

The mechanics are deliberately un-mysterious:

- **A trilinear sample at the player's position** of the published `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)` (the ≈ 6 MiB publish, `corpus-reconciliation.md`; the Weatherglass's own §1.4 pattern) — the seek is *one more read of the same channels*, not a ranged scan of every block.
- **A bound target**: the seek is bound to *one* named anchor (its persistent-Π phase-structure, the same structure the name binds in `the-name.md` §1). The read aggregates the local gradient of the already-published channels — over the seek's sampling window and the player's position — into a **direction** (which way the anchor's well's pull points) and a **distance** (how the well's strength at the player decays with range).
- **[design]** The exact aggregation — how the local gradient of the published channels over the seek's window and the player's position is turned into a *pointing* — is this doc's designed surface, probe-calibrated (§5a), exactly as the Weatherglass's lume mapping and the Clock's tempo mapping are their docs' designed surfaces over real channels (`field-instruments.md` §1.2; `the-clock.md` §2). What is engine-real is the well the read points at: a true name's held `q`-core is a real elevated structure in the published channels.

### 2.2 A consumer of the publish with a presentation idiom — the family rule, held

The Seeker obeys the instrument family rule exactly (`field-instruments.md` §2.1):

> **The seeking read is a consumer of the same publish with a presentation idiom — never a new channel.**

- **It reads only the published channels.** The bound anchor's well is read off `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)` at the player's position and over the seek's sampling window — the same channels the reader and Weatherglass already show. It adds no `seek-x` field to the publish, asks the engine to publish nothing new, and is read-only (`coherence-magic.md` §5.1's discipline — it never touches physics state).
- **It aggregates, never detects remotely.** The Seeker does not "sense" the named thing's coordinates through an invisible ranged feeler. It *aggregates the local read of the anchor's well* into a pointing — the same read the glass shows at the player, *pointed*. This is what keeps it a consumer and not a new reader: the information it presents is already in the publish, resolved as a direction.
- **One bound name at a time.** The seek carries a *single* bound target — you bind it, it points at that one anchor, and it stays bound until you re-bind it to another name. It does not chart a whole named-index; it is a homing anchor, not a catalog. (The map is the catalog's instrument — `the-map.md` §1 — and the seek is the pointer to *one* of its entries; §4 this doc.)

### 2.3 Range — the seek's reach is the publish's reach

The honest boundary of how far a seek points:

> **The seek reads the coherent well the field actually holds, at range, from the player's position — it never invents a target, and its range is bounded by how far the anchor's well is legible in the published channels at the player's position.**

Two consequences:
- **Range is a real, decaying read.** The anchor's well is strongest at its site and its pull is legible out to where the anchor's held `q`/`ε²` gradient is distinguishable from the surrounding field. **[design]/[probe]** How far a well's pull resolves into a direction at the player's position is a probe-calibrated dial — the same range question as the Far Mirror's sparse read (`field-instruments.md` §3.2), posed for a *near* well. The seek is honest about its reach: a well too faint at the player's distance reads as "no direction," not as a wrong direction.
- **A well is read at range, never invented at range.** The Seeker does not extrapolate "the anchor must be over there" from absence. If the field does not hold the well at the player — the anchor decayed, the player is too far, or the name was false (§3) — the seek reads *nothing*, honestly (§3.2). It never fabricates a pointing to fill the gap.

## 3. The honest bound — you cannot seek falsely

### 3.1 Seeking is the name's truthfulness, pointed

`the-name.md` §2 is the heart: **you cannot name falsely** — the field will not hold a lie. A name bound to a true structure sits in a real coherent well the field prefers to hold; a name bound to a falsehood is a Π configuration with nothing to stabilize it, and it sheds the way any unsupported coherence does. The Seeker inherits the bound exactly, turned from *holding* to *finding*:

> **A Seeker points only at what the field actually holds. You cannot seek falsely — the Seeker never invents a target; seeking is reading the coherent well the field actually holds, and where the well is not there, the seek reads its absence, not a direction.**

Just as the name's honesty is a *field consequence, not a rule* (`the-name.md` §2.1), so the seek's honesty is structural: the instrument can only present what the published channels hold. A player cannot point a Seeker at a place where the binding is false and "get" a fake location — there is no coherent well at the false binding for the seek to aggregate, so there is no direction. **The no-false-seeking is not a rule the game enforces; it is the read's only possible output.**

### 3.2 A dead anchor points nowhere — the decay read

`the-name.md` §2.2: a false name's anchor **decays** — the lie erodes, the "name" fails and un-binds; a true name that outlives its structure (a named house that falls to a fallen floor, `house-that-steers.md` §3.2 — the scar, unrecoverable) decays as the structure's well dissolves. The Seeker bound to *either* reads the field's truth:

| The bound anchor | What the field holds | What the Seeker reads |
|---|---|---|
| A **true name on a live/held structure** | a real coherent well — elevated `q`, flat-low `ε²` (`the-name.md` §6e) | **a direction and distance** toward it — the seek's honest pointing |
| A **false name** (named what is not there) | nothing to stabilize the anchor — the lie sheds as a `(1−q)` thinning (`the-name.md` §2.2) | **the decay, not a direction** — the seek reads the anchor's shed, and the pointer stays *still*: "this binding does not hold a well" |
| A **true name on a dead structure** (a named house fallen to a scar; a companion whose run ended, `field-npc-ai.md` §2.4) | the structure's well dissolved; only the deep-rung residue remains (the skeleton's ghost-halo, `house-that-steers.md` §3.1; the fossil, `field-archaeology.md` §2.1) | **the residue's read, not the live thing's** — a seek at a dead anchor points at the residue's dark q-locked core (`field-archaeology.md` §3.1) if one remains, and reads it honestly as *residue* (a record), not as the *live target* |

The through-line is the archaeology doc's honest determinism applied to finding (`field-archaeology.md` §1.2): **the Seeker does not lie, and it does not pretend.** A dead anchor points nowhere, or at its residue — the seek presents the field's truth about the named thing, and when the well is gone the pointer goes still rather than guess.

### 3.3 The misattribution guard — a different well must not read as the target's

The sharpest failure a seek could have is **misattribution**: the well of a *different* named thing reading as the target's direction. The name's truthfulness is, mechanically, the seeker's discrimination:

> **A seek distinguishes the target's well from every other well by the same phase-structure the name binds.** A name is a persistent-Π anchor — a specific Yang-Yin configuration at a rung-depth (`the-name.md` §1; `field-npc-ai.md` §2.1). Two named structures are *different* coherent wells: different phase-structure, different rung, different place. The seek binds to the target's *specific* phase-structure, and its aggregation reads *that* well's pull — not the pull of an adjacent named ruin or a passing companion's run.

The honest guard has two legs, both inherited from the name's own discrimination:

- **[design]** The binding is to the target's **phase-structure** (the same structure the name binds — its rung, its organization), not merely "a high-`q` thing over there." The seek's direction comes from *the bound well's* gradient, so a high-`q` forest or the settlement's hall does not read as the named ruin unless its well is the one bound. **[design]** The exact discrimination dial — how much phase-matching the aggregation requires before a well reads as the target's — is the instrument's [design], probe-calibrated against the family of named wells in a test locality.
- **[inherited]** The name's own truthfulness is the first guard. A *false* name decays (§3.1), so a misattributed read of "a well that is not the named thing" is structurally impossible for the *true* case — the seek can only point at what the name genuinely holds. The decay is the physical form of the guard, exactly as `the-name.md` §3.2 applies the misattribution guard to names (the misattribution in `shared-ledger.md` §3.3/§6e — never blame a scar on a player who didn't cut it — has its seeker-counterpart: a seek must never present *another* named thing's well as the target). Where two *true* names sit close, the phase-discrimination is the dial that keeps them separate — the same classifier honesty as `field-archaeology.md` §7 open-Q3 / `life-signal.md` §3, applied to which well is which.

### (b) PHASE-1-LEAN — a bound seek over the existing publish, with a Phase-1 slice

The full seek — companions, seed-orders, the family's joint hearth — is **LATER**, gated on the same frontier every identity claim is gated on: **the reason-field / persistent-Π frontier** (`reason-field.md` §6a — the meshless/Π probe: do the sites actually carry readable, long-lived Π structure?) plus each target's own system's gate (house-that-steers §3 for the ruin, seed-garden §6b for the seed, field-npc-ai/the-family for the companion and the joint anchor). A seek pointed at a companion's *run* or a family-member's *held anchor* is a seek over a persistent-Π *identity*, and it cannot stand before the field holds persistent-Π structure mechanically.

**But there is a PHASE-1-LEAN slice that needs nothing beyond the existing publish and the name's already-designed anchor:**

> **PHASE-1-LEAN — a bound seek over the existing publish.** A Seeker bound to a **name** — which, at Phase-1, is the name's anchor's coherent well read off the already-published `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)` at the player's position (the name's holding is theory-ratified; the *binding-and-pointing* is this doc's [design]) — is a **bounded consumer**: one trilinear sample at the player's position plus the aggregation over the seek's window, inside the ≈ 1–6 ms/tick server sample budget (`corpus-reconciliation.md`), on the Weatherglass's §1.4 cost profile. It is gated on the **named-anchor read** — that a name's held well is legible in the published channels (the reader/glass already show it, `the-name.md` §6e) — not on the persistent-Π frontier, because a *name* held by the field is the theory's own persistence, not the identity frontier's separate claim.

**The Phase-1 slice, stated: the ruin-seek over the living-terrain demo.** Bind the Seeker to a named ruin's skeleton (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2) in the Phase-1 coupled box (`chunk-field-quantization.md` §1.2: 192³ box, 64³ cells, `dt = 0.05`, the ≈ 6 MiB publish), read the local `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)` at the player's position, and measure whether the seek's pointing **tracks the skeleton's held well as the player walks toward, away from, and around it** — deterministic (same anchor, same field → same pointing), honest (a dead/anonymous site reads no false direction), and distinguishable (the skeleton's well does not read as the settlement's hall or another ruin). This mirrors the Weatherglass de-risking the family (`field-instruments.md` §6) and the map's Phase-1 aging probe (`the-map.md` §6b): **ship a bound seek over the name's already-legible well, prove the pointing is field-true, then let the LATER identity seeks (companions, the joint hearth, seed-orders) read through the same pointing.** The ruin-seek needs the named anchor (the theory's own persistence) and the skeleton (house-that-steers §3, designed) — neither is the identity frontier.

### (c) Determinism — same anchor, same field state → same pointing (a hard gate)

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `field-archaeology.md` §1.2; `life-signal.md` §6d), and the name inherits it (`the-name.md` §2.4 — a name is a pure function of the state it binds). The seek inherits both as a **hard gate**:

> **Same anchor, same field state → same pointing.** The seek's direction and distance are a deterministic function of (the bound anchor's well × the published channels at the player's position), mapped through the designed aggregation. A player who stands in the same spot facing the same anchor twice gets the same pointing, every time; a reloaded world reads the same homing direction from the same field. There is no seeded-RNG judgment about whether a seek "locks on" — the anchor either reads as a well at the player (and points deterministically) or does not (and points nowhere), reproducibly. **[design]** The aggregation thresholds are designed constants; the well they read is deterministic.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — a seek informs, never mints

`energy-harnessing.md` §6's cap (`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`), inherited through the instrument family (`the-lantern.md` §5d, `the-clock.md` §5d, `the-map.md` §6d), applies hard:

> **The Seeker informs, never mints.** It is a *read* (of the bound anchor's well at the player) cast as a pointing — it converts no coherence, sheds no `(1−q)`, writes nothing, and grants the player no find, no material, no coherence they did not earn by the named thing's own existence. **There is no find that generates:** the seeking read does not *produce* a found ruin, a recovered companion, or a harvested seed-order — it points at the well the field already holds, and the finding itself (walking to the skeleton, reaching the companion, retrieving the seed) is the field's own act, not the instrument's. A pointing that "summons" the named thing, or a seek that reads as a free locate-and-teleport, is a physics lie the cap forbids — exactly as the map cannot create a harvest (`the-map.md` §6d) and the Lantern's glow is a warning, never a reward (`the-lantern.md` §5d). The seek's only output is information and timing — *which way, how far* — costed as information, never as energy.

Three honest consequences:
1. **No "seek-to-extract."** Knowing where the skeleton is does not let you mine its stored coherence from range; the pointing is a read of the well, bounded by what the field already provides, with no write-back that could mint (`energy-harnessing.md` §6; `coherence-magic.md` §5.1).
2. **The seek does not move the field it reads.** A pointing does not pull the named thing toward the player, raise the local `q`, or change the anchor — it shows the well's direction; it never alters it. (A *re-authoring* player walking to the skeleton does the conservation-and-re-lock act of `house-that-steers.md` §3.3/§3.4 — that is the field's own act, not the seek's.)
3. **The seek's honesty is the no-mint bound's form.** Because there is no find that generates, there is also no *fake* find to sell: a player cannot use a seek to present a located-and-recovered thing the field does not hold. The no-false-seeking of §3 and the no-free-energy cap of §5d are the same honest boundary, viewed from the read and the economy.

### (e) Accessibility — the seek is an enhancement, never hidden-only

The seek's pointing must remain a layer of the existing field-reading surfaces, per the family rule (`field-instruments.md` §2.1) and `field-music.md` §6:

> **The seek is an enhancement — the same well is readable from the reader/glass, never hidden-only.** Everything the Seeker points at — the named anchor's held well, its direction, its distance, its decay — is readable, more laboriously, from the reader overlay (`coherence-magic.md` §2, the same channels) and the Weatherglass (`field-instruments.md` §1.2, the same channels glanceable). A player who never carries a Seeker loses *nothing informational*: they can still reach the reader-computed direction of a named well by walking with the reader, or learn the anchor's well is gone from the glass's flat-read. The Seeker is the *pointed* form of the same shared publish, never a second information source and never a *requirement* — because the same truth is always on the reader. **[design]** The binding-and-pointing is the designed idiom; the well it points at is the field's own, never hidden.

## Open questions

1. **The binding-and-pointing calibration (§2/§3).** What, numerically, turns the local gradient of the published channels over the seek's window and the player's position into a *direction-and-distance* that tracks a bound well as the player walks? The Phase-1 ruin-seek slice is the pre-registered probe that answers it; a negative (a name's well too weak at range to aggregate into a stable pointing) would push the seek toward coarse region-band "that general direction" reads rather than per-well direction. **[design]/[probe]**
2. **The decay read's presentation (§3.2).** When the seek is bound to a decaying or dead anchor, does a still read present as "this binding holds no well" (an honest absence), or should the seek also distinguish a *false* name's shed (a `(1−q)` thinning) from a *dead true* name's residue (a dark q-locked core, `house-that-steers.md` §3.1) — presenting "this well decayed" vs "this well is residue"? The name's decay honesty (`the-name.md` §2.2) suggests the distinction; the pointer's bare still-ness may be truer to a carried device. The presentation of the decay read is **[design]**, inheriting archaeology's residue-vs-live classifier.
3. **The discrimination dial's noise-floor honesty (§3.3).** When the target's well sits close to another true named well, the aggregation must read the target's and not the neighbor's. Is the target's phase-structure distinguishable from another name's well at the noise floor, or does the seek need an explicit "ambiguous — two wells this way" read rather than a false lock? Same classifier honesty as `field-archaeology.md` §7 open-Q3 / `life-signal.md` §3; the dial is **[design]/[probe]**. The seek must never present a best-guess between two wells as the target.
4. **The seek's reach and the map's remembered sites (§2.3/§4).** When the player is too far from the anchor's well for a stable pointing, does the seek hand off to the map's drawn-and-re-read record of the named site (`the-map.md` §2 — a charted site's line reads maintained or stale) — "the well is out of range, but your map remembers this way" — or is the seek strictly honest "no direction"? The complement is designed (the seek points, the map records and remembers); the hand-off dial is **[design]**, Phase-1.5.
5. **The binding's scope — what counts as a bound seek target (§4).** The seek binds to *names* — the findable things named in §4. But can a player bind a seek to a *region*, a *machine*, a *dormant seed-order* not yet named, or a *pocket-cosmos* — the same [design] scope question `the-name.md` open-Q1 raises for naming? The honest rule is the same: a seek binds only what the field can be said to hold as a persistent, legible well (§2.3) — the same structures a name can truthfully bind. The exact scope is **[design]** against the plausible Π-anchor ground.
