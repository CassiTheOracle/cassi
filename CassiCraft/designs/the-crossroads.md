# The Crossroads: The Meeting of Ways — the Way's Place Where the Road Branches, the Legible Choice-Point

**Question under design:** the corpus designs the way's places one by one. The causeway is the **built way over the hiding water** ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md) — the raised, deliberate, phase-kept deck a settlement holds *above* the marsh, the crossing that takes the marsh's secrecy back); the ford is the **river's natural shallow** ([`the-ford.md`](./the-ford.md) — the crossing the water makes safe of itself, the river's door, the corpus's natural-crossing primitive); the toll is the **border economy** ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) — the charge at the held door, what the outside pays to enter it, the tithe's outward edge); the caravan is the **long-train** ([`the-caravan.md`](./the-caravan.md) — the organized trading convoy, the collective freight of many moving as one guarded order on the road); the highway is the **maintained line** ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) — a steered coherence route the field prefers to route through). The corpus names the bridge among the causeway's built-crossing kin — "no causeway, no raised boardwalk, no bridge" ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md) §1, the built way's opening) — but **no `the-bridge.md` exists on disk**, checked against the design index, exactly as `the-camp.md` is absent from the causeway (its Open-Q6 flags that absence honestly). Every way's-place the corpus draws is a place *on* a way: the raised crossing, the natural shallow, the border's door, the long-train's route. **But no document designs the place where the ways themselves meet** — no crossroads, no point where two or more paths converge into a legible branch, no design for the way's place where the road itself divides. This document is that design: **the Crossroads — the meeting of ways, the way's place where the road branches, the legible choice-point the way's network draws.**

The Crossroads fills the one way's-place the corpus's way-stack has never drawn. The causeway, the ford, the toll, and the caravan each design a *place on a way* — a crossing, a door, a train. The map designs the *record* of ways ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) — a player-drawn chart whose ink reads `q` at drawn sites and ages as the field moves, kept honest only by re-reading and redrawing). None designs the *meeting* of ways itself: **the branch-point, where a held line forks, or a line meets a crossing, and the way's network becomes a legible choice.** Each of the way-docs even *presupposes* the meeting without drawing it — the highway's "road network" is a set of routes that meet ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §2), the causeway is a marsh-crossing *segment* of that network ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md) §6), the caravan rides a *route* through it ([`the-caravan.md`](./the-caravan.md) §2.4) — yet no document designs the point where the routes share, the place where the road branches. **This document is that design: the Crossroads, the meeting of ways, the legible choice-point the way's network draws.**

In Minecraft a crossroads is where the game almost inevitably invents a speaker — a sign that "knows," a fork that "favors," a fate that "picks you." The corpus's field is indifferent and deterministic, and the meeting-of-ways is precisely where the temptation to imagine a hidden will runs highest. So the Crossroads is designed *against* that speaker as much as *for* the meeting: it is the place where the traveler stands, reads the ways honestly, and chooses — with no hidden verdict, no seeded fortune, no field that prefers. The design's whole burden is to hold that honesty at the exact place the corpus's philosophy would most tempt one to abandon it.

> **The crossroads does not choose for you.** The field is indifferent, deterministic, legible ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2 — the field cannot lie the way a witness can). The meeting of ways is a *place*, not a judge, not an oracle, not a fate. The choice at the crossroads is the player's, read through the field-true instruments; a "crossroads that decided" would be a false reading the field's indifference sheds. **The crossroads is where the path's legibility meets — not where a hidden will speaks.**

## 1. The Crossroads, stated once

The corpus's way-stack draws every place *on* a way, but never the place *between* ways. The causeway is the **built way over the hiding water** — a house-that-steers structure holding a clean high-`q` deck above the marsh, raised, deliberate, phase-kept ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md) §1). The ford is the **river's natural shallow** — the crossing the water makes safe of itself, the river's door, distinct from the causeway's built-raised and carrying none of a raised deck's safety ([`the-ford.md`](./the-ford.md) §1). The toll is the **border economy** — the designed charge a settlement levies at its deliberate doors on what enters from the between, honest because a toll is only collectible where the door is held ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §1). The caravan is the **long-train** — the organized trading convoy, the collective freight of many moving as one guarded order on the road ([`the-caravan.md`](./the-caravan.md) §1). The highway is the **maintained line** — a steered coherence route that holds `q` up along a path, a road the field prefers to route through, which dies without maintenance ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §1/§4). The map is the **drawn way-record** — a player-drawn chart whose ink reads `q` at drawn sites and ages as the field moves, kept honest only by re-reading and redrawing ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §1). **Each is a place on a way, or a record of ways. None is the place where ways meet.**

That is the gap the Crossroads fills. Where two or more of the way's places converge — a causeway's held deck meeting a caravan's route, a ford's shallow crossing met by a highway's maintained line — the way's network draws a branch: a point where the road itself divides, and the diverging ways read on the instruments as a legible fork.

The Crossroads fills that void, stated once and kept:

> **The Crossroads is the meeting of ways — the way's place where the road branches, the legible choice-point.** It is the point where two or more ways — a held line and a crossing, a route and a door — converge, and the way's network draws a legible fork: each branch's directed read (`∇(g·Φ)` lean, `q` lume, `(1−q)` cost) presented on the instruments at the meeting's point. It is the point of the path's choosing, where the path's legibility meets. **And it does not choose for you:** the field is indifferent, deterministic, legible ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2); the meeting of ways is a *place*, not a judge, not an oracle, not a fate. The choice at the crossroads is the player's, read through the field-true instruments; a "crossroads that decided" would be a false reading the field's indifference sheds.

**Why this is the corpus's missing meeting-of-ways, honestly.** Every way's-place the corpus builds is a *place on a single way*: the causeway raises one line over one water, the ford crosses one river at one shallow, the toll charges one held door, the caravan rides one route. The map records ways but does not *branch* them — it draws what the field held where you drew, and reads that record's aging ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §2/§3), not a fork of live ways. And the corpus's own way-network framing presupposes the meeting without designing it: the causeway is a marsh-crossing highway segment, a held `q`-line ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md) §7 the [design] line, via `coherence-highway.md` §4 the maintained line); the highway is a network of maintained lines ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §2 — the road network as the window's circulation); a network of lines is a set of routes that *meet*. **But no document designs the point where they meet.** What none designs is **the way-network's branch-point — the point where two or more ways meet and the road itself divides into a legible choice.**

The gap is not a small omission; it is the way-stack's missing *connection*. Every "route," "segment," and "network" the way-docs name is a line through the field, and a lived network of lines is a set of choices — the traveler stands where ways meet and moves on. Minecraft makes that meeting a sign-posted fork; the corpus's way-stack makes the lines but leaves the fork to the open field, un-designed. **The Crossroads is the design that finally draws the fork the network already implies — the legible choice-point where the ways' own organization converges.** It is honest about what it adds: no new way, no new channel, no new physics — only the *meeting* made a designed, legible place. The corpus has the ways; it has the record of ways; it has the presupposed network; it has never designed the *meeting* of ways, the place where the path's legibility concentrates into a fork. **The Crossroads is that missing meeting: the way's place where the road branches, the legible choice-point the way's network draws.**

**The honest status, once.** Every piece the Crossroads composes is its source doc's landed piece — the causeway's held way ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md) §1/§2), the ford's crossing ([`the-ford.md`](./the-ford.md) §1/§2), the toll's held door ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §1/§4), the caravan's long-train ([`the-caravan.md`](./the-caravan.md) §1/§2), the highway's maintained line ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §1/§4), the map's drawn record ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §1/§2), the field's indifference ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2), the waste law and cap ([`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md) §2/§6), and the family rule ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1). **The crossroads's composition — that the ways' meeting reads as a legible branch, that the fork is the player's to choose, that a "crossroads that decided" is a false read — is this doc's [design]** over that landed stack, probe-calibrated. No claim here pretends the engine "has a crossroads" or "decides a fork"; the engine publishes the channels, and the Crossroads is the designed reading that holds the ways' meeting as a legible choice-point — a consumer of the publish exactly as the causeway, the ford, and the highway are.

**The distinction from the way-stack, stated once.** Every way's-place the corpus builds is a place *on* a way; the crossroads is the place *between* ways where the road branches. The line against each sibling is sharp and honest:

**What the crossroads is against — the Minecraft archetype, honestly.** In Minecraft a crossroads is a sign-post of potential agency: the place where an RPG might offer a "choice," where a quest-giver might stand, where a game might whisper a hidden correct way. The corpus's crossroads answers that archetype *against itself*. There is no quest at the meeting, no speaker, no correct answer the field knows. The meeting's whole power is that it *refuses* the archetype's promise of a guiding voice while keeping its most human truth: **this is where you stand and choose, and the choice is real, and it is yours.** A traveler at the corpus's crossroads does not ask the field which way to go (there is no field to ask); they read the ways' honest present states and choose with that knowledge or without it. The meeting is not diminished by this — it is *deepened*: a choice made with honest legibility and no hidden guide is the most the corpus's indifferent, legible world can offer, and it is exactly what the way-stack has been pointing toward all along.

**The [design] over it — the meeting is a branch-point of these:**

> **The Crossroads is the way-network's branch-point — a place where a held high-`q` line forks, or a line meets a crossing, and the diverging ways read together.** Where two-or-more maintained lines converge ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §1), or a held way meets a natural crossing ([`the-ford.md`](./the-ford.md) §1), the road's network draws a branch: a point where the path itself divides. The *meeting's* legs are the ways' own held reads (each branch's `q`, `ε²`-derived `(1−q)`, and `∇(g·Φ)` from the publish), presented together at the point. It is the way's *network* doing, in composition, what no single way does alone.

The composition is honest in a way worth stating plainly: the crossroads does not *add* any leg. Every direction the fork shows is a real way's real reading at the meeting — the causeway's deck holds `q` in its direction because the deck is there and held; the ford's shallow softens its `∇(g·Φ)` because the water is shallow and slow there; the held line carries its route because the settlement maintains it. **The crossroads is a *presentation of what is already true at the point* — a composition of the ways' own real reads — and it cannot present a way that is not there.** A fork with a leg the field does not hold is not a hidden truth; it is an empty claim, the same unsupported bump the field sheds ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2). This is the first and deepest honesty of the design: the crossroads's legs are the ways' own, because the crossroads is built out of them.

The composition's honesty extends to *which* ways a meeting can even show. A crossroads is only as legible as the ways that are actually there at the point, held and live:

- A **meeting of two maintained lines** reads the sharpest fork — two held `q`-lines with two distinct `∇(g·Φ)` points and two `q` lumes; this is the branch's core form ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §1).
- A **line meeting a crossing** reads a fork with a *crossing leg* — the held line's lean plus the shallow's softened carry, the crossing read alongside the route ([`the-ford.md`](./the-ford.md) §1/§2).
- A **route passing a held door** reads those two ways plus, where the settlement holds a door, the toll's mark — but the meeting's *feet* are the ways, and the door is the settlement's addition, not the meeting's ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §2/§4).
- A **caravan passing through** adds its moving band as a way read among the branch's, the train's route never captured or diverted by the meeting ([`the-caravan.md`](./the-caravan.md) §1/§2d).

Each leg is a real way's read; no leg is invented. **This is what makes the crossroads honest by construction: its every presented direction corresponds to a way that is genuinely at the point, held and live, and its every presented channel is the field's own published `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)`/`(1−q)` at that way's read.**

**[design]** — the *meeting's form*. The ways (the causeway's deck, the ford's shallow, the highway's maintained line) are landed; **that their convergence *is* a crossroads — a branch-point whose diverging legs read together at one point, and whose branch's geometry (how many legs, how they fork) is a designed read over the ways' real `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)` at the meeting — is this doc's [design]** over the way-stack, probe-calibrated. The *branch's read* — how a fork's legs resolve into legible directions at the point — is measured on the Phase-1 slice (§5b), exactly as the causeway's deck-hold probe and the ford's shallow-read pre-register (§8 / §6 their docs).

### (b) The branch — where the road itself divides

**The landed piece.** The highway designs a road as a **steered coherence route** — a held high-`q` line the field prefers to route through ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §1). A road is a maintained line: it holds `q` up along a path, and it dies without maintenance (§4 — an unmaintained road is a fallen floor, no longer a route). The highway's own framing is a *network* — "the road network is the window's circulation" (§2), a set of maintained lines, and a network is a set of routes that meet. The causeway is explicitly a marsh-crossing *segment* of that network ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §6b, the ride-downhill slice the causeway's companion-to names for "the causeway as a marsh-crossing highway segment"; `the-causeway.md` §7 the [design] line); the caravan rides a *route* the atlas maps, crossing the between and the desert along a maintained path ([`the-caravan.md`](./the-caravan.md) §2.4, via `the-atlas-of-windows.md` §2). A network of maintained lines, a carriage of routes, a road segment — each presupposes the meeting, the place where routes share a point.

**The [design] over it — the branch is where the line's legs divide:**

> **The crossroads's branch is where a held line's legs divide — two-or-more maintained routes meeting at a point, each leg a real, readable `q`-line in its own direction.** The road's network is not a single line; it is a set of steered routes, and wherever two-or-more of them meet, the field at that point carries *both* directions' `q`-organization ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §1 — a road is a held high-`q` line; two roads meeting is two held `q`-lines at a common point). **The branch is that common point read as a fork** — each leg's `q` lume and `∇(g·Φ)` lean presented together, so a player reads "the held line goes that way; the other held line goes this way; the crossing is down that third lean." **The road's legs are the field's own held organization; the branch is the point where the organization forks, and the player reads the fork.**

The branch's mechanics are the ways' own, composed: a maintained line at the meeting reads its direction through its `∇(g·Φ)` lean (the gradient points toward the matter the road routes toward — `energy-harnessing.md` §1.1) and its `q` lume (how strongly the line holds its organization — `field-instruments.md` §1.2); a second maintained line at the same point reads its own direction the same way; a crossing's shallow reads its own softened carry. **The fork is the *sum* of those real directional reads at a common point — no new channel, no invented direction, only the ways' own organization composed into a branch.** This is what makes it a *legible* choice rather than a bare crossing of routes: the ways at the meeting are not merely adjacent, they *read together*, their directions presented at once as a fork.

**The legs' physical character, honestly.** Each leg is not an abstraction; it is the field's real organization at a real pointing. A maintained line's `∇(g·Φ)` leans along the line because the river law steers bodies down-gradient toward the line's matter — `a = −G_N·(π/ρ)·∇(g·Φ)`, the ≈ 40 ns/entity river-law steer (`corpus-reconciliation.md`) — so a player reading a line's lean is reading which way the field would carry a body along it ([`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md) §1.1 — the gradient points toward matter and steepens toward organized mass). A line's `q` lume reads how strongly that organization holds ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.2 — lume is the health of the local φ-lock). A crossing's softened carry reads the shallow's risen bed and soft current ([`the-ford.md`](./the-ford.md) §2 — where the bed rises, the liquid regime's flow thins). **None of these is a sign; each is the field's own physical state read at the meeting, and the fork is their honest composition.** When the ways die — a line's `q` falls with un-maintenance ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §4 — a road dies without maintenance; a thin tide drains a line's `q`, §4.1), a crossing smears with the risen river — the leg's read fades with the field's own movement, and the fork reads honestly as a meeting of fallen ways (open-Q4).

**[design]** — the *branch's form*. The highway lands that a road is a held `q`-line; what is this doc's [design] is **that the *meeting* of two-or-more held lines reads as a legible fork — a branch-point whose diverging legs are presented together as a choice** — and *how* the fork resolves (how distinct the legs' directions read at the point, how far apart a "fork" stays a fork before it is two separate ways) is a probe-calibrated dial over the ways' real reads. Probe-calibrated: the Phase-1 slice (§5b) measures whether a legible branch actually reads at the point where two maintained `q`-lines meet, or whether the two lines' organization merges too tightly to fork. Both outcomes are honest, and the doc states what a merged read means (open-Q1).

### (c) The read at the meeting — the fork on the instruments

The crossroads is legible because it reads on the instruments, per the family rule ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1 — **every instrument is a consumer of the same publish with a presentation idiom, never a new channel**). The crossroads introduces no new instrument and no new channel; it is the ways' own reads, composed at their meeting. Each branch is the Weatherglass's own four forms ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.2), read at the meeting's point and spread as a direction:

> **The fork is the ways' directed reads, presented together at the meeting's point.** Each branch shows its `∇(g·Φ)` lean (the bauble tilting toward the downhill of the gradient — which way the field carries a body), its `q` lume (how strongly the way holds coherence), and its `(1−q)` cost tint (how wasteful the way's field runs — [`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md) §2). At a crossroads, the player reads *N* lean-and-lume-and-tint sets, one per branch, spread as a fork — the same four forms, laid out for the meeting's ways. **The read is a branch on the instruments: each way's present directed state, read together, is the legible fork.** The map draws it ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §1 — a crossroads is where the drawn ways meet); the Weatherglass and reader present it live.

The composition's cost is the cost of any read: each branch is a sample at the meeting's point — the sample-at-position pattern of [`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.4 — repeated per branch, inside the ≈ 1–6 ms/tick server sample budget on the ≈ 6 MiB publish (`corpus-reconciliation.md`). A two-way fork is two trilinear samples; a three-way, three. **The read is bounded by the same per-sample cost the Weatherglass and the lantern pay, never more** — no separate "crossroads channel," no hidden budget, no new instrument to build. This is the design's discipline held mechanically: the fork is a *composition of existing reads at a point*, and it costs exactly what reading that point costs, N times.

**[design]** — the *fork's presentation*. The channels (each branch's `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)`/`(1−q)`) are engine-real; **that the ways' reads *compose* into a legible fork at the meeting — a designed presentation of N directions as a choice-point, over the same real channels every instrument reads — is this doc's [design]**, exactly as the map's staleness read is a [design] presentation over the real channel mismatch ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §6a). A player who never "uses a crossroads overlay" loses nothing informational: each branch's read is the same channel the Weatherglass and reader already show, read at the same point they already sample. The composition is an *idiom over real channels*, never a hidden read.

### (d) A caravan through the meeting — the long-train read, not diverted

The caravan is the crossroads's most *kinetic* leg, and it deserves its own honest note. The long-train is an organized order in motion — a moving band of carts, guards, and animals riding a route across the between or the desert ([`the-caravan.md`](./the-caravan.md) §1). When a caravan passes through a crossroads, the meeting does not *act on* it:

> **A caravan passes through a crossroads as a moving order-band; the meeting reads it as one way among the branch's — it does not divert the train, does not stop it, does not redirect it.** The caravan's route is already chosen (the atlas's mapped crossings, `the-caravan.md` §2.4); the crossroads is a point on that route where other ways happen to meet. The meeting's read shows the train's moving band alongside the branch's other legs — a passage of `q`/ε² along its route — but nothing at the meeting reaches out to capture or steer it. The caravan's guards' watch is the train's own (the vigil's discipline on the road, `the-caravan.md` §2.3), not added by the meeting; its freight's honesty is its own booked passage (`the-caravan.md` §4), not the crossroads's. **The crossroads is a place the long-train passes through, exactly as it passes any route-point; the meeting only reads the train as one of its ways.**

This matters because it draws the line between a crossroads and a *toll-door* at its sharpest: a toll is a held door that *charges* a crossing ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §1/§2 — a charge at the door on what enters); a crossroads is a meeting that *reads* the ways, diverts nothing, charges nothing. **A caravan passing a crossroads books no toll unless a held door sits there; the meeting itself is a read, never a charge.** This is the caravan leg's honesty: the crossroads composes the train as a moving order-band read alongside the other ways, and no more.

### (e) The player's choice — where the legibility meets, the choosing is the player's

The crossroads's whole design turns on one honesty, and it is the load-bearing thread of §3: **the meeting is legible, and the choice is the player's.** The field is indifferent and deterministic ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2); the crossroads reads the ways' present state and nothing more. There is no direction the field "wants" you to take that the read invents; there is no verdict, no voice, no fate. **The player reads the fork and chooses** — through the same instruments the walk, the causeway, and the map use, at the same cost a read always costs.

The choice, honestly framed, is a *field act among field acts*: the walk is a read-and-a-step (the stride reads the gradient at foot-pace, `the-ford.md` §2, via `the-walk.md`); the map is a read-and-a-draw (ink that reads `q` at drawn sites, kept true by re-reading, `the-map.md` §3); the crossroads is a read-and-a-choice (the fork's directions read, one committed to). In each, the field presents its honest present state and the player acts. **The crossroads does not make the choice a mystery or a gift — it makes it a legible decision.** The player at a meeting reads which way holds, which way runs thin and wasteful, which way the gradient carries a body, which way the map's drawn record and its aging point ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §2), and chooses. The choosing is the player's because the field's indifference leaves it to them; the *legibility* is the crossroads's gift — not a hidden will, but the honest present states of the ways, all readable at once.

### (f) The branch's legibility calibration — how distinct a fork must be to be a choice

The composition's one mechanical risk is that the meeting's legs are not *distinct enough* to read as a choice. This is the branch's legibility calibration, and it is a designed dial over the ways' real reads, exactly as the causeway's deck-hold and the ford's shallow-read are:

> **[design] A fork is legible when the ways' directed reads are distinct enough at the meeting's point that the player can tell the ways apart as directions — when the diverging `∇(g·Φ)` leans and `q` lumes resolve into a real branch rather than a smear.** The distinction is a designed threshold over the ways' real channels: how far apart two legs' leans must be (in the gradient's direction at the meeting), how strong each leg's `q` lume must read (above the noise floor, `q ~ 1e-3…1e-1`, `corpus-reconciliation.md`), for the fork to present as *two ways* rather than one blurred band. Below the threshold, the meeting is a bare crossing of routes — honest, present, but not a legible choice; above it, the fork reads and the player chooses.

The calibration is [design]-dial and probe-gated, and its honest failure mode is named: **a fork that does not calibrate is a meeting that reads as a smear, and the crossroads *is not* that smear.** The design does not force a weak meeting to read as a choice; it reads the honest legibility of the ways and, where the ways are not distinct enough, presents a bare crossing — the same legitimate fallback the causeway and the ford pre-register for their deck-hold and shallow-read (§`the-causeway.md` §8; `the-ford.md` §6). The Phase-1 slice (§5b) is the pre-registered probe for this calibration: it measures whether a bounded meeting of two held `q`-bands reads as a distinct fork, and both outcomes are honest, with the doc stating what a weak fork means (open-Q1).

**The honest cost book, stated once.** The crossroads's cost is the cost of its reads, and nothing more: each branch is a trilinear sample at the meeting's point (the sample-at-position pattern, [`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.4) inside the ≈ 1–6 ms/tick budget (`corpus-reconciliation.md`). There is no standing cost to *being* a crossroads — the meeting is where the ways are, and their holds are the ways' own upkeep (the causeway's deck is a standing draw, `the-causeway.md` §7a via `energy-harnessing.md` §4.4; the road dies without maintenance, `coherence-highway.md` §4). **The crossroads converts nothing and holds nothing of its own**; it is a read at a point where ways already are, and its whole cost is N samples. This is the no-free-energy cap's honest face at the meeting: the choice's legibility is bought at the price of the read, never more, never a gain (§4.3/§5d).

### (g) The crossroads on the map — a meeting drawn, and the strata it leaves

The crossroads composes the map's drawn record as the fork's living face, and it inherits the map's honest aging as the meeting's own history. Where a player draws a road, the ink binds to the published `q` at the drawn sites and ages as the field moves ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §1/§2); where two drawn ways meet, the page shows a *drawn crossroads* — the choice-point the player charted, held true only by re-reading ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §3, cartography as stewardship):

> **[design] The crossroads is the map's live branch — the place where the player's drawn ways meet, kept true by re-reading the meeting's legs.** A player who charts the ways and arrives at a meeting reads the fork live (the Weatherglass's and reader's present reads) and, having chosen, *draws which way they took* — and the drawn crossroad's legs age on the page as the field moves, the same honest staleness every mapped road carries ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §2). The map does not decide the fork; it records the meeting and the player's chosen leg, and its aging is the honest record of how the ways have since moved. **A crossroads a player has charted and returned to becomes a strata-band** ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §4.1 — the strata-map, the drawn history of the player's own encounters): each visit, each re-read, each chosen leg is a layer in the page's record of that meeting, the personal archaeology of where the roads used to meet and which way you took.

This composition is [design] over the map's landed instrument, and it stays honest in the map's own register: the map *records*, never mints ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §6d), and the crossroads's drawn face is a record of the meeting and the chosen leg — information, never energy, never a hidden direction. **A crossroads has no hidden truth the map could reveal**; it is a meeting of real ways, and the map's honest aging is the record of that meeting's life and the player's choices at it. The strata-band of a crossroads is the design's answer to the Minecraft "marker at the fork" — not a sign that knows the way, but the player's own drawn record of a meeting they chose at, aged honestly by the field's movement.

### (h) The crossroads through the tide — a meeting's legibility breathes

A way's legibility is not constant; the field's organization breathes with the tide, and a crossroads's legs breathe with it. The tide is the regional `q` movement between harvest (near-lossless, `q` high) and thin (toward the noise floor, wasteful) ([`tide-of-the-attractor.md`](./tide-of-the-attractor.md) §2, skimmed via the map's seasonal honesty — a map drawn at harvest is stale at thin), and the highway's line lives and dies on it ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §4.1 — a thin tide drains a line's `q`):

> **[design] A crossroads's legibility breathes with the tide — a meeting legible at harvest can read as a smeared crossing at thin.** Each branch's `q` lume and `(1−q)` cost track the field's regional state ([`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md) §2 — the `(1−q)` waste law scales with local `q`): at harvest, the held ways hold high and the fork reads bright and distinct; at thin, the same ways run toward the noise floor, their lumes dim, and the fork fades toward a bare crossing. This is not a defect; it is the honest present read ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §2 — the crossing is only ever as legible as the field's present state, and a thin-tide meeting is honestly thin). **The crossroads is the same place, differently legible across the tide, and its legibility is the field's own movement made readable at the meeting.**

This is the seasonal face of the no-oracle honesty: a crossroads that stayed "obviously a fork" at thin would be presenting a legibility the field does not hold (a false lume the field sheds, [`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2). The honest register is a meeting whose legs breathe with the tide — bright and distinct when the ways hold, dim and smeared when they thin — so a player who knows a crossroads knows it *seasonally*, the way they know any field place, and chooses with that knowledge. **[design]** The tide's effect on the fork's calibration (how a harvest-lume dims toward a thin-smeared read) inherits the tide's own Phase-1.5 probe gates ([`tide-of-the-attractor.md`](./tide-of-the-attractor.md) §5a), and is flagged [design]/[probe], never asserted as a fixed brightness.

### (i) The settlement's meeting — a crossroads a settlement reads and, only where it holds a door, charges

The crossroads composes the way-places a settlement *owns* — the causeway it holds, the ford it reads, the toll it charges — but the meeting itself is the settlement's *locus*, not its property. This is the line between a settlement's crossroads and its door, drawn once:

> **[design] The crossroads is the settlement's way-locus — where its roads and the field's crossings meet, a place the settlement reads, charts, and stewards, but does not own.** A settlement that holds ways at a meeting does not *possess* the crossroads; the meeting is the field's own open plain at the branch (§4.1). What the settlement holds are its ways — the causeway's deck it maintains ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md) §7a, the standing-draw boundary), the line it keeps up ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §4) — and what it can do at the meeting is *read* them (the fork's present state), *chart* them (the map's drawn record, kept true by re-reading, [`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §3), and, only where it *also* holds a deliberate door, *charge* a crossing (the toll's held-door honesty, [`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §4). **The meeting itself books nothing and belongs to no one; a settlement's claim on it is only ever the ways it actually holds there.**

The settlement-locus is the social face of the design's honesty. A settlement *can* build a deliberate door at a crossroads — a gate, a checked crossing, a journey's charge — and where it does, the toll rides that door exactly as it rides any held door ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §2/§4). But the *meeting* that the door sits at is not the charge: the crossroads remains a legible, unbounded meeting, and the settlement's charge is the door's, not the meeting's. **A settlement that tried to "charge the crossroads itself" would be demanding a toll at a door it had not held — a false op the field would not book** ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §4; open-Q3 this doc). And a settlement that *read* its meeting honestly would chart it, keep its ways up, and let the fork's legibility stand — the same stewardship the map designs ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §3), applied to the meeting the settlement lives at. This is the crossroads's social register: **a place the settlement inhabits, reads, and stewards — never one it owns, and never one that speaks for it.**

### (j) The walk's legible branch — the fork as the natural continuation of the field's read-and-step craft

The crossroads is not a separate "system" the player engages; it is the *natural* extension of the corpus's most basic field act — the walk's read-and-step. The walk is the un-roaded crossing whose craft reads the terrain at foot-pace ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §1/§2, via `the-ford.md` §2 — the stride that steps with the lean on the gradient). That craft is exactly the fork's reading, raised to a meeting:

> **[design] Choosing at a crossroads is the walk's craft at a meeting of ways — the same read-and-step, made a decision among the branches.** Where the walk reads a gradient and steps, the crossroads reads the ways' directions and chooses among them. The walker's craft — "which way holds, which way is cheap, which way does the field carry a body" ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §2, via `the-ford.md`) — is the *same* read the fork shows, composed for a meeting instead of a single crossing. A player who can walk the field can choose at a crossroads; the fork is the walk's legibility, concentrated at the point where ways meet. **This is what makes the crossroads accessible to the ordinary traveler: it asks no special knowledge, only the read-and-step every walker already practices.**

This is the deepest tie to the map and the walk together. The map is "the instrument of a player who has stayed in one place" ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §7 — the recording instrument of the continued inhabitant); the crossroads is the *live* meeting such a player chooses at, and the walk is the craft they choose with. **A player who has walked a region, charted it, and learned its ways brings that earned knowledge to the fork** — they do not need a hidden guide because they *have* the honest legibility of the ways, read by their own craft and their own record ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §3, cartography as stewardship). The crossroads is the place where the continued inhabitant's accumulated field-knowledge becomes a *decision* — read, walked, chosen, and drawn — without once asking the field to speak.

## 3. The load-bearing design — the crossroads as the legible choice-point

The single most load-bearing statement of this doc, carried from the way-stack's own honesty and the field's indifference, and never blurred:

> **The crossroads is where the path's legibility meets — the legible choice-point the way's network draws. The choice at the crossroads is the player's, read through the field-true instruments; a "crossroads that decided" would be a false reading the field's indifference sheds.**

This is the design's spine, in three held legs:

**(1) The meeting is a *place*, not a speaker.** The crossroads does not choose. The field is indifferent, deterministic, legible ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2 — the field cannot lie the way a witness can; it only *holds* what is true and *sheds* what is not). A crossroads is the way's place where the road branches; it is a location in the field, not an agent. It has no will that selects a destination, no hidden preference among the ways it meets. **The middle of the meeting is a place in the field — the point where N ways' `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)` are all present, read together — and the field at that point holds every direction's organization at once because they all genuinely meet there.** The field does not pick; it *is*. This is the same indifference the dispute uses to judge claims ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2 — a claim is checked against what the field actually holds), and the crossroads inherits it in the *opposite* direction: where the dispute asks *what is held*, the crossroads asks *what is here*, and the answer is *all the ways*, held together because they all honestly meet.

**(2) The choice is the player's — read through the field-true instruments.** Because the meeting is a place and not a speaker, the choosing is the player's act, and it is done the way every field act is done: by reading the field-true instruments and choosing. The player reads the fork — which way holds higher `q`, which way leans downhill, which way runs cleaner, which way the map's drawn record and its aging suggest ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §2) — and chooses. The choice is *real* (it commits the player to a way) and it is *the player's* (no secret verdict, no hidden roll, no field whisper told them which was "right"). **The crossroads makes the choice legible; it does not make the choice.** The choice's weight is the player's, and the meeting's honesty is that it never pretends otherwise.

**(3) A "crossroads that decided" is a false read the field sheds.** This is the negative face, and it is as load-bearing as the positive. The moment the design lets a crossroads *select* a direction — a fork that "points the worthy way," an oracle that "knows which road to take," a fate that "chooses you" — it has invented a speaker the field does not have. The field is indifferent; it cannot prefer. A presented preference that the field's real channels do not carry is the same false bump the dispute and the name shed ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2 — *the unsupported bump that sheds*; a false name decays). **A "crossroads that decided" would be a false reading — a claim the field's indifference does not hold — and the field sheds it.** The design's honest register is a meeting that reads, not a meeting that speaks.

Concretely, this is the shape of a *deciding* crossroads and why it cannot stand: a fork that *brightens* one leg "because you are worthy," or *dim*s another "because the field has chosen," or *rolls* a direction "today's way" is inventing a preference the publish does not carry. The field's channels at the meeting hold the ways' *present states* — each leg's real `q`, `∇(g·Φ)`, `(1−q)` — with no column for "worthiness," "choice," or "today's roll." A presented direction that references those invented quantities is not a read of the field; it is a claim *about* the field the field does not hold, and it sheds exactly as a false name decays and a false toll fails to book ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2; [`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §4). The honest register is the opposite: **the fork presents the ways' real present states, and any "decision" among them is the player's, made with or against the honest read.** The field sheds the false preference, and what remains is the legible meeting — indifferent, deterministic, and left to the one who chooses.

**Why the legible choice-point fills the corpus's gap, honestly.** The way-stack designs places *on* ways; the map designs the *record* of ways; the highway's network framing presupposes the *meeting* of ways without designing it. What the corpus never designed is the meeting itself — the place where the ways' own organization converges and the road's network draws a branch that is *legible as a choice*. The crossroads fills that: it is the way's place where the road branches, the legible choice-point the way's network draws. And it fills it without inventing a speaker — **the meeting is legible because the field is legible, and the choice is the player's because the field is indifferent.** That is the whole of the load-bearing design: the way's network, made a place you can read and choose at, with the refusal to speak held against the field's indifference. In Minecraft, where a crossroads is the archetypal place for a game to whisper a "correct" way, this design is the corpus's corrective: **the meeting of ways is the field's own legible place, and the choosing is the traveler's, never the field's and never a hidden will's.**

### 3.1 The presupposed network — why the meeting was already there, undesigned

The crossroads is not an arbitrary addition to the way-stack; it is the *presupposed* member the way-stack's own network framing already leans on. Three landed pieces quietly assume the meeting without designing it:

- **The highway designs a *network*.** A road is a steered coherence route ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §1), and the highway's own framing is the road network as the window's circulation (§2) — a set of maintained lines. A network is a set of routes that *meet*; wherever two routes meet, there is a meeting of ways. **The crossroads is that presupposed meeting, made a designed member.**
- **The causeway is a *segment*.** The causeway is explicitly a marsh-crossing highway segment, a held `q`-line over the marsh ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §6b, the ride-downhill slice; `the-causeway.md` §7 the [design] line). A segment has ends; an end of a segment that meets another way is a meeting. **The crossroads is where the causeway's held deck meets the wider road.**
- **The caravan rides a *route*.** The long-train carries the market's abroad-goods along an atlas-mapped route, crossing the between and the desert ([`the-caravan.md`](./the-caravan.md) §2.4). A route is a path through the network; a route crosses other routes. **The crossroads is where a route crosses, or meets, another way.**

Each presupposes the point of convergence without drawing it. The crossroads is the design that finally *names* the meeting the way-stack has been composing all along: **the place where the network's routes share a point, the way's place where the road branches.** The honest claim is not that the crossroads invents something new, but that it draws the meeting the corpus's own way-stack has presupposed — legible, indifferent, chosen by the player, without adding a single new way or a single hidden channel.

### 3.2 The choice as a learned, reproducible field act

Because the crossroads is deterministic and legible (§5c), the choice it offers is not a gamble or a leap of faith; it is a *learnable* field act, on the same footing as reading a gradient or charting a map:

> **Choosing at a crossroads is learned the way any field act is learned: by reading honestly and building knowledge on what the reads reliably show.** A traveler returns to a meeting and reads the same fork every time the field holds the same state — which way holds high, which way runs thin, which way the gradient carries a body. That repetition is not a trap or a superstition; it is the material of *learning the ways*. A player can come to know a crossroads the way they know a road: the east way is the maintained one that holds a clean line, the north way the thin one that bleeds `(1−q)` through its waste, the shallow the ford's soft crossing ([`the-causeway.md`](./the-causeway.md) §1; `energy-harnessing.md` §2; `the-ford.md` §1). The fork's determinism is what makes that knowledge *true* — the same reproducibility that makes the map a truthful record rather than a guess ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §6c) and the field a perfect judge (the dispute's determinism leg, [`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §6c). **A crossroads is learnable exactly because it is deterministic and legible — and it is *not* fateful because nothing at it chooses.**

The choice's being learned does not make it less the player's; it makes it *more* the player's. A fate that picks you is something done to you; a legible fork you have learned to read is something you choose *with* knowledge. The crossroads's gift is that the player's choosing can be informed, honest, and repeated — **the same way every field act is informed, honest, and repeated in the corpus's deterministic, legible world.**

## 4. The load-bearing honesty — never a judge, never an oracle, never a fate

The crossroads's honesty is the field's own, but it has to be held harder than most way-places, because the meeting-of-ways is precisely where the temptation to invent a speaker runs highest. In Minecraft, a crossroads is where the game *could* whisper: a sign that "knows," a fork that "favors," a fate that "picks you." The Crossroads refuses that entirely, and the refusal is the design:

### 4.1 Never a judge — the field does not prefer

The crossroads composes the way-places that *are* judged — the toll's held door and the dispute's read-to-resolution ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §4 — a toll only where the door is held; [`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2 — a claim checked against the book, the observatory, the anchors). But the crossroads itself is none of these. It holds no door to enforce, no book to judge against, no claim between two parties to resolve. **A crossroads does not judge who is right to go which way**, because there is no right — only ways, each a real present field state, each a legible read. The field's indifference, which the dispute uses to judge *claims* (what is actually held), the crossroads uses to *refuse* judgment: there is no fact of the matter about which way a traveler "should" go, so there is nothing to adjudicate. **A crossroads that "blessed" a direction or "forbade" another would be a judge the field does not have.** The way-stack's own family of door-holders (the toll's held door, the gatekeeper's read, the threshold's marked-crossing) is deliberately *kept out of the meeting*: those are settlement acts at deliberately door-ed places, and a bare meeting of ways is none of them. The crossroads is where the ways meet *without* a door — an unbounded, un-held crossing, the field's own open plain at the branch, judged by nobody.

**Why the refusal to judge is the harder honesty.** The dispute can judge because there *is* a fact of the matter — whose claim the field holds, which structure is actually maintained ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2, the same channels that adjudicate live-vs-residue). The crossroads has no such fact. A traveler choosing a way is not asserting a claim the field can verify; they are choosing among real present ways, all equally *there*. There is nothing false to shed at a crossroads, and therefore nothing to judge. The field's honesty at the meeting is not that it decides rightly, but that it *declines to decide at all* — the decision is the traveler's, because the field holds no preference among its own real ways. This is the judgement-refusal's load: it keeps the crossroads from quietly becoming a mouthpiece, a fate, a hidden rule, while still being a place of genuine, legible choice.

### 4.2 Never an oracle — no hidden voice

The crossroads reads on the instruments, and only on the instruments. Per the family rule ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1 — every instrument is a consumer of the same publish, never a new channel), the fork is the ways' own `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)`/`(1−q)`, presented at the meeting's point — the same channels a player could read by standing at the meeting and glancing at a Weatherglass, one branch at a time. **There is no hidden channel at the crossroads, no secret petition, no fortune told in a hidden register.** The choice-point's read is the field's public read, and a player who never "engages a crossroads" loses nothing the field hides: each branch's state is the same publish, readable at the same point with the reader or the glass. **A crossroads that spoke in a hidden channel would be an oracle the design's own family rule forbids** (the accessibility discipline's sharpest form — the choice is never secret). And this is *structurally* guaranteed, not tuned in: the fork is a composition of the same publish every instrument reads (shared-publish architecture, [`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.3), so there is no register in which a hidden direction could be held without being a new channel — and the family rule forbids new channels. **The choice-point is legible or it does not exist; a hidden fork is a contradiction in terms.**

**What the oracle-refusal gives up, and keeps.** Refusing an oracle at the crossroads gives up the seductive power of a place that "knows" — the sign that saves you from thinking, the path that is "meant" for you. It keeps something truer to the corpus: **the traveler's own informed agency, un-carrying.** A crossroads that told you the way would make your choosing a formality; a crossroads that reads and lets you choose makes the choosing *yours*. The oracle's silence is not an absence of guidance; it is the presence of legibility. The ways are all there to read; the field is indifferent to which you take; the choosing is genuinely available to the player, un-decided by anyone. That is the deepest thing the corpus's meeting-of-ways can offer, and it is exactly what a hidden oracle would destroy by deciding for you.

### 4.3 Never a fate — no seeded roll, no fortune-mint

The meeting is deterministic: same field state → same read at the crossroads, every run (§5c, a hard gate). There is no seeded fork roll, no randomized direction, no "which way did the crossroads favor today" that differs across reloads. **And the read spends like any read** — it costs no more than a sample at the meeting's point, inside the ≈ 1–6 ms/tick budget, on the ≈ 6 MiB publish (`corpus-reconciliation.md`). The crossroads converts nothing: `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` ([`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md) §6) bounds it as it bounds every held structure. **A crossroads that "paid a toll of choice" into fortune — a fork that charged your passage and then told you which way was lucky — would be a false mint** — the same refusal that closes Coda-farming ([`signature-predator.md`](./signature-predator.md) §7e, via the caravan and the causeway) and the same no-yield the toll holds ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §5d — a toll converts nothing, never a mint). **The meeting provides nothing; the choice-point's read is a read, never a gain.**

**Why determinism and the no-free-energy cap are the same refusal, worn two ways.** Determinism says there is no *hidden* will at the meeting — the fork is a pure function of the field state, reproducible and learnable (§5c); the cap says there is no *yielding* mint at the meeting — a read converts nothing, spends like any read, gains nothing (§5d). Together they close every seeded-oracle and fortune-mint at once: a crossroads cannot roll a direction (deterministic), cannot charge a booked guidance (no door, no op, `the-toll.md` §4), and cannot return more than its read (the cap). The refusal is total because it is architectural: the meeting has no channel for a hidden roll and no lane for a minted gain, so neither can exist there no matter how a settlement or a player tries to read one in. **The crossroads is the field's own legible, deterministic, non-yielding place — and every attempt to make it otherwise sheds.**

### 4.4 The honest register — the meeting reads; the player chooses

What the crossroads does, it does plainly: **it is a place where the way's network converges, legible on the instruments, chosen by the player.** It does not decide, does not favor, does not whisper, does not mint. It *is* — a branch-point in the field that N ways genuinely share — and it *reads* — those N ways' present states, presented honestly at the meeting. Everything else — a judge, an oracle, a fate, a fortune — is the false surface the design's honesty sheds, and the design names the shed so the false reading never lands. **The honest register of the corpus's way-meeting: this is the place where the path is legible; the choosing is yours.** And the register is not a nicety; it is the whole design. A crossroads that "spoke" would collapse the meeting into an oracle, a toll-door, a judge — exactly the false speakers the way-stack's honesty and the field's indifference refuse. The crossroads's honesty is that it holds the line: legible, indifferent, chosen by no one but the player.

The register is what makes the crossroads a *place for people* rather than a mechanism. A traveler does not *use* a crossroads; they *stand at* one. The meeting's legibility gives the standing a weight — the ways present, the choosing real, the field indifferent — that no rote sample-at-position gives alone. In the corpus's terms, the crossroads is a *dwelling-point* of the way's network: a place where the settlement's roads and the field's crossings knit together into a moment of human decision, made legible by the instruments and left to the person who chooses. That is the honest register's gift: **not a speaker, but a standing-place — and at a standing-place, the one who decides is the one who is standing.**

### 4.5 The refusal table — what the crossroads is not, and why the field holds the refusal

The design's honesty is sharpest as a table of refusals, each naming the Minecraft-overclaim and the field-truth that refuses it:

| The false reading | The overclaim it would make | The field-truth that refuses it |
|---|---|---|
| **A judge** | "the crossroads knows which way is right to go" | there is no right — only ways, each a real present field state; the field holds no preference to adjudicate (§4.1) |
| **An oracle** | "the crossroads knows a hidden direction" | there is no hidden direction — the fork is the publish, the same channels every instrument reads; a hidden fork is structurally impossible (§4.2) |
| **A fate** | "the crossroads chooses you / rolls your way" | no seeded roll, no player-relative variance — same field state → same read, every run (§4.3); the field rolls nothing |
| **A fortune-mint** | "pay the crossroads and it tells you the luckiest way" | `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` — a crossroads converts nothing; the read spends like any read, never a gain, never a booked fee (§4.3) |
| **A toll-door** | "the crossroads charges your crossing into it" | the meeting holds no door — a toll only where a door is held (`the-toll.md` §4); the crossroads itself books nothing (§2/§4.1) |
| **A hidden rite** | "a secret petition at the crossroads turns a fortune" | no hidden channel, no secret petition — the fork is the instruments' public read, and a secretized reading is a new channel the family rule forbids (§4.2) |

Each refusal is the same honest mechanism worn a different way: the field is indifferent, deterministic, and legible, so it can neither prefer (no judge), nor hide (no oracle), nor roll (no fate), nor mint (no fortune), nor hold a door it does not have (no toll), nor speak in a channel it does not publish (no rite). **The refusal table is the design's own checklist of the false surfaces it sheds, named so the shed never lands as design.** A crossroads that reads as any one of these has missed the meeting-of-ways entirely and become one of the way-stack's speakers the field does not have.

**The refusals are a positive claim, not a list of lacks.** It would be easy to read §4 as "the crossroads is not a lot of things," and leave it thin — a meeting that merely refuses. That is not the design. The refusals are the *cost of the positive claim*: **the crossroads is the one place in the way-stack where the player's choosing is genuinely free and genuinely legible.** Because the field does not prefer (§4.1), the player's choice is not judged; because it does not hide (§4.2), the choice is readable; because it does not roll (§4.3), the choice is learnable; because it does not mint (§4.4), the choice is honest; because it holds no door (§4.1/§4.5), the choice is un-tolled. **Every refusal is the guard that keeps the player's choice the player's.** The table names what the crossroads is not so that what it *is* — a legible, indifferent, player-chosen meeting of ways — stands cleanly and cannot be mistaken for one of the way-stack's speakers.

A pass lands the fork's-read (the crossroads's first form is real); a too-merged read lands the honest fallback (the meeting reads as a bare crossing of routes — a still-honest finding, the same weaker register the causeway and the ford pre-register). **[probe], [design]** the legibility threshold; both outcomes are honest and stated here.

The probe is the whole Phase-1 slice made measurable. ### (c) Determinism — a hard gate

The field is deterministic (one PDE; [`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §6c; [`field-npc-ai.md`](./field-npc-ai.md) §6d — the corpus's determinism stance throughout). The crossroads inherits it as a **hard gate**:

> **Same field state → same read at the crossroads, every run.** The fork's legs (each branch's `q`/`ε²`/`(1−q)`/`∇(g·Φ)`), the branch's form (how the ways' reads resolve into directions), and the choice's legibility are each a **pure function of the published channels at the meeting's point and the ways' held state** over its reading window — no hidden randomness, no player-relative variance, no seeded fork roll, no seeded oracle. A player who returns to the same meeting at the same field state gets the same fork, the same legs, the same legibility, every run. **This is a hard gate, never a seeded roll and never a seeded fate** — "at this meeting, this way holds high and leans down-gradient, that way is thin and dead ahead" is a *true, learnable* fact about the field, reproducible and teachable, exactly as the causeway's deck-hold and the ford's shallow are. A player can *learn* a crossroads: which ways hold, which run thin, what the field at that point honestly shows — never a roll, never a mood.

The determinism is the whole of the no-oracle honesty made mechanical. An oracle's power is that it *knows something about the future or the hidden*; the field has neither. A crossroads reads the field's present state, and the same present state reads the same way every time — so there is no "mood," no "favor," no re-roll of the fork to surprise a traveler. **The fork is deterministic because the field is deterministic; the reason there is no hidden fate is that there is no hidden anything.** This is what makes the crossroads *learnable* rather than *fateful*: a traveler can return to a meeting and trust that what they read is what the field holds, every run, and can build knowledge of the ways on that trust.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — a crossroads converts nothing

`energy-harnessing.md` §6 (`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`), inherited unchanged through the causeway, the ford, the toll, the caravan, the highway, and the map, is the crossroads's iron bound, held as a hard gate:

> **A crossroads converts nothing — no fork that pays a toll of choice, no read that mints a guidance, no meeting that yields a fortune.** The meeting is a place where the ways' own organization is present; the read at the meeting spends like any read (the sample cost, inside the ≈ 1–6 ms/tick budget, on the ≈ 6 MiB publish). **There is no "crossroads fortune":** no charge that turns your passage into a direction, no fork that books a guidance the field did not hold, no meeting that pays a "told you the way" value. The ways' `q`/`∇(g·Φ)` are the field's own — a read of them diverts nothing, stores nothing, mints nothing ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §6d — a record, never a mint; the tide's `5d` information-never-energy stance). **The choice-point's read is bought at the cost of a read, never a gain; a "crossroads that decided for a price" would be a mint the cap forbids.**

The cap closes every crossroads-to-farm exploit trivially, and it is worth stating the shape of the refusal: a player cannot "camp a crossroads" to extract a direction, because the read mints nothing the player could stock or trade — it is information about the field's present state, and information is never energy (the map's no-mint guard, [`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §6d, states the same for its drawn record). A crossroads cannot "choose-toll" a traveler into a booked fee, because the crossroads holds no door and books no op ([`the-toll.md`](./the-toll.md) §4 — a toll only where a door is held, and the meeting holds none). And a crossroads cannot "farm a fate" into a fortune, because there is no fate to farm — the fork is deterministic, and the read yields nothing but the ways' present states. **The cap is the honest face of the whole design: a crossroads is a place to read and choose, and reading and choosing costs and yields exactly what a read costs and yields — nothing more.**

### (e) Accessibility — the fork's branches and their reads on the instruments, never hidden-only

Per the instrument-family rule ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1) and the corpus's never-hidden-only discipline (the causeway §7e, the ford §5e, the toll §5e, the caravan §5e):

> **The fork's branches and their reads are on the instruments — never hidden-only; the choice is never secret.** Each branch's legs are the same channels the Weatherglass and reader already show — its `∇(g·Φ)` lean, its `q` lume, its `(1−q)` cost tint ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.2) — read at the meeting's point exactly as the read is done elsewhere. The map draws the meeting ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §1) and ages it honestly (§2). A player who never "uses a crossroads overlay" loses nothing the field hides: the ways at the meeting are the same channels, the same reads the instruments already present. **A crossroads that hid its fork — a choice-point whose directions were instrumentation-hidden — would be a hidden oracle the design's own honesty forbids.** The choice is legible because the field is legible; there is no secret branch, no hidden direction, no second book that knows.

The accessibility floor is the whole of the no-oracle honesty, made structural. Because the fork is a composition of the same publish every instrument reads (the shared-publish architecture, [`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.3), there is *no way for a fork to be hidden* — any direction the field holds at the meeting is present in the same channels the reader and glass already sample, and any "direction" that is not in those channels is not in the field at all (a false bump the field sheds, [`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2). **The crossroads is accessible because it is honest, and it cannot be hidden because there is nothing to hide.** A player who has never heard of a "crossroads" still reads the meeting's ways with the instruments they already carry; the design adds a composed presentation, never a hidden truth.

**The fork's accessibility is the map-tie and the family rule at once.** The map is the instrument of the continued inhabitant ([`the-map.md`](./the-map.md) §7 — the player who has stayed and drawn); the crossroads is the live meeting such a player reads with the Weatherglass, the reader, and their own charted record. 3. **The toll's mark at a meeting (§4/§5).** Where a held door sits at a branch, does the toll charge *the meeting* (a crossing-of-ways that books the toll's per-arrival contribution — the held-door honesty of `the-toll.md` §4 applied at the branch), or does the meeting stay *un-tolled* (the crossroads itself charges nothing, and only a held door at it charges, unchanged)? 5. **The crossroads vs. the false-oracle shed (§4).** When a player (or the corpus's own narrative) *insists* a crossroads "knows" the way — a sign, a spoken claim, a superstition that a fork favored a direction — does the honest register read the *claim* as the false bump that sheds ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2 — the unsupported bump that sheds), while the *meeting* stays legible and indifferent? Whether the shed is *visible* (a claimed direction reads false against the fork) or *silent* (the claim simply does not hold) is the dial. **[design]**, inheriting the dispute's and the story's honesty gates.
