## 1. The Inn, stated once

The corpus draws the stranger's arrival (`window-guests.md` — the walking provenance
and the visitor line it books), the dwelling that holds the local field
(`house-that-steers.md` — the room that holds a `q`-core, the bath, the ruin), the
boundary the crossing is a marked act at (`the-threshold.md` — the doorstep, the gate,
the arch), and the human office that reads who may enter (`the-gatekeeper.md`). What
none of them designs is **the place a stranger rests *inside* that boundary**: the
roofed room a settlement holds for the transient, the guest who has crossed the arch
and been read at the gate and now needs *a held bed under the maintained roof*. The
guest's arrival is designed; the house's dwelling is designed; the **room that holds
the guest** was never drawn. The Inn is that room.

> **The Inn, stated once.** It is the **held bed** — the traveler's roofed room in a
> settlement, the window-guest's held shelter: a bounded room inside the threshold
> (the-threshold's boundary, held by the settlement) where a stranger may rest under
> the settlement's maintained roof. It is a room the house holds — the house-that-
> steers's guest-door (a room the house holds, the way the house holds its bath — the
> guest's rest is the house's own held order, never a free grant); the gatekeeper's
> read at the inn's door (who enters the held bed is read the way who enters the
> settlement is read — the provenance read at the held bed); and the wage's guest-form
> (the inn is the *paid* held bed — the traveler pays in held order for the room, the
> way a worker is paid for a day). **The traveler's welcome made an economy; the
> house's guest-door.**

**Why an inn, and why *held*.** The traveler who crosses the settlement's arch has been
read (`window-guests.md` §3 — the visitor line books from the moment their provenance
crosses a named edge), and has been admitted or questioned by the gatekeeper
(`the-gatekeeper.md` §2.1). But being admitted to the settlement is not having a place
to rest in it. In Minecraft a stranger sleeps in the open or on a borrowed bed — outside
the field, no room, no book, no trust, the same hole the corpus has closed for the
arrival and the dwelling but not for the *rest*. The Inn closes it the thesis-true way:
a **held room** — a bounded high-`q` locality inside the threshold, extended by the
house's own order to a guest, and *paid for* with held order the way any booked act is.

**The honest flag, once.** The pieces an inn composes are all landed — the room that
holds a `q`-core (`house-that-steers.md` §1.1), the visitor line (`window-guests.md`
§3), the gatekeeper's three reads (`the-gatekeeper.md` §2.1), the wage's paid-labor

cap (`energy-harnessing.md` §6). **The inn's *composition* — that a held room *is*
bookable to a guest, that its door is read, that its rest is bought with held order —

record the engine writes beyond the Q4 op it already writes: an inn is a *booked held
