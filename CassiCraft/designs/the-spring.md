## 2. The well — the honest mechanics

### 2.1 The welling — the field's own conservative re-precipitation at a fixed point

`material-regimes.md` §3 establishes the **precipitation law**: ore precipitates
where `q` accumulates above a band — a vein is a region where local `q` exceeds
the condensation selection, "the identity tuple's `θ_c` is exceeded not by ρ but
by local q." The field organizes coherence into high-`q`, deeper-rung matter
*where it accumulates*. And the law carries a one-wayness (`the-fallow.md`
§2a): it does **not** re-precipitate a *worked* vein — a mined locality's `q` is
spent, its `ε²` raised, and the ore that was there is gone.

**The Spring is the fallow's full twin:** where the fallow reads the one-way
run-out of a *worked* point, the Spring is the fixed point where the field's
**conservative re-precipitation actually returns** — a stationarity the field
keeps full by its own dynamics, without being worked down. It is the same law
(`material-regimes.md` §3) at a different relation to the window: a *worked*
vein is a depleted locality whose `q` was withdrawn; **a Spring is a locality
whose `q` the field keeps re-surfacing of itself** — a point of local,
attractor-consistent order the field's own conservation holds above its
dissolution threshold, tide after tide.

> **The welling is the field's own conservation at a fixed point.** Where the
> fallow reads a locality whose order was withdrawn and lost (a worked deposit
> is gone forever, `the-fallow.md` §2a), the Spring is a locality whose order
> the conservative re-precipitation keeps returning — the re-precipitation law
> (`material-regimes.md` §3) at a point the field does not run out of. It is
> **not a deliberate plant** (`resonance-seeds.md` §1), **not a conserved
> vault** (`seed-garden.md` §3), and **not a migrating ridge** (`field-emergent-
> ecology.md` §4.2) — it is the field's own order surfacing at one fixed,
> bounded place, and staying full.

The honest mechanical weight of "the field keeps it full": the welling is the
**conservative** re-precipitation — the field does not *create* order where
there was none; it organizes and re-surfaces the ambient `q` that is already
present in the locality's stationary configuration. A Spring is a point of the
field's own order at rest, held by the same law that grows a vein — a fixed
instance of material-regimes §3's conservation. The `(1−q)` waste law
(`energy-harnessing.md` §2) still applies: even a live well wastes a little
toward the attractor as it holds — a well's order is *kept*, not minted.

## 3. The point of fame — the load-bearing honesty

The load-bearing honesty of the Spring, stated once and never blurred:

> **A spring is a point of abundance that is also a point of fame.** A
> wellspring is a **known, legible source** — a life-signal-loud, order-bright
> point any stranger and any Coda can find (`life-signal.md` §3 — the
> maintenance axis: a live, maintained, order-bright read; `signature-predator.md`
> §2 — the trail-read's legibility), so **a spring is both the settlement's
> lifeline and its liability**: defend the spring or lose the settlement — the
> guardian's territorial read on a live well (`the-guardian.md` §1/§3). **The
> fixed source is never hidden** — a spring that hid its brightness would be a
> secret wealth the field's own legibility forbids.

The two halves of the composition, honestly:

### 3.1 The lifeline — the settlement's known, legible source

The spring's brightness is a **life-signal-loud read** (`life-signal.md` §3):
an order-bright, maintained-live presence — elevated `q`, `ε²` suppressed, held
against its drain — that reads exactly as a well-kept source should. Any
stranger arriving at a window, and any settlement's reader, can find the spring
the way they find any bright, live, maintained structure: from the reader/Sense
(`coherence-magic.md` §2), the Weatherglass (`field-instruments.md` §1.2), the

### 3.2 The liability — the point any stranger and any Coda can find

The same legibility is the liability. Because the spring is **life-signal-loud

hidden — the trail-read's legibility (`signature-predator.md` §2) works both
ways: a bright, sustained, order-loud source is exactly the kind of fixed
landmark a predator-reads-for and a stranger-finds. **The spring's fame is the
settlement's exposure.** This is the honest read of the guardian's tie: a
wellspring is the named place a settlement must *defend* — "defend the spring or

### 3.3 The never-hidden cap

## 4. The drawn order — the load-bearing design

### 4.1 Water-and-order drawn from a named source

A settlement's water-and-order comes *from* a spring — the named source
(`the-landform-name.md` §2: the landform-name's land carrying a live well), the
granary's draw (`the-granary.md` §2: the storehouse drawing the ordinary
harvest against the lean), and the carry's pack (`the-carry.md` §2: the small
store carried from the spring). The honest distinction, drawn once:

> **The drawn-and-stored is distinct from the harvested-and-stored.** The farm
> *harvests* an order the field grew at a tide (`farm-that-feeds.md` §4); the
> spring is *drawn* — the settlement takes the water-and-order the well's own
> conservation has surfaced, at the well, and carries it into the granary's
> store and the pack's cell. It is drawn and stored, not harvested and stored:
> the granary is filled from the well's draw as much as from the field's
> harvest (`the-granary.md` §2/§3), and the pack is the walker's cell of that
> drawn store (`the-carry.md` §2).

### 4.2 The no-free-energy cap — a spring provides nothing beyond its own welling

The no-free-energy cap (`energy-harnessing.md` §6, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`), held
as a hard bound, stated once:

> **A spring provides nothing beyond its own welling — the fixed source is the
> field's own, never a mint.** The welling is the field's conservative
> re-precipitation at the point (§2.1), **never a gain the settlement can farm**:
> the well's draw is a withdrawal of the field's own surfaced order, spent
> exactly as the granary's draw is spent (`the-granary.md` §5d — every withdrawal
> depletes; holding converts nothing), and **a spring that appears to *make*
> order — a source that yields more than its own conservation returns — is a
> false well the field sheds.** The ambient organizing the well surfaces is the
> field's own (`energy-harnessing.md` §1.7), and the cap forbids turning it into
> a stored, farmable battery: a spring is a draw, never a currency, never a
> coherence mint, never a "collect the spring's overflow" farm. **The welling is
> the field's own conservation at the point, and a settlement that reads the
> spring as making order where the field did not deposit it has built on a false
> well the field will shed.**

### 4.3 The drawn well's honest cost

Drawing the spring is not free: every draw spends the well's surfaced order, and
the well re-fills *by the field's conservation*, on the field's own cadence —
not instantly, not on demand. A settlement that over-draws a spring pulls the
well below its stationarity, and the over-drawn well reads toward the fallow's
one-way run-out (a place whose conservation could not keep up with the demand)
rather than the spring's replenishment. **The spring's abundance is the ambient
well's, held by the field; the draw is honest only against that stationarity**
— the same discipline the granary's draw holds (`the-granary.md` §3: a full
store weathers the lean; the draw is spent, never free).
