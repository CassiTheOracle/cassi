## 3. The legible vulnerability

**The load-bearing honesty of this design: a roost is the field-being's one
legible vulnerability — the place you can *find* a being, the place a being
defends, the place a being abandons if the order thins.** This is what makes the
Roost the *opposite* of a hidden spawn, and it is what keeps it honest.

Every being in the corpus is otherwise *findable only through its act*: the Coda
is a resonance that only *accumulates* toward a signature you shed, the mirror is
beatable only by *testing* whether it holds its lock, the feral's intent is read
from its provenance, the scavenger's count from the spent's census. **But a being
that lives *somewhere* is a being you can find at its home** — and the field's
legibility rule, `readable-before-it-arrives` (`field-hazards.md` §5.1), is the
same legibility the danger layer holds for every hazard, applied here to *home*
rather than to the hunt. The field that announces a storm before its front arrives
is the same field that announces a predator's den before the predator does:

- **The c_s-nest is a landmark.** The predator's home is the desert's one bright
  organized thing — the `ε²` pocket with a coherent phase that stands out
  against flat disorder (`signature-predator.md` §1.2). **The trail-read applied
  to *home*, not to the hunt** (§2 — the read that previously found a channeler's
  clean trail now finds the resting place that carries the same signature). A
  predator is not invisible when it sleeps; its home is the c_s signature made a
  place, and that signature is what makes the den findable. **This is the honest
  cost of the predator's power: it is the desert's one bright thing, and a bright
  thing has a home.**
- **The margin-den is defended.** A scavenger fits the spent's residual `ε²`
  (`the-scavenger.md` §2.2) — but its den is *its* residual pocket, the one it
  returns to, and it defends it (the being's fitting is a hold, and the hold's
  place is kept; the honest constraint §5d — the defense is the field's own order,
  never a reward). The den is where a scavenger can be found — the same spending
  that makes the exhausted locality a home makes it a findable one.
- **The steadied hollow is abandoned if the order thins.** The commensal lives on
  the order's surplus at its smallest (`the-commensal.md` §2.2) and holds a small
  patch against it (§3). **Its vulnerability is that its home is only as healthy
  as its habitat** — a hollow in a patch whose order thins (the `q` falls, the
  `(1−q)` surplus dries) is a hollow with nothing to graze and nothing to hold,
  and the commensal abandons it (§4's wild's-own-order). The warm companion's
  one weakness is exactly the honesty of its fitting: **it keeps company only
  where the field keeps its order.**
- **The bound keep is the guarded place.** A guardian's roost *is* the named
  place it is tied to (`the-guardian.md` §1/§3) — and that is both its power and
  its one legible line. The keep is stationary and findable *because it is named*;
  a player who reads a named place's live-and-bound presence reads where the
  keeper is, before the keeper acts (§5e — readable before it is enforced). The
  keeper's honesty is the same as the hazard's: **no hidden guardian, a named
  keep's defense is legible before it is enforced, exactly as the danger layer is
  readable-before-it-arrives** (`field-hazards.md` §5.1).

**The honest frame.** The field's beings are not hidden when they rest. A roost
is the layer's being-there made findable — the same legibility the danger layer
holds (`readable-before-it-arrives`, `field-hazards.md` §5.1), applied to home.
This is not a "weakness to exploit" (that would be a loot-den, closed by §5d); it
is the honest consequence that **a being that lives somewhere can be found where
it lives**, and that the field always announces what it holds. The being that is
only legible through its act becomes, at its home, legible through its resting
place — and the resting place is the field's own order made findable.

## 4. The wild's own order

**The second load-bearing honesty: a roost is the field's own order — it provides
nothing, and it is only as healthy as its habitat.**

**A roost provides nothing.** The no-free-energy cap (`energy-harnessing.md` §6:
*no conversion yields more than it sinks, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`*) holds for a home
the way it holds for a cave, a marsh, a fallow: the home is the field-being's own,
never a gain. Concretely:

- **No shelter-yield.** Entering or holding a roost yields no shelter-state, no
  protection, no buff — the nest is the being's own order, not a structure that
  shelters the taker. A roost does not "refuge" a player (against a storm, a
  desert, a Coda — a high-`q` refuge is a *maintained* Qi bath,
  `energy-harnessing.md` §4.4, never a spendable den).
- **No loot-den.** The being that lives in a roost yields no deposit on the place.
  A predator's nest holds no trophy, a scavenger's den holds no harvested fuel, a
  guardian's keep holds no guarded treasure. (§5d — the vulnerability is honest,
  never a loot-den; the moment a roost "drops" on being found, it becomes a
  loot-den and the free-energy bug.)
- **No farming a nest.** The §7e closure of the danger layer
  (`signature-predator.md` §7e — a being sheds nothing a collector can tap, and
  its death is not a loot event) holds for the roost unchanged (§5d). You cannot
  farm a predator by finding its nest; finding the nest is reading the field, and
  reading pays in knowledge, never in energy.

**A roost is only as healthy as its habitat.** The home is the field-being's own
order, and the field-being's order is a run the field holds against the `ε²`
drain (`field-emergent-ecology.md` §2.2). So the roost inherits its habitat's
health, with the honest consequences the sources already name:

- **A roost in a blighted region turns or empties.** The blight is a region's
  life turned — a sustained wrong-band corrupts the organisms whose feeding turns
  to the region's own coherence, a living drain (`the-blight.md` §2). A roost in
  that band **turns** (the being's run, held too long in the wrong-band, becomes
  part of the drain) **or empties** (the being's habitat de-orders beneath it, and
  a being with no healthy habitat sheds or leaves). **The home is not a shelter
  from the field's extremes; it is continuous with them.**
- **An abandoned roost leaves residue the archaeology reads.** When a being's run
  ends — the nest darkens, the den's residual pocket dissolves, the hollow
  thins, the keep sheds with the spent named place (`the-guardian.md` §4's
  converse) — the deep-rung, q-locked fraction persists as residue
  (`field-archaeology.md` §2: "scars heal, but leave residue"; the deep-rung core
  survives dark, `ε²≈0`). **The spent den is the field's honest record of the
  being that lived there** — readable by the archaeology's residue model (§3.2's
  ghost halo + q-locked core) as the place where a field-being was. The roost's
  death is not erasure; it is the residue that tells the window what lived there.
- **Conservation is the home's lifeblood.** The ecology's conservation act —
  `conservation = anti-corruption` (`field-emergent-ecology.md` §5.3) — is what
  keeps a habitat (and so a roost) alive: a player who keeps a region's `q` up
  keeps its beings' homes healthy; a player who de-orders a region empties its
  roosts. **A roost is not a thing you build or own; it is a thing you keep by
  keeping the field, and it empties when the field's order thins** — the exact
  honesty of the commensal's hollow (§3) and the scavenger's spent (§2.3) made a
  general rule of this design.

**The through-line:** the wild's beings live in the field's own order, and the
field's own order obeys the no-free-energy cap. A roost is therefore **never a
gain and never a shelter** — it is the honest place where a being holds, findable
because the field is legible, healthy only as long as the field keeps its order,
and leaving, when it ends, the residue that tells the window what lived there.
