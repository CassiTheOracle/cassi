# The Working-Song: Music for Labor — the Coordinated Rhythm That Makes Work a Coherent Act

**Question under design:** field music designs the world's *art* — the composed
scores of `field-music.md` §4, the coherence score a player writes and plays
back to steer the field; the festival designs the *celebration* — the
settlement's one chord at tide-high, the commons brought to a single resonance.
**But no design applies music to *labor***: the repetitive coordinated work a
group does, and the melody that keeps a working commons phase-locked. This
document designs that bridge — from music-as-art to music-as-craft.

The **Working-Song is music composed for labor** — a maintained rhythm that
keeps a group of workers phase-locked while they do a repetitive field-op
(mining, forging, husbanding a farm, holding a shared heal) — because **a group
acting phase-locked IS a working** (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.2, `M ≈ 1`), and a song
is the honest, learnable, deterministic way to hold many hands on one phase.
It aims the field-music machinery at *work* rather than art: a lumber-camp's
rhythm keeps the axe-strokes on the field's tempo so the work wastes less (the
`(1−q)` floor held low by the shared phase); a forge-chant paces a smith's
discharge within the fire-vs-explosive line (a controlled discharge held at
the line by the rhythm, `material-regimes.md` §3); a heal-song soothes a shared
long-bind into fewer toll-cycles (`the-healer.md` §2 — the group's bound act
held phase-locked). **The honest cost: a song that stops IS a broken phase-lock
— the moment the rhythm falters, the work scatters and the group's efficiency
snaps back to ordinary.**

This is a **practice-design**, the corpus's second *practice* alongside
[`the-vigil.md`](./the-vigil.md): the vigil is music-as-craft's sibling — the
nocturnal guard composes the night's instruments into a crafted act (the first
practice, reading and guarding); the Working-Song composes the field-music
machinery into a crafted act (the second practice, singing the work). Where the
vigil *reads* the night, the working-song *holds* the day's rhythm. It
introduces **no new channel and no new physics**; it is a **designed use of the
landed mechanics** — field-music's composition machinery aimed at a
work-rhythm, over the group-phase-lock and `(1−q)` laws the corpus already
designs.

## 2. The phase-lock mechanic

### 2.1 The load-bearing claim — a phase-locked group IS a working

`field-npc-ai.md` §3.2 is the corpus's designed commons law, grounded on the
theory's phase-matching factor (`magic-systems.md` §1 — a working is `M ≈ 1`,
random perturbation `M ≈ 0`):

> **Coordination between NPCs is the docking mechanic's `M` applied to
> cooperation.** Two NPCs acting in the same phase window are one organized
> perturbation (each holds phase with the others, so the joint ε² vent is
> shared, the joint injection's `M` sums coherently). **A group acting
> phase-locked IS a working**: the group's combined organized perturbation
> couples at `M ≈ 1`, producing an effect no member could alone.
> (field-npc-ai.md §3.2, verbatim)

The Working-Song applies this to *labor*. A repetitive field-op — the tool's
bite (`the-tool.md` §2a), the forge's transfer, the farm's tide-lift, the
healer's bind — is a **sequence of perturbations**. When N workers each perform
their op independently, they are N random perturbations: `M → 0`, the field
absorbs them separately, and each wastes its `(1−q)` floor (energy-harnessing
§2 — `E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput`). When the same N workers are **phase-locked
to one rhythm**, their joint sequence is **one coordinated working**: the ops
compose coherently (`M ≈ 1`), the shared vent is amortized, and each op's waste
floor is held low by the shared phase.

**The honest framing of the gain.** A phase-locked group's ops compose — the
group acts as one working at `M ≈ 1` rather than N workers at `M ≈ 0`, so the
group's *total* efficiency is the working's, not the sum of the individuals'.
This is the canonical **`(1−q)` waste law** held from the group side: an
uncoordinated group wastes `(1−q)` per op, N times; a phase-locked group wastes
`(1−q)` per *working*, once, on a shared phase (energy-harnessing §2 — the
waste is a floor, and the floor is a per-coherent-act quantity). **[design]**
The *phase-lock's efficiency gain* is [design]-landed over the `(1−q)` waste
law — a coordinated group's ops compose, always within the cap's bound
(`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`, §5d). **[design]** The *song's rhythm* is field-music's
composition (`field-music.md` §4). **[design]** **The working-song's framing —
music aimed at work, the phase-lock as the gain — is this doc's [design]**
(§5a).

**Why a song, and not "just coordinate"?** The corpus's coordination is real —
but it is *earned, not ambient*: an NPC mind must hold phase against its own
drift (`field-npc-ai.md` §2.2), a player must read and match phase
(`field-npc-ai.md` §5.2). A song is the **honest, learnable, deterministic
device** that does that holding *for* the group:
- **learnable** — the rhythm is a composed score (`field-music.md` §4), audible
  and reproducible; any member who can follow the rhythm can join the working
  (field-music §6 — sound is the enhancement that makes coordination
  hands-free);
- **deterministic** — the rhythm is a fixed Q4 op sequence, so the same song,
  same group, same field state produces the same working (§5c);
- **honest** — a song is *maintained attention*: it must be sung, so the
  phase-lock is held by real effort, not a free buff (the honest cost, §4).

The song is therefore the *composition* of the phase-lock — the mechanical way
many hands are held on one phase — not a claim that music itself is a new
physics. The music is the device; the working is the law it holds.

### 2.2 The honest boundary of the gain

The phase-lock's gain is the group's *own composed efficiency* — it composes
operations the group was already doing, so it **never exceeds the cap**:
`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` (energy-harnessing §6, inherited unchanged). A working's
output is bounded by its members' channeled input, and the working-song's gain
is the multiplicity of the phase-lock (`M ≈ 1`, field-npc-ai §3.2), *not* a
mint. There is no song that yields more than its singers channel; the rhythm
does not summon coherence, it *composes* the coherence the workers already
spend. This is the same honest bound the festival holds (§5d) — the
working-song is the festival's law applied to the workday.

## 3. The three songs

### 3a. The lumber-camp's rhythm — axe-strokes on the field's tempo

The tool's bite is a bound perturbation (`the-tool.md` §2a — a pick's bite is
its rung-depth; mining is a perturbation that lowers local ρ and raises local
ε², material-regimes §3/§4). A lone woodcutter or miner swings at their own
tempo, each bite an uncoordinated perturbation that wastes its `(1−q)` floor
against the local `q` (`energy-harnessing.md` §2). The lumber-camp's rhythm is
the design that **phases the strokes**:

> **A lumber-camp chants its tempo, and the axe-strokes land on the field's
> beat.** Every bite is a perturbation at the swing's position (the tool's
> bounded Q4 write, `the-tool.md` §4b); when the camp's axes land
> phase-locked, the strokes compose as one coordinated working (`field-npc-ai.md`
> §3.2) rather than N uncoordinated perturbations. **The shared phase holds the
> local `(1−q)` floor low** — the work wastes less because the working's waste
> is the once-per-working `(1−q)`, not N times the single-stroke floor.

**[design]** What is designed: the *rhythm's composition* (the tempo, the pick
against the field's attractor `q ≈ 0.947` tonic, the members' strokes on the
shared beat) is field-music's composition machinery (§4) aimed at the tool's
repetitive op. What is landed: the `(1−q)` waste law (energy-harnessing §2) and
the phase-lock (\(M ≈ 1\), field-npc-ai §3.2). The honest gain is bounded by
the cap (§5d) — the lumber-camp wastes less, never *more* than the work's own
input. **[design]/[probe]** The *felt* gain (whether a phase-locked camp's
`(1−q)` actually reads lower than a scattered one at swing-pace) is the
pre-registered Phase-1 measurement §5b.

### 3b. The forge-chant — pacing a smith's discharge within the line

`material-regimes.md` §3 fixes the boundary: a **controlled organizational
discharge = fire**; an **uncontrolled full-cascade discharge = blast**. The
smith's craft — drawing a deep-rung metal's stored coherence through the forge —
is a *controlled* release held *at* that line: the controlled-burn rate the
metal can sustain (`material-regimes.md` §5) without crossing into the
full-cascade regime. `the-tool.md` §4a names the hand-scale of the risk: a deep
bite is a "controlled discharge held at the fire-vs-explosive line, and
over-forcing it is the tool's blast." The forge-chant is the design that holds
the smith's discharge *on* the line:

> **The forge-chant paces a smith's discharge within the fire-vs-explosive
> line.** Forging is a sequence of controlled discharges — each transfer a
> bounded release of the metal's stored coherence (`material-regimes.md` §3/§5),
> never a full-cascade collapse. The chant holds the discharge *rate* on the
> controlled side of the boundary: the rhythm is the metronome that keeps each
> transfer's warmth under the line, so the work never tips organized → random
> and detonates. A forge that chants together is a forge that paces together —
> and the shared rhythm keeps every smith's discharge at the safe rate the
> material's `ω₀²` lets it re-lock.

**[design]** What is designed: the *chant's role as rate-keeper* — specifically,
that a coordinated rhythm modulates the temporal spacing of a controlled
discharge so each transfer stays under the blast line (the rhythm as the
discipline of the line). What is landed: the fire-vs-explosive boundary
(material-regimes §3), the discharge rate/ratio read (material-regimes §3b; the
`ω₀²` re-lock threshold), the tool's "over-forcing is the blast" warning
(`the-tool.md` §4a). The chant does not change the line — it *holds* the work
on the safe side of it. **[probe]** The exact pacing window that keeps a metal's
discharge under its own `ω₀²` re-lock is a Phase-1.5 calibration of the
fire-vs-explosive rate boundary.

### 3c. The heal-song — soothing a shared long-bind into fewer toll-cycles

`the-healer.md` §2 designs the **long bind**: to heal a wound (a deliberate-break
scar) or a deep Burden, the healer holds the torn edge and draws the patient's
ε²-debt *into themselves*, so the patient's field can re-settle against the
attractor. `the-healer.md` §2.2 names the one honest amplification: **a healer
holding a bind *with* the patient's own moderated re-hold (or a settlement
singing a dirge for the worst wound) couples at `M ≈ 1`** — the multiplicity
gain of the phase-locked working, never a mint. The heal-song is that amplification,
made a designed act:

> **A heal-song soothes a shared long-bind into fewer toll-cycles.** A long
> bind is a sustained, phase-locked hold (`the-healer.md` §2 — the healer holds
> the torn edge while the attractor re-tensions). When the bind is *shared* —
> the healer holding the edge *with* the patient's moderated re-hold and the
> settlement singing the same phrase — the group acts as one working
> (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.2). The song's rhythm keeps the group's hold
> phase-locked, the way any working is held on one phase. **The honest gain is
> fewer toll-cycles: because the bind is one coordinated working rather than
> the healer's lone hold plus the patient's separate struggle, the re-settle
> composes at `M ≈ 1` and the *healer's* carried toll (the-burden's transferred
> ε²) is what the shared phase amortizes** — not a lighter heal, the same
> physics held faster and with less of the lone healer's overdraw risk.

**[design]** What is designed: the *heal-song as the shared-bind's composition*
— the phrase that holds the healer, patient, and surrounding singers on one
phase so their combined working is `M ≈ 1`. What is landed: the long bind
(the-healer §2), the multiplicity amplification of a phase-locked hold
(the-healer §2.2 — "couples at `M ≈ 1` the way any phase-locked working does"),
the toll's honest ledger (the-healer §3 — the toll is spent, but it is what the
shared phase amortizes, never removed: the no-free-energy cap holds, the-burden
§2b/the-healer §2.2). The heal-song does not repeal the toll — it composes the
bind so the same healing is held once, not fought separately. **[design]**
Whether a *shared* heal-song's toll-cycles actually read lower than a lone
healer's (the multiplicity gain at bind scale) is a Phase-1.5 measurement over

Each of the three is a designed act for a designed field-op — the tool's lot,

## 4. The honest cost

### 4.1 A song that stops IS a broken phase-lock

The working-song's gain is held *only while the song is held*. There is
**no hysteresis and no stored phase-lock** — the phase-lock is not a state the
group achieves and keeps; it is a *maintained rhythm* that must be sung to be
held:

> **A song that stops IS a broken phase-lock.** The moment the rhythm falters —
> a singer drops, the tempo slips, the chant ends — the group's ops stop
> composing. N workers' strokes scatter back to N uncoordinated perturbations,
> the shared vent un-amortizes, and **the group's efficiency snaps back to
> ordinary**: each op is again its own `(1−q)` floor (energy-harnessing §2)
> instead of the once-per-working waste of the phase-locked state. There is no
> carryover, no "the camp remembered the beat for a minute" — the working was
> the rhythm, and when the rhythm stops, the working is gone.

This is the honest, load-bearing asymmetry: a working's gain is **not owned, it
is performed**. The festival is a one-time chord and its re-bind lingers
(`the-festival.md` §2.2 — the re-raised core and re-bound names persist after
the celebration, within the bed/core's q-draw). **The working-song is the
inverse**: it is a *continuous* act whose *only* effect exists while it is
performed — the held rhythm IS the phase-lock, and there is no post-song
residue to bank. This is what makes the working-song a *practice* and not a
buff: its gain is the act of singing, not a meter that filled.

### 4.2 The honest cost is the singers' attention and the rhythm's maintenance

Because the song's gain is performed, its cost is the performance:

| The cost | What it is | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **The singers' attention** | a working-song is *maintained attention* — the members hold the rhythm for the shift, following the composed score (field-music §4), matching phase (field-npc-ai §5.2). A member who drops attention drops the phase, and the working scatters | the phase-lock must be *held* (field-npc-ai §2.2 — coordinating is earned); the honest cost of a maintained act |
| **The rhythm's maintenance** | the song is not free sound — it is the group *performing* the work-rhythm, spending the same coherence the work itself spends (the ops the rhythm phases are real ops with real `(1−q)` cost), plus the members' coordinated vent | energy-harnessing §6 — music costs coherence (field-music §4.1); the `(1−q)` waste is the floor, and a phase-locked working's floor is the once-per-working one, never zero (energy-harnessing §2) |
| **The broken-lock's return** | when the song stops, the group does not merely lose the gain — it *snaps back to ordinary* (§4.1): the scattered ops waste their individual `(1−q)` floors again. The cost of stopping is the loss of the performed working, immediate and complete | the honest asymmetry of §4.1 — no hysteresis |

**Never free, never a mint.** The working-song cannot be looped to harvest
phase-locks — a song that "runs on its own" is not sung, and an unsurged rhythm
holds no phase (the sung rhythm is the device; a recording that plays without
the group's attention composes nothing, per §4.1). And the song coordinates
*existing* work; it never generates work or coherence (§5d). The honest cost
is simply the singers' attention and the rhythm's maintenance — the price of
holding many hands on one phase, and the reason a working-song is a *craft*,
not a cheat.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — a song coordinates, never generates

`energy-harnessing.md` §6 inherited unchanged: **no conversion yields more than

> **A song coordinates, never generates — the phase-lock's gain is the group's
> own composed efficiency, never a mint.** The working-song composes operations
> the group is already doing; it does not create work, coherence, or matter. The
> phase-locked working's output is bounded by its members' channeled input —
> the multiplicity gain (`M ≈ 1`) being the only amplification, and that gain is
> the corpus's own designed commons law (field-npc-ai §3.2). There is no song
> that yields more than its singers channel: a lumber-camp wastes *less*, it
> never harvests extra coherence off the beat; a forge-chant holds a discharge
> *on* the line, it never releases more work than the metal's stored coherence;
> a heal-song composes the toll's shared hold, it never removes the toll the
> bind transfers.

Three hard consequences:
1. **A working-song cannot mint.** No member's op exceeds `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`;
   the whole song's gain is bounded by the sum of its members' channeled input.
   A camp that "sings to recharge for free" is impossible — the singing *is* the
   spending.
2. **A working-song cannot be farmed.** Because a song that runs without the
   group's attention holds no phase (§4.1), and because the gain is the 
   performed `(1−q)` efficiency (never a stored state), a settlement cannot
   loop a song off the calendar to harvest the phase-lock — the phase-lock must
   be re-performed every shift, at the real coherence cost of the work itself.
3. **A working-song cannot be free.** The rhythm's gain is the group's own
   composed work efficiency, at the `(1−q)` waste of a working (never zero,
   energy-harnessing §2); a song that held the group at full efficiency for
   free would violate the cap. **The honest cost of §4 — the singers' attention
   and the rhythm's maintenance — is the cap's external audit**: a song that
   costs nothing would be a cheat, and the continuous (1−q) hum of a held
   rhythm is the visible proof the work is genuinely being coordinated, not
   waved.

## Open questions

1. **The alignment dial at work scale (§2/§5b).** At what phase tolerance and
   tempo do a group's *work ops* (strokes, transfers, holds) compose at `M ≈ 1`
   — and how does the group know it is aligned (the audible efficiency hum,
   field-music §1; the lower `(1−q)` read)? The alignment rule is a [design]
   dial over the corpus's commons law, the same probe the festival's chord
   rides; the `(1−q)` probe (§6) is its pre-registered measurement.
   **[design]/[probe]**
2. **Does the phase-lock's `(1−q)` gain read felt at work-pace (§3a/§6)?** A
   lone stroke's `(1−q)` floor is small; does a phase-locked camp's lower waste
   read *measurably* over the published `q`/ε², or does the spread wash out at
   swing-pace (pushing the working-song's gain to presentational)? The same
   honest question the vigil's exposure and the tool's wear pre-register; a flat
   read is a legitimate, weaker finding. **[probe]**
3. **The song-that-stops boundary (§4.1).** How complete is the broken
   phase-lock's snap-back — does a momentary slip scatter the working for a
   beat and re-compose, or is a dropped song a full return to ordinary until a
   fresh song re-holds it? The design's honest default is *complete* (no
   hysteresis, no stored phase-lock), but whether a *partial* rhythm (a few
   members holding) composes a partial working is a designed dial over the
   alignment rule, never an accidental carryover. **[design]**
4. **The three songs' separation (§3).** Do the lumber-camp's `(1−q)`-composing
   rhythm, the forge-chant's rate-pacing, and the heal-song's toll-composition
   read as *distinct* designed acts, or do they collapse into "one phase-lock
   applied to three fields"? The design intends three honest emphases of the
   same law (each grounds a different landed mechanics); whether they remain
   mechanically distinct is a Phase-1.5 judgment over the probes.
   **[design]**
5. **The song's duration and the shift's honesty (§4.2).** How long is a
   working-song held — a shift, a work-cycle, until the op is done — and is a
   fully-maintained rhythm (the singers' full attention, no drop) a *realistic*
   ask over that duration, or does the rhythm's maintenance become the work
   itself (a song that is *more* tiring than the work it paces)? The honest
   frame is that a working-song is never free whether or not it is used; whether
   a long-held song's attraction-cost *feels* costed is a Phase-1.5 design
   judgment. **[design]**
