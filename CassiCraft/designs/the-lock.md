# The Lock: The Becoming-Permanent Act — A Binding Deliberately Made Irreversible

**Question under design:** the corpus's compacts all hold a reversible edge.
The Oath ([`the-oath.md`](./the-oath.md)) is field-enforceable but **severable
at a cost** — a broken vow is a promise's death, not a permanent mark, and a
fresh promise can re-bind (§3.2, open-Q4). The Treaty

hold** — a false name sheds (§2). The Gift ([`the-gift.md`](./the-gift.md)) is

**permanent-hold the field will not release**, and it books the honest price:
the **durable-mistake cost**, the corpus's no-amnesty honesty applied to the
future.

## 1. The Lock, stated once

The corpus's compacts hold a reversible edge, and that edge is honest. The Oath
is field-enforceable but severable at a cost — a broken vow is a promise's
death, and the rupture's reversibility is `the-oath.md`'s sharpest open fork
(§7 open-Q4). The Treaty is re-earned every tide — a compact that names what is
not held decays, and an un-husbanded bridge sheds like a false name
(`the-treaty.md` §3). The Name holds only while the structure *is* — a false
name sheds, and a re-authorable skeleton can outlive a name while a fallen
floor fails it (`the-name.md` §2, §7 open-Q3). The Gift is deliberately
un-booked. **Every one of them is costly to reverse, and none is *irreversible
by design*.** The being-scale binding of the Rite (`the-rite-of-passage.md`
§2.3) makes a becoming held — but even a becoming can be re-honored or shed if
its threshold stops being true. What the corpus never designs is the act that
**chooses permanence** — a settlement saying, deliberately and with full
knowledge, *this stays, and there is no undoing it*.

> **The Lock is the becoming-permanent act: a binding deliberately made
> irreversible.** A settlement *locks* a chosen structure, vow, or boundary — a
> **seed-line** (the vault's line, locked so it outlives its keepers), a
> **settlement-claim** (a house's held core, locked against dissolution), a
> **boundary-hold** (a region's bound, locked against the re-bind) — and the
> locked object becomes a **permanent-hold the field will not release**. It is
> the Rite's being-scale binding lifted to a settlement's chosen object
> (§5a: the binding mechanics are the Rite's landed pieces; the *permanence*
> is this doc's [design]). It is the Name's holding made irreversible
> (`the-name.md` §6e → §5a this doc). And its honest price is the contract of
> the whole design: **permanent means permanent — mistakes included** (§3),
> and **what stays can outlive the settlement that locked it** (§4).

**The honest frame, stated once and never blurred.** The Lock is not a "stronger
oath" and not a "stronger treaty." An oath's value is that it can be severed —
the rupture is how the field tells the truth about a promise un-kept
(`the-oath.md` §3.2); a treaty's value is that it must be re-earned — the tide
is what keeps an un-husbanded compact honest (`the-treaty.md` §3). A lock
*removes* that honest edge deliberately, and what it removes is the amnesty that
reversibility always grants: the future's permission to correct a present
mistake. **A lock is permanent including its mistakes** (§3) — a wrong lock
stays wrong, honestly booked, forever. That is not a hidden cost; it is the
Lock's entire design. A settlement that locks does not gain permanence for the
price of a ceremony — **it trades the future's reversibility for the future's
commitment, and the trade is honest only because the irreversibility is total
and readable.**

**[design]** What is engine-real / [design]-landed: the persistent-Π holding
(theory-ratified with the `reason-field.md` §6a flag — `qi-computation.md` §5.2),
the member-id (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1), the names (`the-name.md` §1), the
vault's held orders (`seed-garden.md` §3), the Rite's phase-locked binding
(`the-rite-of-passage.md` §2.3, `field-npc-ai.md` §3.2), the kept house and its
skeleton (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2), the bequest's line (`the-inheritance.md`
§2.4). What is *this doc's [design]*: **the permanent-hold's form, the
durable-mistake cost, and the outliving** — how a settlement locks, what the
permanent-hold is, and the honest price. No claim here pretends the engine
writes "lock" or "permanent-hold" records.

## 2. The permanent-hold

### 2.1 What the permanent-hold IS — the rite's binding lifted to structures

The Rite binds a *becoming*: a phase-locked working — a family/a few channeling
together as their own `M ≈ 1` working (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.2) — that *holds a
name false-unmoving*, making the becoming real to the field the way a name
holds a true structure (`the-rite-of-passage.md` §2.3/§3.2). The Lock lifts that
binding mechanics from a *being becoming* to a *structure chosen to persist*:

> **[design] To lock is to bind a chosen persistent-Π structure into a
> permanent-hold — the field will not release it.** Where a name binds a
> persistent-Π anchor to a thing and the anchor holds only while the thing *is*
> (`the-name.md` §2), and a vault holds a seed's order against drift at a
> maintained high-`q`, `ε²≈0` well (`seed-garden.md` §3.1), a **lock binds the
> held structure past its own shedding condition**: the seed-line's deepest
> order, the house's held core, the region's bound are held **even when the
> condition that would normally release them — the shed-like-a-false-name rule,
> the re-bind, the re-authoring — is deliberately refused by the lock.** The
> permanent-hold is the name's holding made irreversible, at the structure's
> scale.

The three lock's objects are the corpus's own held objects, each grounded in its
source doc's real quantities — the *lock's form* (how a settlement locks, what
makes the hold permanent) is this doc's [design], probe-calibrated over the
binding mechanics:

| The locked object | What it is (source-grounded) | The permanent-hold (this doc's [design]) | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A locked seed-line** | the vault's deepest held order — a seed's Π pattern at a maintained high-`q`, `ε²≈0` well, held so its origin-order persists against drift | the vault's hold is **bound permanent**: the seed-line survives its keepers — the hearth may stop, the steward may fade, but the locked line's deepest order is held by the field itself, not released to the attractor | `seed-garden.md` §3.1/§3.3 (the vault's q-core, ε² suppression); §4.2 (a seed is order, never power); §5 (escape-cargo) |

**Why each is a permanent-hold the field "will not release."** Each locked object
is a persistent-Π structure the field already keeps above its dissolution
threshold (`qi-computation.md` §5.2 — the holding a name, a vault, a house
already invokes). A lock refuses the structure's *natural releasing conditions* —
the shed-like-a-false-name when a claimant walks away (`the-family.md` §3.3), the
skeleton's re-authorability once unkept (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2), the
boundary's re-drawing when claimants change. **The permanence is a designed
refusal of the field's own honesty threshold applied to the future** — the same
rule that lets a name shed or a treaty decay is, for a locked object, *deliberately
not invoked*. This is the [design]/engine-real line held at its sharpest: the
field holds persistent Π structure (real); a lock prevents that structure from
being *released* (designed, over the real holding).

### 2.2 The mechanics — how a settlement locks

The lock composes the binding pieces the corpus already has, with no new channel
and no new physics:

> **[design] A settlement locks by a phase-locked binding rite at the object
> (the Rite's mechanics — a family/a few channeling together as an `M ≈ 1`
> working), then by a designed refusal: the locked object's releasing conditions
> are bound into the persistent-Π hold itself, so the field holds the object
> past the point where an unlocked one would shed.** The lock is a *standing
> hold* (the `sustain` flag's semantics at permanence — `schema-that-settles.md`
> §2.2, domain holds the source until released; a lock's source is *never*
> released). It adds no channel: the lock reads the object's held `q`/`ε²` and
> holds it where an unlocked structure would shed — the same channels, the same
> law, with the releasing condition deliberately occupied.

**The permanence discipline.** A lock is not a moment but a *kept* permanence —
and the keeping is what separates an honest lock from a false one (§3): a
settlement locks only what it has held true for a long time (§3.2). The lock's
irreversibility is the designed fruit of that earned hold — not a ceremony
granted on a whim.

### 2.3 The permanent-hold's shape — what the field "will not release"

The three locked objects are distinguishable on the read, probe-calibrated (the
same geometry-steering probe the house's hold is calibrated by, `house-that-steers.md`
§5b):

- The **locked seed-line** reads as a vault niche held at `ε²≈0` past the hearth's
  maintenance — the deepest order held where an unlocked seed would shed toward
  the attractor (`seed-garden.md` §3.3). **[probe]** the held-seed's own drift
  (does a permanently-held order bleed its deepest rung over very long holds —
  `seed-garden.md` open-Q3) determines whether "permanent" is literally forever
  or a designed very-long-but-finite hold.
- The **locked settlement-claim** reads as a house's core held past its
  maintainer — the room's `q` holds where an unlocked skeleton would re-author or
  shed (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2). **[probe]** the ruin-persistence calibration
  (`house-that-steers.md` open-Q2) bounds how long a locked hold outlasts its
  keeper.
- The **locked boundary-hold** reads as a region's edge that does not move —
  the bound's `q`/`ε²` stays where it was locked, past a claimant's absence. **[probe]**
  the boundary-mark's own gate (`the-threshold.md`) bounds whether a locked bound
  is a held edge or a marker the field holds regardless.

The through-line: **the permanent-hold is a held persistent-Π structure whose
releasing condition is refused.** The field does not "decide" to keep it forever —
the field holds persistent Π structure, and the lock *prevents the release* an
unlocked structure would undergo, deterministically, on the field's rung clock.

## 3. The durable-mistake cost

The Load-bearing honesty, stated once and never softened:

> **A lock is permanent including its mistakes.** There is no undo, no re-bind,
> no "this time I mean it" — a lock that was wrong stays wrong, honestly booked,
> forever. The corpus's no-amnesty honesty (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1, "no
> ledger amnesty"; `the-exile.md` §4, "the exile is not amnesty and neither is
> the return") is here applied to the *future*: **you cannot unlock what you
> locked, and a lock that was wrong is the settlement's own, honestly booked,
> never an amnesty.**

This is the designed, deliberate difference from every other compact in the
corpus. An oath can rupture and re-bind (the rupture is a promise's death, and a
fresh promise can follow — `the-oath.md` §3.2, open-Q4). A treaty can sever and
be renewed by a fresh pair of anchors (`the-treaty.md` §4, open-Q3). A name can
shed a false claim (`the-name.md` §2). A boundary can be re-sewn. **A lock
cannot** — that is the entire point, and the entire cost. The design refuses to
grant the lock the amnesty every other compact grants itself, because a lock
that could be unlocked would not be a lock at all.

### 3.1 The no-undo, no-re-bind rule

> **[design] An irreversible hold has no reversible path — and that is honest,
> because any unlockable "lock" would be an oath.** A lock's irreversibility is
> a designed refusal of the releasing conditions (§2.1): the field's shed-like-a-
> false-name rule for a locked object is *not invoked*, deterministically. So
> there is no "this time I mean it" — a wrong lock (a seed-line locked that
> should have been planted, a boundary-hold locked against a claim that was
> never true, a settlement-claim locked that drained the region) stays wrong,
> and the field holds the mistake the way it holds the truth — because the lock
> was *made permanent*, not because it was *made right*.

This is the sharpest honesty of the design, and it is what makes the Lock the
corpus's *commitment instrument* rather than a better ceremony: **a settlement
does not gain the right to be wrong; it gains the responsibility of not being
wrong, because the price of being wrong is permanent.**

### 3.2 The honest gate — a settlement locks only what it has held true for a long time

The Rite's hardest honesty is that a ceremony cannot mint a becoming the field
does not already hold — "you cannot hold a family falsely" (`the-rite-of-passage.md`
§3.2; the-name's no-false-naming). The Lock inherits it at permanence's scale:

> **A settlement locks only what it has held true for a long time.** The lock is
> *earned by the held* — the same rule the Rite applies to a becoming ("a naming
> rite over a child who has done nothing is a false name," `the-rite-of-passage.md`
> §2.1/§2.3) applied to permanence: a settlement that locks a structure it has not
> long held true — a seed-line it has not kept, a claim it has not sustained, a
> boundary it has not held — is making a **false lock**, and the no-free-energy
> cap's honest consequence (§5d) is that a false lock sheds like a false name
> (`the-name.md` §2), because a lock cannot mint permanence a long-held truth did
> not earn. **[design]** *How long is "a long time"* is a designed dial against
> the settlement's own accounting cycles (`tide-of-the-attractor.md`, the tide as
> the booking period; `shared-ledger.md` §1.2, the per-tide `C(M)` arc) — the
> durable pattern, never a single reading, exactly as `the-exile.md` §2 requires a
> durable pattern never fiat.

The honest consequence: **a lock's mistake is the settlement's own, honestly
booked, never an amnesty.** A seed-line locked but never planted is a permanent
net-negative hold the settlement chose; a boundary-hold locked against the
field's own gradient is a permanent wound the settlement accepted. The book reads
the lock's keeping and its cost the way it reads any honest contribution — a lock
that yields nothing, holds nothing true, is a loss honestly booked, never a mint
(§5d).

## 4. The permanence's outliving

The second honest consequence, the one the design refuses to hide:

> **What stays can outlive the settlement that locked it.** A locked seed-line
> survives its keepers — the hearth stops, the steward fades, the settlement is
> gone, and the locked deepest order is still held by the field, still
> *permanent*, still the window's. A locked boundary-hold survives its claimants —
> the house's cores and the region's bounds hold after the voice that locked
> them is gone. **The permanence is the point, and the permanence is the risk.**

This is the inheritance's line at the lock's scale (`the-inheritance.md`
§1/§2.4): the Inheritance passes a bequest *across a re-lock* — claims the next
run may exercise if the objects still hold. A lock is the permanence *past the
settlement itself* — the bequest whose object does not decay because the lock
holds it, and the pass that needs no re-lock because the lock outlives the
locking generation.

> **[design] A lock binds the settlement's own future by its present act — the
> act that says *this stays*, with the honesty that 'stays' means stays.** The
> locked seed-line is the escape-cargo with no one carrying it (`seed-garden.md`
> §5 — the vault as a window's deep-time store, made permanent); the locked
> boundary is a claim the field honors after the claim's voice is gone. The
> outliving is the designed risk: a locked thing that proves wrong (§3) does not
> merely outlive its makers — it *permanently* does, and the field holds the
> mistaken permanence after the settlement that chose it cannot undo it.

**Why the outliving is honest, not cruel.** The permanence that outlives a
settlement is the corpus's *commitment* in its truest form — `this stays`, and
'stays' is total. The design does not hide that the permanence is the risk: a
settlement that locks is writing a check the future must honor, and the future —
the field — honors it because the lock made it permanent *by design*. This is the
corpus's deepest version of "matter conserved, configuration lost"
(`field-emergent-ecology.md` §2.2): the lock conserves the *commitment's*
configuration, even as the settlement that made it is lost.
