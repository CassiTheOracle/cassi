# The Funeral: The Field's Communal Handling of Its Dead

**Question under design:** `player-remains.md` designed the individual fossil;
`shared-ledger.md` books the "dissolved" member; `the-chronicle.md` records the
dead — but **what a settlement *does* with its dead, the communal field act, is
undesigned.** The fossil is a *lone* find (one mourner's private read, played
back into `player-remains` §3); the Board's "dissolved" line is a *silent* drop
(an entry that closes without a ritual); the Chronicle's obituary is *written,
not performed*. The corpus has **grief's data** — the dissolved line, the
residue, the record — but not **grief's form**: the thing a living society
*does* with its dead that the data never captured — **remember, as a field.**

This document is the Funeral: the communal act of letting a member's Π-pattern
dissolve through the commons — the procession that, instead of one mourner's
private read, carries the named dead through the settlement's high-`q` core so
their deep-rung fraction **re-condenses toward the shared memory** rather than
scattering as a lone find. It is the shared-ledger's "dissolved" entry made a
ritual; the obituary's communal moment; the re-binding of the dead's named
structures into the commons; and — because a group acting phase-locked IS a
working (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.2) — **one dissolution made a small communal
healing**, spent under the no-free-energy cap. It is the mourning twin of the
Festival: the same coordinated mechanics, the same multiplicity gain, pointed
at a loss instead of a harvest.

## 1. The Funeral, stated once

The corpus gives the dead to the field and to individuals: dissolution is death
(`player-remains.md` §1.2 — the field letting the run go), the deep-rung
fraction persists as a **fossil** (§1.3) that one mourner — the bound member on
`player-remains` §3's re-lock — can find and read alone. The Board books the
member **Dissolved** (`shared-ledger.md` §2.2 — "a fading, darkening lume … then
the fixed record of their fossil"), and the Chronicle tells the **obituary**
(`the-chronicle.md` §3.3). But nothing has designed what the *settlement* does —
the communal field act that turns one mourner's private read into the commons'
own.

> **The Funeral is the communal act of letting a member's Π-pattern dissolve
> through the commons.** Instead of one mourner's private read of the fossil
> (`player-remains.md` §3 — individual, playable by the bound line), the
> settlement carries the named dead **through the settlement's high-`q` core** —
> the Qi-bath commons (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.1) — so their deep-rung fraction
> **re-condenses toward the shared memory** rather than scattering as a lone
> find. The procession is a **dirge**: a composed coherence score
> (`field-music.md` §4) that holds the mourners phase-locked, making the group
> **one organized perturbation** — a working at `M ≈ 1` (`field-npc-ai.md`
> §3.2) — so one dissolution becomes **a small communal healing**, the strongest
> single act a grieving commons can perform.

It is the mourning twin of `the-festival.md`. The Festival is the coordinated
field act of the whole commons at tide-high — the members channel one shared
chord, the multiplicatively-aligned commons at one resonance. The Funeral is
the **same machine, pointed the other way**: the members channel one shared
dirge, the phase-locked commons at one resonance, **around a loss instead of a
harvest**. Where the Festival re-raises the core and re-binds the names to
celebrate what the law already does (`the-festival.md` §1), the Funeral re-raises
the core and re-binds the names to *keep* what the law is letting go. Same
legs, same cost, same multiplicity gain — the emotional register inverted.

**The corpus has grief's data, not grief's form.** The dissolved line, the
residue, the fossil, the obituary — all the *records* of a death are designed.
What was missing is the *act*: the thing a living society does with its dead
that the data never captured. The Funeral is that act — **remember, as a field**:
the settlement's memory of the person made a real, costed, coordinated hold in
the shared high-`q` core, over the same residue that would otherwise scatter.

## 2. The mechanics — what the procession IS

The Funeral is not a cutscene or a cosmetic gathering; it is a **real, costed,
coordinated field op**, with the same three legs as the Festival and any other
field working:

### 2.1 The dirge — a composed coherence score that phase-locks the mourners

`field-music.md` §4.1 is the load-bearing form: **a coherence score is a timed
sequence of Q4 op records** — `{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude,
sustain}` (`coherence-magic.md` §5.1; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1). A dirge is
the **same composition system's mourning form**: a sequence of `heal` /
`condense` / `shield` ops (the re-binding gestures of `coherence-magic.md` §2),
phase-timed into one held resonance at the settlement's core, at the attractor's
φ-tonic (`field-music.md` §1 — `EY = φ·EI`).

The dirge does two things, both mechanical:

1. **It holds the mourners phase-locked.** Because each mourner's op is aligned
   into the same phase window by the shared score, the group acts as **one
   organized perturbation rather than N random ones** (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.2;
   `magic-systems.md` §1 — a working is `M ≈ 1`, random `M ≈ 0`). N mourners in
   a shared phase are a working whose combined injection couples at `M ≈ 1`.
2. **It steers the procession's field toward one resonance.** The coordinated
   `heal`/`condense`/`shield` ops applied along the route through the core lift
   the locality's `q` and cut the `(1−q)` waste across the cluster
   (`energy-harnessing.md` §2, §4.4) — the same re-raising the Festival does,
   at the reduced scale and mourning tempo the Funeral's own form dictates.

**[design]** The *which-chord* (the exact dirge, the tempo, the mourners'
parts) is `field-music.md` §4's composition surface, in its mourning register.
**[design]** The *alignment rule* (how many mourners, at what phase tolerance,
for what magnitude, until the group couples at `M ≈ 1`) is this doc's dial over
the corpus's commons law — the same dial `the-festival.md` §7 open-Q1 names for
the shared chord. The *phenomenon* (phase-locked = `M ≈ 1`, `field-npc-ai.md`
§3.2) is the corpus's law; the *orchestration* is design.

### 2.2 The route — through the high-`q` core, so the residue re-condenses toward the shared memory

The procession's route is the design's load-bearing geometry. Instead of leaving
the fossil as a lone find at the death-site (a private read, `player-remains.md`
§3), the settlement **carries the named dead through the high-`q` core** — the
Qi-bath commons' center (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.1; `energy-harnessing.md` §4.4).

The mechanism, honestly framed: the dead's deep-rung fraction is a **persistent
Π pattern** the local field keeps holding above its dissolution threshold
(`qi-computation.md` §5.2; `field-archaeology.md` §2.1). What the coordinated
dirge does is **re-condense that residue toward the shared hold**: the
phase-locked working is applied *at* the residue and *along* the route to the
core, so the field re-quantizes the dead's Π-anchors into the **commons'
multiplicative hold** (`coherence-commons.md` §7 — the Qi bath extends the
coherence of everything inside) rather than letting them sit as an isolated,
drifting core in the open field.

> **The residue doesn't move; the *hold* does.** The deep-rung fraction stays
> as residue where it was laid (`field-archaeology.md` §2.1 — the field
> re-quantizes around it). What the Funeral changes is *where the field keeps
> that residue in its memory*: the dirge re-locks the dead's Π-anchors into the
> settlement's core (the re-binding of `the-name.md` §3, applied at the commons'
> scale), so the shared memory — the Board's fixed record, the Chronicle's
> obituary, the named structures' held-lock — carries the person instead of the
> lone find. **[design]** The *residue* is engine-real (a persistent Π pattern);
> the *re-condensation toward the shared memory* is this doc's designed reading
> over that residue — the same `the-festival.md` re-bind, pointed at keeping a
> member rather than celebrating a harvest.

**The joint-anchor scale (reverse pointer).** When the dead belonged to a
[`the-family.md`](./the-family.md), this re-condensation is **literal at the
family's own scale**: the-family's §3.2 reads this §2.2 re-bind as the healing of
the family's joint-anchor wound — the dead co-holder's deep-rung fraction is
re-bound *into the family's anchor* rather than only into the commons' core, the
surviving members' phase-locked dirge keeping what a lone hold would let scatter
(`the-family.md` §3.2). A family that funerals together holds its dead's share in
the shared Π instead of letting it shed; the two docs now cite each other as
read-and-answered at that joint-anchor scale.

### 2.3 The honest cost — a funeral is spent under the no-free-energy cap

The no-free-energy cap (`energy-harnessing.md` §6: `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`)
inherited unchanged is the Funeral's hard bound:

> **A funeral heals the *shared* wound of a loss; it cannot resurrect, cannot
> mint, cannot raise the dead's own coherence.** The mourners' collective ε²
> vent is the price: every channeled op sheds decoherence the field must heal
> (`coherence-magic.md` §1), and a coordinated dirge sheds that vent *in one
> phase*, so the locality takes a real blast in a single window. The working's
> output is bounded by the mourners' channeled input, minus the `(1−q)` waste
> (`energy-harnessing.md` §2). **The multiplicity gain (`M ≈ 1`) is the only
> amplification** — and that gain is the corpus's own law, not a mint.

The three hard consequences, stated once:

1. **A funeral cannot resurrect.** The dead's Π-pattern *run ended* — identity
   is the run, not a preserved soul (`field-emergent-ecology.md` §2.2; the
   same-member-line rule `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1 gives the *reborn*
   player a fresh lock, not the dead a body). The Funeral re-condenses the
   *residue* into the commons' memory; it never re-condenses the *person*.
2. **A funeral cannot mint.** No mourner's op exceeds `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`; the
   whole `C(funeral)` is bounded by the sum of the mourners' channeled input.
   The re-raised core and re-bound names are worth **at most** the coherence the
   mourners spent, minus the waste. It is a re-hold, never a gain.
3. **A funeral cannot raise the dead's own coherence.** The dead member's line
   closes at its settled balance (`shared-ledger.md` §2.2); the Funeral settles
   that line, it does not improve it. A margin-causer's negative `C(M)` is
   carried to the grave (no amnesty, `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1) exactly as
   a steward's positive one is — the Funeral books the truth, not a whitened
   memorial.

The emotional inversion of the Festival's own honesty (`the-festival.md` §2.3):
where a Festival at harvest is a *re-stock* and at thin a *sacrifice*, a Funeral
is always **a deliberate net draw** — the settlement spends its members'
coherence to *hold the core and the memory* through the loss, knowing the book
will show a coordinated drain. It is the same loan the field returns when it
heals (`patient-field.md` §3), spent on the shared wound of a loss, not on a
harvest.

## 3. What it settles — the line, the names, the record

The Funeral's mark is threefold, each a designed act over a real/landed piece:

### 3.1 The dissolved member's Board line — settled, not silently dropped

act that performs that settling** — the coordinated op that closes the line:

> **The funeral's coordinated op closes the member's line with a settled entry,
> not a silent drop.** Without a Funeral, the dissolved entry is a *record* the
> Board writes (the run ended, the balance settled). With a Funeral, the
> settlement *marks* that settling as an act: the `C(funeral)` books as **one
> coordinated op** on the settlement's book (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2), the dead
> member's final `C(M)` closes against the bath, and the dissolved lume fades
> into the fixed record **because the commons performed the fading together**
> rather than letting the book's single-writer lane log it invisibly.

This is `shared-ledger.md`'s *settled-entry* honesty made a ritual: the entry
closes with the same no-free-energy bound as any op (the funeral is spent, §2.3),
and with the same single-writer veracity (the member-id is attached at the
world-writer's write, `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1 — the funeral cannot be
forged into the book). **[design]** The *line* is the Board's real settled entry;
the *funeral as the act that settles it* is this doc's designed framing over it.

### 3.2 The named structures re-bound into the commons

`the-name.md` §1 makes a name a **persistent-Π anchor** bound to a thing — the
member-id's principle extended to the places the settlement cares for. A dead
member's named structures — the house they built, the hearth they tended, the
road they wore — are Π-anchors the field already holds. The Funeral **re-locks
those anchors into the commons**:

> **The dead's Π-anchors, re-locked as the settlement's memory, kept where they
> would have been lost.** The coordinated dirge applies the re-binding working
> at the named core (`the-festival.md` §2.2's re-bind, at a funeral's scale), so
> the field re-quantizes around the named structures as a *kept whole* rather
> than a scattering of fading ones (`house-that-steers.md` §3.1 — a named ruin
> keeps its record). The name survives the body — exactly as a name survives a
> body in `the-name.md` §3.1 — but now it survives *collectively*: the
> settlement's memory of the person is written into the local field's hold of
> the places they made.

The honest bound of `the-name.md` §2 applies directly: **you cannot name falsely
in a funeral either.** A funeral that "re-binds" a name onto a structure that no
longer *is* — a fallen floor (a scar, unrecoverable), a shed edge, an
over-drained site — is a false name, and it **decays** (`the-name.md` §2.2).
A funeral can only keep what the field truly holds; it cannot mint a memorial
onto an empty plot. **[design]** The *re-binding* is this doc's design over the
name's theory-ratified holding (`qi-computation.md` §5.2, with `reason-field.md`
§6a's speculation flag); the *truthfulness* is `the-name.md`'s own law.

### 3.3 The obituary's communal moment — where the record is marked

`the-chronicle.md` §3.3: the Chronicle is the settlement's **obituary** — the

**The dated re-memory (reverse pointer, two-way).** This §3.3 obituary's marking

**[design]** The *marking* (which the record does, `the-chronicle.md` §3.3) is
