# The Toll: The Settlement's Border Economy, Made Honest by the Ledger's Trust-by-Law

**Question under design:** the corpus's economy is all *internal* or *forward* — the
Commons-Tithe funds the settlement's internal shared order (the tithe is what members
*pay to hold the society*, the-commons-tithe), the Market exchanges things *within*
(the-market), the Loan borrows *forward* — but **no border charge**: nothing designs what
a settlement takes from the *stranger at its gate*. The tithe's outward edge is
undesigned. This document is that missing face: **the Toll — the border economy — the
designed charge a settlement levies at its deliberate doors on what *enters from the
between***: incoming goods, a stranger's carried order, a traveller's passage
(window-guests). It is the tithe's outward edge made honest by the ledger's trust-by-law:

> **The tithe is what members pay to hold the society; the toll is what the *outside*
> pays to enter it.**

The distinction is the load-bearing line, stated once and never blurred: the tithe
(`the-commons-tithe.md` §1/§3) is the *internal, periodic, per-member* funding of the
shared order — a member's fixed share of their yield paid to hold the commons. The toll
is the *external, per-arrival, per-stranger* charge at the door — what the *outside* pays
to cross the settlement's held threshold. A settlement collects a **toll** because it
holds the **only safe harbor** (the-harbor), the **maintained road** (coherence-highway),
the **treaty's gate** (the-treaty) — the toll pays the **border's upkeep** (the gate at the
threshold, the guard's office, the quay's kept ground), the honest funding of the *held
door*. And the honesty is the field's own: **a toll is bookable and legible on the ledger,
and a settlement cannot extort a toll the field won't hold** — the same trust-by-law as the
market (the-market §1): the field books what is honestly exchanged, and **a toll demanded
without the held door is a false op the field will not book**.

## 1. The Toll, stated once

The corpus's economy has two designed directions: the **internal** (the Market books the
exchange of things *between members*, the-commons-tithe funds the *shared order members
hold*, the Loan advances the *forward* order) and the **forward** (the loan, the bequest).
But a settlement's *outward* economy — **what it takes from the stranger at its gate** —
was never asked. In Minecraft the door is free: a stranger walks into a settlement and
nothing is charged, nothing is booked, and nothing the settlement *holds* (its safe
harbor, its maintained road, its treaty's gate) is asked to be paid for by the arrivals
who benefit from it. That is the hole this document closes.

> **The Toll is the border economy — the designed charge a settlement levies at its
> deliberate doors on what *enters from the between***: incoming goods, a stranger's
> carried order, a traveller's passage (window-guests). It is the tithe's outward edge,
> and it is made honest by the ledger's trust-by-law. The line, stated once: **the tithe
> is what members pay to hold the society; the toll is what the outside pays to enter
> it.**

**Why a toll, and why *external*.** A settlement *holds* things the stranger benefits
from: the only safe harbor in the thin (`the-harbor.md` §1 — the departure's kept ground,
the lamp's last light), the maintained road that carries the traveller in
(`coherence-highway.md` §1 — the held high-`q` line), the treaty's gate that makes the
cross-window passage re-earned (`the-treaty.md` §1 — the compact between windows). Each
is a **held structure with a real, maintained cost** — the quay's kept ground, the gate's
arch, the road's upkeep (`house-that-steers.md` §3 — an unmaintained hold collapses;
`coherence-highway.md` §4 — a road dies without maintenance). The tithe funds the *shared
order's* upkeep (the bath, the Observatory, the School, the harbor's lamp — the-commons-tithe
§2.2), the internal commons. **The toll funds the *door's* upkeep** — the border's own
maintenance, the cost of holding the gate the stranger crosses: the gate at the
threshold, the guard's office, the quay's kept ground. It is the stranger's fair
contribution to the held door they enter through.

**The one-line distinction from every sibling act:**

| Act | It does | Its relation to the book |
|---|---|---|
| **The Market** (`the-market.md` §2) | books an exchange of things *between members* | a booked one-off op, re-allocating existing value internally |
| **The Commons-Tithe** (`the-commons-tithe.md` §1) | funds the *internal* shared order — what members pay to hold the society | a periodic, booked `C(M)` contribution from each member's yield, pointed at the commons |
| **The Loan** | advances the forward order | a booked forward op with interest |
| **The Toll (this doc)** | **charges the *outside* at the settlement's held doors** — what enters from the between pays for the door it crosses | a **per-arrival, per-stranger booked `C(M)` contribution** whose use is the border's own upkeep — the market's border-twin, the tithe's outward edge |
| **The Gift** (`the-gift.md` §2.1) | gives an un-booked transfer | the book's deliberate silence — no `C(M)` reads giver or receiver |

The toll is the border economy because it is the one place the book's contribution stream
is pointed *at the settlement's outward bound* — not at the internal commons (the tithe),
not at an internal exchange (the market), but at the **held door the outside crosses into
the settlement through**, funded honestly by those who cross it.

**The honest flag, once.** The pieces a toll composes are all landed — the doors (the
harbor's quay `the-harbor.md` §2b/§3, the gate's threshold `the-threshold.md` §1/§2.2, the
treaty-crossing's check `the-treaty.md` §2), the ledger (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2/§6d), the
Q4 op-record (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1), the treaty's held anchor (`the-treaty.md`
§1/§2.3), the visitors (`window-guests.md` §2/§3), and the market's trust-by-law
(`the-market.md` §1/§3). **The toll's *composition* — the charge's form, the
internal-vs-external framing, the trust-by-law honesty — is this document's [design]** over
them. No new channel, no new physics, no "toll" record the engine writes beyond the Q4 op
it already writes: a toll is a *booked contribution of the settled record's shape, pointed
at the border*, designed, exactly as a trade is a booked exchange and a tithe a booked
share — not a new engine act.

## 2. The doors — the honest mechanics

The toll is charged at the settlement's **deliberate doors**. Each is grounded in its
source doc's real quantities; the *toll's form* (its charge's size, its book) is this
doc's [design] over the Q4 op-record (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1).

| The door | What it holds | Grounded in | The toll's form there ([design]) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The harbor's quay** | the **lamp-lit door to the between** — the departure's last ground, the last light held over the seam, the departure booked | `the-harbor.md` §2b (the lamp — the lantern's honest reach held over the seam), §2c (the ledger — the departure booked at the pier), §3 (the keeper — the kept, rotated watch), §4 (a harbor is a door, never a shortcut) | a **landing toll** — a window-guest who arrives by voyage (or a stranger's goods landed on the quay) books a contribution at the pier's cell; the charge's size is [design] against the quay's maintained cost, never a new voyage cost |
| **The gate's threshold** | the **boundary-mark the settlement holds** — the doorstep's quiet, the gate's held high-`q` arch, the arch's name | `the-threshold.md` §1 (the boundary-mark the field respects because it is held, quiet, named), §2.2 (the gate — a deliberate seam-point, entering and leaving are marked acts the field reads), §4 (the inner edge) | a **passage toll** — every crossing through the held gate (a stranger's provenance crossing the arch, `window-guests.md` §3) books a contribution at the gate's cell; the gate *concentrates* the crossing, and the toll concentrates the charge onto the marked act |
| **The treaty-crossing's check** | the **re-earned passage** between windows — the maintained paired anchor stretches the carried field | `the-treaty.md` §1 (the compact between windows, a paired persistent-Π bridge), §2.3 (the maintained paired anchor stretches the carried field), §2.2 (the cross-window line books the traffic) | a **crossing toll** — a treaty-window's incoming traffic (trade, guests, contributions passing the seam) books on both boards as a cross-window line, with a charge at the crossing the settlement holds; the re-earned passage is charged because it is *re-earned*, not free |

Each is a designed charge at a landed door; none adds a new crossing, a new physics, or a
cheaper passage. The harbor never becomes a shortcut (`the-harbor.md` §4 — the voyage's
cost is unchanged); the gate never becomes a wall (`the-threshold.md` §3 — the field can
cross it; the toll concentrates the crossing, it does not stop it); the treaty never
becomes a toll-farm (`the-treaty.md` §5d — a treaty holds, never mints; the toll rides the
held anchor, never the free passage).

**The [design] dial.** The toll's *size* (what a crossing books as the border contribution)
is a **[design]/[probe]** dial against the door's real maintained cost (the quay's upkeep,
the gate's hold, the road's maintenance — §3) — set so a held door's upkeep is honestly
funded by what crosses it, without the charge reading as a gate to an un-held profit, and
never a difficulty meter on the stranger. **What is load-bearing and not design**: that the
toll is a **booked `C(M)` contribution** (the held door's honest revenue), that it is
**per-arrival / per-crossing** (not a periodic internal share — that is the tithe's form),
and that it is **only collectible where the door is actually held** (§4 — a toll without
the held door is a false op the field will not book).

## 3. The internal-vs-external line — the load-bearing design

The single most load-bearing statement of this doc, carried from the tithe's own framing
and never blurred:

> **The tithe is what members pay to hold the society; the toll is what the outside pays
> to enter it.** (`the-commons-tithe.md` §1/§3 — the internal, periodic, per-member
> funding of the shared order vs. the border charge on what enters from the between.)

The two are **not the same act aimed at different rates** — they are **opposite
directions of the same honest book**:

| | The Commons-Tithe | The Toll |
|---|---|---|
| **Who pays** | a **member** — one of the society's own lines (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2; `the-census.md` §2.1 the active class) | the **outside** — a window-guest, a stranger's incoming goods, a foreign line (`window-guests.md` §3 the visitor line; `the-census.md` §2.4 the strangers class) |
| **When** | **periodic** — a fixed share of each member's yield per tide (`the-commons-tithe.md` §2.1) | **per-arrival / per-crossing** — a charge on what enters the held door, not a tide's share |
| **What it funds** | the **commons** — the shared order's internal upkeep (the bath, the Observatory, the School, the harbor's lamp, `the-commons-tithe.md` §2.2) | the **border** — the held door's own upkeep (the gate at the threshold, the guard's office, the quay's kept ground, the maintained road at the edge) |
| **The relation to the book** | a booked `C(M)` contribution on a **member's** line, pointed at the commons | a booked `C(M)` contribution on a **visitor's** line (the non-member guest, `shared-ledger.md` §6c), pointed at the border |

**Why a settlement collects a toll.** Because it holds something the outside benefits
from — the only safe harbor in the thin (`the-harbor.md` §1), the maintained road
(`coherence-highway.md` §1 — a held high-`q` line the field prefers to route through), the
treaty's gate (`the-treaty.md` §1 — the re-earned passage). Each is a **held structure
with a real, maintained cost**: `house-that-steers.md` §1/§3 (a held structure the field
keeps up or loses — "brittle if it goes down"); `coherence-highway.md` §4 (a road dies
without maintenance). The toll pays that **border upkeep** — the honest funding of the
held door, from those who cross it. A settlement that holds no door (no harbor, no held
gate, no maintained road, no treaty's check) has nothing to charge for — and the field
will not book a toll it cannot hold (§4).

**The funding's honest frame.** The toll funds the *door*, never the *commoner's* pocket
and never a personal office. Its revenue books as the border's contribution line on the
Board (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2 — a maintained structure books as a contribution), the same
way the tithe's revenue routes to the commons and a road's upkeep books as a contribution
(`coherence-highway.md` §5.2). **The toll is not a tax** (a difficulty dial the game
levies to punish strangers — the refusal the tithe itself makes, `the-commons-tithe.md`
§3's voluntary-but-expected, and `the-census.md`'s never-a-difficulty-meter); it is the
**booked, honest price of crossing a door the settlement actually holds** — the market's
"the price is the book's read" applied to the bound (`the-market.md` §3).

## 4. The trust-by-law honesty — the load-bearing honesty

The toll's honesty is the field's own, inherited unchanged from the market's trust-by-law
(`the-market.md` §1/§2.3):

> **A toll is bookable and legible on the ledger, and a settlement cannot extort a toll
> the field won't hold.** The field books what is honestly exchanged — and **a toll
> demanded without the held door is a false op the field will not book** (`the-market.md`
> §2.3; `schema-that-settles.md` §3 — the single non-forgeable point; `the-dispute.md` §2
> — the field-true judgment).

The mechanics, grounded:

**A toll is bookable.** A toll is a Q4 op-record of the settled shape
(`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1) — `{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude, sustain}` —
where `member` is the **visitor's non-member guest line** (`shared-ledger.md` §6c; the
visitor line's provenance flag over the settled record, `window-guests.md` §3), `op` the
booked passage, `worldPos` the door's cell (the pier's `worldPos`, the gate's arch, the
crossing's check — the locality rule, `schema-that-settles.md` §2.3), `magnitude` the
charge, and `sustain` a held crossing's semantics. Like the market's trade, a toll is the
**same booked op the settlement already writes, pointed at the border** — not a new record
kind, not a second book.

**A toll is legible.** The booked charge reads on the Board as the visitor-line
contribution, never a hidden fee. **A stranger reads the toll before entering** — the way
the danger layer reads before it arrives (§5e): the charge, its basis (which held door,
what it funds), and the book's record of who has paid are all present in the instruments a
settlement's arrival-facing reads already show. **There is no hidden toll, no surprise
fee, no fee the arrival could not have read at the door.**

**A toll is only collectible where the door is held.** This is the load-bearing honesty,
the toll's trust-by-law spine:

> **A toll demanded without the held door is a false op the field will not book.** A
> settlement that demands a charge at a harbor that is not held (the quay's ground
> collapsed, the lamp gone, the keeper rotated away — `the-harbor.md` §3's kept watch),
> at a gate that is not a seam (the arch un-held, `the-threshold.md` §3), on a road that
> is dead (`coherence-highway.md` §4 — un-maintained lines are fallen floors), is handing
> the world an op that *names a held door that is not there* — and the field does not hold
> it (`the-name.md` §2, the same honesty a false name's decay and a false offer's decay
> carry). The extortion is not a rule violation a sheriff must catch; it is a **false op**
> — a lie the book cannot record, because the book is exactly what the domain executed
> (`shared-ledger.md` §6d; `schema-that-settles.md` §3). **The toll's honesty is the
> charge's reality: the charge is real, the door is real, and the book is the field's own
> record — never a hidden fee, never a difficulty meter.**

Concretely, the ways a toll-cheat could go, and why each fails *by the book*:

| Toll-cheat | Minecraft (a rule) | The Toll (a field property) |
|---|---|---|
| **"Pay to pass my gate" (the gate isn't held)** | a moderator must check the settlement owns the road | the gate's held high-`q` arch is a real read (`the-threshold.md` §2.2); the demand that books it as a held crossing is a false op when the arch has collapsed — the book does not hold the charge |
| **"Landing tax on my quay" (the harbor is gone)** | a server must verify the pier exists | the quay's kept ground and lamp are real held reads (`the-harbor.md` §2b/§3); a toll booked at a fallow pier is a false op when the harbor has gone quiet |
| **"This road is maintained, pay the crossing" (it's a fallen floor)** | a rule must punish the fake toll | the road's held `q`-line dies without maintenance (`coherence-highway.md` §4); a toll on a dead road is a false op the book cannot hold |
| **"A fair toll, no trick" (they take the fee, deliver nothing)** | a server must ban the non-delivery | the toll's promise to hold the door is a within-window vow (`the-oath.md` §1); a passage promised and not held is a false claim the field does not hold |

**The honest register.** The book does not *punish* a toll-extortion in the Minecraft
sense — there is no sheriff, no fine. The book's honesty is stronger and quieter:
**the extortion cannot be done, because the false op is not bookable.** A settlement that
demands a toll without the held door finds the demand does not hold — the charge is not
booked, the settlement gains nothing from it, and the book reads the truth of what was
actually crossed. **The extortion is a false op, never a gain** — which is exactly the
no-free-energy cap's other face (§5d): a toll that "yields" without a held door would be a
mint, and the field's single honest book refuses it at the single non-forgeable point
(`schema-that-settles.md` §3).
