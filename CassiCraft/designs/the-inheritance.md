# The Inheritance: The Will as a Field Act

**Question under design:** what a player *deliberately passes* to their own
future run. `player-remains.md` designed death (the fossil, the re-lock);
`schema-that-settles.md` made the member-line carry the ledger across a death;
`the-name.md` made a name a persistent-Π anchor that outlives one body;
`resonance-tutor.md` made your past hands a readable trace; `seed-garden.md`
made preserved order a held thing — but **nothing designs what the player
*chooses* to leave.** A tutor holds your past hands (recorded whether you
meant to leave them or not); a named structure binds to your Π-line
(whether you husband it or walk away); the seed-garden holds your preserved
orders (if a steward keeps feeding the hearth). None of these is *agency about
the hand-off*. This document is that agency: **the Inheritance is the
deliberate bequest — the field act of passing your craft across your own
re-lock.**

Because identity is a fresh Π-pattern over your remains (`player-remains.md`
§5), the Inheritance is what makes the reborn run *yours* rather than a
stranger inheriting a corpse:

> **You do not preserve a soul; you choose which of your field-acts the next
> run can claim.** And a will left badly — unwritten, mis-named,
> un-husbanded — dissolves with you, exactly as a will left badly does in the
> world the field's honesty already holds.

It is the **third-order composition** of the corpus's identity stack: the
reason-field's intent-channel, the schema's member-id, the tutor's trace
journal, the named Π-anchors, and the seed-garden's holdings all become
*yours to hand down*. This document is the capstone of that stack — the one
that gives the player *agency over the continuity* the other docs only
establish as capability.

## 1. The Inheritance, stated once

The corpus repeatedly establishes that things can *persist* in ways a player
did not choose: a heavy channeler leaves a trace (`resonance-tutor.md` §1 —
"every op is already recorded"); a name holds beyond a body (`the-name.md` §1
— "a name is the member-id's principle extended to things"); a death leaves a
fossil whether or not anyone returns to it (`player-remains.md` §3; the
residue is "the given part"). Persistence is not agency. This document adds
the missing axis: **choice about what passes.**

> **The Inheritance is the deliberate bequest — the field act of passing your
> craft across your own re-lock: which of your field-acts the next run can
> claim.** It is the capstone of the identity stack because it is where the
> stack's capabilities become *yours to hand down*. The reason-field's
> intent-channel (§3.1 — a mind IS a persistent Π pattern) names the self the
> field holds; the schema's member-id (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1) names
> the line that line rides; the tutor's trace journal, the named Π-anchors,
> and the seed-garden's holdings are the *objects* of a continuity — but until
> this doc, nothing says the player chooses which of those objects the
> reborn run inherits. The Inheritance is that choosing.

**The honest frame, stated once and never blurred:**

> **You do not preserve a soul.** Identity is a fresh Π-pattern over your
> remains (`player-remains.md` §5; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1 — the same
> member line, new lock, no ledger amnesty). Nothing is carried *inside* the
> body as a continuity of self. What the will does is far narrower and more
> honest: it marks a **bounded set of claims** — this trace, this name, these
> held orders, this Board place are *yours to take if you return*. The field
> honors those claims **because the single-writer records say so**
> (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1 — the world-writer's non-forgeable write),
> the same way the Board honors a member's book (`shared-ledger.md` §2.3: the
> Board is "as honest as the op-stream"). And a will left badly — unwritten,
> mis-named, un-husbanded — **dissolves with you**: the claims go unmarked,
> the false name decays (`the-name.md` §2), the un-tended order drifts, and the
> reborn run wakes a stranger over your remains.

The three notes that make it a *field* act rather than a game feature:

1. **The writing is a Q4 operation.** A bequest is not a menu the server
   remembers; it is **records the player's own channeling writes**, through
   the same single-writer lane every field act rides
   (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1; `coherence-magic.md` §5.1). To will
   something is to *perturb the field* to record the intent — a real act with
   a real cost (the coherence budget `B` and the ε² vent of the write; the
   no-free-energy cap, §5e).
2. **The claims are exercised, not endowed.** A bequest is not a guaranteed
   inheritance loaded into the reborn run's inventory. It is a **set of
   claims the reborn run may go and exercise** — re-read the trace, re-lock
   the named structure, reopen the vault, stand on the Board's line — and
   each claim only holds if the thing it names is still *there to be claimed*
   (§3.2): a will marks a *possibility*, the husbanding holds the *object*.
3. **A will is written over the body's own dissolution.** The bequest records
   are the player's last deliberate Q4 write boundary — the mark left as the
   run ends — so the will is itself a **strata event** (`player-remains.md`
   §4.1: every death is a strata event; the bequest is the *chosen* stratum
   laid down beside the *given* fossil).

**[design]** What is engine-real: the Q4 op-record with its `member` id, the
single-writer lane, the trace as an ordered Q4 sequence, the name as a
persistent-Π anchor (holding theory-ratified), the vault's held orders — all
already designed or landed. What is this doc's [design]: the **`bequest`
mark on a bounded set of those records**, and the **claim semantic** (the
reborn run may exercise the marked claims if the objects still hold). No
claim pretends the engine writes "will" or "inheritance" records today.

## 2. The bequests — each a real existing thing

What can be passed is not invented by this doc; it is the set of persistent
objects the corpus already designs, gathered under one hand-off. Each bequest
is a real claim over a real existing thing:

### 2.1 The trace journal — your past hands

`resonance-tutor.md` §1: the trace is the Q4 op-record
`{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude, sustain}`
(`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1 — the extended record, `member` first), the
same records a score composes (`field-music.md` §4.1). **The trace outlives
you** — it is `player-remains.md`'s "informational twin" to the fossil: a
fossil is the field's kept structure of what you were; a trace is the Q4
channel's kept sequence of what you *did*. The tutor's journal lives in the
domain-side publish (`reason-field.md` §4.2 — the trace is a π-trail in the
field itself; `reason-field.md` §7 — gated on the meshless/Π frontier).

> **A willed trace is a past hand deliberately left.** Without a will, your
> trace is recorded anyway — and the tutored re-enactment teaches any run
> (yours or a stranger's) whatever the record holds. With a `bequest` mark, the
> trace becomes *yours to hand down*: the reborn run may claim it as its own
> past hand — the phase-gap exercise against a self it can call itself
> (`resonance-tutor.md` §2.2). A trace willed but **never re-read dissolves
> with you**: the claim marks it, but a trace no future run returns to record
> is a past hand nobody takes — it fades back into the field's π-trail it was
> read out of.

### 2.2 The named structures — your Π-anchors

`the-name.md` §1: to name a thing is to **bind a persistent-Π anchor to it** —
the member-id's principle (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1) extended from
*players* to *things*. §3: a name keeps the record — the named ruin keeps its
record (`house-that-steers.md` §3 — the skeleton is re-authorable), the named
contribution is legible, the named stratum reads as its own record, the named
companion's run holds continuity.

> **A name you leave is a structure your future run can re-lock.** The name
> (§2.1.1 of the-name's stack: a name is a member-id's principle applied to a
> thing) is a Π-anchor the field holds for that structure beyond one body. A
> willed name marks the anchor as *your* hand-off: the reborn run inherits
> the *legibility* — "this was mine; I may re-lock it" — where an unnamed or
> un-willed anchor is just a structure a stranger finds. The honest bound of
> `the-name.md` §2 applies directly: **you cannot will falsely** — a name
> bound to a fallen ruin, a shed scar, an over-drained site (the named thing
> no longer *is* in the field) **decays with you** (the false name's decay,
> `the-name.md` §2.2). The will marks *which* anchors are yours to claim; the
> anchor only holds if the structure it names is still there to be re-locked.

**A will to the house is a will to the family (reverse pointer).** These named
structures have since gained a *collective* subject: [`the-family.md`](./the-family.md)
§3.2/§4 lets the bequests target a **household held together** — a willed
structure the Family's anchor held is claimed by the family line, the surviving
co-holders re-locking the hearth within the joint rather than leaving it to a
stranger's find (`the-family.md` §3.2 — "a will to the house is a will to the
family"). The two docs now cite each other as read-and-answered: the bequest's
named subject may be a family, not only a future self, exactly as that doc reads
this §2.2 (§4 there).

### 2.3 The seed-holdings — the preserved order

`seed-garden.md` §1 (landed): the vault is a deep-rung, high-`q` structure
holding seeds' Π patterns so their origin-order persists against drift; §4.1
the stewardship decision (kept / planted / traded / faded) is a live curation
costed by deep-rung maintenance; §4.2 the cap — **a seed is order, not power**
(a vault preserves, never mints, never consumes its charges as fuel).

> **A willed seed-holding is preserved order handed on.** The vault already
> holds the orders a steward chose to keep; the `bequest` mark names *which
> held orders the next run may reopen* — the escape-cargo principle of
> `seed-garden.md` §5 (a decaying window's vault is the escape arc's most
> precious cargo) made a personal hand-off. The honest cost is inherited
> whole: **an un-husbanded holding dissolves with you.** A vaulted order whose
> hearth is not fed sheds toward the attractor — the shallow rungs first, the
> deep core last, the orders' loss a wound-class event (`seed-garden.md` §3.3).
> The will marks the order as yours to inherit, but **a seed-order that drifted
> (the vault went dark, the steward stopped feeding) is gone with the vessel**
> that held it — the claim dissolves because the object did.

### 2.4 The Board place — your stewardship's continuity

`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1: **same member line, new lock** — a reborn
player keeps the same `member` id, the re-lock re-binds to it, "no ledger
amnesty." `shared-ledger.md` §1.2/§2.2: the Board renders each member's net
`C(M)` over a tide; §2.1.1's continuous line is what makes a long steward's
arc bookable across deaths (`shared-ledger.md` §7 open-Q1 — the Board
aggregates one continuous `C(M)` per member, closed only on the dissolved
entry).

> **Your Board place is your member-line's continuity, carried by the re-lock
> itself.** The will does not *create* this bequest — the member-id's
> same-member-line rule (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1) already hands your
> line to your reborn self. What the Inheritance adds is the *framing*: the
> line is not a ledger accident but a **place the reborn run stands on** — the
> stewarded `C(M)`, the settled balance, the record of whose hand the window's
> core was in. A willed Board place is the bequest that needs no extra object:
> it is the one claim the re-lock carries structurally. The honest caveat: the
> line is the *account*, not a *clean slate* — the margin-causer's negative
> `C(M)` is also carried (no amnesty, `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1), so a
> willed place can be a burden of record, never a ledger reset.

### 2.5 The honest exclusions — what is NOT passed

The Inheritance is defined as much by what it refuses to carry as by what it
passes. Two exclusions, both grounded in the corpus's honesty:

**The Burden is not passed.** `the-burden.md` §1.2: the fossil is the Burden's
death-state — the running scar a heavy channeler carries is the *living form
whose terminal is the corpse's fossil*. §7.5 (open-Q5) states the default:
**the debt dies with the run — the fossil holds it, the fresh lock starts
clean.** The Inheritance adopts that default as an exclusion:

> **The debt dissolves with the run.** The stored, carried ε² that a dead
> channeler shed is a debt against the run that shed it — the field's
> patience was loaned and the run ended before it was repaid (`patient-field.md`
> §3.2). Carrying that debt forward would be a **cruelty the corpus's honesty
> forbids** — not a game-balance choice but the same "matter conserved,
> configuration lost" (`field-emergent-ecology.md` §2.2) that makes a
> dissolved run's debt a terminal. A will that tried to name "my Burden for my
> next run" would name something the field does not carry: the debt is bound
> to the run, not to the line, and it dissolves with the run that owed it.

**The fossil is not claimed.** `field-archaeology.md` §2.1/§3.1: the fossil is
the deep-rung residue of a dissolved structure — readable, findable, whether
or not anyone comes for it. `player-remains.md` §3: the deep-rung core where
you died persists as residue; §3.3 you may leave it in place (your own
conservation) or reclaim it (burning your own past).

> **The fossil is residue — the given part, not the chosen part.** The
> Inheritance is the *chosen* hand-off; the fossil is the *given* residue,
> laid down by the field's own dissolution whether or not a will was written.
> A will does not mark the fossil (it is findable regardless —
> `field-archaeology.md` §3.1 is a read, free, in place); a will *distinguishes
> the reborn run's claim* from a stranger's find by provenance, the same way
> `player-remains.md` §3.2 makes "this was you" a designed labeling. The fossil
> is archaeology to any reader; **the Inheritance is the part only you (by
> member-line) may claim as self** — and the fossil remains un-marked,
> findable to all, because the will governs the chosen, not the given.

The through-line of the exclusions: **the Inheritance is deliberately
lighter than a soul.** It carries no carried self (identity is a fresh
Π-pattern, `player-remains.md` §5), no carried debt (the Burden dies with the
run, `the-burden.md` §7.5), no claimed residue (the fossil is the given,
`field-archaeology.md` §2.1). It carries only **claims over chosen objects** —
and a claim is the least-spirituous, most-honest continuity the field can
hold.

## 3. The will as a field act

### 3.1 Writing the will — the `bequest` record

A will is not a separate system or a server-stored file. It is a **bounded set
of Q4 records with `member` id, marked `bequest`** — a designed flag on the
op-records the corpus already writes (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1: the
extended `{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude, sustain}`). Concretely:

- Each bequest is one Q4 record carrying the extended schema **plus a
  `bequest` designator** naming what is willed (a trace's sequence id, a named
  structure's anchor, a vault's held order) — the `op`/`worldPos`/`rung`
  locate the object in the field, the `member` binds it to the line
  (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1), and the `bequest` marks it as a claim the
  next lock of that line may exercise.
- The record is **single-writer and non-forgeable** by construction: it is
  written through the Q4 lane by the world-writer, the only server-side
  mutator (`async-field-domain.md` §5.2; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1) — the
  `member` on it is the world-writer's, so a player cannot write a bequest
  onto another line, and cannot erase a bequest off the executed stream. **A
  will is exactly as honest as the op-stream** (`shared-ledger.md` §2.3), and
  the op-stream is single-writer.
- The writing is a **costed field act**: it is a Q4 write (the coherence
  budget `B` + the ε² vent of `coherence-magic.md` §1.2/§3), bounded by the
  no-free-energy cap (`energy-harnessing.md` §6). **Writing a will perturbs
  the field it will leave** — the bequest, like any op, is a real act, never
  free, never a mint (§5e).

### 3.2 The claim semantic — a will marks possibility, not endowment

The load-bearing honesty of the whole document:

> **A bequest is a claim the reborn run may exercise, not a guaranteed
> endowment.** The records say *these are yours to take if you return and the
> thing still holds* — they do not load anything into the reborn run. The
> reborn run must go, read, re-lock, reopen, stand — and each claim only holds
> if its object is still there to be claimed.

Three ways a bequest dissolves with you, each the corpus's own physics:

| Un-husbanded bequest | What happens | Physical ground |
|---|---|---|
| **A named structure fallen to ruin** | the anchor decays — a name bound to a structure that no longer *is* sheds the way a false name does | `the-name.md` §2.2 (a lie erodes); `house-that-steers.md` §3 (a fallen floor is a scar — unrecoverable; only the skeleton keeps its name) |
| **A seed-order drifted** | the vault's hearth goes unfed; the held orders shed toward the attractor, shallow rungs first, until the claim's object is gone | `seed-garden.md` §3.3 (an abandoned vault's decay is the orders' loss, a wound-class event); §2.1 (the field cannot re-precipitate a drifted birth-order) |
| **A trace never re-read** | the willed past hand is no hand any future run takes; the claim fades with the π-trail it was read out of | `resonance-tutor.md` §1 (a trace outlives you, recorded whether meant or not — but a *willed* trace is the *chosen* claim, and a claim nobody exercises is a past hand nobody takes) |

The rule, stated once: **the will and the husbanding are two halves of one
hand-off.** The `bequest` record names the possibility; the husbanding (the
named structure's maintenance, the vault's fed hearth, the trace's being
worth returning to) holds the object the possibility points at. A will with
nothing husbanded is a set of claims over ruins — marked, but dissolved,
exactly the way a will left badly dissolves with you.

### 3.3 Determinism — a hard gate

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `field-archaeology.md` §1.2;
`field-npc-ai.md` §2.3/§6d). The bequest inherits it:

> **Same will, same remains, same field → same claimable bequest.** A bequest
> record is a pure function of (the writing player's member-id × the object it
> names × the field state at the write) — the same way a trace is a
> deterministic record (`resonance-tutor.md` §6e) and a name's holding is a
> pure function of the state it binds (`the-name.md` §2.4). The *claim*
> (whether the reborn run *can* exercise it) is deterministic too: it is a
> function of (the bequest record's marks × the present object's holding) —
> a named structure that still holds is claimable every run; a drifted order
> is not, every run. There is no seeded-RNG judgment about whether a will
> "sticks" — determinism is what makes the Inheritance *honest*: the reborn
> run can rely on a willed trace being what its records say, and can rely on
> an un-husbanded bequest being gone.

This is a **hard gate** (not a preference), inherited from the corpus's
determinism discipline and the schema's no-amnesty rule (`schema-that-settles.md`
§2.1.1). A will is a true record of what the player chose over their remains,
not a seeded-RNG costume.

## 4. The reborn run's agency

### 4.1 Waking over your own remains — a fresh Π-pattern with a claim

`player-remains.md` §5 makes identity a fresh Π-pattern over your remains;
`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1 binds the fresh lock to the same member line.
Without the Inheritance, that is a *stance*: the reborn run is a new run that
inherits the residue, able to *find* its own past but given nothing to *claim*
as self. The Inheritance converts the stance into **agency**:

> **You wake over your own remains with a member-line, a trace, a name, a
> place.** The Member-line is carried by the re-lock (`schema-that-settles.md`
> §2.1.1) — your Board line, your settled balance, your stewardship record. The
> trace is the past hand you willed (resonance-tutor §1/§2) — an exercise you
> can close the phase-gap against as *your* self, not a stranger's read. The
> name is the Π-anchor you left (`the-name.md` §1/§3) — a structure you can
> re-lock as inheritor, not find as archaeologist. The Board place is your
> line's continuity (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2/§2.2) — the window's core was in a

## 7. Open questions

1. **The `bequest` record's exact shape over the settled schema.** Is the mark
   a distinct `op` value (a seventh field operation alongside
   `condense/dissolve/steer/ignite/shield-heal/sense`, `coherence-magic.md` §2),
   or a `sustain`-like flag (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.2) on an existing
   willed op's line — `{member, op: bequest, worldPos, rung, magnitude, …}`
   vs. a `WILLED` designator on a trace/name record? The distinction changes
   whether the bequest is itself a perturbation (a new op) or a label on an
   existing one (a designed tag) — a cost and determinism fork worth settling
   with `schema-that-settles.md`. **[design]**
2. **The will's timing.** Is the bequest written *at* death — the last
   deliberate Q4 boundary as the run ends (§1.3) — or *any time before*, a
   standing will updated across the run? The former makes the will a strata
   event chosen at dissolution; the latter makes it a living commitment that
   rides the same-member-line. The two have different husbanding (a standing
   will must be re-confirmed; a death-bed will cannot be mis-written as a lie
   the field holds). The design leans toward a **livable will over the run,
   confirmed at death by the same write lane** — but the exact fence is open.
   **[design]**
3. **The named-anchor's fall past the will (§2.2/§6(a) of `the-name.md`).** If
   a name holds only while the structure *is* (`the-name.md` §2), and a willed
   name marks "mine to re-lock," does a name survive a *re-authorable skeleton*
   (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2) — re-lockable by its line — while a fallen
   floor (a scar) fails the will? The determine-vs-fall line is `the-name.md`'s
   own continuity question (§7 of that doc), inherited by the bequest. **[design]**
4. **The claim-vs-find boundary for a shared window.** In a window with other
   players, a willed trace/name/holding is claimable by *your* line
   (§2.3/§4.1); a *stranger's* line can still read the trace (it is recorded,
   `resonance-tutor.md` §1), find the fossil (`field-archaeology.md` §3.1), and
   stand on the Board — but cannot *claim* the bequest as self. What exactly
   a non-claiming reader may *do* (teach from a gifted trace, `resonance-tutor.md`
   §5.3; re-author an unclaimed structure) is the shared-window social boundary
   this doc's claim-semantic opens, and it needs the Board's ethics
   (`shared-ledger.md` §3.3/§6e — never blame, never steal) extended to
   bequests. **[design]**
5. **The conservation-of-you across the claim.** The will makes the reborn run
   *claim* your craft; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1 gives it the same line.
   But claiming a willed trace as "your own past hand" — reading the phase-gap
   against a self it calls itself — is a *stronger* reading than
   `player-remains.md` §5's "fresh run over your own remains" stance. Is the
   claim a *continuity* (the reborn run is, by the will, the willer's) or a
   *legacy* (it takes the craft, remaining a fresh run)? The design states it
   as the latter (a claim to *objects*, not to *selfhood*), but the emotional
   and social sharpness is worth naming as the identity open-question the whole
   stack keeps surfacing. **[design]**
