# The Hourglass: The Duration Instrument — How Long a Moment Takes

**Question under design:** the Clock ([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md)) reads the
local *tempo* — how fast the field is running where the player stands. But the
patient acts — the long bind ([`the-healer.md`](./the-healer.md) §2), the Still
Room's clearing ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.2), the
storm's sheltering ([`weather-not-storm.md`](./weather-not-storm.md)), the
harvest's timing ([`tide-of-the-attractor.md`](./tide-of-the-attractor.md)
§3.2), the bed's night ([`sleep.md`](./sleep.md) §3) — all ask a *different*
question: **how long does this moment take?** The Clock answers *how fast*; the
Hourglass answers *how long*. Where the Clock is the rate-read, the Hourglass is
the **accumulated** read — the Clock's tempo integrated over a chosen interval,
presented as draining sand.

> **The Hourglass is the duration instrument — the Clock's accumulated twin: a
> carried device that reads the local tempo *over time* and answers "how much
> time do I have".** It runs a designed timer at the local `(1−q)`-derived tempo
> (the Clock's read, integrated), so a Still Room's patient hour and a desert
> margin's rushed hour are *different amounts of the same thing* — the sand
> drains at the field's local rate, and the sand is the honest measure of the
> moment's real length. **Patient where the field is patient (the sand falls
> slowly in the Still Room), rushed where it is rushed (the sand races on the
> margin).**

This is a **lens-on-a-lens-on-a-lens**: the Weatherglass reads the field's
*state* ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.2); the Clock reads
the field's *rate* — the `(1−q)` re-read as a tempo ([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md)
§1); the Hourglass reads the *duration* — that tempo accumulated over an
interval the player chooses, presented as sand. It is a **pure consumer of the
published `q`/`(1−q)` channels and the Clock's already-designed tempo mapping**,
obeying the instrument family rule ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md)
§2.1) exactly: **never a new channel, never new physics.** The honest boundary —
the mixing clock is theory; the tempo read is [design] over the published
time-series, gated on the L1–L3 probe; the accumulation is this doc's [design]
over the Clock's read — is drawn in §2 and never blurred.

## 1. The Hourglass, stated once

The instrument family reads the field's *state* (the Weatherglass;
[`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1) and the Clock reads the
field's *rate* (the local `(1−q)` tempo; [`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md) §1).
**None of them reads the field's *duration*** — how long a *patient act* takes
at the local tempo. `patient-field.md` designed local time as a real quantity
(the mixing clock `T = 2π/[λ(1−q)]`, theory-cited); the Clock gave that tempo a
*watch face*; the corpus's patient acts — the long bind, the storm's shelter,
the harvest's wait, the bed's night — all need to know **how long the moment
takes**, not how fast it is running. The Hourglass is that missing instrument: the
tempo read, *accumulated*.

> **The Hourglass, stated once.** It is the duration instrument — the Clock's
> accumulated twin: a carried device that reads the local tempo *over time* and
> answers **how much time do I have**. The Clock tells you *how fast the field
> is running here*; the Hourglass tells you *how long the thing you are doing
> will take in that tempo*. The sand drains at the field's local rate; the sand
> is the honest measure of the moment's real length. Patient where the field is
> patient (the sand falls slowly in the Still Room); rushed where it is rushed
> (the sand races on the margin).

The one-line distinction from the Clock — its direct sibling:

| Instrument | It asks | It reads | It answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Clock ([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md) §1/§3.1) | *how fast is the field running here?* | the local `(1−q)` tempo (a hand-rate) | *when do I have* — the tempo in which the next deep op fits |
| **The Hourglass (this doc)** | ***how long does this moment take?*** | **the tempo accumulated over a chosen interval (draining sand)** | ***how much time do I have*** — the honest duration of the act at this tempo |

The Clock is the field's **speedometer-at-a-glance**
([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md) §4); the Hourglass is its **trip-meter** — the
same rate read, but *counted over time* so it answers "how long until".

The honest scope, stated once: the Hourglass **never claims the engine "slows
time"**. It does not touch a rate, a constant, or a write. It presents the
*existing* local-tempo difference — theory-cited, published-channel-grounded,
already given a face by the Clock — as an *accumulated duration*, exactly as the
Clock "presents the existing local-tempo difference in the family's idiom"
([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md) §2) and the tide is "a pacing lens, not a new
engine term" ([`tide-of-the-attractor.md`](./tide-of-the-attractor.md) §1.2).
A player's tick is a constant 20 Hz (`dt = 0.05`); the Hourglass only makes that
constant *legible against* the field's proposed local tempo as a length of
moment, not as a rate.

## 4. The design surface — the family-rule forms

The Hourglass obeys the instrument family rule ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md)
§2.1) exactly: **every instrument is a consumer of the same publish with a
presentation idiom, never a new channel.** It adds no channel, no telemetry, no
write — it reads the published `(1−q)` (through the Clock's already-designed
tempo mapping), the *same* channel the Weatherglass's waste tint and the Clock's
hand already render, and re-presents it as an *accumulated duration*. It is the
**Weatherglass idiom's temporal sibling**: where the Weatherglass is the
magnetometer-at-a-glance (`field-instruments.md` §1) and the Clock is the
field's speedometer-at-a-glance (`the-clock.md` §4), the Hourglass is the
field's **trip-meter** — the held glass with draining sand.

| Form | Idiom | Channel it reads | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The held glass** | a carried glass with sand that drains — the Weatherglass's sibling object, the sand's rate the local tempo | the local `(1−q)` (through the Clock's tempo mapping) | **the duration itself**: a full top chamber (a long, patient moment — the sand falls slowly), an emptying one (the act's time is running out at the local rate). The glanceable primary read. |
| **The amount of sand** | the chosen interval — how much time the player commits to the act | the designed interval dial (how long a "still-room hour", a "bind", a "night", a "front" is set to) | **how much time the player believes they have** — the sand's *amount* is the designed commitment; the sand's *rate* is the field's honest tempo. The two together are "how long this moment will really take." |
| **The mark when the sand empties** | an *audible* form — field-music's accessibility rule held: sound is an enhancement, never the only channel (`field-music.md` §6) | the same `(1−q)` + the interval-end | **a soft mark when the sand empties** — the audible "the act's honest bound is reached". The mute player reads the empty chamber directly; the mark is the ambient twin of the identical read. |

All three are consumers of the published `q`/`(1−q)` (plus the Clock's
already-designed tempo mapping) — the ≈ 6 MiB publish unchanged
(`corpus-reconciliation.md`). **No new channel, no new physics.** The Hourglass
is a consumer in exactly the corpus's sense: it does not read a new quantity; it
reads a *duration* out of the tempo the Clock already presents as a rate. Each
later instrument in the family inherits the same freedom — a consumer may
re-present the published channels at a new *axis* (rate, position, scale,
**duration**) but never at a new source.

**The field-music accessibility rule, held hard.** The mark is an enhancement,
never the only channel (`field-music.md` §6; `field-instruments.md` §1.3). The
duration is always *also* derivable — more laboriously — from the Clock's rate
plus a manual count of the interval (§5e). A player with sound off reads the
empty chamber and loses nothing; a player who never carries a glass reads the
`(1−q)` and the Clock and derives the duration by hand. The Hourglass is a
*layer* over the channels, structurally guaranteed by the shared-publish
architecture — never a hidden-only instrument.

## 5. Honest gates

### (a) The theory/[design]/engine-real line

Drawn in §2, restated once here: the mixing clock `T = 2π/[λ(1−q)]` and the
local-tempo *variation* are **theory** (`qi-as-time-clock.md` is a speculation;
the engine kernel's conversion term is q-free); the tick, the publish, and the
`q`/`(1−q)` channels are **engine-real** (canonical); the Clock's **tempo
mapping** is **[design]** over the published time-series, gated on the L1–L3
probe ([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md) §5a); **the Hourglass — the accumulation
of that tempo over a chosen interval, presented as sand — is this doc's
[design]** over the Clock's read (§2). The Hourglass never claims the engine
"slows time" (§2).

### (b) PHASE-1-ABLE — a bounded read-only consumer, gated on the Clock

The Hourglass is **Phase-1-able** because it is the *simplest extension* of the
cleanest second-order instrument in the corpus: **an accumulation of the
Clock's already-[design] tempo read over a player-chosen interval, rendered as
sand.** It needs:

- **The published `q`/`(1−q)` at the player's position** — already in the
  ≈ 6 MiB publish; one more trilinear sample inside the ≈ 1–6 ms/tick budget,
  exactly as the Weatherglass and the Clock are (`field-instruments.md` §5d;
  `the-clock.md` §5(b)). Nothing new to budget.
- **The Clock's tempo mapping** — the designed mapping from `(1−q)` to a *rate*
  the Clock already specifies ([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md) §3.1). The
  Hourglass *reuses it*; it needs the Clock's mapping, not its own.
- **The interval dial** — the chosen amount of sand, a designed commitment
  ([design]).

**The Phase-1 slice, stated:** **the first sand over the living-terrain demo.**
Carry the glass in the Phase-1 coupled box (`chunk-field-quantization.md` §1.2),
set a nominal interval (a still-room hour, a bind), and read the sand's rate —
the local `(1−q)`-derived tempo accumulated — as the player walks ridge → scar →
margin. This is a bounded read-only consumer, shipable on the same probe the
Clock's meaningful-hands gate runs (§6). **It is gated on the Clock's tempo
mapping (the L1–L3 verdict): if the probe returns "vary enough to feel", the
Hourglass's sand drains at genuinely different rates across the window — a real,
changing duration. If it returns flat, the sand barely differs — a legitimate,
weaker, stated finding (§5b's honest fallback), not a failure.** The Hourglass
de-risks the whole *duration*-bearing layer of the corpus the way the Clock
de-risked the tempo layer: **ship the accumulated read first, prove the duration
is felt or honest-atmosphere, then let every later duration-bearing system (the
bind's bound, the harvest's countdown, the night's length, the front's passing)
read through the same sand.**

### (c) Determinism — same locality, same field state → same sand's rate

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `the-clock.md` §5(c); `life-signal.md` §6d).
Therefore:

> **Same locality, same field state → same sand's rate.** The accumulated tempo
> over an interval at a position — and its presentation as a sand-level — is a
> **pure function of the published channels** over that interval. A player who
> sets the same amount of sand in the same spot at the same field state gets the
> same drain, every time.

This is a **hard gate**, inherited unchanged from the Clock's determinism
([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md) §5(c)) and the corpus's discipline. **[design]**
The interval dial (the amount of sand) is a designed constant once set; the
*sand-level it produces* is a deterministic read of the real channels over that
window. The Hourglass adds no randomness.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — an hourglass informs, never mints

`energy-harnessing.md` §6's no-free-energy discipline holds:

1. **There is no "sand that harvests."** The Hourglass's reading is information
   and timing, never a source. Knowing that a Still Room's hour is long does not
   convert the field's patience into coherence, ε², or power — `output ≤
   φ⁻¹·input` (`energy-harnessing.md` §6) bounds every conversion, and the
   Hourglass converts nothing. This is the same refusal that closes tide-farming
   (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §5d) and the Clock's "no tempo harvest"
   (`the-clock.md` §5(d)), applied to the *duration*: an hourglass informs, never
   mints.
2. **The hourglass does not buy time.** A player cannot "set a long interval on
   the margin" to *get* a patient duration there — the sand drains at the
   *field's* rate, not the player's commitment. The act's honest bound is the
   sand's real drain at the local tempo, and the sand runs out sooner on the
   margin because the field is rushed there. The duration is a read, not a
   negotiation.
3. **The hourglass never earns by accumulating.** Counting how long the bind
   takes does not make the field heal faster; the accumulation is the honest
   span of the patient act under the cap's own discipline. The Hourglass's gift
   is *how much time you have* — the timing knowledge to bound a patient act,
   costed as information, never minted as power.

### (e) Accessibility — the duration is never hidden-only

The corpus's hard accessibility rule holds unchanged (`field-music.md` §6;
`field-instruments.md` §1.3): **the duration is also derivable from the Clock's
rate + a manual count — never hidden-only.** A player who never carries a glass
reads the Clock's hand (`the-clock.md` §3.1) and counts the interval by hand to
derive the same duration. The Hourglass is the idiomatic layer that makes the
duration *feelable as sand*; it is not a second information source. The mark is
the ambient form, the emptying chamber the glanceable guarantee — the mute player
is never diminished, and there is no state of the Hourglass that is not also on
the published channels and the Clock's rate. [design], structurally guaranteed by
the shared-publish architecture (`field-instruments.md` §1.3).

## 6. Feasibility verdict

## Open questions

1. **The probe's verdict on the meaningful duration (§5b/§6).** If the L1–L3
   probe (`patient-field.md` §5(b)) returns no meaningful `(1−q)` spread, the
   Clock reads flat and the Hourglass's sand barely differs across the window. Is
   that presentation *genuinely felt* (a glass whose sand moves a little, honestly,
   over the field's real `(1−q)` gradient) or does it ring hollow against a
   mechanically-flat time-series — the same presentation-vs-mechanic honesty
   question `patient-field.md` open-Q5 / `the-clock.md` open-Q1 records? The doc
   commits to presentation-only being a legitimate finding; whether it *holds as
   a feel* is a Phase-1.5 design judgment. **[probe]**
2. **What a "still-room hour" or "a bind" is, in sand-units.** Even if `(1−q)`
   varies, what *amount* of sand a patient act is set to — the interval dial that
   makes a "Still Room's patient hour and a desert margin's rushed hour" legibly
   different (the `the-clock.md` open-Q2 "what feels like a different tempo"
   question, made a duration) — is a **[design]** dial over the patience the
   field's docs name. Set after the L-probe, never assumed. **[design]**
3. **The accumulation's linearity.** Is the sand's rate the raw `(1−q)`-derived
   tempo accumulated linearly (a still-room hour is exactly K× a margin hour at
   the ratio of their `(1−q)`), or a designed ease (a patient field reads *even
   more* patient, the way the tide's season-length dial shapes the tempo)? The
   honest default is linear — the sand is the honest measure, not a shaped mood —
   but the dial shape is a [design] choice the L-probe's spread informs.
   **[design]**
4. **The four uses' mechanical gating (§6).** When (if ever) the four patient
   acts key mechanically on the sand's read — the healer's bind length shortening
   at a fast locality, the farmer's harvest window shortening at thin, the night's
   danger lengthening with the bed's tempo — each must inherit the gate of its
   source doc (`the-healer.md` §5b's wound/burden gates, `tide-of-the-attractor.md`
   §5a's probe, `sleep.md` §6b's noise-floor line, `weather-not-storm.md` §6a's
   provenance probe). Which uses may ride *mechanical* duration (post-probe) vs.
   stay *presentational* is a designed decision this doc does not settle.
   **[design]**
5. **The hourglass vs. the Clock's wasted read.** Since the Clock reads the same
   `(1−q)` the Weatherglass already renders as its waste tint
   ([`the-clock.md`](./the-clock.md) §2), and the Hourglass accumulates that same
   read, do three instruments for one quantity risk *redundancy* (the glass is a
   pretty clock face) or is the *rate-vs-duration* axis genuinely distinct the way
   speedometer-vs-trip-meter is? The design's commit is the latter; whether the
   player feels the trip-meter as a separate instrument is a Phase-1.5 design
   judgment. **[design]**
