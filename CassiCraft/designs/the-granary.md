# The Granary: The Storehouse — the Settlement's Everyday Order-Vault, Held Against the Lean

**Question under design:** the corpus gives a settlement its *rare orders* (the
seed-garden's vault, `seed-garden.md`), its *circulating goods* (the market's
booked exchange, `the-market.md`), and its *chosen memory* (the memory-palace's
rooms, `the-memory-palace.md`). The farm yields (`farm-that-feeds.md`) and the
tool works (`the-tool.md`). But nothing designs the **everyday store of ordinary
harvested order** — the granary that holds the farm's yield at its tide-high and
the forge's kept steel against its wear, the honest buffer a settlement keeps
*between a full table and a thin one.* The seed-garden conserves *rareness*; the
market *circulates* flow; the memory-palace holds *chosen memory*. This document
designs the piece none of them is: **the Granary, the settlement's everyday
order-vault — the storehouse holding the ordinary harvest against the lean.** It
is the rare seed-vault's everyday twin — **the granary holds *plenty*, the garden
holds *rareness*** — and the honest *"what we have saved against winter."*

The storehouse is where the settlement's ordinary order settles when it is not
circulating: the harvested field at tide-high, the forge's tool-steel kept
against its wear, the everyday surplus the market moves and the granary *holds*.
It is a *store*, never a mint (§5d): a full granary is the settlement's weather
against a thin tide, a drought, or a bad harvest; an empty granary **is** the
lean — the pulse reads the empty store honestly (§6), because the granary's
draw is *spent*, never free.

## 1. The Granary, stated once

moves its *circulating goods* (the market's booked exchange), and keeps its
*chosen memory* (the memory-palace's rooms). None holds the settlement's
**ordinary enough to be plenty** — not the one-of-a-kind seed, not the traded
pick, not the remembered self, but the *quotidian harvest*: the grain the farm
grew at tide-high, the steel the forge kept against its own wear, the everyday
surplus that would otherwise run straight from the field to the table and leave
the settlement with nothing when the field thins.

> **The Granary, stated once.** The settlement's everyday order-vault — the
> storehouse that holds the ordinary harvest against the lean. It is the place
> the farm's yield sits at tide-high instead of being spent as it comes in, the
> forge's tool-steel kept against the tool's wear, the everyday surplus the

> **the granary holds *plenty* and the garden holds *rareness*** — and it is the
> settlement's honest *"what we have saved against winter."*

## 2. The store — the ordinary harvested order, held rather than circulated

The granary's holdings are **ordinary harvested order** — the everyday surplus a
settlement grows, forges, and reaps, held *at rest* against the lean rather than
run straight through to circulation. Each store is grounded in its source doc's
real quantity; **the store's form — how order is held, how it is drawn — is this
doc's [design]** over the farm/tool/seed-garden holding mechanics.

### 2.1 The farm's yield — held at tide-high, not circulated

`farm-that-feeds.md` §2 designs the crop as a shallow-rung regime whose stored
coherence *peaks at tide-high*: the longer and higher the bed's `q`, the deeper
the crop's run holds, and the richer the harvest carries into the eater's budget
(§4 — "field-fed, edible stored coherence"). §5's harvest/thin table means a
well-stewarded bed yields *richly* at harvest tide and *less, wasting more* at
thin — **the yield is tide-bound** (§4: "yield is bounded by the bed's `q` and
the tide").

**The granary's first store is that yield, held at tide-high instead of spent as
it comes in.** Where the market would book the harvest straight into a trade
(`the-market.md` §2.2 — a harvest's magnitude is booked, its field-bound yield
never inflated), the granary holds the tide-high harvest *as a store*: the field
is most organized at the high (`q` high, `ε²` low, near-lossless), so the yield
taken *then* carries the most stored coherence per unit, and holding it lets the
settlement draw that organized order **through the thin** — exactly the cold's
"stretch the store" (§3), the vault-as-pantry made *everyday*.

**[design]** The concrete "how the harvest sits in the granary" — the store as a
held high-`q` volume (the Qi-bath idiom of `energy-harnessing.md` §4.4 applied
at storehouse scale), the deposit as the harvest's magnitude booked into the
record (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1), the draw as a withdrawal of that stored
coherence (`mouths-eye.md` §3.2 via `farm-that-feeds.md` §4) — is the designed
surface. The *yield's magnitude* is the field's real, booked quantity; the
*granary form* is this doc's [design], probe-calibrated against the same living-
terrain demo the farm and the cold calibrate their dials on.

### 2.2 The forge's tool-steel — kept against the tool's wear

`the-tool.md` §2 designs the tool's two honest reads: the **wear** (§2b — the
`(1−q)` waste law read as the alloy's life: a high-`q` low-waste alloy bites
clean and lasts; a scar-tainted one dulls fast) and the **scar** (§2c — the
charge/scar asymmetry: a deep tool cuts deep but wounds the ground it cuts).
A tool is a **held regime** (§3 — the worn-field's tuple realized as an alloy),
and "the tool does not hide its lineage" (§3 — the alloy carries the field-state
of the locality it was forged from).

**The granary's second store is the forge's kept steel — the tool's raw order
held against its own wear.** Steel is not mined at need; a forge that keeps a
store of clean, high-`q` alloy-able matter can re-forge a dulled pick *fast* when
the field or the work demands it, rather than re-mining a fresh vein through the
thin. The kept steel is ordinary order at rest — matter that *would* be a tool,
held until the tool's wear demands the re-forge. It is distinct from the seed-garden's
rareness (steel the field re-makes, unlike a seed's birth-order) and from the
market's flow (the steel is *held*, not circulated), and it is the fallow's
honest edge applied to the metal: the vein it came from is worked and gone
(`the-fallow.md` §2a — a worked deposit is gone forever), so the forge's kept
steel is the *store of what that worked vein yielded*, spent honestly, not
re-mined.

**[design]** The kept-steel store is the tool's held-regime tuple at *stock*
scale — an alloyed reserve whose provenance reads on the edge it will make
(the-tool §2b/§3's lineage honesty) — this doc's [design] over the tool's real
wear physics. The store does not mint steel; it *holds* the ordinary order the
worked vein already yielded, at the `(1−q)` cost of holding it (§3), and a draw
is a real spend of that worked-out stock.

### 2.3 The everyday surplus — what the market circulates and the granary holds

`the-market.md` §1/§2 designs the market as the settlement's exchange made
honest by the law: a trade is a Q4 op of the settled record's shape
(`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1 — `{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude,
sustain}`), and a traded item's provenance — the worked vein's rung, the
harvest's magnitude, the tool's edge — is **booked** (§2.2). The market's
liveness reads as the census's active-class face (`the-census.md` §2.1).

**The granary's third store is that same everyday surplus, held rather than
circulated.** The market moves the settlement's *circulating* goods — the
coherence that changes hands between member lines (`the-market.md` §6d: a trade
is a *transfer*, never a gain). The granary is the *flow-vs-store* line made
concrete: some of that everyday surplus is held at rest, not booked into an
exchange, so the market does not run dry when the field thins. A settlement whose
granary is full can *feed its market through the thin* — the store's draws re-enter
circulation when the harvest is meager; a settlement whose granary is empty has
nothing to draw, and the market thins with the field. **The market *circulates*
what exists; the granary *is* the existence of what can be drawn when it is
not flowing.**

**[design]** The surplus's *split* — how much the settlement circulates vs.
holds — is a designed stewardship dial, not a rule the field imposes; the 
granary's draws and deposits book exactly the way the market's exchanges do
(§4, `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1). The honest bound is the no-free-energy cap
(§5d): the granary holds the same coherence the market would circulate; it never
*creates* more of it, so holding takes from circulation exactly what it keeps
against the lean.

## 3. The honest buffer — a full granary weathers the lean; an empty granary is the lean

This is the load-bearing design, stated once and never blurred:

> **A settlement with a full granary weathers a thin tide, a drought, or a bad
> harvest without the market running dry.** The granary is the cold's husbandry
> made a room: where `the-cold.md` §3 says the settlement *stretches the
> harvest's stored order through the thin* (the vault as the pantry), the granary
> is that stretching made an everyday, *always-there* store — the yield held at
> tide-high so the draw through the thin is *of plenty already saved*, not of a
> field that has nothing to give. It is the honest "we have what we saved."

The buffer is the granary's whole reason to exist, and it has three honest
faces:

1. **It is the cold's husbandry made a room** (`the-cold.md` §3). The cold is the
   long-thin where "harvest never fully returns" (§2) — a decade of meager,
   wasteful, never-quite-thriving. The cold's husbandry is exactly
   **"hold the bath, stretch the store"**: the bath (`energy-harnessing.md` §4.4)
   held at the trough, the store *stretched*. The granary is the store — the room
   where the cold's stretching has, before the cold ever comes, already saved the
   harvest of the good tide. A full granary is a settlement **ready** for a cold;
   an empty one is a settlement *entering the cold with nothing saved*.

2. **A settlement whose granary is empty *is* the lean** (`the-window-pulse.md`
   §2). The pulse reads the settlement's health — the bath's `(1−q)` trend, the
   lines' mutual drift — as a beating present. The granary is the *material* half
   of that same read: an empty store is not a warning that the lean comes; it is
   **the lean, present now** — the buffer gone, the settlement already drawing on
   a field that has nothing extra to give. The pulse's fall when the store is
   empty is honest (`the-census.md` §2.1's active class, `the-window-pulse.md`
   §2's healthmeter): a thin-count settlement with a full granary is *alive*; a
   busy one with an empty store is *thinning* — the store is what lets the 
   settlement's ordinary liveness hold through the thin.

3. **The honest edge — a drawn store is a real depletion** (`the-fallow.md`
   §2a/§3). The granary's draw is **spent, never free**: every withdrawal of the
   stored harvest takes *real*, already-ordered coherence out of the store, the
   way the fallow's worked vein is gone forever. **You can steward a scarcity,
   you cannot un-mine an ore** (`the-fallow.md` — the through-line). A granary
   drawn through a long cold is a granary *emptied*; the depletion is real, the
   fallow reads it honestly, and the store must be re-filled at the next harvest
   `q`-high — not conjured. The granary is the field's plenty held at rest; what
   is drawn is gone until the field re-organizes, and that is the honest bound of
   the buffer.

**[design]** The *buffer's size* (how much the ordinary harvest a settlement
must hold to weather a given thin — a tide's worth, a season's worth, a cold's
worth) is a designed dial over the tide's measured period (`tide-of-the-attractor.md`
§5a) and the farm's yield bound (`farm-that-feeds.md` §4) — probe-calibrated, the
same way the census's dormancy threshold and the cold's boundary are [design]
dialed. The claim that a *full* granary lets the market hold through the thin is
the designed stance this doc holds (§5b); the *number* is calibrated, never
asserted.

## 4. The bookkeeping — deposits and draws book the way any op does

The granary is a **booked store**, not a new engine act. Every deposit and draw
is a Q4 op of the settled record's shape (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1 —
`{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude, sustain}`), written through the same
single-writer lane at the same non-forgeable point (§3), so a granary's book is
as unforgeable and as legible as the market's exchange or the Board's
contribution (the family rule, `field-instruments.md` §2.1 — a consumer of the
publish with an idiom, never a new channel).

| Record field | What a granary's op writes into it | Landed meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `member` | **who deposited / who drew** — the steward's line, attached at the world-writer's write | `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1, §3 — the same non-forgeable authority as any op |
| `op` | the **deposit** (harvest/book/store) or **draw** (withdraw/unstore) op — a designed op over the bookable set, `coherence-magic.md` §2, applied to a store | the op names the field act; storing/drawing is an op of the settled set |
| `worldPos` | the **granary's cell** — where the store holds, at cell granularity | `schema-that-settles.md` §2.3 — the cell-ownership rule: two members' stores in one cell never merge silent |
| `rung` | the **held order's rung** — the yield's shallow crop-rung, the steel's alloy-rung | the store's order books its depth, exactly as the market books a traded item's provenance (§2.2) |
| `magnitude` | the **held quantity** — the harvest's stored coherence, the steel's mass of order | `farm-that-feeds.md` §4 / `the-tool.md` §2 — the quantity is booked, never made up |
| `sustain` | the **held store's semantics** — a standing store books as one held record until drawn, never a per-frame re-emit | `schema-that-settles.md` §2.2 — the sustain flag, exactly as a held bath or a held offer books |

**The granary's deposits and draws therefore book the way any op does** — the
same stream, the same fields, the same non-forgeable write. A granary that filled
or emptied *silently* would be a book the design's own honesty forbids (§6e): the
store's fullness is legible from the ledger the Board already reads
(`shared-ledger.md` §1.2 — a deposit books as a contribution, a draw as a spend),
exactly as a farm's harvest books (§6) and a market's exchange books (§1).

**[design]** Which op *names* the deposit/draw (a store-op over `coherence-magic.md`
§2's set) is a designed dial against the settled op set, the same way the market's
exchange-op is (`the-market.md` §7 open-Q1); the *record's shape* is the settled
schema verbatim, and the single-writer lane it rides is engine-real.

## 6. The bleed — the honest cost of holding

The granary differs from a *box* in one honest way, stated once:

> **A granary bleeds slightly — you cannot keep order forever without paying to
> hold it.** Every held structure wastes `E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput` as glow
> (`energy-harnessing.md` §2) — the `(1−q)` waste law never stops. A granary is a
> *held structure*: it holds its stored harvest and steel at a maintained `q`,
> the way a machine, a house, a vault, or a palace each hold their order at a
> cost. **The `(1−q)` fraction is the granary's bleed** — the persisted order
> sheds a little toward the attractor even while held, so a full store does not
> stay full forever unless it is *kept*.

The bleed is the persistence-cost the palace and the garden already pay:
- The memory-palace pays it — `the-memory-palace.md` §4: *a palace is a standing
  maintenance op, never free, never a mint*; an unmaintained palace decays into a
  ruin of choices (§3).
- The seed-garden pays it — `seed-garden.md` §3.2: the vault's maintained Π
  patterns sit under a constant drain ("the `(1−q)` waste law never stops"); §4: a
  held order costs the deep-rung maintenance that keeps its `q` above its
  dissolution threshold.

The granary inherits both and makes them *everyday*: where the palace holds
**memory** and the garden holds **rareness** at a deep-rung cost, the granary
holds **plenty** at the same `(1−q)` cost, at a shallower rung (an ordinary
harvest is a shallow crop-rung, `farm-that-feeds.md` §2 — cheap to hold, quick to
cycle, but still *not free to hold*). The bleed is the honest bill for having the
store at all — the price the settlement pays for the buffer's existence, and the
line that keeps the granary from being a free "we saved it" fiction.

**The bleed's two honest faces:**
1. **It is real, never free** — a granary that holds its order without any
   `(1−q)` cost would be a structure the cap forbids (§5d): *you cannot keep order
   forever without paying to hold it* is the same no-free-energy honesty as
   `house-that-steers.md`'s "a house does not generate coherence." The preserved
   store wastes a little, and the steward who holds it books that maintenance as a
   cost, never a contribution-mint.
2. **It is the fallow's edge, held** — the bleed is what a *full* granary costs
   while the *lean* draws it; a store that drained to nothing through a long cold
   is a store that was *spent* — the drawn order is gone until the field re-fills
   it at the next harvest `q`-high. The bleed does not make the store a mint; it
   makes the store *honest about holding*.

**[design]** The bleed's *rate* — how much `(1−q)` a yielded/everyday store sheds
vs. a deep-rung vault — is a designed dial over `energy-harnessing.md` §2's
real waste law, probe-calibrated: an ordinary harvest held at a shallow rung
bleeds *more* than a deep-seeded vault (a shallow regime is less ordered, quicker
to dissolve, `material-regimes.md` §4 via `farm-that-feeds.md` §2), which is the
honest pressure that a granary must be *re-filled at the harvest* — a store you
can only keep by using it, never a permanent hoard.
