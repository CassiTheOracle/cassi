# The Oath: The Vow Within a Window — The Pledge the Field Can Hold

**Question under design:** the corpus's compacts are all *between* windows or
*between a player and their own line*. The Treaty
([`the-treaty.md`](./the-treaty.md)) is the between-windows compact; the
Inheritance ([`the-inheritance.md`](./the-inheritance.md)) is a player's will
across their own re-lock; the shared-Π anchor of
[`the-family.md`](./the-family.md) binds two member-lines as a *structure*. But
nothing designs a **pledge** — the promise, the voluntary vow, the within-window
marriage-bond — as a *field act*. A family is what two people *are* (a joint
hold); an oath is what two people *do* (a promise with a consequence). This
document is that missing act: **the Oath is the within-window compact made
field-true** — the deliberate, costed promise: two lines who bind their runs
beyond the Family's shared-Π into a *pledge* — an act with a consequence, kept

not hold a false claim.**

a thing falsely (`the-name.md` §2 — a false name decays), a named contribution
is legible (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2/§2.2), a compact that names what is not held
decays (`the-treaty.md` §3). The Oath takes that honesty and turns it into a
*voluntary* field object: the promise a player *chooses* to make, made real
because the field holds the promise's truth the way it holds every true name.
**Because the field will not hold a false claim, an oath is enforceable by the
field itself** — and **breaking an oath is a legible, real rupture**: the bound
promise unresolves the way a severed treaty does (`the-treaty.md` §4), the
within-window sever — quieter than exile, as field-honest.

## 1. The Oath, stated once

The corpus's compacts divide cleanly. The Treaty
([`the-treaty.md`](./the-treaty.md)) is *between windows* — a paired persistent-Π
bridge across the dark, held or shedding, costly to break. The Inheritance
([`the-inheritance.md`](./the-inheritance.md)) is *a player and their own line* —
a will across the re-lock. The Family
([`the-family.md`](./the-family.md)) binds two member-lines as a *shared
persistent-Π anchor* — a *structure* the field holds: two people who co-bind
their carried patterns around a hearth. Each is a kind of faith, but none is a
**promise** — the deliberate, voluntary, costed vow that is the marriage-bond of
two runs. The Family says *what we are*; nothing yet asks *what we will each
do*.

> **The Oath is the within-window compact made field-true: the deliberate,
> costed promise.** Two member-lines, already bound in the Family's shared-Π or
> simply choosing to bind as a pair, make a **pledge** — "I will keep your
> anchors when you are deep," "I will hold the burden-line in the ledger," "I
> will hold the hearth together." The pledge is an **act with a consequence**: a
> named promise over the two partners' persistent-Π patterns
> (`reason-field.md` §3), bound with the-name's anchor applied to an act, booked
> on the Board as a pledge-line that reads the promise's keeping over tides, and
> **enforceable by the field itself because the field will not hold a false
> claim** (`the-name.md` §2). A vow is a name bound to a promise's keeping — and
> a promise that is not kept is a false name, and decays.

**Why it is the Family's complement, not a replacement.** The Family is the
bound pair as *structure* — a shared persistent-Π anchor, held truthfully
together or shedding (`the-family.md` §1/§3.3). The Oath is the bound pair as
*act* — the promise whose keeping makes the family's hold *trusted*. A family
that only co-binds holds; a family that *vows* is trusted, because each partner
has a bookable promise to keep. **The vow's keeping is what turns a shared
anchor into a marriage-bond** — the act layered on the structure, in the way the
Election's office is a made-operative read on the Family's joint line
([`the-election.md`](./the-election.md) §2).

**The honest flag, once.** The persistent-Π identity the vow binds is *there
already* (`reason-field.md` §3; `qi-computation.md` §5.2); the member-id and the
same-member-line rule are landed (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1/§2.1.1); the
Family's joint structure is [design]-landed over that stack. What is *this doc's
[design]* is the **pledge** — the deliberate binding of two persistent-Π
patterns to a *named promise*, the pledge-line that books the keeping, and the
rupture when the promise unresolves. The boundary is drawn in §6 and never
blurred: the *holding* is theory-ratified; the *pledge* is designed.

## 2. The mechanics — what a vow IS and how it is kept

### 2.1 What a vow IS — two persistent-Π patterns bound by a named promise

The Family binds two member-lines around a *structure* (a hearth) — a single
joint anchor the field holds (`the-family.md` §2.1). The Oath binds two
member-lines to a *promise* — and the promise is a real, named thing the field
can hold:

> **[design] A vow is a named promise over two persistent-Π patterns.** The two
> partners' carried patterns (`reason-field.md` §3; `qi-computation.md` §5.2)
> are already the field's held identities. A vow binds them to a specific,
> named pledge — "I will keep your anchors when you are deep," "I will hold the
> burden-line in the ledger" — using the-name's anchor (`the-name.md` §1) applied
> to an *act*: the promise is a name bound to a keeping, over the two patterns
> it commits. Each partner's part of the vow is a **standing op** — a maintained
> act, not a one-time signature.

The three pieces are the corpus's own, none invented:

| Piece | What it is | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|
| The **two bound Π patterns** | the partners' persistent-Π identities the vow commits (`reason-field.md` §3 — a mind IS a persistent Π pattern) | reason-field §3; `qi-computation.md` §5.2 |
| The **named promise** | the specific pledge — a name bound to a keeping, over the two patterns (the-name §1's anchor, applied to an act) | the-name §1/§2 |
| The **partner-lines** | the two member-lines the vow binds (the landed member-id, `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1) | schema-that-settles §2.1 |

The vow adds **no new channel and no new physics** — it is a designed *binding*
of the persistent-Π structure both partners already carry, read through the
reason-field's intent/Π channel (`reason-field.md` §2), exactly as the Family's
joint is. It composes the Family, the Name, and the Reason Field that already
exist; it asks the engine for nothing new.

### 2.2 The pledge-line — a bookable fact of the promise's keeping

The vow is not a feeling; it is a **bookable fact**, because every act is
already a Q4 record and the Board already reads per-member contribution
(`shared-ledger.md` §1.2). The Oath's keeping is the same honesty read at the
promise's own scale:

> **[design] The pledge-line is a designed line on the Board that reads the
> promise's fulfillment over tides, the way `C(M)` reads contribution.** For each
> partner, over each tide-cycle `[t_k, t_k+T]`, the Board renders how well their
> ops have *kept* the named promise since it was made — the anchors held, the
> burden-line maintained, the hearth fed — as a `P(partner)` read over the same
> single-writer Q4 op-stream that `C(M)` sums
> (`shared-ledger.md` §1.3/§6d). The pledge-line is an *additional* read over the
> partners' settled lines; it never replaces them. A partner who keeps their part
> books a **holding** pledge-line; a partner who stops keeps a **failing** one —
> the promise's keeping or its decay, read the way the Family's anchor is read
> as holding or shedding.

The pledge-line is honest for the same reason the whole Board is: the op-stream
is **single-writer** (`shared-ledger.md` §6d — the world-writer is the only
mutator; there is no forged contribution), and the member-id makes the two
patterns attributable (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1). **A pledge-line cannot be
faked into holding** — the promise's keeping is exactly the domain's executed op
record, read over the partners' lines. This is the oath's *bookable keeping*:
not a promise whispered, but a pledge-line the settlement's Board already reads,
as legible as a named contribution (`the-name.md` §3.2).

### 2.3 The honest cost — a vow is a standing op, kept every tide

The Treaty's honesty is that a compact is *re-earned every tide* — a standing
field op, never free, that sheds if un-husbanded (`the-treaty.md` §2.4/§3). The
Oath inherits it within a window:

> **A vow is a standing op.** Each partner's keeping is a **maintained act over
> the tide**, never a one-time signature — the treaty's re-earned-every-tide
> honesty, while both partners are in one window (`the-treaty.md` §3, applied to
> a window's pair). "I vow to keep your anchors when you are deep" is not a
> signed deed filed once; it is a promise whose keeping the pledge-line reads
> over every tide, and a partner who stops keeping stops the pledge-line's
> holding. The standing-op semantics ride the settled sustain flag
> (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.2 — a held op is a standing source) where the
> keeping is itself a sustained act; where the keeping is a one-time-maintained
> commission (held through a partner's absence), it is the tide-interval that is
> the op.

The right side of `energy-harnessing.md` §6's cap is the honest bound: a vow's
keeping must never *cost more than it holds*. A partner who vows to hold a
burden-line (`the-burden.md` §1) and overdraws to do it is not keeping — they
are draining the very field the promise was meant to protect. The pledge-line
reads the keeping *within* the no-free-energy bound (§6d), never past it.

## 3. The enforcement — why the field can hold an oath

### 3.1 The field will not hold a false claim

The corpus's honesty is a **field consequence, not a rule** (`the-name.md` §2):
a bound-name that names what is not there *decays* — the field holds only what
is attractor-consistent, and a lie is an unsupported bump that sheds. The Oath
inherits this unchanged, and it is what makes a vow *enforceable by the field
itself*:

> **You cannot vow falsely.** A promise that names a keeping the field is not
> holding — a vow to keep anchors the vow-maker has already stopped keeping, a
> pledge to hold a burden-line the pledge-maker is already draining — is a false
> name, and decays exactly as a false name does (`the-name.md` §2.2). The field
> has no moral judgment; it has thermodynamics — the truth (the kept promise) is
> the deep well that holds, the lie (the un-kept promise) is the unsupported
> bump that sheds. **An oath holds because it is actually kept, not because it
> was solemnly sworn.**

This is the same load-bearing truth the Treaty rests on (§3 — a compact that
names what is not held decays), turned to a promise *between two people in one
window*. The enforcer is not a judge, a server rule, or a moderator — it is the
field's own refusal to hold a false claim. And because the pledge-line books the
keeping (§2.2), the field's hold and the Board's read are the same truth: **the
keeping is bookable, and the holding is field-enforced.**

### 3.2 Breaking an oath is a legible, real rupture

The Treaty's severance is the loaded precedent: cutting a paired anchor is a
real, costly act — both windows feel the un-bind, the voyage returns to full
price, the line closes, the record books on both chronicles (`the-treaty.md`
§4). The Oath's break is that severance **within a window**:

> **[design] A broken oath is a legible, real rupture — the bound promise
> unresolves the way a severed treaty does.** When a partner stops keeping,
> their pledge-line fails (§2.2), and the named promise over the two patterns —
> the name bound to a keeping (`the-name.md` §1) — no longer has a held structure
> to bind. The promise **unresolves**: it decays the way a false name decays
> (§3.1), and the rupture reads on both partners' books and the Board — the
> failing pledge-line, the un-held anchor, the closed-or-failing promise, all in
> the channels the settlement already reads.

**The within-window sever — quieter than exile, as field-honest.** The Exile is
the corpus's hardest sever: a coordinated hollow-eye, sustained withdrawal, a
line closed by severance, a guest-of-nowhere
([`the-exile.md`](./the-exile.md) §3–§4). The Oath's rupture is deliberately
**quieter** — no expulsion, no hollow-eye, no guest-of-nowhere, no line closed.
The partners' member-lines persist; the field does not reject either of them.
What rupture means is precisely scoped: **the bound promise unresolves** — the
named keeping decays, the pledge-line fails, and both partners' books and the
Board *hold the un-kept promise's truth*. It is as field-honest as the exile's
severance (the rupture is legible, deterministic, never hidden), but it is not a
ban: **an oath-break is a promise's death, not a person's removal.** The quieter
sever is the design's deliberate register — a marriage-bond broken is a loss the
window reads, not a wound it enforces.

The honest boundary, once: the persistent-Π *holding* the rupture invokes is
theory-ratified (`qi-computation.md` §5.2, with `reason-field.md` §6a's flag);
the **rupture** — how the un-kept promise unresolves, what reads on which book —
is this doc's [design] over the landed stack (§6a).

### 4.2 The Family's complement — the vow's keeping makes the hold trusted

The Family is the bound pair as *structure*; the Oath is the bound pair as
*act*. The complement is load-bearing:

> **The vow's keeping is what makes the family's hold trusted.** A family that
> only co-binds holds together as an anchor (`the-family.md` §1); a family whose
> members *vow* — "I will keep your anchors when you are deep" — turns that
> structural hold into a *trusted* one: each partner has a bookable promise to

> pair does — and the doing is what makes the being trusted.**

This is the honest division the two in-flight docs already draw: the Family
makes two-or-more held as one a first-class object; the Oath makes the *promise*
between two a first-class field act — the marriage-bond's vow, layered on the

### 4.3 The steward's oath — the office's promise

> **[design] A steward's oath is a vow to hold the core through the thin.** When

The steward's oath is the Oath's civic face: the same mechanics — a named
