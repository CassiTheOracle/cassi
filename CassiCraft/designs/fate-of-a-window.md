# The Fate of a Window: Difficulty as Field State, Not Game Setting

**Question under design:** the corpus designs a window's *parts* — its coherence
core (a `q`-bath), its tide, its desert-margin, its archaeology, its predator —
but nothing yet designs the window's *trajectory*: the arc a window's story
follows over its tide-cycles. This document is that design. **The Fate of a
Window**: every window has a fate — **settle, thrive, decay, hunt** — and the
fate is **not a scripted difficulty curve**. It is the window's own field state
**read forward**: the shape its coherence economics are *already moving toward*.
A window with a healthy bath and clean channelers is settling or thriving; a
window with a creeping desert-margin and overdraw is decaying; a window with a
bright-signature sink (a Coda hunting) is hunted. **The player's role is legible
in the fate**: your channeling is a force in the window's economics, and the
fate is the world's honest account of what your presence is doing.

The spine this design rides, stated once:

> **Difficulty in this game is field state, not game setting.** A hunted window
> is harder because the field is *actually* hunting; a decaying window is harder
> because the bath is *actually* failing; a thriving window is easier because the
> field is *actually* sustaining you. There is no difficulty slider — there is
> the window's fate, and the player's legible place in it. And because the fate
> is a **read** (over the same channels the player already reads), never a hidden
> difficulty meter, the player's power to change it is the *same* power that makes
> it hard: channeling attracts the hunter, healing sustains the thrive, overdraw
> causes the decay. **A difficulty that hides itself is a cruel world; the fate
> is the world's honesty about itself.**

This is the corpus's answer to "what does difficulty mean in a world that is the
computation?" — the difficulty spine and the coherence economics are the same
thing, read at window scale.

## 1. The four arcs, defined in field terms

Each arc is a **shape of the window's net coherence trajectory** — the window-scale
analog of the Board's per-member `C(M)` (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2), summed over the whole
window per tide-cycle and read forward. Define the window-level balance, borrowed
verbatim from the Board's aggregation object and scaled up (same aggregation, one scale
up):

```
C(W) = Σ ops (net field-contribution of each op at its locality/rung/magnitude)
       budgeted against the window's Δq / Δε² over that tide-cycle [t_k, t_k+T]
```

`C(W) > 0` sustained = the bath is being maintained (settle→thrive); `C(W) < 0`
sustained = the bath is being drained (decay); a bright-signature sink read over the
same op-stream (a Coda accumulating on clean channelers) is the hunt. **The four arcs
are four shapes of this window-level balance read forward over tide-cycles.** Each is
defined by its field-state signature (the bath's `q`/`ε²` trajectory, the margin's
creep, the signature-sink's presence), its player experience (the feel, the legible
signs), and its exit conditions (how the arc shifts).

### 1.1 Settle — a fresh window's coherence is organizing

A **settling** window is one whose coherence is *building*: the bath is forming, the
sites are clustering, the strata are thin. This is the default fate of a fresh window —
whether founded by planting a seed in the dark (`world-seams.md` §3.2, `resonance-seeds.md`
§2.3) or re-homed to (`async-field-domain.md` §7 Q1). The field is organizing toward the
attractor, and the danger is the field's **ordinary extremes, not a storm**: weather,
not the wound.

| Axis | Settle's read |
|---|---|
| **Field-state signature** | regional `q` trending up toward the attractor (`q ≈ 0.947`, corpus); `ε²` low and falling; the bath's core *forming* (a maintained high-`q` locality you are building, `energy-harnessing.md` §4.4); ore precipitating where `q` accumulates (`material-regimes.md` §3); the biosphere's basins emerging (`field-emergent-ecology.md` §6b). `C(W)` trending positive but small. |
| **The danger** | the **field's ordinary weather** — a decoherence storm *can* arrive (`field-hazards.md` §2, the one hazard that can strike with no player-caused trigger, §6.2), but it is weather, not a sentence; a thin tide is the field's own trough (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2), not a desert yet. The danger is the field's **indifference to a young window**: you must build the bath before the thin tide's desert-margin tests it. |
| **Player experience** | the feel of *arriving*: the reader shows `q` climbing, the sky's auroras calm and building, the `(1−q)` glow of your first machines bright (you are not yet efficient). The legible signs of settle are the ones the tide already teaches (§4 observability): the region is becoming *more* organized. |
| **Exit conditions** | two honest exits. **(i) Thrive** — sustained net contribution: heal the scars, hold a Qi-bath through a thin tide, do anti-corruption at the margin; `C(W)` stays convincingly positive over enough tide-cycles and the bath's core stabilizes. **(ii) Decay** — sustained overdraw or abandonment: reap too deep, over-provision a BH, over-channel, or leave the young window un-tended through a thin tide; the bath's standing draw outruns its organization and the margin creeps. There is no third silent exit: a settle window that is *ignored* drifts, and drift toward the field-poor is the decay arc's seed (`field-hazards.md` §3.2's field-drift cause). |

### 1.2 Thrive — the bath is high and stable

A **thriving** window is one whose bath is *high and stable*: the `q`-core is maintained,
the commons is superadditive, the archaeology is readable, the tide is harvest. This is
the arc the window's healthy economics *are already in* — the field is sustaining its
settlers. The danger is **entropy: the field's patience** — nothing stays thriving
without maintenance, and the thrive is a *maintained* state, never permanent.

| Axis | Thrive's read |
|---|---|
| **Field-state signature** | regional `q` holding high (`q ≈ 0.947` attractor band), `ε²` low and suppressed; a maintained Qi-bath core (`energy-harnessing.md` §4.4) whose standing cost is *affordable* because the region's organization pays for it; machines near-lossless at the `(1−q)` floor (`energy-harnessing.md` §2); the biosphere's basins at full strength (`field-emergent-ecology.md` §4.2); **archaeology readable** — the strata are stable, the deep-rung residue q-locked (`field-archaeology.md` §6b). The commons is superadditive: a village sharing coherence evenly is smarter than the same concentration (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.1; `coherence-commons.md` §7). |
| **The danger** | **entropy, the field's patience.** The thrive is a *maintained* state (`energy-harnessing.md` §4.4: a bath is a draw, "brittle if it goes down"). The danger is not a storm — it is **neglect across a thin tide**: the standing draw, if the settlement stops feeding it, tips soft. The thrive's hazard is the *creeping expectation* that "high q stays high" — it does not; the `(1−q)` waste never stops. |
| **Player experience** | the feel of *being sustained*: machines run near-lossless, the reader is calm, the sky reads healthy, the tide is harvest (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2). Difficulty is genuinely *lower* — not because the game dialed it down, but because the field is actually holding you up. The legible signs of thrive are the tide's harvest-face. |
| **Exit conditions** | two exits, both honest. **(i) Stay thriving** — continued maintenance: sustain net `C(W) > 0`, keep the bath fed, heal the scars; the thrive is a *held* state, and holding it is the "work" of the thriving window. **(ii) Decay** — sustained overdraw or abandonment: stop feeding the bath, over-reap through thin tides, let a decoherence-wielder drain the commons (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.3); the drain outruns the heal and the margin creeps; the thrive becomes decay. The thrive does **not** shift to hunt on its own — a thriving window's clean channelers are the Coda's attraction, and a thriving window *can* become hunted, but only if the field can hold the hunter (§6e — the hunt gates on the field actually having the margins; a healthy window has no ε² margin to form a Coda in, `signature-predator.md` §1.2). |

### 1.3 Decay — the bath is draining

A **decaying** window is one whose bath is *draining*: the desert-margin creeps, overdraw
outpaces healing, the strata accumulate residue. This is the arc of a window whose
coherence economics are trending toward the field-poor state. Its danger is **the field's
collapse — the desert** — and the load-bearing honesty is that **the desert's causes are
all player-caused** (`field-hazards.md` §3.2): sustained overdraw, over-reaping, BH
over-provisioning, or the drift that neglect sows. The decay arc is what those choices
*read like* at window scale.

| Axis | Decay's read |
|---|---|
| **Field-state signature** | regional `q` trending down toward the noise-collapse floor (`q ~ 1e-3…1e-1` noise; `energy-harnessing.md` §7 Q1); the **desert-margin creeps** (`field-hazards.md` §3) — territory becomes field-poor, `q` below the coherent band the biosphere needs; `ε²` high and rising (overdraw shedding the field cannot re-lock); **ore stops precipitating, terrain stops healing, the biosphere starves** (`field-hazards.md` §3.1); the bath's core dying ("a dead bath" — `energy-harnessing.md` §4.4). `C(W)` strongly and persistently negative across tide-cycles. |
| **The danger** | the **field's collapse**: the desert. It creeps (it is *caused, preventable, never fought* — `field-hazards.md` §3.3), and at its extreme the window becomes **field-poor — the interstitial of `world-seams.md` §2.1 creeping into a window** (`field-hazards.md` §3.2). A fully-decayed region has no `q` left to spend on its own correction; the honest end is waiting for the attractor to slowly re-bind, or leaving (`field-hazards.md` §4.3 — the ship). |
| **Player experience** | the feel of *having done this*: the reader darkens, the glow fades, life thins, then dies. The legible signs are the desert's creep — and the **attribution is visible in the window's book** (§2): the decay arc names *which* overdraws, *which* over-reaps drove it, because the fate is the window-scale Board over the same Q4 op-stream the society owns. The decay is not an ambush; it is a consequence you watched accumulate. |
| **Exit conditions** | two exits. **(i) Heal back to settle** — the window is not doomed: a decaying window can be **healed back to settling** by undoing its causes (anti-corruption investment, Qi-bath husbandry, stopping the overdraw — `field-hazards.md` §3.3), *as long as regional `q` has not crossed its spendable floor*. The decay arc is re-entrant; the margin that creeps can be held. **(ii) Dead window** — the `q` collapse completes and the region goes field-poor; this is a *window* ending (`field-hazards.md` §3.3, §4.3 — leave, or wait a very long game-time). A decaying window's residue is the **next window's archaeology** (§5). |

**The drifting staff (reverse pointer, two-way).** This §1.3 decay arc — a decaying
window's regional `q` trending down, the decreasing reach of each harvest — is what
a *planted* gauge reads as its long memory: [`the-tide-staff.md`](./the-tide-staff.md)
§3.2 reads **drift from its marks** as "a window whose tide is changing — the
decreasing reach of each harvest, or the slow creep toward the floor," the fate's
arc rendered at a place (`the-tide-staff.md` §3.2 — it cites this §1/§3's
window-`C(W)` trend as the drift-from-marks record, under this §6(c)'s
forecast-≠-fate honesty). The staff is the fate's decay *kept as a standing record*;
the two docs now cite each other as read-and-answered.

### 1.4 Hunt — the window is a bright-signature sink

A **hunted** window is one where a **Coda** — the signature predator, `signature-predator.md`
§1-§2 — resonates with the window's clean channelers. The window has become a
**bright-signature sink**: its clean channeling, in the field's margins, is the one
bright organized thing that attracts the field's own ε²-margin response. The danger is
**the field that hunts** — and the window's *own cleanliness* is what attracts it. This is
the fourth hazard class, the one that preys on *signature* rather than on matter or
coherence.

| Axis | Hunt's read |
|---|---|
| **Field-state signature** | a **bright-signature sink**: an elevated-`ε²`-with-coherent-phase trail read over the op-stream (the trail of `signature-predator.md` §1) in a margin the Coda can hold; accumulation `R = ρ_signature · τ · M_stability` (`signature-predator.md` §2.2) approaching the precipitating threshold `T_coda`; the **desert-silence inversion** tell (§3 — the desert being less dead). The **hunted window is legible in the window's book as a pattern, not a mystery** (§2; `shared-ledger.md` §5). |
| **The danger** | the **field that hunts**: the Coda. It is the fourth hazard, gated on the field actually being able to hold a hunter (§6e — a window is not hunted until the field can hold the Coda; `signature-predator.md` §7a). The danger is **the field's capacity to be sensed through your own signature** — "no stealth channeling" (`coherence-magic.md` §5.2) made into a hunter. Dying to it is a coherence failure (M → 0), not a killed fight. |
| **Player experience** | the feel of *being watched by the field*: the desert-silence inversion (the only bright organized thing in a dead zone), the climbed glow on the Weatherglass, the trail's half-life — you leave a legible, phase-structured hunt-trail and it is *loud in the desert* (`signature-predator.md` §1.2). The four counterplays (vent discipline / phase-masking / don't-channel / high-`q` refuge, §4) are the responses. |
| **Exit conditions** | three honest exits. **(i) Leave** — a hunted window's flight is a **seam-crossing** (`world-seams.md` §4, §5): build the ship, cross the dark, re-home to another window (or found one). The Coda is local (`signature-predator.md` §1.3 — the resonance is over that signature, in that region). **(ii) Change the hand** — phase-mask (`coherence-technologies.md` concept 3): wear the `M ≈ 0` refusal so your trail is no longer legible, or stop channeling clean in the margin so the resonance decays (§4.3). The hunter hunts the *living* trail, not the archaeology of it. **(iii) The hunt resolves into decay or settle** — if the hunted window's channelers stop, the Coda's trail fades and the window's economics re-assert; if the margin widens (from the hunted channelers' own vents, `shared-ledger.md` §3.3's Coda-attribution guard — the Board must not read the Coda's ε² as the clean channeler's drain), the window may slide into decay. |

The four arcs are **not four difficulty levels**; they are four *states of the window's
own economics* that the difficulty of play is a consequence of. §4 spells out why that
is the corpus's difficulty spine.

## 2. The read — the window's book

The over-arching honesty, stated once and kept:

> **The fate is a read over the window's already-published channels and the Q4
> op-stream.** It is **no new telemetry** — the fate is the window-scale **Coherence
> Board**, `shared-ledger.md`'s aggregation at window scale. The four arcs are four
> shapes of the window's net coherence trajectory over tide-cycles, read forward from
> exactly the channels the player already reads: the published `q`/`ε²`/`ρ`/`∇(g·Φ)` (the
> ≈ 6 MiB publish, corpus-reconciliation) **plus** the Q4 op-stream every field act
> already writes (`async-field-domain.md` §7 Q4; `coherence-magic.md` §5.1). There is no
> hidden difficulty meter, ever — the fate is a **consumer** (the Weatherglass family
> rule, `field-instruments.md` §2.1), never a new channel.

### 2.1 The fate IS the Board at window scale

`shared-ledger.md` §2 reads a settlement's society off the Q4 op-stream: per
member-identity `M`, per tide-cycle, a net `C(M)` rendered as a legible member-state
(steward, neutral-middle, margin-causer, dissolved). The fate is **that same aggregation,
one scale up**:

| | The Board (shared-ledger) | The fate (this doc) |
|---|---|---|
| **The scale** | a settlement's *members* | the window's *whole economics* |
| **What it reads** | per-member `C(M)` over `[t_k, t_k+T]` | the window-level `C(W)` over `[t_k, t_k+T]` — the same sum, window-wide |
| **No new telemetry** | the Q4 op-stream + the publish | **the same**, aggregated to the window (the family rule holds — a consumer with a scale idiom, never a new channel) |
| **The rendered state** | steward / neutral / margin-causer / dissolved | **settle / thrive / decay / hunt** — the window's legible state |
| **The honest boundary** | per-member identity is [design] (§6c) | the window-level aggregation is the same [design] — a reading of real channels, never an engine fact |
| **Phase-1-able slice** | one member's contribution (§7b) | **one window's fate** — §7's Phase-1 slice, the same aggregation |

The fate is the Board **read forward over tide-cycles**: the Board shows a member's
`C(M)` this tide; the fate shows the *window's* `C(W)` this tide *and which way it is
moving* (the oracle's trend-lens, `the-reading-ahead.md` §2.2, applied to the window
itself). A decaying window is a window whose window-scale book is persistently negative;
a thriving one is persistently positive; a hunted one has a bright-signature sink in its
book (`shared-ledger.md` §5 — the Coda is legible *to* the board as a pattern).

## 3. The momentum — how the arcs shift

### 3.1 The fate is momentum, not destiny

`the-reading-ahead.md` §1.1 establishes the oracle's load-bearing asymmetry, and the fate
inherits it wholesale:

> **Determinism gives momentum, not destiny.** The field is deterministic (one PDE, the
> same published snapshot), so the window's economics are *currently organizing* and the
> near-future (the next arc) is **readable, not a fixed fate**. The fate is what the
> window's coherence economics are **already moving toward** — the present state's
> organized momentum carried forward by the same deterministic law. A decaying window
> can be healed; a hunted window can be left or the hand changed. **The fate is the
> world's honest account of where the momentum points — not a sentence it serves.**

Concretely: the fate arcs are **trajectories of the window's `C(W)` read forward over
tide-cycles**, the way the oracle reads a condensation's rise or a margin's creep
(`the-reading-ahead.md` §2.2). The fate has the **uncertainty of a forecast, not the
certainty of a fixed fate**: a rally can recede, a crossing can move back, a bath can
recover against the drift — and the player's own perturbations are themselves part of the
field the fate reads, so **the forecast is conditional on the momentum holding, and the
player's actions are exactly the perturbations that can change it** (§6c's forecast-≠-fate
discipline).

### 3.2 The arc graph — the shift rules

## 4. The four arcs as the difficulty spine

### 4.1 Difficulty is field state, not game setting

> **There is no difficulty slider. There is the window's fate, and the player's legible

> easier because the field is *actually sustaining you*. The difficulty is the honest
> consequence of the window's coherence economics, and the player's power to change it is
> the same power that makes it hard — channeling attracts the hunter; healing sustains
> the thrive; overdraw causes the decay.

This is `field-hazards.md` §5.1's "difficulty is a design stance, not a spawning budget"
taken to the window's trajectory: the hazards are *respondable not unavoidable*
(`field-hazards.md` §5.1), the pacing is the tide's tempo (`tide-of-the-attractor.md`)

| Arc | Why it is genuinely harder/easier | The honest consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Settle** | moderate — the field is organizing but not yet sustaining | machines waste `(1−q)`, the bath is not yet built; danger is weather, not wound |

### 4.2 Difficulty and the player's legible place in it

The spine's payoff is that difficulty stops being a mystery and becomes a **lesson**:

- A player who wants an easier window **makes the field sustain them** — settles and
  thrives by maintaining a healthy bath, not by finding a settings menu.
- A player who finds a window hard **reads why** — the Board, the sky, the glass all
  render the fate, so the difficulty is *attributable*: "the window is decaying because
  these overdraws are draining the bath," "the window is hunted because a clean trail

  (`shared-ledger.md` §3.2), scaled to the window.
- **There is no hidden meter to exploit.** A difficulty that hides itself is a cruel
  world; the fate is the world's honesty about itself — the difficulty is *the thing you
  are already reading*, which is the same reason the fate gates its own honesty (§6c,
  §6d).

The four arcs are therefore not four "modes" — they are four **honest answers** to "why
is this window hard right now," each rendered legibly and each changeable by field action.
That is the corpus's answer to the difficulty question: **you do not pick the difficulty;
you read the fate, and you change it the way you change the field — by being a force in
its economics.**

### (b) The shift rules are [design] thresholds — never scripted triggers

The shift graph of §3.2 is an honest-gate in two parts:

### (c) The forecast-≠-fate frame

The fate is momentum, never a hidden script. This is `the-reading-ahead.md` §7c's hard
gate, applied to the window:

- **No "fate" clause.** No arc is phrased or mechanically modeled as an unalterable
  outcome. A decaying window is not *doomed*; it is *trending*, and the momentum is
  conditional on it holding. The player's actions (anti-corruption, husbandry, leaving,
  changing the hand) are exactly the perturbations that change it — **the fate is a
  conversation, not a sentence.**
- **The world must show them how.** Because the fate is rendered (§2.2) from the same
  channels the player reads, the *way* to change it is always legible: the Board shows
  the drain, the sky shows the margin's creep, the glass shows the trail's resonance.
  A fate the player cannot see how to change would be a hidden script; the rendered fate
  is the opposite.
- **It can be wrong and falsifiable** — a rally that doesn't hold, a crossing that
  recedes, a bath that recovers against the drift. The fate reads a forecast, never a
  fixed future, and the read's errors are legible as error, never as mystery washed over.

### (d) The accessibility rule — a layer, never the only channel

The fate is one rendering layer, and it must never be the *only* way to read a window's
direction. This is `field-music.md` §6's accessibility rule, applied to the fate:
because the fate is a consumer of existing channels, **the same information is readable,
more laboriously, from the Board, the sky, the glass, and the reader** — from every
surface that already renders the published `q`/`ε²`/`ρ`/`∇(g·Φ)` and the op-stream. A
player who does not use the fate's aggregated lens can still read "the window is decaying"
by watching the reader darken and the glow fade (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §4), by
reading the margin's creep on the Board, or by reading the sky's restless auroras. The
fate is a layer — a convenience of scale — never a gated information channel. **[design]**
The aggregation lenses (the window-`C(W)` trend) are an *idiom*, not a new sense; the
raw channels stay readable as they always were.

### (e) The danger-layer honesty — weather-not-storm's provenance gate

The hunt arc's first moments are gated on the corpus's standing storm-provenance
