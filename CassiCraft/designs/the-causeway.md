## 1. The Causeway, stated once

The corpus's water has a character. [`the-marsh.md`](./the-marsh.md) designs the **slow
sea — the shallow, textured water that hides**: a low-flow, low-depth band of the liquid
regime where `q` is *patchy*, the sea's uniformity replaced by a fine-grained tessellation
of coherent hummocks and thin pools (§2). Its honest hiding is that the tessellated `q`
**diffuses a signature** — the walker's `ε²` wake, the clean presence, the trail all blur
into the texture, the pattern does not carry (§3). The walk gives a body's *ground*-act in
that water ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md)) — the un-roaded crossing whose craft, taken
*through* the marsh, muddies: the stride that steps with the lean on a ridge is, in the
patchy shallow, a wade that sheds a blurred wake. The cart gives the road's vehicle
([`the-cart.md`](./the-cart.md)) — the wain whose wheels convert a maintained line's
descent into a carried load. **None raises the crossing above the water.** Between the
wading-through (which muddies) and the natural crossing (which crosses the water's own
texture), there is a band the corpus never draws: **a built way over the hiding water** —
a structure that carries the walk's craft *above* the marsh instead of through it.

The Causeway is that structure, stated once and kept:

> **The Causeway is the built way over the hiding water — a house-that-steers structure
> that carries a walker (and a cart) *above* the marsh**: raised, deliberate, phase-kept,
> holding a **clean high-`q` deck** above the blur. Distinct from wading (which muddies
> the signature — the marsh's wake's-blur, `the-marsh.md` §3) and from the natural
> crossing (which crosses the water's own tessellation), the causeway is a held line the
> settlement maintains over the patchy field — so crossing the marsh on a causeway is a
> **true crossing**: a clean, legible transit that does not muddy your read nor the
> life-signal. And because the marsh's hiding was never a stealth trick but the field's
> own texture, **a causeway over a hiding water is a settlement's deliberate claim that
> even the field's blur can be crossed cleanly** — the marsh's secrecy *taken back* by a
> held structure, not stolen by a hidden way.

**Why this is the corpus's missing built way, honestly.** Every other crossing the corpus
builds is either the *through* act or the *natural* line: the walk goes *through* the
terrain ("where the road steers and the voyage sails, the walk gets there yourself"),
the road is a *manufactured line on the land* (`coherence-highway.md` §1), the cart rides
*that* line's descent. What the corpus has never designed is the **built way over a water
whose whole character is to hide** — the crossing that is itself a *structure*, holding a
steady line *above* the very patchiness that blurs every through-passage. The marsh hides
the wader (§4 — what hides you hides what hunts you); the causeway is the first built form
that **breaks that symmetry for the raised**: the deck holds its own clean `q` above the
blur, and the walker on it crosses legibly (a clean transit — and, honestly, a legible one,
§4) instead of covertly. That is the crossing that takes the marsh's secrecy back.

The honest scope, stated once: **the marsh's texture, the walk's craft, the cart's ride,
the house's architecture, the standing draw, the threshold's marks, the maintenance axis,
and the trail-read are their source docs' landed pieces; the causeway's composition — the
raised way's form, the clean crossing, the honest trade and the swallow — is this doc's
[design]** over them. The Causeway never claims the engine "builds a bridge" or "holds a
