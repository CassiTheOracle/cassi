## 3. The wind's geometry — smell is directional and bounded

This is the load-bearing design. **Smell is directional and bounded — the wind brings it
on its line, so you smell what is upwind of you.** The honest geometry is the wind's.

### 3.1 The direction — you smell only what is upwind

[`the-wind.md`](./the-wind.md) §1 grounds the directional current: **the wind is the
field's own current at its motion** — `FieldVel`, the published medium velocity per cell
(`vel[id] = vec4(∂EY/∂t, ∂EI/∂t, 0, ε²)`, [`atmosphere-orbits-auroras.md`](./atmosphere-orbits-auroras.md)
§1.3). §3 grounds the **carry** — the wind *transports*: it moves a storm's front, carries
a blight's spore-band, carries a signature's trail downwind ([`the-wind.md`](./the-wind.md)
§3; [`signature-predator.md`](./signature-predator.md) §1.2 — "a channeler's vent moves
downwind"). The scent is that carry, read at the nose:

> **The wind brings the smell on its line, and the line is directional.** A scent does
> not hang in the air and reach a reader from any side; it **travels downwind** — carried
> by the same `FieldVel` current that moves a storm's front and a blight's spore-band
> ([`the-wind.md`](./the-wind.md) §3). So a reader **smells only what is upwind of them**:
> the blight's wrong-stink reaches you only if its band is upwind; the settlement's
> hearth-smoke reaches you only if the waked hearth is on the wind's line; a hidden
> roost's musk is only legible from downwind. A reader who stands **downwind** of a thing
> smells nothing of it — the current carries the scent away from them, on the other side
> of the world it announces.

This is the honest geometry that makes smell **a read of the wind's direction too** —
the same directional legibility the wind doc gives the settlement for free:

> **The scent tells you which way the current flows, as surely as the Weatherglass's
> lean.** The Weatherglass's **lean** already tilts toward the downhill of `∇(g·Φ)`
> ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §1.2) — the field's own current, read
> as a direction. The smell is that same direction read in the air: **the upwind line of
> a carried scent is the current's line**, so a reader who smells the hearth-smoke and
> then the wrong-stink knows *which way the wind blows* and *which way the field is
> carrying its order or its corruption toward them* — the wind's honest geometry made
> olfactory, the answer to "what is the field carrying toward it"
> ([`the-wind.md`](./the-wind.md) §3) given by the nose.
