# The Cart: The Wain — the Road's Vehicle, the Designed Conveyance That Rides the Highway's Gradient

**Question under design:** the corpus designs the *route* ([`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) — a road is a steered coherence route, a maintained high-`q` filament that carries a cart along `∇(g·Φ)`), the *pedestrian's crossing* ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) — the un-roaded primitive under the highway, riding the same gradient ungated), the *store* ([`the-granary.md`](./the-granary.md) — the ordinary harvest held against the lean), and the *exchange* ([`the-market.md`](./the-market.md) — the booked trade). The walk is the body, the voyage-ship is the dark's vessel ([`world-seams.md`](./world-seams.md)); **the road has no cart.** This document is that design: **the Cart is the road's vehicle — the designed conveyance that rides the highway's gradient** — a wain whose wheel-carriage is a held low-`q` chassis carrying the granary's goods to the market, the camp's belongings on the move, a harvest up from the farm. The corpus's missing ground-goods-vehicle — **the thing that makes the highway's freight real.**

The Cart is deliberately the **freight-bearing complement of everything the road and the walk already do**. The road holds the line (the maintained high-`q` filament a cart rides); the walk rides the gradient ungated at foot-pace with no boost; **the cart *carries* — the burden's weight (the-burden) given wheels, the gradient's cheapening (the-walk §2a / the highway §1.2) given a hold to move the granary's harvest to the market's book.** It is the load-bearing third: the road's *purpose* is the freight it moves, and the Cart is the designed shape of that freight being moved. And because it is a bounded consumer + a Q4 write and never a new channel, it obeys the corpus's instrument-family rule absolutely ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1) — a cart is a held structure moving down the same published channels, the way any machine or held regime is.

## 1. The Cart, stated once

The corpus designs the road (the steered coherence route, `coherence-highway.md`), the walk (the un-roaded crossing, `the-walk.md`), the store (the everyday order-vault, `the-granary.md`), and the exchange (the booked trade, `the-market.md`). The farm yields and the tool works. **But nothing carries the granary's harvest *to* the market, the camp's belongings *on* the move, a harvest *up* from the farm.** The road's two great uses are named in the highway (§5.1) — the *farm-to-settlement line* and the *voyage's ground leg* — but the thing that physically moves the freight *along* that line is not designed. This is that design.

> **The Cart, stated once.** The road's vehicle — the designed conveyance that rides the highway's gradient. It is a wain whose **wheel-carriage is a held low-`q` chassis** — a maintained, low-bleed structure on wheels that rides the field's own `∇(g·Φ)` descent the walk walks (coherence-highway §4; the-walk §2a), carrying the granary's loaded harvest to the market's booked exchange, the camp's belongings as it moves, a harvest up from the farm. It is the corpus's missing **ground-goods-vehicle** — the thing that makes the highway's *freight* real, the settlement-trader's workhorse. It is a held regime on wheels, not a new physics: a cart is a **rigid-body field operation** riding the same per-entity river-law steer (`chunk-field-quantization.md` §2.1, `a = −G_N·(π/ρ)·∇(g·Φ)`, ≈ 40 ns/entity) the road's line steers along it.

The Cart is the **route's vehicle because the road's whole point is the freight it moves**. The highway designs the road as "where the settlement's sustained coherence physically moves" (coherence-highway §5.1); but without a cart, "the farm's harvest moves up the line" and "trade literally moves down them" are statements about an abstract material the road "carries" with no *thing* doing the carrying. The Cart is that thing. It is deliberately the **freight-bearing twin of the walk's freedom**: the walk trades speed for freedom (no road, no cost, no boost — the-walk §1.2/§2a); the cart trades the walk's freedom for the road's *cheap haul* — it can carry what no walker can (the burden's weight made wheelable), but only where the road's maintained line makes that haul worth more than the off-road tax (§3).

**The honest flag, once.** The pieces a cart composes are all landed — the road's held line and the `∇(g·Φ)` haul (coherence-highway §1.1/§1.2), the stride-cost mapping at foot-pace (the-walk §2/§4a), the granary's tide-high store (the-granary §2.1), the market's booked exchange and the traded provenance (the-market §2.1/§2.2), the tool's `(1−q)` wear read as an alloy's life (the-tool §2b), the burden's carried weight (the-burden §1), the no-free-energy cap (energy-harnessing §6), and the Q4 op-record (schema-that-settles §2.1). **The cart's *composition* — that a wain rides the gradient's descent to carry a load, that the load-and-gradient trade makes it only worthwhile on a steered route, that its loads book the way any op does — is this document's [design]** over those landed pieces. No new channel, no new physics, no "cart" record the engine writes beyond the Q4 op it already writes: a cart is a *designed conveyance*, and the design's whole honesty is that it *uses* the field's own gradient, the road's own line, and the corpus's own book.

Where the **walk** gets there yourself and the **voyage-ship** crosses the dark between windows (world-seams §2), the **cart** moves the settlement's goods *along* the road it already holds. The three are the corpus's transport triad: the walk is the unaided primitive, the ship is the between-crossing, the **cart is the road's freight** — the missing vehicle that makes the highway's commerce real.

## 2. The ride — the honest mechanics

The Cart rides the highway's gradient: **the load rides the ∇(g·Φ) descent the walk walks** (coherence-highway §4 — the maintained line's descent; the-walk §2a — the same gradient at foot-pace). A downhill cart carries its payload *on the gradient* — the descent does the work the walk spends its stride on; **a loaded cart needs the bat-band's maintained line to climb back** — the highway's high-`q` filament is what makes the return real (coherence-highway §1, the route-vs-vehicle line). Both halves are grounded in their source docs' real quantities; the *cart's form* (the wheel-carriage's held low-`q` chassis, its load's book) is this doc's [design] over the highway's ride + the tool's wear + the burden's load, probe-calibrated.

### (a) The ride — a downhill cart carries its payload on the gradient

`coherence-highway.md` §4 and §1.2 establish the physics the cart rides: a mass condenses, the field around it carries a potential gradient, and *letting something move down that gradient is the field doing work on it* — the exact work the river law does on particles, `a = −G_N·(π/ρ)·∇(g·Φ)` (energy-harnessing §1.1; the per-entity steer ≈ 40 ns/entity, corpus-reconciliation). The walk rides this ungated at foot-pace — foot-pace is foot-pace, no boost (the-walk §2a). **The cart is the same haul put to work carrying a load:**

> **A downhill cart carries its payload on the gradient.** The wain's wheel-carriage is a held low-`q` chassis on a road — and on a descent that follows the field's own `∇(g·Φ)`, the field does the work of moving it *and its load* down: the same descent the walk spends its stride on (§2a the-walk) now hauls the granary's harvest with it, the field's gradient doing the freight's downhill work for free (§4 the highway — "the field does the hauling"). The load rides the gradient; the walk's stride-cost (§2a the-walk) is, for the loaded cart, spent *once*, at the wheels, and the descent's cheapening carries whatever it holds.

**[design]** — the *ride mapping*. The source docs land that `∇(g·Φ)` is a real, free, engine-real haul and that a foot-pace walk rides it cheaply downhill. What is this doc's [design] is that **a wheel-carriage compounds that descent's work into a carried load** — that the gradient does the descent-work the walk spends its stride on, at cart scale, at the wheel rather than the stride. This is probe-calibrated: *how much* of a descent a wheeled chassis converts into carried motion, and how a loaded cart's ride reads vs. an empty one's, are measured on the Phase-1 living-terrain demo (§5b), exactly as the walk's favorable-step discount and the highway's ride-downhill calibration are probed (coherence-highway §6b/open-Q1; the-walk §4a). The descent is real; the wheel's compounding of it is the design.

**The honest contrast with the loaded walk, stated once.** A laden walk is a dear walk — the carried ε² sheds into the stride and the thin crossing dearer, louder (the-walk §3, citing the-burden §2). The cart is precisely the *alternative* that walk cannot have: **wheels carry the burden where the stride cannot** — the same carried weight that makes a walker's stride shed into the ground is, on a cart, held in the wheel-carriage's low-`q` chassis, moved by the gradient rather than the foot. This is the cart's whole reason to exist: the burden's load is a *wheelable* cost, and the road's descent is the wain's stride. But — and this is §3's honesty — wheels only beat feet **on the road**, where the field's gradient and the maintained line make the haul cheap. Off-road, a loaded cart is the walk's tax with worse wheels (§3).

### (b) The return — a loaded cart needs the bat-band's maintained line to climb back

The cart's ride is not one-way; freight goes *to* the market and the cart comes *back*. **The return is where the road's route-vs-vehicle line bites:**

> **A loaded cart needs the bat-band's maintained line to climb back.** The gradient the cart rides downhill is the field's own `∇(g·Φ)` pointing toward matter — the descent is free because the field does the work. But the *return* — moving the cart (and the empty wain, or a fresh load) back against the gradient, or up from a settled sink back toward the farm — is work the field does **not** do. On the road's **maintained high-`q` filament** (coherence-highway §1.1), the return rides the road's line: the same held filament that made the path "the field's preferred channel for routing coherence" (§1.1) also holds the cart on the line, so the climb back is a *route-riding* act — the road's steering, the line's `q`, and any station's re-raise (field-instruments §2.2) make the return real. **A cart without the road is a cart without a return** — off the maintained line, climbing back against the gradient is a foot-pace labor the wain's wheels do not spare (§3), and the field-highway's line is exactly what makes the round trip worth doing.

**[design]** — the *line's-return mapping*. The source docs land that the road holds a high-`q` line (coherence-highway §1.1, the conduit idiom of energy-harnessing §4.1) and that the `∇(g·Φ)` haul is directional (toward matter, energy-harnessing §1.1/§4.2 — always downhill in `∇(g·Φ)`). What is this doc's [design] is **that a cart's return rides the road's line as its counter-gradient** — that the maintained filament which routes coherence downhill also steers the cart's climb back, and that a station's Still-Room re-raise (field-instruments §2.2) is where the returning cart's own shed `q` is re-raised (§4). This is probe-calibrated with the ride: *whether* a maintained line measurably makes the return cheaper than the off-road climb is the same Phase-1 measurement. The direction honesty is the highway's own (§3.2/§6d): **the road does not steepen the gradient out of nothing; a road placed where there is no gradient has nothing to ride, and a cart placed where there is no line has no return.** The return is the road's maintained line doing what the descent cannot — the route-vs-vehicle line held on the way up.

**The through-line, once:** the cart rides *down* on the field's own `∇(g·Φ)` and rides *back* on the road's held line — the descent is the field's work, the return is the road's work, and **a cart's round trip is the road's whole reason to exist rendered as freight actually moving**. Where the walk's descent is free-and-freedom and its return is the walker's own stride, the cart's descent is the gradient's and its return is the line's — the road and the cart are one system, and neither works without the other (§3).

## 3. The load-and-gradient trade — the load-bearing honesty

The Cart is not a free mover; it is a **deliberate, economical choice**, and its honesty is the load-and-gradient trade, stated once and never blurred:

> **A cart is only worthwhile on a steered route.** Off the road's maintained line it is *worse than a walk*: a loaded cart off-road carries the gradient's **full tax with none of the road's cheapening** — the stride-cost mapping of `the-walk.md` §2 says a walker who steps *with* the lean moves cheaply and against it labors, and a loaded cart off the road is not only laboring against the gradient's own pull but dragging wheels and a burden the walker could carry with more freedom (the-walk §1.2 — the walk trades speed for freedom; a cart trades that freedom away and gets, off-road, nothing in return). The **road's maintained line is what makes the cart's cheap haul real** — the cart's whole value is that the road's high-`q` filament turns the descent's work into carried freight, and off the road that compounding is undone. And **a cart's `(1−q)` wear** (the-tool §2b — the wheel's alloy life: the wheel-carriage is a held structure bleeding `E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput` as glow, exactly as a tool's alloy dulls) **plus its burden-carried weight** (the-burden §1 — the load is a real cost, never free) mean **a cart is a deliberate, economical choice — not a free mover.**

Three honest faces of the trade, each grounded in its source doc:

| The trade's face | What it costs | The source-doc ground | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Only on the road** | off the maintained line, a loaded cart is the gradient's full tax with none of the road's cheapening — the walk's stride-cost applied to a wheel-dragging burden | the-walk §2 — a step with the lean is cheap, against it labors; the-walk §1.2 — the walk trades speed for freedom, which a cart gives away off-road | `the-walk.md` §2/§1.2 |
| **The `(1−q)` wear** | the wheel-carriage is a held structure — it bleeds `E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput` as glow (energy-harnessing §2), read as the wheel's alloy life: a low-`q` chassis dulls/wears faster than a clean one | the-tool §2b — the `(1−q)` waste law as durability ("a high-q low-waste alloy bites clean and lasts"); energy-harnessing §2 — every held structure wastes | `the-tool.md` §2b; `energy-harnessing.md` §2 |
| **The burden-carried weight** | the load is a real cost — the cart's carried weight sheds into the ride the way the walker's carried ε² sheds into the stride, and it must be drawn from the granary's store and booked (spent, never free) | the-burden §1 — the load is a real cost, never free; the-granary §3/§5d — the store's draw is spent, never free | `the-burden.md` §1; `the-granary.md` §3/§5d |

The deliberate economics, stated as the design's spine:

> **A cart is worth it exactly when the road's cheapening exceeds its wear plus its burden.** The road makes the haul cheap (§2a); the wheel-carriage's `(1−q)` wear (§2b of the-tool, applied to the wain) and the burden's real weight (the-burden §1) are the honest bills. A farmer with a harvest to move to the market on a maintained road — the farm-to-settlement line (coherence-highway §5.1) — finds the cart *worth it*: the gradient hauls a load no walker could carry, and the road makes the round trip real (§2b). A player with one light item crossing off-road finds the cart *a worse walk*: wheels, wear, and burden for no line, no gradient compounding, no return. **The cart is the settlement-trader's workhorse precisely because it is only worth hitching where the road is — the deliberate, economical, honest conveyance, never a free mover.**

This is the cart's answer to the highway's open-Q5 (the road as a travel-mint risk): a road could *feel* like free travel forever (ride down, path hoppers up); the cart is the honest bound — **the cart's wear and burden are the same cost the highway's §6d cap names, made into the thing that rides the line** — and the cart never reads as a free mover because its ride is the road's own cheapening, priced honestly against off-road and against the fallow's depletion (§4).

## 4. The honest book — the load's deposit and draw, the no-free-energy cap held

The Cart is not merely a ride; it is a **booked act** — its loads book the way any op does, and the no-free-energy cap is held hard. Two halves, both load-bearing:

### (a) The cart's loads book the way any op does

`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1 settles the op-record `{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude, sustain}` — the shape every field act books on the single-writer Q4 lane at the non-forgeable point. The cart's loads are **no exception and no new kind of record** — a load is a Q4 op of that settled shape, exactly as a trade is (the-market §2.1) and a granary's deposit/draw is (the-granary §4):

> **A cart's load books the way any op does.** Loading the wain is a **draw from the granary's store** (the-granary §4 — the deposit/draw op of the settled record, the store's draw *spent*, never free) booked with the load's magnitude (the harvest's stored coherence, `farm-that-feeds.md` §4 — the yield's magnitude is booked, never made up); the cart rides its route with that booked load; and **unloading at the market is the freight's book carried to the exchange** — the traded item's provenance (the worked vein's rung, the harvest's magnitude, the-tool §2 / farm-that-feeds §4, booked as the-market §2.2's traded provenance) is *carried on the cart and booked at the market's trade*. The load's deposit, the burden's carry, and the exchange's book are all **the same settled op-record** at three points: the granary's draw, the cart's haul, the market's transfer — each `{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude, sustain}`, each at the single non-forgeable point. **A cart is the settlement's goods moving along the road, and the road's freight is the book moving with it.**

**[design]** — the *load-book mapping*. The source docs land that a trade is a Q4 op (the-market §2.1) and a granary's deposit/draw books the same way (the-granary §4, schema-that-settles §2.1). What is this doc's [design] is **that a cart's load is that same bookable op at the wheels** — that the load's deposit and draw, and the provenance carried to the market, are the cart's honest book, no separate "cart" or "haul" record the engine writes beyond the Q4 op it already writes. The cell-ownership rule (schema-that-settles §2.3) bounds the load's locality; the sustain flag (schema-that-settles §2.2) books a held load as one standing record, never a spam of re-emits.

### (b) The no-free-energy cap — a cart converts nothing

The corpus's single hard rule (energy-harnessing §6: **no conversion yields more than it sinks**, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`, coded as amplitude caps on the write-back lane) applies to the cart as its most personal register:

> **A cart converts nothing — there is no transport that yields.** The ride a cart takes is the **gradient's own descent** — the `∇(g·Φ)` haul is the field's own work on a thing moving down it (energy-harnessing §1.1; the highway §1.2: "the haul is the field's own; the road's located, maintained, gradient-facing line is the design"), never a travel-mint. The cart **cannot be farmed into a travel-mint**: ride down, path back up, repeat, is not free travel, because the return (§2b) rides the road's *maintained line* at its real cost (maintenance, the station's trickle, the `(1−q)` waste — the highway §3.2's three road caps) and the cart's own wear + burden are real, never free (§3). The descent's cheapening is the field's own gradient doing what it always does — free *movement*, never free *energy* (the-walk §4d: a walk converts nothing; the cart converts nothing, harder). **There is no cart that yields: the wear is real, the load is real, the gradient's cheapening is the field's own, and a cart that appeared to make hauling *worth more* — to turn a ride into a stored yield, a load into a net gain — is a mint the cap forbids** (energy-harnessing §6; the-market §6d — an exchange is a transfer, never a gain).

Three concrete guards, all inherited:

1. **No harvesting the ride.** The cart's descent is free *movement through the field's own gradient* — never a stored yield, never a capacitor charged by rolling downhill. The highway's road cap (a road cannot store a harvest as wealth, §3.2 #2) holds the cart: an empty cart riding down to "collect" the descent is not a harvest; it is the walk's free-movement honesty on wheels.
2. **No shedding into a harvestable drain.** The cart's `(1−q)` wear is the wheel-carriage's glow, spent — not a waste a collector can tap (the scan-vs-Coda refusal, the-burden §4 / signature-predator §7e, held unchanged: the wain's wear is a held structure's honest cost, never a shed a machine reaps).
3. **The cart is never a travel-mint.** Cheap haul is a *relation to the road*, not a free-transport exploit — the cart neither speeds up forever nor pays nothing forever, because the wear (§3) and the return's line-cost (§2b) bound the very cheapness the gradient grants. The highway's own §6d/§3.2 caps hold the cart as the same honesty at the wheels.

## 6. Feasibility verdict

**PHASE-1-ABLE — the road's vehicle, the thing that makes the highway's freight real.**

The corpus designs the route (the highway), the primitive one uses on foot (the walk), the store (the granary), and the exchange (the market) — but **nothing carries goods *along* the road**. The Cart is that missing ground-goods-vehicle: a wain whose wheel-carriage is a held low-`q` chassis that rides the highway's gradient — carrying the granary's goods to the market, the camp's belongings on the move, a harvest up from the farm. It is the load-bearing third of the transport triad (the walk gets there yourself, the ship crosses the dark between, **the cart moves the settlement's goods along the road it already holds**), and it is Phase-1-able because its whole design is a **bounded consumer + a bounded Q4 write** — the honest core slice, the ride-downhill slice's sibling, is testable directly on the publish.

It de-risks the highway's ride-downhill slice and the walk's crossing by measuring the same engine-real `∇(g·Φ)` haul with a real burden on it, at the wheel.

**The gates, all held (§5):** (a) the [design]/engine-real line — the highway's route, the stride-cost, the granary's store, the market's book, the tool's wear, and the burden's load are their source docs' landed pieces; **the cart's composition — the ride's form, the load-and-gradient trade, the honest book — is this doc's [design]** over them; (b) **PHASE-1-ABLE** — a held low-`q` chassis rendered as a gradient-riding conveyance over the published channels is a consumer + Q4 write, the ride-downhill slice's sibling; (c) determinism — same route, same field state → same ride, a hard gate, never a seeded roll; (d) the no-free-energy cap — a cart converts nothing, no transport that yields, the gradient's descent is the field's own, the wear and load are real; (e) accessibility — the load and the ride's cost readable from the instruments, never hidden-only.

**Binding risks, in order:** **(a)** the **ride/return calibration** — does a wheeled load *measurably* ride a real `∇(g·Φ)` descent on the Phase-1 demo, and does a maintained line measurably make the return cheaper than the off-road climb? **None contradicts the async, dual-world, or regime-collapse architecture** — the Cart adds no physics and no channel: it is a designed conveyance and a bounded read/write over the field's own gradient, the road's own line, and the corpus's own settled book — a consumer of the publish and a Q4 write through the existing lane, the vehicle the highway's freight was always missing.

> **The honest statement that makes this doc load-bearing: the corpus designs the road and never designs the thing that uses it. The road is a steered coherence route carrying a cart along `∇(g·Φ)`; the walk is the un-roaded primitive; but nothing carries the granary's goods to the market, the camp's belongings on the move, a harvest up from the farm. ## Open questions

1. **The ride/return calibration (§5).** Does a wheeled, loaded cart *measurably* ride a real `∇(g·Φ)` descent on the Phase-1 demo — and does a maintained line measurably make the return cheaper than the off-road climb? The Phase-1 slice (a loaded chassis down a real descent, back on the line) is the pre-registered probe that answers it; a negative answer (the gradient too weak at cart scale to haul a load, or the return's line-cost no better than walking back) would push the Cart to pure presentation — the haul honest at the wheel's feel, but not a mechanical free-ride the design's honesty would have to weaken to claim. **[probe]** — the sibling of `coherence-highway.md` open-Q1.
2. **The wheel-vs-stride margin (§3/§5).** How much does a cart actually beat a laden walk on a maintained road — enough that the wheel's `(1−q)` wear and the burden's real weight are worth it, or does the walk carry nearly as cheaply? The design only coheres if the road's cheapening *exceeds* the cart's wear + burden; if the margin is small, the cart reads as a decoration, and if the wheels win everywhere, the "only worthwhile on a steered route" honesty collapses. Needs the Phase-1 ride measured against the walk's stride-cost (the-walk §2a) on the same descent. **[design]/[probe]**
3. **The off-road-worse-than-walk balance (§3/§5).** The load-and-gradient trade requires a loaded cart off the maintained line to be *genuinely* worse than a walker carrying — the walk's stride-tax with worse wheels. Is that honest gap (the cart's wheel-drag + burden vs. the walker's freedom) calibrated so the cart is a real deliberate choice, not a free mover? The Phase-1 off-road-vs-road comparison measures it. **[design]/[probe]**
4. **The return's line-cost ownership (§2b/§5).** The cart climbs back "on the bat-band's maintained line" — but is that line's return-cost booked to the road's upkeep (a station's re-raise, the `(1−q)` waste, the highway §3.2's caps) or to the cart's own draw? The honest register is that the road's maintained line is the cart's return, priced in the road's upkeep — but who *pays* the station's re-raise and how the returning cart's shed `q` is re-raised is a [design] dial over the road's economics and the Still Room's Idle-line re-raise (field-instruments §2.2). Inherits `coherence-highway.md` open-Q5's travel-mint framing from the return side. **[design]**, once the ride/return probe lands.
5. **The load's book across the trip (§4a/§5).** The cart's load books as the granary's draw and the market's trade (schema-that-settles §2.1) — but does a load *en route* book as one standing record (a `sustain` flag over the haul's `worldPos`, the settled record's §2.2), or as the deposit and the trade with nothing between? The honest default is the settled record's `sustain` semantics (a held load books as one standing record, never a spam of re-emits) — but the mid-route load's bookability is a [design] dial over the op-stream's shape, exactly as the market's exchange-op scope is (the-market §7 open-Q1). **[design]**
6. **The camp's absent doc.** [`the-camp.md`](./the-camp.md) does **not exist on disk** (checked against the design-doc index) — the wandering wain, the camp's belongings on the move, is named in this doc's framing (the granary's goods to the market, **the camp's belongings on the move**, a harvest up from the farm) but has no source doc to ground it. The Cart's *camp-freight face* — a caravan moving a camp's store along a road — is therefore **[assumption]**, flagged honestly: it rides the same ride, load, and book as the market and farm freight, but until a camp doc lands its specific carry (the camp's store drawn to the move) is open. **Noted honestly, not filled in.**
