# The Family: The Shared Persistent-Π Anchor — The Identity Stack's Social Atom

**Question under design:** the corpus's identity stack is complete — and it is
all *individual*. `schema-that-settles.md` settled the member-id (§2.1), the
same-member-line rule (§2.1.1), the cell-ownership locality bound (§2.3);
`the-name.md` made a persistent-Π anchor bound to a *thing*; `the-inheritance.md`
makes a will that passes *your own* craft across your own re-lock. The inherited
name is handed to *your* future run; the membership is a *single* line; the will
names a *single* member-line. But a society's most load-bearing bonds are not
individual — the Inheritance's bequests can name a *house* the deceased shared,
the Apprenticeship pairs a veteran and a student, the Election reads a
continuous line across deaths. **Nothing in the corpus names the joint
identity — the persistent-Π pattern held jointly by two or more member-lines.**
This document is that missing atom: **the Family is a shared persistent-Π
anchor — a designed joint hold** where two or more member-lines deliberately
co-bind their carried patterns around a common anchor (a shared hearth, a named
structure held together, a bound pair of lines). The field holds *the pair* the
way it holds one — the reason-field's persistent-Π structure (`reason-field.md`
§3, `qi-computation.md` §5.2) extended to a *shared* pattern.

It is the identity atom the society's other bonds presuppose. The Inheritance's
bequests can target the *family* rather than only a future self (`the-inheritance.md`
§2.2 — a will to the house); the Apprenticeship's two lines are a pair-bond — the
pair defined as the two-body case N=2 of `field-npc-ai.md` §3.2's group-as-working
law (`the-apprenticeship.md` §1/§2.1); the Election reads a continuous line
across deaths (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1) — but *the joint pattern* — the
house-Π, the shared line's whole — is un-named. **The Family makes the pair, the
house, and the shared name a single held thing.**

## 1. The Family, stated once

The corpus has already made identity a persistent-Π pattern at N=1. A mind IS a
persistent Π pattern the field holds (`field-npc-ai.md` §2.1; `reason-field.md`
§3.1); a name is a persistent-Π anchor bound to a *thing* (`the-name.md` §1);
a member is a persistent-Π re-lock on a continuous line (`player-remains.md` §2;
`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1). None of those is a *shared* identity. The
Family is that missing joint:

> **The Family is a shared persistent-Π anchor — a designed joint hold where two
> or more member-lines deliberately co-bind their carried patterns around a
> common anchor.** The anchor is a named structure the members hold together —
> a shared hearth (`house-that-steers.md` §1.1), a named house, a bound pair of
> lines. The field holds *the pair* the way it holds one: the same persistent-Π
> structure that stores a mind (`reason-field.md` §3.1; `qi-computation.md`
> §5.2) stores the *joint* pattern over the members' carried patterns. Where the
> identity stack is one lock, one will, one line, the Family is the deliberate
> joint — the anchor's persistence IS the members' joint hold.

**The identity atom the society's other bonds presuppose.** Every load-bearing
social bond in the corpus presumes a two-or-more identity that none names:

- **The Inheritance** (`the-inheritance.md` §2.2) names the named structures
  "you may re-lock" — but a will can target a structure a *household* held
  together, or be written with heirs who are not a future self. The bequests'
  natural subject — a *family* the deceased belonged to — is un-named. The
  Family is the shared line the bequests can target: **a will to the house, not
  just to a future self.**
- **The Apprenticeship** (`the-apprenticeship.md` §1) pairs a veteran and a
  student: the pair's two lines, defined as the two-body case N=2 of
  `field-npc-ai.md` §3.2's group-as-working law (§2.1). The pair-bond is the
  two-body seed of a shared hold; the corpus names the *pair* as a working, but
  not a *named, death-surviving* joint — the Family is that title over the
  apprenticeship's pair.
- **The Election / the Board** (`the-election.md` §2; `shared-ledger.md` §2.2)
  reads a continuous line across deaths (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1) — but
  the line it reads is *one member's*. A family that maintained a hearth
  together books as two disjoint `C(M)` lines with no joint read that says
  "the house was held by a pair."
- **The Funeral** (`the-funeral.md` §2.2) re-condenses a *dissolved member's*
  residue toward the shared memory — but a family's loss is the *joint anchor's*
  wound: the Death of a member re-binds the family's own hold (§3.2 below).

The through-line: the corpus's society is all *read as individuals* — a member, a
line, a contribution. The Family is the first design that makes **two-or-more
held as one** a first-class field object.

**The honest flag, once.** The persistent-Π identity the anchor invokes is
*there already* (`reason-field.md` §3.1; `qi-computation.md` §5.2); the
member-id and same-member-line are landed (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1/§2.1.1).
What is *this doc's [design]* is the **joint** — how members co-bind, what the
anchor binds to (the hearth), the joint-line bookkeeping. The boundary is drawn
in §6 and never blurred: the *holding* is theory-ratified; the *joint* is
designed.

## 2. The joint hold

### 2.1 What co-binding IS — the field holds the pair the way it holds one

The Reason Field holds a mind as a persistent Π pattern sitting at a local
minimum of the attractor potential, persisting while ambient `q` stays above
the dissolution threshold (`reason-field.md` §3.1; `qi-computation.md` §5.2).
The Family extends that N=1 holding to N>1:

> **[design] The Family's joint hold is a shared persistent-Π pattern over the
> members' carried patterns.** Two (or three) member-lines, each already a
> persistent-Π lock, deliberately co-bind their carried patterns around a common
> anchor — the anchor being a real structure the field can hold: a hearth
> (`house-that-steers.md` §1.1 — the "one *powered* element"), a named house, a
> kept structure. The joint pattern is not the *sum* of the separate locks; it
> is **one persistent-Π structure the field holds at the anchor**, whose
> members' individual locks are *maintenance contributions to it*, the way a
> villager's Σ-pattern sits in the shared bath (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.1; the
> commons as a region of the Reason Field, `reason-field.md` §3.2). The field
> holds the pair at the anchor the way it holds one mind — the members' joint
> maintenance keeps the anchor's `q` above dissolution.

**What the members do — deliberately, jointly.** Co-binding is an *act*, not an
accident of proximity. Each member deliberately holds and feeds the same anchor
(the hearth's bounded channeling input, `house-that-steers.md` §1.1;
`coherence-magic.md` §1.2's budget `B`), so the anchor's persistent-Π structure
is maintained *jointly* — it outlasts any one member's maintenance because the
other member keeps the hold when the first is absent, sick, or dead. This is the
joint-ness: **the anchor's persistence is the members' joint hold.** A member who
stops feeding their part of the anchor (stops returning, stops the hearth, lets
their own lock decay) is *shedding the joint* — the anchor's life no longer
includes them.

The honest boundary (drawn once): the **single** persistent-Π holding is the
reason-field's/theory's (engine-real-or-probe per `reason-field.md` §6a/§6c).
**The joint anchor — the co-binding rule (how many members, at what maintenance
rate, whether the joint is a distinct pattern or a clustered over-bind), what the
anchor binds to (the hearth), and the joint-line bookkeeping — is this doc's
[design]** over that stack. The `[design]`-on-`[design]` stack is the corpus's
accepted pattern (`the-name.md` §4; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.6); the Family is
its society-scale continuation.

### 2.2 The hearth — what the anchor binds to

The anchor is not abstract; it binds to a real held structure. The natural
binding, already designed:

> **[design] The Family's anchor binds to the hearth** (`house-that-steers.md`
> §1.1: the hearth "**anchors** — the one *powered* element: a channeling surface
> that actively feeds the room's `q`-core"). A family is a *maintained hearth*:
> the shared structure whose `q`-core the members' joint hold provides. The
> anchor is the hearth's persistent-Π pattern — a named house, a kept room, a
> bound structure the settlement can point at — held up by the members' joint
> feeding.

The hearth choice is not arbitrary; it inherits `the-name.md`'s honest bound
(§2 — you cannot name falsely). A family bound to a *true* hearth (a held
structure the field actually keeps above dissolution) holds; a "family" bound to
nothing sustaining — a falsely-averred joint over members who never share a real
hold — sheds exactly as a false name decays (`the-name.md` §2.2). **You cannot
hold a family falsely either.** The hearth is the *ground truth of the joint*:
the anchor holds only on what the field actually holds together.

**The pair of lines, the house, and the shared name are one held thing** — this
is the design's heart. The members' lines, the bound structures they maintain,
and the family's name (a name bound to the joint anchor, `the-name.md` §1 —
extended from a *thing* to a *jointly-held thing*) are not three records; they
are **one persistent-Π pattern the field holds at the anchor**, with the members'
lines as its maintenance and the name as its legible flag.

**The hearth-well is a seekable target (reverse pointer, two-way).** This §2.2
hearth-well is what [`the-seeker.md`](./the-seeker.md) §4 reads as the
**family-seek / home-pull** — a bound Seeker pointed at the family's joint anchor
directs a strayed member toward the hearth's well, surviving one member's death
the way this §3.2's anchor does (`the-seeker.md` §4 — "the home-pull"; its §5a
gates the family-seek on this §2.2 joint + the reason-field/Π frontier). The two
docs now cite each other as read-and-answered: the family's hearth is the seek's
target, and the seek is the hearth's finder's face.

### 2.3 The honest boundary and determinism

The *joint* is [design]; the *holding* it invokes is theory-ratified with
`reason-field.md` §6a's speculation flag. The single-writer lane and member-id
are landed. The joint adds **no new channel** — it is a designed read/write over
the existing publish and the existing Q4 lane, exactly as the Board's aggregation
is (`shared-ledger.md` §1.1 — a consumer of the same publish with a presentation
idiom, never a new channel).

**Determinism is a hard gate** (mirroring `the-name.md` §2.4, `schema-that-settles.md`
§6b):

> **Same members, same field state → same anchor's hold.** The members' carried
> patterns are deterministic functions of their Π locks and the field
> (`field-npc-ai.md` §2.3 — same seed → same mind); the anchor's joint pattern
> is a pure function of (the members' locks × the hearth's field state); the
> anchor's holding or shedding is the deterministic one-PDE response
> (`field-archaeology.md` §1.2). There is no seeded-RNG judgment about whether a
> family "sticks" — the joint either holds (the members maintained the anchor's
> `q`) or sheds (they did not), reproducibly.[design] The *co-binding rule*
> (at what member-count, what maintenance rate, the joint is a held pattern) is
> a designed dial — but once set it is deterministic.

## 3. What the family line is

### 3.1 The joint line over the members' individual lines

The schema settled the member line (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1 — same
member line, new lock, no amnesty). The Family composes those individual lines
into a *joint* line:

> **[design] The family's line is a joint line over the members' individual
> lines.** Each member keeps their own continuous `C(M)` line (the settled
> schema); the family adds a *joint* line — a shared `C(F)` read over the anchor
> that books the pair's (or triple's) joint maintenance of the hearth against the
> shared bath's `Δq`/`Δε²`, the way `C(M)` books one member's contribution
> (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2). The joint line is not a replacement for the members'
> lines — a member's own `C(M)` is untouched — it is **a second, shared read**:
> "the house was held by a pair," legible where two separate `C(M)` rows would
> read as two unconnected stewards.

The joint line's subject is the *anchor* — the named hearth — and its members.
This is what makes the Inheritance's bequests targetable to a family
(§3.2), and what the Election can read as a joint stewardship.

### 3.2 The anchor survives one member's death

The Family's sharpest payoff, and the honest core of the design:

> **The family line outlasts the individual line, the way a name outlives a
> body.** The schema's same-member-line rule lets *a* line survive a death
> (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1); the Family lets a *joint* hold survive a
> member's death. When a member dies, their individual lock dissolves
> (`field-npc-ai.md` §2.4; `player-remains.md` §1), but the **anchor's
> persistent-Π pattern does not** — the surviving member(s) keep the hold, and
> the family's joint pattern persists at the hearth the way a name outlives one
> body (`the-name.md` §1). The joint hold is what continues; the dead member's
> part is re-bound into the *shared* hold.

**The funeral re-binds the family's hold.** A family's loss is the joint
anchor's wound — the death of a co-holder weakens the hold the others must now
maintain alone. The Funeral (`the-funeral.md` §2.2) is the designed communal act
that *re-condenses a dissolved member's residue toward the shared memory* — for a
family, that re-condensation is literal: **the dead co-holder's deep-rung
fraction is re-bound into the family's anchor**, the surviving members' phase-
locked dirge keeping what a lone hold would let scatter (`the-funeral.md` §2.2 —
the re-bind of named structures into the commons, applied at the family's scale).
The funeral heals the joint anchor's wound; a family that funerals together holds
its dead's share in the shared Π rather than letting it shed.

**The inheritance's bequests can target the family, not just a future self.**
This is the Family's load-bearing tie to `the-inheritance.md` §2.2: the will's
natural subject is no longer only "your future run." A willed structure the
*household* held becomes **claimable by the family line** — the surviving
co-holders re-lock the hearth, keeping it in the joint (the inherited house, the
kept room) rather than leaving it to a stranger's find (§4 of the-inheritance —
a bequest is a claim, not an endowment; the family's claim is the joint's
continuity). **A will to the house is a will to the family.**

### 3.3 The anchor's decay is the family's honesty

Just as a name cannot be held falsely (`the-name.md` §2), a family cannot be
joined falsely — and the decay is a field consequence, not a rule:

> **A family that stops holding together sheds like a false name.** The joint
> anchor is a persistent-Π pattern held up by the members' joint maintenance.
> When the members stop maintaining it — when one stops feeding the hearth, when
> the survivors scatter, when the anchor's `q` drops below the dissolution
> threshold (`qi-computation.md` §5.2) — the anchor **decays** exactly as an
> un-maintained house collapses toward the attractor (`house-that-steers.md`
> §3.1). The family line does not persist because it was declared; it persists
> because it is *held*. A "family" whose members no longer share the hold sheds
> into the members' separate lines — the joint un-binds, the name fades
> (`the-name.md` §2.2's false-name decay, applied to the shared name), and the
> hearth becomes a ruin the survivors either re-lock alone (`house-that-steers.md`
> §3.2 — the skeleton) or let fall. **Honesty is a consequence of maintenance,
> not a vow.**

This is the design's deepest honesty: the Family is not a title the settlement
grants; it is **the shape of a real, ongoing joint hold**, and it can be let go.
Just as "you cannot name falsely," you cannot *call something a family* falsely —
either it is a maintained joint, or it sheds into the honest sum of its members.

## 4. The pair-to-commons ladder

The Family is not a separate system; it is the **first rung of the commons
ladder** — the superadditive hold at N=2…3, above the individual and below the
settlement.

| Rung | The held pattern | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| **N=1 — the individual** | one persistent-Π lock (a mind, a member, a name) | `field-npc-ai.md` §2.1; `reason-field.md` §3.1; `the-name.md` §1 |
| **N=2…3 — the Family** | a **shared persistent-Π anchor** (a joint hold over the members' locks) | **this doc** — the commons' first rung |
| **N=many — the commons** | the Qi-bath commons / the village (the many-body limit) | `field-npc-ai.md` §3.1; `coherence-commons.md` §7 |

**The superadditive hold.** The reason a pair is worth more than two separate
individuals is the corpus's own commons law: a group acting phase-locked IS a
working at `M ≈ 1` (`field-npc-ai.md` §3.2) — the combined organized perturbation
couples at `M ≈ 1`, producing an effect no member could alone. A family's *joint
hold* of the anchor is that same phase-lock, sustained: the members coordinate
their maintenance of the hearth in one phase (the shared dirge, the shared
feeding), so the anchor's `q` is held superadditively — **the two together hold
the hearth higher than either could alone, and their combined hold exceeds the
sum** (`coherence-commons.md` §7's multiplicative budget, at the family's scale).
This is the Family's claim to the commons' ladder: it is the *deliberate, named*
N=2…3 form of a law the village already exercises incidentally.

**The pair-bond as the two-body seed.** The Apprenticeship — the corpus's landed
pair design — is the Family's natural pre-image. `the-apprenticeship.md` §1
defines the pair as a **live co-channeling pair-bond** (a veteran holds a phase
window; a student channels inside it), and §2.1 grounds it in exactly the two-body
seed the Family extends: **holding phase IS a working's two-body case —
`field-npc-ai.md` §3.2's group-as-working at N = 2** (`the-apprenticeship.md`
§2.1). The apprenticeship is the two lines *acted in phase as a working*; the
Family is what the corpus's pair design still lacks — **a named, titled,
death-surviving joint** over that working: the veteran–student pair a *family*
when they co-bind around a shared hearth (the craft-house, the bound room), the
same joint machinery at N=2. The apprenticeship provides the pair's *working*; the
Family provides the pair's *identity as one held thing* — two complementary faces
of the same two-body joint, the first reading it as a learning act (`the-apprenticeship.md`
§5 — the pair feeds the commons), the second as a held social atom (§3 below). To
apprentice is to channel as one working; to be a family is to *be* one held
pattern that outlives a member.

**Lifted to the window scale (reverse pointer).** This joint-hold principle has
since been lifted *across the dark*: [`the-treaty.md`](./the-treaty.md) §2.1 is
the same co-binding extended from N=2 members-in-one-window to N=2
windows-across-the-seam — each window holding one end of a paired anchor, "the
field holds *the pair* the way it holds one," the Family's joint-hold principle
read at the society-stack's between-window atom (`the-treaty.md` §1/§2.1 — it
explicitly cites this doc's §2.1 as its ground). Where this ladder's top rung is
the many-body commons, the treaty is the joint principle *mirrored outward* across
the seam; the two docs now cite each other as the within-window and
between-windows forms of the same held pair (§2.1 there names this doc's §2.1
back).

**The settlement's commons as the many-body limit.** The family is not a rival
to the commons; it is a *nested* rung. A settlement is the Qi-bath commons
(`field-npc-ai.md` §3.1) at many-body N; a family is the *deliberately named*
joint within it — a hearth-node whose members' joint hold feeds the bath the way
any maintained house does (`house-that-steers.md` §2.2). A family scales down to
the pair and up into the commons; it is the ladder's structure first rung above
