# The Pilgrim: The Named-Place Walk as a Field Act

**Question under design:** the corpus designs every *reason* to move — the seeker
finds a named thing (`the-seeker.md`), the highway carries you across the ground
you live on (`coherence-highway.md`), the world-seam's voyage goes *between*
windows (`world-seams.md`), the ship sails (`ksp-kernel.md`). But every one of
those journeys is **instrumental**: it moves you toward a find, a harvest, a
crossing, a founding — the movement is a means. The corpus has the seeker
(finding), the voyage (transport), the walk (movement) — **but not the journey
as a field act for its own sake**: the player who walks to a named place not to
mine or harvest or find, but because the place *is*. This document designs that
missing act.

> **The Pilgrim is the named-place walk as a field act: leaving the road to
> walk, un-instrumental, to a place the field holds — a named ruin you do not
> excavate but *attend*, a seed's origin you return to, a window's anchor you
> approach as its own end. The walk is itself a working** — a costed, purposeful
> crossing undertaken *on purpose*, the pilgrim's own Burden carried deliberately
> and cleared at the destination's quiet, and the walk **leaves the field
> changed**: a pilgrim's visited named place is re-bound — the anchor refreshed
> by the keeping. It is the corpus's honest answer to *why walk anywhere you
> could sail.*

The honest once-flag, matching the corpus's discipline (`corpus-reconciliation.md`,
the canonical set — cited, not re-derived): the **crossing's costs** (the un-roaded
gradient, the thin's drain, the carried Burden, the Still-Room clearing) and the
**anchor's holding** (a true name holds, a false name decays) are their source
docs' *engine-real / landed* mechanics; the **pilgrim's composition** — which named
places are attended, the *un-instrumental walk's framing*, the clearing at the end
— is this doc's **[design]**, drawn in §5 and never blurred.

This document reads between two sibling absences honestly: the corpus names the
seeker (finding) and the highway (transport) but had **no dedicated un-roaded-
crossing doc** — the walk that is itself a working was, as the corpus stood,
*undesigned*. That absence is precisely the hole The Pilgrim fills. The crossing's
*constituent costs* are not invented here; they are the existing, landed pieces —
the `∇(g·Φ)` haul and the site-map read (`coherence-highway.md` §1/§2), the
coherence loan (`patient-field.md` §3), the thin's drain (`tide-of-the-attractor.md`
§2), the deliberately-carried load (`the-burden.md`) — composed by this doc into
**the walk that is itself a working.** Where this doc was written, no `the-walk.md`
existed in the corpus; it has since **landed (wave 27)** —
[`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) is now the corpus's **un-roaded-crossing doc** — the
*craft* of where to step, when to wait, how much to carry, and what a crossing
leaves, at foot-pace (`the-walk.md` §1/§3). **The Pilgrim is the walk's craft
composed at the pilgrimage's purpose**: the un-roaded crossing the walk designs is
here turned into the deliberate, un-instrumental walk to a named place — the same
craft, pointed at attending rather than transporting (two-way with `the-walk.md` §3
open-Q5, §3.1 this doc).

## 1. The Pilgrim, stated once

The corpus's instructions for moving are all instrumental by design. The seeker
points at one named thing so you can *go find it* (`the-seeker.md` §2 — the finder's
face of a bound name). The highway carries you so the farm's harvest reaches the
settlement and the voyage reaches the anchor (`coherence-highway.md` §5.1 — the road
is where sustained coherence *moves*). The world-seam's voyage crosses the dark to
*found or find* another window (`world-seams.md` §2.3/§5). In every one, the
movement is a means to a harvest, a find, a crossing, a founding — the walking is
the cost of the gain.

What no design states is the walk that is *itself* the act — the deliberate,
un-instrumental crossing to a place the field holds, undertaken not to take
anything from it but because **the place is**. The corpus's answer to why the
field-world is worth crossing when nothing is to be gained but the crossing is not
yet designed. The Pilgrim is that answer, stated once and kept:

> **The Pilgrim is the named-place walk as a field act. It is the player who
> leaves the road — the kept high-`q` line (`coherence-highway.md` §1) — to walk,
> un-instrumental, to a place the field holds: a named ruin attended rather than
> excavated, a seed's origin returned to, a window's anchor approached as its own
> end. The walking is the working. It is not free — it is a costed, purposeful
> crossing taken *on purpose*, the pilgrim's own Burden carried deliberately and
> cleared at the destination's quiet — and it **leaves the field changed**: the
> visited named place is re-bound, the anchor refreshed by the keeping.**

The distinction from the corpus's other walkers, stated sharply:

| The corpus's walkers | The act | The instrumentality | The Pilgrim |
|---|---|---|---|
| The **seeker** (`the-seeker.md` §1) | finds a bound named thing | the pointing *serves* the finding — you seek to *get* to the anchor | the pilgrim *already knows the place*; the walk is not to find it but to *attend* it |
| The **highway traveller** (`coherence-highway.md` §1) | rides the kept line to move a harvest / reach the anchor | the road *routes* the field's haul — the movement is transport | the pilgrim **leaves the road**; the un-roaded walk is itself the act, not the route |
| The **voyager** (`world-seams.md` §2) | crosses the dark between windows | the long dark is a *horizon to reach across* — the cost of founding/finding | the pilgrim walks *within* the field, to a place already there, as its own end |

The through-line: **every other movement is a means; the pilgrim's is the end.**
The seeker's bound name is the pilgrim's destination — but where the seeker reads
the anchor to *locate* it (§2 of the-seeker, the pointing), the pilgrim reads the
anchor's direction to *choose* it as the point of a deliberate walking. The walk
turns the finder's instrumentality into the act itself: you do not walk *because*
the seek points; the seek points *so that* you may walk, and the walking is the
working.

The honest once-flag: **the "un-instrumental walk" framing — that the movement is
itself a working — is this doc's [design]**; the *costs and holdings it composes*
are the landed source-doc mechanics (§5a). No claim here pretends the engine turns
on a "pilgrimage mode"; the composition is designed surface over the field the
corpus already computes.

## 2. The named places of pilgrimage

A pilgrimage needs a destination, and the corpus already holds the three kinds of
place a field-world makes that are *worth attending as their own end*. Each is a
**keeping act, never a harvest** — the pilgrim does not take from the place; the
pilgrim keeps it, and the keeping (this doc §4) is the whole of what the walk
does.

### 2.1 The named ruin you attend — keep by visiting, not excavating

A ruin is the corpus's built-form residue: an unmaintained high-`q` structure
*"collapses toward the attractor"* and leaves, in three classes, the fallen floor
(a scar), the ghost-halo (residue — findable archaeology), and the **skeleton** (the
still-holding low-rung structure, re-authorable) (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2).
A **name** binds a persistent-Π anchor to that structure — and crucially, a true
name on the ruin **keeps its record**: *"a named skeleton is a re-authorable Π the
field holds longer … the ruin keeps its record because it has a name, and a
nameless ruin's core has nothing to anchor the record to"* (`the-name.md` §3.1).

The pilgrimage's first destination is that named ruin, *attended*:

> **A named ruin you attend is a structure you keep by visiting, not excavating.**
> The corpus already designs the conservation default: *"reading a ruin in place
> is free, safe, keeps the regional bath healthy"* (`house-that-steers.md` §3.3),
> because excavation-vs-conservation are rivals and *"leave-in-place is a
> legitimate archaeology"* (`field-archaeology.md` §5.3 — reading the ghost-halo
> costs nothing, no perturbation, no bath-drain). The pilgrim takes that designed
> default and turns it into the *point of the journey*: not to mine the skeleton's
> stored coherence, not to re-lock it into a new build (both are the harvest path),
> but to **attend the named ruin in place** — to visit the structure the field
> still holds and *keep it by the visit*.

The honest grounding: a named ruin already holds because the name sits in a real
coherent well (`the-name.md` §2.1/§6e) and the skeleton is a persistent-Π the
field keeps holding (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2). The pilgrim does not *add* to
that holding mechanically; the visit **refreshes the attention** that the name's
holding already is — the keeping is what the name already does, touched again
(this doc §4). The named structure is attended as its own end; the walk that
reaches it is the act, and the keeping is its only output. **Never a harvest**:
attending a ruin does not yield the ruin's stored coherence, does not convert it,
does not raise it — the field-archaeology conservation default (read-in-place, §5.3
#3) is the entire mechanic, walked to.

### 2.2 The seed's origin — the place the order first took, returned to

The corpus's deepest object is the **resonance seed** — *"the deepest birth-strata
artifact: the first matter a window condensed"*, the single deepest persistent-Π
the window's whole field state holds above its own dissolution threshold
(`resonance-seeds.md` §1.1). It is the *origin of authorable matter* — the place
the window's order first took — and finding it is the archaeology crown: the
deepest core sample, the rarest find, the found-economy's crown above the fossil
crown (§4.1).

The pilgrimage's second destination is that origin, *returned to*:

> **The seed's origin is a place you return to, not a store you plunder.** The
> seed's own arc — find → plant → choose (keep/plant/share) (`resonance-seeds.md`
> §2.4/§4.3) — is a *harvest-shaped* arc: every option takes the seed's order
> somewhere (the ultimate fuel, a growth, the ultimate sample). The pilgrim's act
> is the fourth, un-harvesting option the corpus's own conservation-refusal
> implies: **return to the seed's origin — the birth-strata site where the order
> first took — and keep it by attending, not by removing it.** Where the seed's
> §4.3 keeps-it-un-planted as *"the deepest store,"* the pilgrim keeps it *in
> place* as the window's origin — the birthplace attended, not the treasure
> withdrawn. The archaeology doc's own rival (`field-archaeology.md` §5.2:
> *"read in place (free, keeps the window's origin anchored) vs remove (gain,
> lose the anchor)"*) is the pilgrim's choice made explicit: **the reader's path is
> the pilgrimage** — the deepest store stays anchored, and the walk to it is the
> returning.

The honest tier: the seed is the most speculative object in the corpus — **deep-later**
(`resonance-seeds.md` §6 — nothing Phase-1-exceptable), a **[design]-on-[design]**
object over the residue model. The pilgrim's *seed-origin* destination therefore
rides every gate the seed does; what is statable now is the *principle* — that a
window's origin is a keep-by-attending place, the sibling of the attended ruin —
while the seed's actual deep-later findability waits on archaeology's residue +
core sample and custom-blocks' lab (this doc §5b).

### 2.3 The window's anchor — a place approached as its own end

The corpus's horizon is the window: the anchored 192³ box where the law runs, and
the dark between windows is the mixture's empty measure (`world-seams.md` §1.1/§2).
A distant window reads as a **star** — its pole discharges and envelope-top band
are the "stars" of this universe (§2.2) — and the voyage to it is a
**coherence-budget problem**, a finite, expensive crossing (§2.3 — the constraint
economy makes a voyage a decision, not a treadmill).

The pilgrimage's third destination is that anchor, *approached as its own end*:

> **A window's anchor is a place you approach as its own end — the beacon you
> walk toward because it *is*, not because it yields.** The seam doc's window-star
> is the corpus's most literal *named place held by the field*: a distant anchored
> window is a place the law keeps organized, legible as a beacon the reader can
> resolve (`world-seams.md` §2.2 — *"there is no map; there is only the reading"*).
> The voyage is designed as a means to found-or-find there (§5 — the founding act,
> the trophies, the settler choice). The pilgrim's reading of the same anchor is
> the *un-instrumental* one: **walk to the anchor as its own end** — the beacon is
> the destination's quiet made visible, a place approached not to found or trade
> but because the field holds it and the walk to it is the working.

The honest framing: the window's anchor is the *largest* named place the corpus
designs, and the un-instrumental approach to it is the same keeping-act as the
ruin's — the frontier re-binding (§4) applied at the window's scale. But the
walk-instead-of-sail meaning does not require the seamless voyage's full
Phase-2 machinery: within a window, the *geography of organized places* (the
site-map ridges of `coherence-highway.md` §2.2) already holds local anchors a
pilgrim can walk to as their own ends.

### 2.4 The through-line — every pilgrimage destination is a keeping, never a harvest

| The named place | The field holds | The pilgrim's act | The harvest it refuses |
|---|---|---|---|
| the **named ruin you attend** (§2.1) | the skeleton's persistent-Π + the name's anchor (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2; `the-name.md` §3.1) | attend in place — visit, keep, read the ghost-halo free (`field-archaeology.md` §5.3 #3) | the excavate-and-rebuild harvest (de-orders the region, `field-archaeology.md` §5.2) |
| the **seed's origin** (§2.2) | the deepest birth-strata Π, the window's origin (`resonance-seeds.md` §1.1) | return, keep in place — the origin anchored | the keep-plant-share harvest (withdrawing the genesis, §4.3) |
| the **window's anchor** (§2.3) | a distant anchored window, a beacon (`world-seams.md` §2.2) | approach as its own end — the walk is the working | the found-or-trade voyage's trophy harvest (§5.1) |

**Each is a *keeping* act, never a harvest.** This is the load-bearing distinction
of the whole design: the corpus's instrumental journeys all *take* (a find, a
harvest, a trophy, a founding). The pilgrimage *keeps* — the visited named place is
re-bound (the anchor refreshed by the keeping, §4), and that re-binding is the
*only* output. There is no walk that harvests under this design; the no-free-energy
cap (§5d) is what forbids it.

## 3. The costed walk

The pilgrimage is not free. The corpus's patient-field doc names the honest shape
of speed — *channeling is borrowing the field's patience* — and the loan is repaid
geographically or by the vent (`patient-field.md` §3.2). The pilgrim does not
channel to hurry the crossing (that would be a different, loan-spending act); the
pilgrim **walks the field's own pace, and pays the crossing's real costs**. Three,
each landed in a source doc, composed here into the costed walk.

### 3.1 The long ungated crossing, taken on purpose

The pilgrim leaves the road — the kept high-`q` line the settlement maintains
(`coherence-highway.md` §1) — and walks the un-roaded field. That is the 
*ungated* crossing: it rides no manufactured conduit, no cart on the `∇(g·Φ)` haul,
no station's re-raise. What it pays instead is the field's own indifference to an
ungated walker:

> **The un-roaded crossing is costed by where the walker steps and when they
> wait.** The highway doc's build-order honesty applies to the *walker*: a road
> laid *"along the site map's organized ridges is nearly free to hold; a road laid
> across the grain fights the field"* (`coherence-highway.md` §2.2 — the site map,
> `chunk-field-quantization.md` §5, is the "where the field does something" read).
> The pilgrim reads that same map on foot: **step with the organized ridges and the
> walk rides field-kept `q`; step across the grain and every meter is a drain** —
> the `(1−q)` waste law (`energy-harnessing.md` §2) bleeding the crossing toward
> the thin floor.

And **when to wait** is the tide's cost. The thin trough is the field's
slow-healing, wasteful state — *the vent backs up, machines bleed, the same act
costs more patience* (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2; `patient-field.md` §3.3: *"the
same channeled act costs more patience at thin tide and less at harvest"*). An
ungated crossing through a thin trough pays that cost as standing expenditure — a
drain, not a path — so the pilgrim's *craft* is the same as the discipline the
corpus teaches everywhere: **read the field, step where it holds, wait out the
thin**. This is the honest cost of walking un-instrumental ground: the crossing is
not free, and the walker who ignores the field pays the `(1−q)` drain the same way
a machine or a misplaced road does.

The once-flag: the *ungated crossing's craft* — how a walker reads the site map
and the tide into a route and a wait — is this doc's **[design]** composition of
the highway's and tide's landed costs (`coherence-highway.md` §2.2,
`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2); the costs themselves (the `(1−q)` drain, the
site-map ridges, the thin's patience-price) are landed. No engine turns on a
"pilgrim route"; the composition is designed surface (§5a).

**The walk's craft, taken on purpose (two-way with `the-walk.md`).** The un-roaded
crossing this §3.1 composes is the corpus's now-landed pedestrian doc's own craft:
[`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §3 designs the *craft of the unaided crossing* —
where to step (ridge lines, gradient-favorable faces), when to wait (harvest vs
thin), how much to carry (the Burden sheds into the stride), and what a crossing
leaves (the faint trail). **The pilgrim takes that craft whole and points it at the
pilgrimage's purpose**: the place is already known (the pilgrim does not find but
attend, §1), so the crossing's only *meaning* is the deliberate walk itself — the
same craft, un-instrumental. This answers `the-walk.md` §3 open-Q5 (**where the
walk ends** — whether a crossing composes into the road/voyage) on the side of the
self-complete: a pilgrim's walk ends at the attended place as its own end, not
handing off to a road. The two docs now cite each other as read-and-answered.

### 3.2 The pilgrim's own Burden, carried deliberately

The corpus designs the cost of sustained practice as a *carried loan*: `the-burden.md`
makes the `ε²` a channeler sheds *"a damaged-locality-you-drag-with-you"* — a
carried, slowly-decaying signature, cleared cheaply in a Still Room's patient time
(§1/§2b) and priced as compounding risk over a tide (§4). The pilgrim's walk
carries that deliberately:

> **The pilgrim carries their own Burden of their own choosing.** Where a heavy
> channeler carries debt because their practice sheds it by necessity, the pilgrim
> **takes the walk's load on purpose** — the un-roaded crossing's real weight
> (the `(1−q)` drain of §3.1, the thin's patience-price, the distance the ungated
> field will not shorten) accumulated as the carried ε² the walk itself leaves, and
> carried forward *because the walk is the act.* The Burden is the honest cost of
> walking for the walk's sake: **it is not sped past by borrowing the field's
> patience harder** (`the-burden.md` §4 — you cannot outrun the debt by channeling
> more; recovery is geographic), so the pilgrim's craft is to walk *within* their
> carried weight — carrying deliberately, not shedding into a harvest.

The tie is exact: the corpus's loan is repaid *"by standing in the field's
patience"* (`patient-field.md` §3.2 — geographic recovery) and cleared *"cheaply in
a Still Room's patient / coherent time"* (`the-burden.md` §2b). The pilgrim's walk
is structured around that repayment: the crossing spends the loan, and the
destination's quiet (§3.3) is where it is repaid. **[design]** The *deliberate
carrying* — that the pilgrim frames the walk's cost as their own chosen burden
rather than an incidental drain — is this doc's framing over `the-burden.md`'s
landed carried-debt read; the cost itself is that doc's (§5a).

### 3.3 Cleared at the destination's quiet

The load-bearing end of the costed walk is the **clearing** — the destination's
quiet is where the pilgrim's carried Burden is finally laid down. Two landed pieces
compose it:

- **The healer's Still-Room clearing.** The corpus's medicine doc designs the
  clearing of a carried debt as a *person-held act with a toll*: *"the toll is
  cleared by coherent rest — the Still Room … a withdrawal from acting, not a
  further op"* (`the-healer.md` §3.1). The pilgrim's clearing is that same
  Still-Room patient time, at the walk's end.
- **The stillness's resolved end.** The corpus's quietest doc designs the field's
  end-state as a place: a region so coherent its channels go flat — *"the silence
  so complete it is not absence but stillness"* (`the-stillness.md` §1), where the
  Weatherglass reads flat, the clock is frozen, and the mirror reads *nothing to
  change* (§4.1). It is *no resource* (§4.2 — the no-free-energy cap trivially
  held because the field provides nothing), and its only yield is **the awe of the
  resolved**.

> **The destination's quiet is the clearing.** The pilgrim walks the costed
> crossing, carries the Burden deliberately, and **clears it at the place that is
> its own end** — a named ruin's kept still (an attended structure's coherent well,
> `the-name.md` §6e), a seed's origin's birth-strata calm, a window-anchor's
> resolved quiet. There, in the field's patient time (the Still Room's clearing
> idiom at the landscape's scale), the carried ε² is laid down — not shed into a
> harvest, not converted, just *set down at the end of the walk that was the act.*
> The quiet's clearing is real, and it is the honest repayment of the loan the
> crossing spent: **the walk's burden is real, the clearing is real, and neither is
> a mint.**

The honest once-flag: the *clearing as the destination's act* — that the walk's end
is where the burden is deliberately set down — is this doc's **[design]**
composition of the Still-Room clearing (`the-healer.md` §3.1, `the-burden.md` §2b)
and the stillness's resolved-awe (§4.1 of the-stillness). The *clearing*, the
*burden's decay in patient time*, and the *quiet as a real end-state* are the
landed pieces; the framing that puts them at the pilgrimage's end is this doc's.

## 4. The leaving-changed — and the honest boundary of the keeping

The corpus's name doc states the mechanic the pilgrimage's whole meaning rides on:
a true name **holds** — it sits in a real coherent well the field keeps
(`the-name.md` §2.1), and a named skeleton is a persistent-Π *the field holds
longer* (§3.1). And it states the cap that keeps the holding honest: *"a name
holds the coherence the field already provides; it never mints it"* (§6d).

The leaving-changed, stated once and kept:

> **A pilgrim's visited named place is re-bound — the anchor refreshed by the
> keeping.** The walk was a keeping act (§2), and the keeping's field-consequence is
> that the visited named place's anchor *holds a little longer* — the attention the
> walk paid is the name's own holding, touched again. The visited ruin stays held,
> the seed's origin stays anchored, the window's anchor stays resolved. **The walk
> leaves the field changed: the visited place is held a little longer than it would
> have been unattended.**

And the honest boundary, stated just as sharply — the no-free-energy cap is a *hard design bound*:

> **The re-binding is the name's own holding refreshed by attention — never a mint,
> never a raise.** A true name already holds while it is held (`the-name.md` §6d);
> the pilgrimage does not *add* holding the field would not provide, does not raise
> the anchor's `q`, does not make the visited place *worth more*. **The pilgrimage's
> only output is the keeping, and the keeping is what the name already does when
> held.** The visit refreshes the attention that holding already *is*; it cannot
> generate a stronger hold, convert the placed coherence, or mint a raise.

This is the load-bearing honesty of the whole design, stated once:

> **A pilgrimage informs and keeps; it never mints.** No walk that harvests (the
> §2/§3 refusals), no keeping that raises (the §6d cap). The visited named place is
> held a little longer *because it was attended* — but the holding is the name's
> own, the keep is what the keep already is, and the walk's only residue is a
> refreshed attention and a cleared Burden. **That is the whole meaning of the
> field-world being worth crossing when nothing is to be gained but the crossing:
> the crossing's gain is the keeping, and the keeping is the name's own acting,
> renewed.**

**[design]** The *re-bind* — the precise way attention refreshes a name's already-
holding anchor, and the "held a little longer" dial over the anchor's natural
holding — is this doc's designed surface over `the-name.md`'s holding (§5a, §5b).
What is theory-ratified/landed is the holding itself (a true name holds while held,

4. **Which places are attended — the pilgrimage's scope (§2).** The three destinations (ruin, origin, anchor) are each *nameable* and *held* — but the binding's scope is the name's own open question (`the-name.md` open-Q1: which structures can be named).
