## 1. The Swim, stated once

The corpus designs the sea as a **place and a surface**. [`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2b designs it as **a flat coherence plain** — a uniform low-drain region crossed as a patient act, where the fast-reader's instruments read *less* because a uniform `q` hides the ridges. [`the-hourglass.md`](./the-hourglass.md) gives that crossing its **duration** — the plain's crossing is a patient act that takes as long as the local tempo says it takes. [`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) designs the body's act *on land*: the unaided crossing, the stride against `∇(g·Φ)`, the thin's cost, the faint wake. **What none designs is the body's act *in* the water** — the stroke, the movement through the liquid regime rather than across its surface or over its far shore. The sea is the medium; nobody designs moving *through* it.

The Swim fills that void, stated once and kept:

> **The Swim is the body's act in the water medium — the sea's third act, the walk's fluid form.** Swimming is the walk's craft applied to the liquid regime: the stroke is the stride's cousin, moved from the terrain to the water. Where the walker steps with the lean ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §2a), the swimmer strikes with the current; where the walker reads the terrain's visible ridges, the swimmer reads the sea's hidden current; where the walker's stride is the cheap, ungated crossing ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §1), the swimmer's stroke is the *expensive* one — it pays the water's own `(1−q)` drag, the liquid regime's elevated waste the land's stride does not carry ([`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md) §2). And the honest truth the sea holds over the swimmer: **you cannot out-swim the sea's patience** — the crossing is the Hourglass's duration ([`the-hourglass.md`](./the-hourglass.md)), and the swimmer who races the plain spends more ([`patient-field.md`](./patient-field.md) §3.3), because the sea's flat plain is the one surface where rushing is never a skill.

**Why this matters, honestly.** Every other crossing the corpus designs crosses *something the field offers a read of* — the walk reads ridges and thins, the highway reads held lines, the voyage reads the between. The sea is the surface that *removes* those reads (uniform `q` hides the ridges, `the-sea.md` §2b); crossing *on or over* it is a patient act. But a body *in* it faces a deeper problem: with no visible ridges, the only thing to read is the **current** — the `∇(g·Φ)` the plain conceals. The Swim is the design of that reading: the corpus's first act of **moving through a regime that hides its own gradient**, and the honest skill is learning to feel what you cannot see.

The honest scope, stated once: **the sea's regime, the `(1−q)` waste, the `∇(g·Φ)` current, and the crossing's duration are their source docs' landed pieces; the swim's composition — the stroke's cost shape, the blind-read craft, the patience truth — is this doc's [design]** over them, probe-calibrated. The Swim never claims the engine "has swimming"; it reads the liquid regime's channels at the swimmer's position and presents the stroke as a costed, readable relation to the current and the patience. No new channel, no new physics, no write — a consumer of the ≈ 6 MiB publish exactly as the walk and the Weatherglass are.

## 2. The two reads of the stroke

The Swim is two reader acts of the same field, each grounded in its source doc's real quantity, and each presented through a **stroke-cost mapping** that is this doc's [design] core (§5a). Together they are *the swimmer's craft*.

### (a) The stroke's cost — the expensive walk, the water's own `(1−q)`

**The landed piece.** [`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md) §2 gives the waste law every working field system obeys — **`E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput`** — the machine-loss floor that scales with the local coherence `q`. [`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §2b reads that same wasting *applied to the stride*: **crossing thin ground spends more `(1−q)` waste per stride's patience** — where `q` is low, the walker's own maintained work against the drain is dearer. [`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2b names the liquid regime's `q` a **uniform flat low-drain plain** — the sea's `(1−q)` is *low* (low drain, cheap movement against a desert's wasteful `q ~ 1e-3…1e-1`), but it is the *regime's own* waste, present at every stroke the way it is at every stride.

**The [design] over them — the swim is the expensive walk: a stroke spends the water's `(1−q)` the land's stride does not have.**

> **A stroke is a stride that pays the medium.** The walker's step rides the terrain's gradient partially ungated — cheap going downhill along `∇(g·Φ)` ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §2a), dear only across the thin. The swimmer's stroke has no ungated cheap direction: the water, however low its drain, is a medium the body must *push against* to move, and every push spends the liquid regime's `(1−q)` — `E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput` ([`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harring.md) §2), the water's own elevated waste, the drag the terrain's stride does not carry. **The swim is the expensive walk**: the same crossing a walker makes cheaply along a visible descent, a swimmer makes at the price of the water's `(1−q)` on every stroke — not a penalty, the honest cost of moving *through* a carrying surface rather than over it.

**The stroke's form is this doc's [design]** — the drag's *mapping* and the stroke's *cost shape* — probe-calibrated on the Phase-1 slice (§5b), never asserted:

> **The stroke's cost shape.** A stroke at the swimmer's position reads the published `(1−q)` (a derived read of the published `q`, `corpus-reconciliation.md`) and shades a base stroke by the current's alignment: **a stroke with the current's `∇(g·Φ)` costs less of the water's `(1−q)` per meter of crossing** (the field does part of the moving, as the river's fluid-drift carries a body it does not battle, `the-sea.md` §2a); **a stroke against it costs more** (the swimmer spends the water's `(1−q)` and then *fights* the same gradient, the walk crossing against the lean's dearness, `the-walk.md` §2b, re-read in the current). The *weights* — how much the current's alignment discounts or the counter-current inflates the stroke's `(1−q)` spend — and the *form* (linear in the `(1−q)` product, or a designed shape) are this doc's [design] dials, calibrated on the Phase-1 living-terrain/water demo, never asserted.

**The cost is honest — the swimmer's `(1−q)` is the water's own.**

> **A swimmer's stroke never mints and never drains the water didn't charge.** The `(1−q)` a stroke spends is the liquid regime's own waste fraction, read at the swimmer's position — the *same* `(1−q)` the plain reads flat and the Weatherglass renders as its waste tint. The swim adds **no second drain** beyond the walk's own wake (the `ε²` a moving coherence store sheds, `the-walk.md` §2c / `life-signal.md` §3.1 — a swimmer trails the same faint wake a walker does); it charges only what the water itself charges, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` held unchanged (`energy-harnessing.md` §6). **A stroke that "spends more than the water wastes" would be a lie the cap forbids; a stroke that honestly pays the water's `(1−q)` is the expensive walk, honestly priced.** The swim's dearness is the liquid regime's own, never a hidden toll and never a harvest.

**[design]** — the stroke's *cost form* (the drag's mapping, the gradient-cardinal shape) is this doc's designed read over the engine-real `(1−q)` waste law and the sea's flat `q`, calibrated on the Phase-1 water patch; what is landed is energy §2's waste law and the-sea §2b's low-drain flat `q`. The swim converts nothing into energy; it *reads* the water's waste as the stroke's price.

### (b) The stroke's craft — feeling the invisible gradient

**The landed piece.** [`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §2 establishes the gradient the field's crossings ride — **`∇(g·Φ)`**, the field-hydro haul (`energy-harnessing.md` §1.1, the river law `a = −G_N·(π/ρ)·∇(g·Φ)`, ≈ 40 ns/entity, `corpus-reconciliation.md`). [`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §2a makes that gradient a *readable* skill at foot-pace — a walker reads the terrain's ridges and steps with the lean, "a step with the lean is cheap, a step against it labors." [`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2b names the sea's **trade**: a uniform `q` **hides the ridges** — the fast-reader's instruments read less, the Lantern has no ridge to brighten, the Mirror reads *you* against the flat plain. [`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2a gives the one exception the sea does offer: the **river as a moving-conduit band**, a high-`q` filament carrying flow along `∇(g·Φ)`.

**The [design] over them — the swim's skill is feeling the invisible gradient: the current the plain conceals.**

> **The sea hides the ridges; the swimmer reads what it hides.** On land a walker sees the slope and steps with it ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §2a). In the water the plain's uniform `q` gives the eye *nothing* — no ridge to brighten the lantern, no lean in the glass, the reduced read of §2b's flat surface ([`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2b). But the gradient is still there, under the flatness: the sea's `∇(g·Φ)` runs toward whatever mass condenses beneath, and the current carries that haul. **The swimmer's craft is reading the current's `∇(g·Φ)` the way the walker reads the terrain's — the same gradient, read blind.** The stroke's idle tells — the water's slight push against the body, the `(1−q)` waste's marginal direction, the felt lean of the current — are the same `/∇(g·Φ)/` the walker reads from the slope, presented where the eye cannot see it.

**The honest skill, framed once:**

> **The swimmer who finds the current rides it; the swimmer who fights it spends the drag** ([`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2a — the river's conduit as the swimmer's highway; [`coherence-highway.md`](./coherence-highway.md) §2 — the gradient the current carries). A swimmer who reads the current's direction and *strikes with it* rides the haul a river gives a body that does not battle it — the plain's one hidden free movement, the fluid-drift a walker cannot get on land ([`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2a). A swimmer who crosses against it spends the water's `(1−q)` *and* the gradient's resistance — the walk's against-the-lean dearness ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §2b) re-read in the current, the expensive walk made dearer. **The swim's skill is not strength; it is reading the direction the field is already willing to move you** — the craft of the gradient under a surface that hides it.

**[design]** — the *blind-read* itself (that the `∇(g·Φ)` beneath a flat plain is legible through the stroke's felt direction, and that striking with it discounts the stroke) is this doc's designed read over the sea's hidden-ridge uniformity and the river's conduit, probe-calibrated; what is landed is §2's gradient, §2a's conduit, and §2b's uniform-plain-reduced-reads.

## 3. The sea's patience — you cannot out-swim it

**The landed pieces.** [`the-hourglass.md`](./the-hourglass.md) §3 gives the sea's crossing its honest duration — **a sea crossing is a duration act**, the plain's crossing takes as long as the local tempo says it takes (the sand drains at the field's rate). [`patient-field.md`](./patient-field.md) §3.3 sets the loan's physical limit — **at thin the vent backs up; the same channeled act costs more patience**, the loan's interest rate is the field's own patience, which swells when organized and bites when not. [`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §2b draws the consequence at the stride: **a rushed crossing is a dear crossing** — hurrying across the thin spends the loan's patience fastest.

**The [design] over them — you cannot out-swim the sea's patience.**

> **The crossing is the Hourglass's duration, and a swimmer who races the plain spends more.** The sea's flat plain hides the ridges *and* the season — a swimmer cannot see whether they cross at harvest (the field heals fast, the loan cheap) or at thin (the field heals slowly, the loan dear) ([`patient-field.md`](./patient-field.md) §3.3, read at the water). Rushing the crossing — stroking hard against the current, hurrying the length — spends the water's `(1−q)` *faster* (the stroke cost rises with the rush, the walk's rushed-crossing dearness of §2b re-read in the stroke) and leaves the longer, louder wake. **The patient stroke is the honest stroke**: a swimmer who reads the current, times the crossing, and strikes with the sea's own direction (not against it) crosses at the plain's honest cost — the way the walker who reads the thin and waits out the trough crosses cheaply ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §3 — "when to wait").

**The one surface where rushing is never a skill.** The walk's rush is a *choice* a walker can read against the terrain. On the sea's flat plain the fast-reader reads *less* (§2b) — there are no ridges to reveal that the rush is cheap now, no thin's edge to show it is dear. The swimmer cannot see the season's cost. **The sea's flat plain is the one surface where rushing is never a skill** — not because the water punishes speed, but because the plain hides the information that would let speed be *earned*. A raced swim is at best a blind gamble that the crossing is at harvest; the honest swimmer learns the patience the plain demands, because rushing on a surface that hides its own price is the walker's dear crossing made inevitable.

**[design]** — the *patience truth* (that a raced stroke on the uniform plain wastes the `(1−q)` and the wake without earning the crossing, and that the patient stroke is the honest one) is this doc's designed reading of the-hourglass §3's duration and patient-field §3.3's loan-at-thin, applied to the stroke; what is landed is the duration read and the loan's interest.

**The honest edge — the swim's beginning and its descent.**

> **A swim is the fall's soft landing; the sea's flat plain is the honest surface a falling body crosses into.** A body that falls into the sea enters the liquid regime through its leveling — the medium that (unlike the terrain) slows the fall into the crossing that becomes the swim. The swim begins where the "fall" ends: the honest carrying surface ([`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2b) receives the body as the swim's first stroke. And the swim's **descent** is the sea-floor's reef ([`the-sea-floor.md`](./the-sea-floor.md) §2b) — the swimmer who crosses the plain to the interface reaches where the sea's sparse life holds and the reduced reads return — **the swimmer crosses the plain to the reef, and the reef is where the swim's honest edge (the plain's end) becomes the sea-floor's structure** ([`the-sea-floor.md`](./the-sea-floor.md) §1/§2).

**[design]** — the fall-as-the-swim's-beginning (that a body entering the sea crosses into the swim, and the plain is the soft landing) and the descent-to-the-reef are this doc's designed reading over `the-sea.md` §2b's carrying surface and `the-sea-floor.md` §2's reef; **the `the-fall` reference is now landed two-way** ([`the-fall.md`](./the-fall.md) §4.2 — a fall into the sea is the swim's beginning; open-Q5 this doc, closed) — see §5a's row and the Cross-references section.
