# The Threshold: The Boundary-Mark as a Field Structure

**Question under design:** the corpus marks boundaries implicitly — the window's edge
([`world-seams.md`](./world-seams.md)), the field's regimes
([`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md)), the property line
([`house-that-steers.md`](./house-that-steers.md)) — but nothing designs the
**boundary-mark**: the deliberate, built thing a player places to say *here is the edge of
my place*, and the field *respects*. The corpus's buildings steer the field
([`house-that-steers.md`](./house-that-steers.md)), and its settlements steal a gradient (*bath
at the center, productive edges, deliberate drains* — §2). But how a settlement *marks its
edges* was never asked. A wall is the obvious answer, and the corpus already knows a wall is
the wrong one: **walls concentrate coherence when sloped, but their open edges drain
(`house-that-steers.md` §1.1), and the layout's *deliberate drains* are its honest edges**
(§2.2) — a walled boundary is a drain, not a hold.

This document designs the alternative: **the Threshold — a boundary-mark as a field
structure.** The doorstep, the gate, and the arch are not walls; they are *deliberate
boundary-structures the field respects because they are held, quiet, and named* — the
corpus's answer to how a settlement marks its edges.

This is a **boundary-making design**: it is PHASE-1-LEAN with one Phase-1 slice (the
doorstep's local-quiet read over the living-terrain demo), and it is the corpus's
boundary doc — the seam between a settlement and the window it lives in
([`world-seams.md`](./world-seams.md) §1.3/§4.3). It introduces no new channel and no new
physics; it is a designed consumer over the architecture and identity machinery the corpus
already designs.

## 1. The Threshold, stated once

The corpus's building form is coherence architecture: geometry is a regime, and what a
player builds steers the local field (`house-that-steers.md` §1). A settlement is that
architecture at scale — a gradient-stealing layout with a bath at its center, productive
edges, and deliberate drains (§2). But nothing says how a settlement *marks its edges* — the
deliberate boundary whose whole is *no one thing* but the seam itself. This doc is that
design:

> **A boundary-mark is a field structure.** The deliberate, built thing a player places to
> say *here is the edge of my place* — and the field *respects* because it is **held** (a
> maintained high-`q` hold, `house-that-steers.md` §1.1's room), **quiet** (a local-`(1−q)`
> pocket where the carried day settles, `field-instruments.md` §2.2's Still Room at the door),
> and **named** (a structure that is itself a boundary, `the-name.md` §1's anchor). The field
> does not recognize "a wall"; it recognizes a *held, quiet, named structure* — a persistent
> high-`q` hold the field prefers to keep. **A threshold is that structure made a boundary.**

Why not walls? The corpus's own honesty answers it. A wall is a *concentrating* element when
sloped (it folds the ambient `Π` field toward its inner face, `house-that-steers.md` §1.1),
but a settlement enclosed in walls is a settlement whose *open edges drain* — and a walled
perimeter is exactly an over-long open edge, a gradient the field routes outward instead of
holding (§2.2's deliberate drains point *away*, not *around*). **Walls drain** — that is the
house doc's honesty, and it is why the settlement's managed gradient uses *open, steered
edges* rather than a sealed ring. The Threshold is the same honesty turned to *building the
edge deliberately*: instead of one draining wall, three deliberate boundary-structures whose
boundary is their *held, quiet, named* state, not their mass.

**The corpus's answer to "how does a settlement mark its edges," stated once:**

> **Not with walls, which drain — with deliberate boundary-structures the field respects
> because they are held, quiet, and named.** The settlement's edge is not a barrier the field
> must respect or fight; it is a set of structures that *read the field as a boundary* — the
> doorstep (a quiet place), the gate (a deliberate seam-point), the arch (a mark the field
> reads) — and the field honors the boundary because the boundary is what it already does:
> hold, settle, and name.

The three forms are each a *designed consumer* with its own honest boundary (§2), the honest
mechanics are §3, the settlement's edge is §4, and the gates are §6.

### 2.1 The doorstep — a quiet place, the Still Room at the door

**What it is.** The doorstep is the boundary's quiet. It is, in `field-instruments.md` §2.2's
idiom, **the Still Room at the body's scale, set at the door**: a small, built
local-`(1−q)` pocket (a designed high-`q` hold, `house-that-steers.md` §1.1's room made the
size of a doorstep) where the day's carried ε² **settles a little** before the player enters
the home.

**The mechanic.** [`the-burden.md`](./the-burden.md) §1 fixes what it is to carry a day: a
heavy channeler drags an accumulated-ε² field — the running scar of their practice. Its
repayment is the Still Room's patient, coherent time (§2b), and [`sleep.md`](./sleep.md) §3 is
that repayment at its nightly body-scale instance. The doorstep is that same repayment **at
its threshold instance**:

> **You leave the day's debt at the doorstep before entering the home.** The doorstep is a
> designed local-`(1−q)` pocket — a small Still-Room hold at the door where the day's carried
> ε² (the vent/(1−q) the player shed to act, `coherence-magic.md` §1.2) settles a little in
> the coherent ground, the way `the-burden.md` §2b's Still Room clears the debt but at the
> scale of a single threshold-crossing and a single small step.

The sleep/burden tie is the honest through-line: the doorstep is the *body-scale Still Room
interval at the front edge of `sleep.md`'s bed decision* — the day's debt settles a little at
the door, so what the bed clears is lighter and the night's risk (`signature-predator.md`
§2's `τ` against the resting body, `sleep.md` §2.2) starts lower. **[design]** The *settling* —
how much of the carried vent/(1−q) the doorstep's quiet draws, over what small arc — is this
doc's designed dial over the engine-real vent/(1−q) channels, probe-calibrated (§6a); the
*fact of the vent and the Still Room's clearing* are engine-real / designed (`the-burden.md`
§6a).

**The honest boundary.** The doorstep **holds and steadies; it never generates** (§5d). It does
not mint a cleared day — it *settles a little* of the debt the day actually shed, bounded by
the no-free-energy cap and by `the-burden.md` §4's "loan, never a mint": the doorstep is a
*withdrawal from acting* (standing in coherent ground a moment), not a new channeled op, and
it can never clear more than the day carried. An unmaintained doorstep — one whose built
high-`q` hold collapses, `house-that-steers.md` §3's ruin — **stops being quiet** (§3): the
pocket drains, the settling stops, and the doorstep becomes an ordinary draining step.

### 2.2 The gate — a deliberate seam-point, a held boundary the hunt must cross

**What it is.** The gate is the boundary's *deliberate seam-point*. Where the doorstep is
quiet, the gate is **held**: a maintained high-`q` arch (the doorway's directed channel,
`house-that-steers.md` §1.1 — a deliberate break in the wall that steers flow *through* it)
over which the crossing is a **marked act the field reads**.

**The mechanic.** By concentrating the crossing — entering and leaving happen *through* the
gate, not around it — the gate makes the crossing legible and bounded:

> **Entering and leaving are marked acts the field reads.** A maintained high-`q` arch
> concentrates the crossing the way a doorway channels flow through rather than around
> (`house-that-steers.md` §1.1). Every crossing through a held gate is a marked perturbation
> on the published q/ε² channels — a bounded event the field (and anyone reading it) can name:
> a body passed the settlement's boundary.

The **Coda tie** is the gate's load-bearing boundary. [`signature-predator.md`](./signature-predator.md)
§2 fixes the Coda's approach as a resonance accumulating on a clean trail — `R = ρ_signature
· τ · M_stability` — against a signature that crosses the settlement's read. The gate does not
stop the Coda; it makes the approach **cross a held boundary**:

> **The Coda can cross a gate — it must merely cross the held boundary, and the crossing is
> the warning.** The gate is a maintained high-`q` hold in the settlement's edge. The Coda is a
> margin-resonance the high-`q` refuge cannot enter (`signature-predator.md` §4.4 — a Still
> Room / Qi-bath is ground it cannot enter), so an approach to the settlement must encounter
> the gates the settlement holds. Each gate crossed is a **marked crossing** on the q/ε²
> channels — the same channels the Bell reads (`the-bell.md` §1/§2): the gate's crossing is a
> *signature reaching the settlement's read*, the Bell's predator channel. **The crossing of
> the held boundary is the warning** — the settlement hears the gate cross before the Coda
> reaches a resting body (`the-bell.md` §4, the night ring).

The **exile tie** (skimmed): the gate is also the boundary the hollow-eye makes explicit —
the exiled line crosses a settlement that no longer feeds it (`the-exile.md` §3.1's
coordinated withdrawal), and the gate reads *against* that severed line the way it reads *for*
a held one. The gate's courtesy is symmetrical: it marks both entering and leaving as acts.

**[design]** The gate is this doc's [design] over the q/ε² channels + the Coda's accumulation
read, with the signature-predator gates carried intact (§6b): the *marked-crossing* read is
designed over the real crossing's channel perturbation; the Coda's *approaching and crossing*
inherits `signature-predator.md` §7's gates verbatim (the Q4 write-lane gate, the provenance
probe).

**The honest boundary.** The gate **concentrates; it does not stop** (§3). It is not a barrier
the field cannot cross — the Coda *can* cross a gate, and the design says so plainly; the gate
is the *deliberate seam-point* at which the crossing is a warning rather than an ambush. An
**unmaintained gate stops being a seam** (§3): its held high-`q` arch collapses toward the
attractor (`house-that-steers.md` §3), the crossing becomes ordinary, and the warning the
settlement once heard is gone — the field no longer marks the boundary because it is no longer
held.

### 2.3 The arch — a mark the field reads, a named threshold

**What it is.** The arch is the boundary's *name*. [`the-name.md`](./the-name.md) §1 fixes
naming as a field act: to name a thing is to bind a persistent-Π anchor to it, the
member-id's principle extended from players to *things*. The arch is the boundary variant: **a
named structure that is itself a boundary.**

> **A named threshold: a named structure that is itself a boundary.** The arch is a name bound
> to a boundary-mark — a persistent-Π anchor over the settlement's edge the way a house's name
> is an anchor over the house. The field holds the arch as it holds any named thing
> (`the-name.md` §1), and because it is *named*, the arch is the first thing a visitor's
> provenance crosses — the moment their carried history passes a *named* boundary, it is a
> legible, booked event.

**The mechanic.** The arch is the boundary's *legibility*, and it ties the two halves the
task names:

- **`the-name`'s anchor.** The arch holds a name that is itself a boundary: the field
  re-quantizes around a named structure (`the-name.md` §1), so a *named threshold* is a boundary
  the field keeps, the way it keeps a named ruin's record or a named settlement's core. And the
  honesty clause binds it hard: **you cannot name falsely** (`the-name.md` §2) — a threshold
  named falsely is a Π-configuration with nothing to hold it; it *decays*, and the arch sheds
  like a false name. **[design]** The naming is [design] over `the-name`'s theory-ratified
  anchor with the flag (§6a — `qi-computation.md` §5.2's persistent-Π, the speculation flag
  `reason-field.md` §6a carries).
- **`window-guests`' visitor line.** [`window-guests.md`](./window-guests.md) §3 books a guest
  as a visitor line — a contribution with provenance, not a resident. The arch is where that
  line *crosses*: **the arch is the first thing a visitor's provenance crosses** — their
  carried Burden/order/signature (`window-guests.md` §2) pass a *named* boundary, and the
  moment of crossing books the visitor line (a contribution with provenance from the moment
  they stepped over the named edge). **[design]** over `window-guests`' visitor line; the
  crossing itself inherits `window-guests`' gates (the visitor book is Phase-1-able, §6b).

**The honest boundary.** The arch does not make the boundary *stronger* — it makes it
*legible*. A named threshold is a boundary the field keeps and a visitor's provenance crosses
as an act; it holds exactly what the field already holds of a named thing and no more (§5d —
a name never mints worth, `the-name.md` §6d). An **un-maintained arch sheds like a false name**
(§3): if the boundary it names is no longer held (the settlement's edge decays, `fate-of-a-window.md`),
the name binds to nothing, and the arch's anchor decays the way a false name's does
(`the-name.md` §2.2) — the arch un-binds and the threshold stops being named.

## 3. The honest mechanics — what a threshold does NOT do

The three forms have one honest spine: **a threshold holds, steadies, and marks — it never
generates, never stops, never lies.** The honesty, stated once for all three:

| What a threshold does NOT do | The reason | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|
| **It is not a wall.** A threshold does not enclose; it does not form a perimeter that drains. The doorstep is a *quiet pocket*, the gate a *concentrated seam*, the arch a *legibility* — none is a concentrating element running as a continuous edge (which would drain, `house-that-steers.md` §1.1/§2.2). | walls drain — `house-that-steers.md` §2's honesty; the settlement's managed edge is open and steered, not sealed | `house-that-steers.md` §1.1/§2/§5 |
| **It is not an uncrossable barrier.** The field can cross a threshold. The Coda *can* cross a gate — but the crossing is the warning (the Bell tie); the gate concentrates the crossing into the marked act the settlement hears. | the Coda is a resonance the refuge excludes but the *edge* can be crossed (`signature-predator.md` §4.4 — it is ground it cannot *enter*; a held *gate* is a seam it crosses *through*) | `signature-predator.md` §2/§4.4; `the-bell.md` §4 |
| **It is not a mint.** A threshold holds, steadies, and marks — it never generates. The doorstep settles the day's own debt (bounded); the gate reads a crossing that was going to happen; the arch reads what the field already holds. No form makes `q`, clears more than was carried, or books more than crossed. | the no-free-energy cap — `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`, `energy-harnessing.md` §6; a house holds and steers, never mints (`house-that-steers.md` §5d); a name never mints (`the-name.md` §6d) | `energy-harnessing.md` §6; `house-that-steers.md` §5d; `the-name.md` §6d |

**The maintained cost.** All three are **held structures** — and the field only respects what
it is still holding. The honest consequence, stated once:

> **A threshold is a maintained structure, and an unmaintained one stops being what it was.**
> The doorstep's quiet is its held high-`q` pocket; let the pocket collapse
> (`house-that-steers.md` §3's ruin) and the doorstep **stops being quiet** — it becomes an
> ordinary draining step. The gate's seam is its held arch; let the arch un-hold and the gate
> **stops being a seam** — the crossing becomes ordinary and the warning is gone. The arch's
> name is its bound anchor; let the edge it names decay and the arch **sheds like a false
> name** (`the-name.md` §2) — the threshold stops being named. **The field does not honor an
> abandoned boundary; it holds what is held.**

This is the same maintenance honesty as `house-that-steers.md` §3.1 (an unmaintained building
collapses toward the attractor) applied to the *boundary*: a threshold is an act of
maintenance — anti-corruption at the edge (`energy-harnessing.md` §5.4) — and the moment the
settlement stops holding its edge, the edge stops being a boundary.

## 4. The settlement's edge — the inner edge

**The Threshold is the *inner* edge:** the seam between the settlement and the window it lives
in. [`world-seams.md`](./world-seams.md) §1.3/§4.3 fixes the window's own edge as the *outer*
boundary — the envelope falling off, the published grid fading, the field-poor interstitial
beginning. The Threshold is where the settlement's **own** boundary begins, *inside* that
outer edge:

> **The settlement's edge is made of held, quiet, named structures instead of draining
> walls.** The window's boundary-layer is the outer edge (world-seams §1.3 — where the field
> thins and the dark begins). The threshold is the *inner* edge: the deliberate seam where the
> settlement *says* — with doorsteps, gates, and arches — *here, inside the window, is the edge
> of my place*. The outer edge is the window's; the inner edge is the settlement's. And it is
> built of held, quiet, named structures — not a draining ring.

Why this division is honest. The window's edge is a *law* boundary (the field's envelope, the
seam of the mixture's empty measure, world-seams §4.3 — "two windows meet at the field-poor
interstitial"). The settlement's edge is a *made* boundary — an act of the settlement, which
the field respects because the settlement respects it:

> **The boundary the field respects because the settlement respects it.** The field's respect
> for a threshold is not the law honoring a wall; it is the field honoring a *held, quiet,
> named structure* — a persistent high-`q` hold it prefers to keep, a Still-Room pocket of
> settled ε², a bound name (`the-name.md` §1). A settlement that maintains its edge — keeps
> its doorsteps quiet, its gates held, its arches named — keeps the field holding that edge.
> A settlement that lets its edge go unmaintained loses it: the quiet drains, the seams stop
> marking, the names decay — and the settlement's own edge dissolves back into the window's
> ordinary field, exactly as a ruined house's gradient collapses toward the attractor
> (`house-that-steers.md` §3). **The threshold is the settlement's self-respect made a
> field-structure.**

This is the corpus's answer to *how a settlement marks its edges* — the honest, wall-free,
maintenance-bound answer, and the load-bearing claim of this doc.

> **Same threshold, same field state → same boundary.** A threshold's read — the doorstep's

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — a threshold holds, never mints

`energy-harnessing.md` §6's cap (`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`), inherited through
`house-that-steers.md` §5d and `the-name.md` §6d, holds as a hard bound:

> **A threshold holds, steadies, and marks — it never generates.** The doorstep settles the
> day's *own* carried ε² and no more; the gate reads a crossing that was going to happen
> (it does not mint a reason to cross); the arch reads what the field already holds of a
> named thing (it does not make a settlement's edge worth more than it is). **There is no
> "build a threshold to mint quiet, to mint crossings, or to mint worth."** A doorstep that
> reads as clearing more than the day carried, a gate that reads as manufacturing a warning,
> or an arch that reads as making a settlement's edge over-valued is a lie the cap forbids —
> exactly as a house that "generates `q`" is a lie (`house-that-steers.md` §5d) and a name
> that makes a thing worth more is a lie (`the-name.md` §6d). The highest a threshold can
> mark is what the field already does at that boundary; the cap encodes it.
