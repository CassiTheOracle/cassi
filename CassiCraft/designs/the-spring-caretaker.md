### 2.1 The rate-read — the welling's read over the long horizon

**The landed piece.** The spring's welling is the field's own conservative
re-precipitation at a fixed point — a locality whose ambient `q` the field keeps
re-surfacing of itself, held above its dissolution threshold tide after tide
(`the-spring.md` §2.1). Its *rate* — the ambient `q` a spring holds above its
locality's baseline, sustained — is `the-spring.md`'s rate-read, this doc's
[design] over the spring's welling (open-Q2 there: "the welling's *rate* ... is
probe-calibrated"). **The read the caretaker performs is that rate-read, over
the long horizon** (the tide's accounting cycle, `tide-of-the-attractor.md` —
`the-spring.md` §2.2 keys the stationarity to the tide it holds):

> **[design] The caretaker reads the well's rate over the long horizon — is
> the well full, thinning, or failing?** Full means the spring's elevated `q`
> sits above its locality's baseline and stays there across the tide-cycles —
> the stationarity holds. Thinning means the well's rate is *trending down* —
> the elevated `q` is falling toward the locality's baseline, the re-precipitation
> is no longer keeping the point as full as it was. Failing means the rate has
> crossed beneath a designed floor — the well is reading toward the spent,
> the stationarity the field once held is no longer held. (Three "states," but
> one continuous rate-read; the class thresholds are designed over the measured
> `q`-trend, exactly as `the-spring.md` open-Q2's rate calibration.)

Each state reads a real, published quantity — the `q`/`ε²` trends over the
tide (`the-spring.md` §2.2; the ≈ 6 MiB publish, on the Weatherglass's
sample-cost profile, `field-instruments.md` §1.4). The *long horizon* is the
load-bearing form: a spring's fullness is a stationarity held across tides, not a
single snapshot — so the caretaker's read is a trend over the tide's
`[t_k, t_k+T]` cycle (the census's and the ledger's accounting cycle,
`the-census.md` §2.1, `shared-ledger.md` §1.2), never a one-time glance. **[design]**
The rate-read is the spring's own open-Q2 (the welling's rate) made an office's

### 2.2 The mouth's keeping — a real, spent act

**The landed pieces.** The spring's interest is the order that *rises of
itself* (`the-spring.md` §2), but a fixed source-place is also a place that can
be **choked** — the same face of the field that feeds a spring can silt it. Two
landed silt-carriers:

- **The compost's spent matter** (`the-compost.md` §2/§3). The corpus turns its
  spent order deliberately — tool-scarred steel, the worked vein's residue, the
  granary's spoiled surplus — back into the field as a slow-release feed. But
  spent matter that is *not* turned — the worn steel left at the well's margin,
  the worked vein's residue shed near the source, the spoiled surplus dumped
  where the water runs — is exactly the **silt that chokes a spring's mouth**:
  the same low-`q`/high-`ε²` spent state (`the-fallow.md` §2a,
  `the-compost.md` §2.2) that, lying across a source-place's rise, blocks the
  order from surfacing. A spring whose mouth is silted is a spring whose welling
  reads low not because the field stopped feeding it, but because the rise is
  blocked.
- **The rain's silt** (`the-rain.md` §3). The rain is nourishing but wet — it
  feeds a plot but muddies a signature, the marsh's blur on a smaller scale
  (`the-rain.md` §3.1). The same wet-wash that carries order across a window
  **carries silt to a spring's mouth** — a fall of rain raises the local high-`q`
  texture and can wash the spent margin's residue across the source-place, the
  wet that feeds also the wet that chokes, the rain's honest two-hand
  (`the-rain.md` §3 — the give is the field's own, and its wet cost is real).

**The [design] over them — the mouth's keeping is the clearing.** Where a
spring's mouth is silted, the caretaker's act is to **clear it** — to turn the
spent matter that choked the rise back to the field (the compost's deliberate
return), to read and clear the rain-carried silt so the rise surfaces again.
The clearing is a **real, spent act**:

> **[design] The mouth's keeping is the clearing — a real, spent act, never a
> mint.** A caretaker does not "maintain" a spring by decree; a caretaker *does*
> — reads where the rise is blocked, clears the spent matter and the silt that
> choked it, and books the clearing the way any op books. The spent matter that
> silted the mouth is returned at a loss (the compost's honest re-turn,
> `energy-harnessing.md` §6 — `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`); the rain's silt is moved
> (an honest, bounded field write). **The clearing costs** — the `(1−q)` waste
> runs through the act like through every spent act, and a caretaker who does
> not clear, or clears too little, leaves the well reading toward the blocked.
> The keeping is never free.

The honest bound: **a silted mouth is the spring's own speakable state** — the
spent matter that choked it and the rain's wash are the field's own (`the-compost.md`
§2, `the-rain.md` §3), and the clearing does not create the welling; it
*unblocks the rise the field already keeps* (`the-spring.md` §2.1 — a spring
re-fills by the field's conservation, never by the settlement's demand). The
clearing restores a source to what its own conservation already returns; it never
mints an extra welling.

### 2.3 The legibility's defense — the point of fame, kept legible

**The landed pieces.** A spring is a **point of abundance *and* of fame** — a

> **[design] The legibility's defense is the keeping that holds the source

> maintenance axis, `life-signal.md` §3), keeps the source's named place held

> early signs — **and keeps the well's legibility so the settlement does not
> read the desert's dry-need in its own window.** The office's weight is real;
> it is never a hidden duty-meter.
