## 2. The counterweighted lift — the honest mechanics

### (a) The lever — geometry redirects the spend

The Crane's whole mechanics is the **lever**: a rigid beam on a fulcrum that
redirects the wielder's force through a distance, trading a long gentle pull for
a short heavy lift. The lever's ratio is the machine's own law — the farther the
wielder's end from the fulcrum, the less force per unit raised, and the more
distance the wielder's end travels for each meter of lift. That ratio is a real
geometry and a real cost: the shorter-force is bought with the longer-motion, and
the `(1−q)` of the lift (the wasted spend, `energy-harnessing.md` §2) is spread
across the whole exchange. **The crane does not reduce the lift's cost; it
changes the lift's *shape* — less force, more distance, the same `(1−q)`,
cheaper per unit by the ratio and never zero.**

### (b) The counterweight — the designed part, a real mass with a real fall

The counterweight is the Crane's defining designed part: a real mass, the same
honest weight the corpus moves everywhere (`the-carry.md` §4 — carried matter,
never free), hung on the beam's short end so its **fall raises the load**. The
counterweight is not free — it is carried to the crane, hung, and its descent is
a real fall with the same `(1−q)` any descent spends (`the-fall.md` §2.1 — the
gradient owns you; here it owns the weight, usefully). A counterweighted lift
borrows the counterweight's fall to assist the load's rise — and the sum is
never a gain: the counterweight descended, the load rose, and the window's order
after the exchange is the same as before, minus the `(1−q)` the whole machine
spent. **The counterweight's fall is the machine's honest fuel, and it pays its
waste exactly as any fall does.**

### (c) The load, read on the instruments — the weight, the balance, the strain

The Crane's load is legible on the same instruments the settlement reads for
every load: the carry's weight reads on the published channels the pack moves
through (`the-carry.md` §5e), the balance reads as the beam's strain and the
fulcrum's load (the `(1−q)` the machine spends, readable the way a cart's ride
is), and the lift's strain reads on the same field-hydro and density channels the
house's structure holds (`house-that-steers.md` §2.2 — the managed gradient).
**A crane's load is not hidden at the machine — the weight, the balance, the
strain are all published reads**, legible before a load is committed, exactly as
the climb reads a wall before it commits (`the-climb.md` §3.3). The instruments
do not add a channel; they read the crane's honest physics as a designed
machine's own.

### (d) The rise — the carry's vertical assist, the shaft's line reversed

Where the Shaft freights *down* the vertical's line — the carry's weight hauled
out of the deep (`the-shaft.md` §2b) — the Crane lifts *up* the same line: the
stone the quarry cuts (`the-quarry.md` §2a) raised off the face, the freight the
shaft lowers raised back into the tower, the building's own weight standing on
the house's hold. **The crane is the vertical's own line, reversed and aided** —
the freight-lane's ascent made mechanical. The lift's height is bounded by the
machine's own geometry (the beam's length, the counterweight's travel), not by
the field's, and the machine raises what the body could only climb with.

### (e) The steersman — an attended lift reads intent, a machine holds no will

A crane is a machine, and a machine holds no will: it raises what the counterweight
balances and the geometric ratio permits, deterministically, never answering to a
hidden intention. The corpus's intent channel (`reason-field.md` §2 — the published
intent/Π field) is the layer that lets an attended lift read as *attended* — a
steersman's balance-read, the settlement's coordinated working — but the machine
itself needs no intent to lift. **The Crane is honest because it is legible as a
machine: its load, its balance, its strain all on the instruments, its lift a
fact of the field's own gravity and the geometry's own law, with no hidden will
behind it** (`the-dispute.md` §2).

### (f) The machine's form — this doc's [design] over the landed primitives

The Crane's mechanics are entirely composed from the landed primitives — the
carry's weight (`the-carry.md` §2), the cart's chassis-and-gradient idiom
(`the-cart.md` §3), the tool's rung-matched regime (`the-tool.md` §3), the
shaft's vertical line (`the-shaft.md` §2b), the quarry's cut (`the-quarry.md`
§2a), the house's structure hold (`house-that-steers.md` §1) — and the machine's
own form remains **this doc's [design], probe-calibrated**: the beam's length, the
fulcrum's height, the counterweight's mass, the lift's rated bound. These are
dials over grounded landed quantities — the carry's weight, the cart's load
idiom, the shaft's vertical, the house's hold — never a new physics. The Crane is
the carry made mechanical; the machine's form is the one designed thing this doc
adds, and it is a lever, never a mint.

## 3. The load-bearing design — why the mechanical lift is honest to design

### (a) The crane is the carry's vertical assist — the same honest weight, raised by geometry

The Carry is the honest weight the body bears — its field-cost the walk's
slowing, the climb's tax, the swim's fight (`the-carry.md` §2/§3). **The Crane is
that same honest weight made mechanical — the carry's vertical assist, raised by
geometry instead of hands.** It does not make the weight lighter and it does not
make it free; it changes *how* the weight is raised, substituting the lever's
ratio and the counterweight's fall for the body's own hand-over-hand climb. The
assist is honest because it is the same load: the stone the body would stagger
under is the stone the beam raises — same carried matter
(`the-carry.md` §4 — carried matter, never free), same `(1−q)` a lift always
spends, just cheaper per unit by the geometry's shape.

### (b) The geometry is honest because it completes the load's trade — vertical as the cart's horizontal

The Cart's whole honesty is the **load-and-gradient trade**: a downhill cart
carries its payload on the gradient, and a loaded cart needs the bat-band's
maintained line to climb back — the trade of load for gradient, never a mint
(`the-cart.md` §3). **The Crane is that same trade turned vertical** — the load
bartered against the counterweight's fall and the lever's distance instead of the
road's descent, the vertical-as-the-cart's-horizontal. It is honest to design
because it is the corpus's own load-law completed: the trade was horizontal (the
cart rides a gradient it didn't make), and the crane makes it vertical (the load
rides a geometry the settlement did make), both trades of real mechanics, both
never free.

### (c) The lift it buys is never a mint — it is the carry's cost, redistributed

The Crane's whole point is the raise — the stone off the quarry's face
(`the-quarry.md` §2a), the freight into the tower, the building's weight stacked
on the house's hold (`house-that-steers.md` §1). What it buys is not a new store
of order but a *way to raise the existing weight higher than the body alone*:
the counterweight's fall, spent usefully. **And because the raise is a real
exchange, it is never free** — `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` holds through the counterweight's
fall, the load's rise, and the machine's strain (`energy-harnessing.md` §6). A
crane does not mint lift; it *redistributes* the carry's own honest cost across a
geometry that changes the force's shape, never its sum.

## Open questions

1. **The counterweight's mass and travel (§2b/§2f).** How heavy may a crane's
   counterweight be, and how far may it fall, before the machine stops being a
   lever and becomes a drop — a fall wearing a lift's shape? The honest default
   is a counterweight whose fall is bounded beneath the fall's own danger
   (`the-fall.md` §3), probe-calibrated against the carry's rated weight.
   **[design]/[probe]**
2. **The rated bound (§2f/§5c).** Is the crane's lift's rated bound a *read*
   (the machine's strain published, the bound legible on the instruments before
   a load is committed, bounded by the field's own hold) or a *physical* break
   point (a beam that fails past its rated load, the failure deterministic and
   pre-read)? The corpus's determinism pulls toward the fully-legible read; the
   honest default is the pre-read bound, never a hidden break. **[design]**
3. **The steersman's intent (§2e).** Is an attended crane's lift *read* as intent
   on the channel (`reason-field.md` §2 — the published intent/Π field, a
   settlement's coordinated working) or is the machine deliberately intent-free
   (a dead lever, the balance purely geometric)? The honest default leans the
   legible attended working (a settlement's coordinated lift reads as the
   cooperation it is), never a hidden will in the machine. **[design]**
4. **The crane in the house's slots (§3c).** Does the crane's stone-raising feed
   the house's coherence-architecture directly (the built hold needs the raised
   stone, the house steers the settled order) or is it only the builder's
   transport (the stone raised, then the house's own phase holds it)? Inherits
   `house-that-steers.md` §1/§2.2; the honest default is the transport-feed
   first, the steer-second. **[design]**
5. **The tension — raising vs. lowering.** The shaft freights down
   (`the-shaft.md` §2b) and the crane lifts up. Is the *lowering* crane (a
   controlled descent of a heavy load into the shaft, the fall's spend braked
   and made useful) a designed twin, or is it the fall's own craft
   (`the-fall.md` §4 — the landing, not the falling) at machine scale? The
   honest default is the machine-assisted lower as a distinct designed part of
   the same lever. **[design]**
6. **The trade's honesty at the ratio.** Is the lever's ratio itself a *found*
   geometry (the longer-pull is a real cost in time and distance, its
   `(1−q)` legible on the ride) or a *free* dial (a ratio the builder sets with
   no cost)? The corpus's no-mint discipline forces the former — the ratio is a
   real trade, cheaper per unit and never zero, exactly as the cart's
   gradient-trade is honest. **[design]**
