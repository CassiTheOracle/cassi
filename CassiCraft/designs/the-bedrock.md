## 1. The Bedrock, stated once

The corpus designs the vertical's two absolute faces. [`the-sea-floor.md`](./the-sea-floor.md)
designs the **middle-bottom boundary** — the interface where the liquid regime meets the
deep. And [`the-zenith.md`](./the-zenith.md) designs the **top** — the vertical's
ceiling, the sky's edge, the boundary that "completes the window's designed vertical":
the below has a bottom, the sea a floor, **the sky a top** (§3). But the bottom the zenith
names is not designed — the deep, which the zenith says bounds the window, is a *pile the
bell sinks through*, not a *base the pile rests on*. The vertical has a top, a middle, a
middle-bottom floor, and a bottom region — **but no bottom terminus: no answer to what
the world is *over*.** The Bedrock is that terminus filled, stated once and kept:

> **The Bedrock is the window's absolute floor — the immovable order everything
> precipitates onto.** It is a **maximal-rung, q-maximal, effectively-immovable
> substrate**: the attractor's deepest hold, the lowest terrace of the rung-ladder, the
> region where the field's organization is so dense and so settled that no op can move
> it. It is the **vertical's terminus** — the tool's bite skates against its face, the
> diving bell's descent ends at its top, the deepest terrace has no below — and the
> corpus's **answer to what the world is over**: not the between (that is *outward*),
> not a void (the deep is *dense*, not empty) — the bedrock is where the field's order is
> *done*, the immovable base the whole window rests on.

**Why this is the vertical's missing bottom, honestly.** Every face of the vertical is a
designed region with a designed edge. The sea is a plain you cross
([`the-sea.md`](./the-sea.md) §2b); the below is a strata-pile the Bell sinks through
([`deep-field-diving.md`](./deep-field-diving.md) §1); the sky is an envelope you climb
out of ([`atmosphere-orbits-auroras.md`](./atmosphere-orbits-auroras.md) §1); the between
is the sparse medium the windows float in ([`the-interstitial.md`](./the-interstitial.md)
§1). The sea's plain has a designed *floor* (the sea-floor, the first interface); the
below has a designed *bottom region* (the strata, the birth-strata of
[`field-archaeology.md`](./field-archaeology.md) §2.4); the sky has a designed *top*
(the zenith, the second interface). **But the strata-pile has no designed *base*** —
nothing the descent ends against, nothing the rung-ladder's lowest terrace stands on.
The dive descends *through* the deep and stops because the hold is net-negative, not
because it has *reached the bottom* ([`deep-field-diving.md`](./deep-field-diving.md) §4
— a descent "is a hold, never a mint", the depth limit a coherence budget, not a floor).
The Bedrock is the corpus's answer: the **third interface** — the deep's own floor, the
immovable base that makes the window's vertical *bottom* legible the way the zenith makes
its *top* legible — and, by contrast with the deep's open descent, gives the deep its
own edge to read *against*.

The bedrock is **not a hidden bottom regime** — that is its fundamental honesty, mirrored
from the sea-floor's neutrality and the zenith's. The regions it sits under each publish
their own channels, and the bedrock reads them at its face:

- **The below's strata** is the bedrock's *upper side* — the rung-terraces the bell
  descends through ([`deep-field-diving.md`](./deep-field-diving.md) §3.1), the deep-rung
  matter whose lowest terrace is the bedrock's own face. The bedrock is where that
  descent *ends*.
- **The window's seed** is the bedrock's *crown* — the first matter the window condensed
  at the bottom of the birth-strata ([`resonance-seeds.md`](./resonance-seeds.md) §1.1);
  the bedrock is the settled ground that seed rests *on*. The bedrock is where the seed's
  deep rest *is*.

**The bedrock's own character — stated in one line and kept:** the Bedrock is **the
immovable substrate (the maximal-rung regime, the lowest terrace, the q-maximal region
no op can move), the precipitating floor (everything that precipitates precipitates onto
it), and the bottom of the story (the first matter, the deep's done order).** Three faces
of one floor; each grounded in a source doc's landed piece; each this doc's [design] over
that landed piece (§2).

## 2. The immovable substrate — the honest mechanics

### (a) The maximal-rung regime — no alloy's rung exceeds the bedrock's

**The landed piece:** [`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md) §4 fixes hardness:
**"Hardness is `n`, the rung depth. A deeper-rung material is more ordered (lower ε²) and
holds its φ-lock against a wider range of perturbation — that *is* hardness,
physically."** And the tool rule: **"Your tool's rung must meet the material's rung. A
coarse-rung pick perturbs the field at a coarse organization scale; against a deeper-rung
material that perturbation is below the material's decoherence-resistance — the material
does not scar, and the tool does nothing."** The regime ladder is a continuum of rung
depth `n`; every alloy a player forges is a point on it ([`the-tool.md`](./the-tool.md)
§3 — the worn-field's tuple realized as an alloy).

**The [design] over it — the bedrock is the maximal-rung regime, the ladder's flat
maximum:**

> **The Bedrock is the maximal-rung region of the ladder — the point beyond which `n`
> does not go, the highest decoherence-resistance the window holds.** Every regime a
> player can reach is a rung on the cascade ([`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md)
> §4; the hardness→rung constant mapping a Phase-1.5 tuning table, §7 Q5). The bedrock is
> the *base* of that ladder — **the deepest rung the ladder descends to, the regime whose
> `n` is greater than every alloy's.** Because hardness is rung and the tool's bite is its
> rung-depth ([`the-tool.md`](./the-tool.md) §2a), **no alloy's rung exceeds the bedrock's**:
> the deepest tool a player forges meets or falls short of the bedrock's rung, and where
> it falls short it does nothing — the material does not scar, the tool *skates*
> ([`the-tool.md`](./the-tool.md) §2a, the honest wall).

**[design]** — the *composition*. What the source docs land is the rung semantics and the
tool-material interaction as one law ("does this perturbation exceed this rung's
decoherence-resistance?" — [`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md) §4). What is
this doc's [design] is *that there is a maximal rung* — a deepest terrace beyond which the
window's ladder does not descend — and that the bedrock sits there. **This is
probe-calibrated** (§5b): the bedrock's rung, like every hardness→rung mapping, is a
Phase-1.5 measurement, not a stated number. The *semantics* — "the deepest rung is the
bedrock, and nothing exceeds it" — is the design; the *number* (which `n` the bedrock
holds) is measurement.

### (b) The tool's bite skates — the honest wall

**The landed piece:** [`the-tool.md`](./the-tool.md) §2a fixes the bite: **"A pick's bite
is its rung-depth. A tool whose rung meets or exceeds the material's rung perturbs the
field at that scale and *takes*… A tool whose rung falls short does nothing against a
deeper-rung material — the same condition as 'a diamond block does not burn with a coarse
fire': its coherence is too well-locked for a coarse attack."** This is the honest wall:
mining is a perturbation, and a perturbation below the material's decoherence-resistance
simply does not take.

**The [design] over it — the bedrock is the honest wall's flat maximum:**

> **The tool's bite skates on the Bedrock.** Because the bedrock is the maximal-rung
> regime (§2a) and a tool's bite is its rung-depth ([`the-tool.md`](./the-tool.md) §2a),
> **the deepest tool the window can forge is at best *rung-equal* to the bedrock, and no
> tool exceeds it** — so no tool's bite breaks the bedrock's lock. The tool skates: the
> swing perturbs at its own rung scale, the bedrock's decoherence-resistance is above
> it, and the material does not scar. **This is the honest wall, made absolute** — not a
> hidden unbreakable block, but the flat maximum of the same law that makes a shallow
> pick skate on deep matter: the bedrock is where that law *ends*.

**[design]** — the immovability is this doc's [design] over the landed bite law. What is
landed: the tool's rung must meet the material's rung, and a short tool skates
([`the-tool.md`](./the-tool.md) §2a; [`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md) §4).
What is this doc's [design] is that a *maximal-rung* region exists and is utterly beyond
every alloy — so the skate, which elsewhere is a "tool too shallow" read, is here
*permanent*. **The immovability is the honest read**: the same channels show the bedrock's
rung exceeding every alloy's — never a difficulty meter (see the honest boundary, §5a).

### (c) The diving bell's descent ends here — the deepest terrace has no below

**The landed pieces:** [`deep-field-diving.md`](./deep-field-diving.md) designs the
