## 1. The Observatory, stated once

The corpus's instrument family is **complete and uncomposed**. Each instrument
reads one real quantity in one idiom — the Weatherglass reads local `q`/`ε²` as
a lume (`field-instruments.md` §1.2); the Clock reads the local `(1−q)` tempo as
a hand (`the-clock.md` §1); the Mirror reads the player's own maintenance across
four faces (`the-mirror.md` §2); the map keeps a *record* of the field's
movement in the player's hand (`the-map.md` §1); the Bell answers when the stack
agrees (`the-bell.md` §1); the sky reads at scale (`atmosphere-orbits-auroras.md`).
But nothing reads the window **as one body**. A player wears a Weatherglass and
a Clock, but the *settlement* — the society the corpus gives a Board, a
Chronicle, a Festival, a Funeral, an Election — has no room where the whole stack
is read together, no place that is the Board's book made spatial.

> **The Observatory, stated once.** The composed instrument room — a
> settlement-scale chamber in the house-that-steers coherence architecture that
> reads the window's whole instrument stack together. It is the **seat of the
> society's reading**: the room you walk into to see the window as one body —
> the map aging on its wall with the field's movement, the clock turning with the
> local tempo, the glass's core holding the bath's health, the mirror's
> aggregated lume reading the settlement's own maintenance, the aurora's
> restlessness through the roof-oculus, and the Bell's tower standing over it
> all, the answered voice. **Where a settlement reads together — the Board's
> book made spatial.**

The one-line distinction from every sibling instrument — the Observatory is the
**composition** the family was always missing, the room rather than the wearer:

### 2.1 The Observatory composes the family, it never adds a channel

The family rule (`field-instruments.md` §2.1) is the room's whole architecture,
stated once: **every surface above is a consumer of a channel a family member
already publishes.** The map wall reads the map's own drawn-site bindings; the
clock above reads the Clock's `(1−q)`; the glass's core reads the Still-Room
locality; the mirror's lume reads the Mirror's channels at settlement scale; the
oculus reads the sky's published glow; the Bell reads the four channels'
coincidence. **The Observatory introduces no channel, no telemetry, no write.**
It is the settlement-scale mounting of reads that already exist — the 
Weatherglass family rule applied to a *room* instead of a player.

Concrete cost: each surface is one trilinear sample (or a bounded cluster) off
the ≈ 6 MiB publish (`corpus-reconciliation.md`), inside the ≈ 1–6 ms/tick server
sample budget — exactly the Weatherglass's own §1.4 cost, repeated per surface,
the same pattern the Board's aggregation slice (`shared-ledger.md` §6b) and the
Bell's first ring (`the-bell.md` §7) already prove Phase-1. The Observatory is
the cheapest possible *settlement* instrument because it is nothing but the
existing reads, mounted in one room.
