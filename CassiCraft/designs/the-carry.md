# The Carry: The Pack — the Designed Physical Weight a Body Carries, and Its Field-Cost

**Question under design:** what a body *carries as matter*, and what the load
*does to the body and the field* under it. The corpus designs the **burden**
([`the-burden.md`](./the-burden.md)) — the *carried ε²-debt*, a field-state the
player drags behind their practice; the **tool** ([`the-tool.md`](./the-tool.md))
— a single held work object; the **instrument family** ([`the-lantern.md`](./the-lantern.md))
— small worn reads; the **granary** ([`the-granary.md`](./the-granary.md)) — a
settlement's everyday store; and the **cart** ([`the-cart.md`](./the-cart.md)) —
a road's conveyance. But nothing designs **the physical carry**: the honest
weight-and-pack of what a body holds on the walk — a pack of tools, meals, a
lantern, a small store — distinct from the ε²-Burden (a carried field-debt) and
the tool (a single held object). The carry-cap and "what the player carries"
surface implicitly in the instruments (the Weatherglass worn, the tool held), but
no doc designs the *carrying economy* — how much a walker, a climber, or a camper
holds, and how the load *rides the gradient* and reads on the field. This document
is that design:

> **The Carry is the designed physical pack — the honest weight a body carries
> and its field-cost.** It is the corpus's inventory-primitive — not a
> slot-system with an invisible cap, but a **field-read weight**: a pack of
> tools, meals, a lantern, a small store that a walker, a climber, or a camper
> holds, bound to how the load rides the gradient. It is carried *matter* — never
> a debt, never a hidden meter — and a heavy carry *feels* in the field: it slows
> the walk's footing, taxes a climb, fights a swim, and reads on the Life-Signal
> as a *heavier live-lock* — a pack is legible to the field, a small extra
> signature a Coda could find. The honest cost: **the carry is carried matter,
> never free — the weight is real, the field reads it, and the choice of how much
> you carry is the corpus's first inventory economics.**

## 1. The Carry, stated once

The corpus's inventory surfaces are all *one thing held in the hand*. The tool
is a single work object (`the-tool.md` §1 — "the primitive work object, the rung
in your hand"). The instrument family are small worn reads (`the-lantern.md` §1 —
the carried night-read). The granary is a *settlement's* store (`the-granary.md`
§1 — the everyday order-vault held against the lean). The cart is a *road's*
conveyance (`the-cart.md` §1 — the wain that rides the highway's gradient). **None
is the pack — the honest weight a single body carries on the walk.** The corps'
closest primitive, the Burden, is a carried *field-debt* (the ε² the player vented,
accumulated on the player as a decaying signature, `the-burden.md` §1) — and it is
explicitly **not matter**. This doc's gap: what *matter* a body carries, and the
load's cost. This is that design.

> **The Carry, stated once.** The designed physical pack — the honest weight a
> body carries and its field-cost. It is the corpus's inventory-primitive, but it
> is **not a slot-system**: there is no grid of invisible inventory cells, no
> hidden carry-cap meter. It is a **field-read weight**: a pack of tools, meals,
> a lantern, a small store that a walker, a climber, or a camper holds, and the
> field reads that load — slows the walk's footing, taxes a climb, fights a swim,
> and reads on the Life-Signal as a heavier live-lock. The load is **carried
> matter**, bound to how it rides the gradient. A light carry moves almost freely;
> a heavy carry is a real, legible, costed weight — the honest price of carrying
> what you need on the walk.

## 2. The pack — the honest mechanics

The Carry is carried **matter** — the designed weight of the objects a body
holds, each grounded in its source doc's real quantity. **The carry's form — its
cap's size, its weight's book — is this doc's [design]**, probe-calibrated. Four
kinds of carried matter, each a landed piece composed into the pack:

| What the pack holds | Its source | The landed quantity it composes | The pack's design over it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The tool** | `the-tool.md` §2a | the single held work object — the rung-matched bite: a deep-rung tool is heavier under the river law (`the-walk.md` §3, cited via the tool's cross-ref) | the pack carries the tool the hand will take up; a deep-rung, heavy tool is a heavier carried weight (§3). |
| **The small store** | `the-granary.md` §2/§6 | the everyday order carried — at body-scale the granary's store drawn as the walker's cell; the store's bleed (`(1−q)` of holding, `energy-harnessing.md` §2) | the pack is a **mini-granary**: a few meals, a lantern, a little store, held at the `(1−q)` cost of holding it (the pack bleeds like any held structure). |
| **The worn reads** | `the-lantern.md` §1 | the small instruments — the carried night-read, the pocketable sibling of the Weatherglass | the pack carries the lantern and the small reads the body does not wear; the pack is the instrument family's *travel* hold. |
| **The meals** | `the-cooked-field.md` §2 | the food carried — a dish as a composed regime, edible stored coherence | the pack is the **walking pantry** of a day's meals — the food that feeds the walker's `B` on the crossing. |

**The debt-vs-matter line, drawn once.** The Burden is the carried *ε²-debt*
(`the-burden.md` §1 — "a damaged-locality-you-drag-with-you," a faint
accumulated-ε² field, legible on the deliberate-vent axis, cleared cheaply in a
Still Room's patient time). **The Carry is carried *matter* — the objects
themselves.** The distinction is the design's spine:

> **The Burden is what your practice leaves on you; the Carry is what you choose
> to hold.** The Burden is a field-debt that *decays* in coherent rest
> (`the-burden.md` §2b — cleared in a Still Room); the Carry is matter that *rides*
> until set down (`the-granary.md` §6's bleed is the cost of holding it, never a
> decay). A heavy channeler carries both — the Burden of their practice and the
> Carry of their pack — but they are two different weights: one sheds, one rides;
> one is a debt, one is a choice. **Both are real, never free** — the Burden
> costs compounding risk (`the-burden.md` §4), the Carry costs the load's feel
> (§3 of this doc) — but a player who reads one never mistakes the other, because
> the Burden reads on the deliberate-vent axis (`life-signal.md` §3.3) and the
> Carry reads on the maintenance axis as a heavier live-lock (§3).

**The pack's form — the cap's size, the weight's book ([design]).** The concrete
"how much can a body carry" — the pack's cap's size — is **this doc's [design],
probe-calibrated**, never asserted:
- **The cap's size is a field-read weight, not a slot count.** A body can carry a
  small, ordinary load (a tool, a meal, a lantern) nearly without cost — the load
  rides lightly (§3's light-carry floor). A body that packs more — a deep-rung
  tool, a granary-drawn store, several meals — carries a heavier weight: the stride
  slows, the stroke dearers, the maintenance cadence changes. **[design]** The
  *boundary* — how much carried matter reads as "light" vs "heavy" — is a designed
  threshold over the published channels, probe-calibrated against the same
  living-terrain demo the walk (§5) and the swim (§5) calibrate their dials on.
- **The weight's book is the op-record.** What is carried books as a **held item**,
  the way any op does (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1 — the settled record
  `{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude, sustain}`). A load is a Q4 op of that
  shape — a `sustain`-flagged held record whose `magnitude` is the carried matter's
  booked weight, never a phantom "carry" record off the stream. The pack's book is
  the same single-writer, non-forgeable point every op uses (§4a).

**[design]** The pack's *composition* — how the tool, the store, the lantern, and
the meals read as one carried `magnitude` on the body — is this doc's designed
surface over the landed pieces: each held item contributes its own bookable
magnitude, and the pack sums them into the load the field reads. The *weight*, the
*cap*, and the *load's cost* are probe-calibrated (§5b), never asserted; what is
landed is the tool's bite, the store's bleed, the lantern's read, and the meals'
feed, each a real quantity already in the corpus.

## 3. The load's feel — the load-bearing design

A heavy carry **feels in the field**. The load is not a hidden inventory meter
behind a screen; it is a *designed weight the field reads* — it slows the walk's
footing, taxes a climb, fights a swim, and weighs on the body's maintenance.
Each feel is grounded in a source doc's real quantity; the *load's cost mapping* —
how much carried matter reads on each — is this doc's [design], probe-calibrated.

### (a) It slows the walk's footing

`the-walk.md` §2 establishes the stride-cost — the gradient at foot-pace, the
thin's dearness, the carried weight that sheds into the stride; §3 is explicit that
*a laden walk is a dear walk* ("the more carried ε² (the Burden), the more each
step sheds into the stride"). The Carry is the *matter* form of that same weight:

> **A heavy carry slows the walk's footing.** The same carried weight that makes
> a laden crosser's stride dearer (`the-walk.md` §3 — the caught weight sheds into
> the stride) applies to the Carry's matter: a body carrying a deep-rung tool, a
> granary-drawn store, and several meals walks a dearer, slower crossing — the
> stride-cost read at the pack's load. A light carry rides nearly at the walk's
> cheap floor (`the-walk.md` §1.2 — the ungated primitive); a heavy carry taxes the
> footing the way crossing thin does (`the-walk.md` §2b) — real, felt, legible on
> the published `ε²`/`(1−q)` the walker already sheds.

**[design]** — the *load-cost mapping* at the pack (how the carried `magnitude`
weights each stride) is this doc's designed surface over `the-walk.md` §2/§3's
carried-weight read, probe-calibrated. The walked direction is landed (a laden walk
is a dear walk); the pack's exact stride-slow is tuned against the same living-
terrain demo the walk calibrates on.

### (b) It taxes a climb (the gradient's cost at load)

A climb is the walk's descent inverted — **carrying *up* the `∇(g·Φ)` gradient the
walk rides down`. `the-walk.md` §2a is explicit: walking a ridge is cheap going
*downhill* along `∇(g·Φ)`; a step *against* the lean labors. A carry's load rides
the same gradient:

> **Carrying up the gradient is the walk's against-the-lean dearness, weighed.**
> The load that rides cheaply down a descent (`the-walk.md` §2a — the field does
> the downhill work) is the same load that costs dearly climbing back — the
> against-the-lean labor (`the-walk.md` §2b) scaled by the pack's weight. A heavy
> pack on a climb is the honest price of taking a load where the gradient resists
> it. **[design, noted honestly]** — this doc's `the-climb` cross-read now **lands** (the-climb is on disk as of wave
> 41); the climb's §4.2/§5d cost-at-load is now read two-way — the pack's
> against-the-lean climb-tax composes the walk's gradient logic **and** grounds on
> the climb's landed no-free-energy cap, no longer a floating [design]. What is
> landed is the against-the-lean labor of `the-walk.md`
> §2a/§2b; the pack's climb-tax composes that, [design]-flagged.

### (c) It fights a swim

`the-swim.md` §2a establishes the stroke's cost — the water's `(1−q)` drag, the
expensive walk; §4 is explicit that *carrying little* is the swimmer's craft ("a
laden swimmer spends more — carried ε² sheds into each stroke"). The Carry is that
laden-ness in matter form:

> **The pack fights the swim.** A body carrying a heavy pack strokes against the
> water's `(1−q)` with the pack's weight on every stroke — the laden stroke of
> `the-swim.md` §4 ("a laden swimmer spends more"), with the Carry's matter added
> to the carried ε² the stroke already sheds. A swimmer who sheds the pack (a
> deliberate choice to leave the load ashore) swims cheaply; a swimmer who crosses
> with a full pack pays the drag for every stroke it holds. The swim is the walk's
> fluid form (`the-swim.md` §1) — so the load's cost on the stroke is the load's
> cost on the stride, re-read in the water.

**[design]** — the *stroke's load-cost* (how the pack's weight weighs each stroke)
is this doc's designed surface over `the-swim.md` §2a/§4's carried-weight read,
probe-calibrated on the Phase-1 water patch.

### (d) It reads on the Life-Signal as a heavier live-lock

`life-signal.md` §3 establishes the maintenance axis — a live thing *actively
holds* its φ-lock against a drain, and the classifier reads the classes off motion,
`(1−q)` cadence, `ε²` gradient, and `∇(g·Φ)` alignment. **The Carry's most
distinctive read is here**:

> **A loaded body's maintenance cadence differs — a pack is legible to the field,
> a heavier live-lock.** The Life-Signal's maintained-live-coherence class
> (`life-signal.md` §3.1) reads a live body by its `(1−q)` cadence — the glow
> *pulses* at the maintainer's rhythm. A body carrying a heavy pack is a heavier
> live body: it maintains the same lock, but at a dearer, heavier cadence — the
> pack is carried matter the body must hold up against the field's drain, so its
> maintenance pulse reads *heavier* than an unloaded body's (§3.1's pulsing cadence,
> with the pack's weight in it). **A pack is not a wound and not a vent — it is a
> *heavier live-lock*: the same maintained-live class, read at a larger, slower,
> more effortful cadence.** This is the Load's legibility: a Coda or a reader that
> can tell "maintained live" can also tell *how heavy* the maintenance is (§3.2 —
> the maintenance window, not a snapshot).

**[design]** — the *heavier-cadence read* (that a carried load reads on the
maintenance axis as a heavier live-lock, distinct from the deliberate-vent distortion
of the Burden) is this doc's designed surface over `life-signal.md` §3's maintenance
axis, probe-calibrated against the §6 N2 probe's noise floor. The distinguishing
line is: **the Burden distorts the *deliberate-vent* axis** (`life-signal.md`
§3.3); **the Carry weighs the *maintenance* axis** — two separate registers, neither
a hidden meter.

## 4. The honest economics — the carry is carried matter, never free

The load-bearing honesty, stated once and never blurred:

> **The carry is carried matter, never free.** The weight is a **real cost on
> every movement** — the walk's stride (§3a), the swim's stroke (§3c), the climb's
> labor (§3b), and the fall's weight: **a heavy pack's fall is heavier**
> (`the-fall.md` §2 — the gradient owns a free fall; a loaded body's fall arrives
> with the pack's weight added to the impact's coherence shock). It is **never a
> slot-system with an invisible cap** — the load is read from the field, felt on
> the body, and legible on the instruments (§5e). **The choice of how much you
> carry is the corpus's first inventory economics** — a deliberate, field-read,
> costed choice, never a hidden meter.

Three honest faces of the economics, each grounded:

| The economics' face | What it costs | The source-doc ground | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The weight is real on every movement** | the walk's stride (§3a), the swim's stroke (§3c), the climb's labor (§3b), the fall's heavier impact (`the-fall.md` §2) | the-stride-cost (the-walk §2/§3), the-stroke (the-swim §2a/§4), the-gradient (the-walk §2a), the-fall (§2) | `the-walk.md`, `the-swim.md`, `the-fall.md` |
| **The load is a field-read, not a hidden meter** | the maintenance cadence's heavier live-lock (§3d), legible to the Life-Signal and the instruments | life-signal §3, field-instruments §2.1 | `life-signal.md`, `field-instruments.md` |
| **The choice is the first inventory economics** | the deliberate "carry light / carry heavy" decision, costed in the load's feel | the walker's four-decisions craft (the-walk §3); the design's own spine | this doc §4 |

**The no-free-energy cap — a carry converts nothing.** `energy-harnessing.md` §6's
hard rule — **no conversion yields more than it sinks, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`** —
holds the Carry without exception, stated in its most personal register:

> **A carry converts nothing — there is no pack that yields.** The pack is carried
> *matter*, never a gain: it does not generate coherence, `ε²`, or movement; it
> cannot be turned into stored power, a charge for a capacitor, or a "carry-mint."
> The load's cost is real, never a source — a heavy pack slows the walk (§3a) and
> fights the swim (§3c) as *costs*, never as the raw of a harvest. **The heavy
> live-lock is a cost, never a gain** — a loaded body's maintenance is dearer, not
> richer. **And the weight cannot be farmed into a carry-mint** — there is no
> "pack the deep-rung tool to synthesize coherence," no "load a pack to charge a
> battery by walking," no way the load's cost becomes a yield. The pack is the
> corpus's first inventory *economics* precisely because it is the first honest
> one: a deliberate weight, costed, never free, never minted.

Three concrete guards, all inherited:
1. **No pack that yields.** The load is carried matter, never a gain — `output ≤
   φ⁻¹·input` bounds any use of the load; no slider makes a heavy pack a net mint.
2. **No shedding into a harvestable drain.** The pack's `(1−q)` bleed (the holding
   cost, `the-granary.md` §6) is a held structure's honest cost, never a waste a
   collector taps — the same refusal that closes Burden-farming (`the-burden.md`
   §4) and Coda-farming (`signature-predator.md` §7e).
3. **The pack is never a travel-mint.** A light carry is *cheap movement*, not free
   movement for nothing — the load's honesty is that a light pack moves lightly and
   a heavy pack moves heavily, and neither is a way to get transport the field did
   not cost.

**The first inventory economics, stated once.** This is the corpus's first
inventory because the corpus has no inventory at all until now — the tool is a
single held object, the granary is a settlement's store, the cart is a road's
vehicle. **The Carry is the first thing at the body's own scale that forces the
choice: how much do I carry?** It is deliberate (a chosen pack, not a hidden grid),
field-read (the load is legible on the instruments), and costed (the weight's feel
is real). That three-part honesty — deliberate, read, costed — is the design's
economic spine, and it is what makes the Carry the inventory-primitive rather than
another slot-system.

### (c) Determinism — a hard gate

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `life-signal.md` §6d; `the-walk.md` §4c;
`the-cart.md` §5c). The Carry inherits it directly:

> **Same load, same field state → same carry.** The load's cost (the stride-slow,
> the stroke-dearer, the cadence-heavier) is a **pure function of the held items'
> booked magnitude and the published field state at the player's position** over
> its reading window — no hidden randomness, no player-relative variance, **never a
> seeded roll**. A player who re-carries the same pack over the same descent at the
> same field state gets the same stride-slow, the same cadence-heavier, the same
> load's feel. **This is a hard gate**, not a nice-to-have: the weight's cost is
> deterministic, so "this pack makes this crossing dear and this maintenance
> heavier" is a *true, learnable* fact about the field — reproducible and teachable,
> exactly as the walk's stride and the cart's ride are deterministic.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — a carry converts nothing

§4b. Held as a hard gate: **no pack that yields** — the carry is carried matter,
never a gain; the weight is real, never free; the heavier live-lock is a cost,
never a gain; the load cannot be farmed into a carry-mint. `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`
(`energy-harnessing.md` §6) bounds any use of the load; no dial makes a heavy pack
a net mint.

### (e) Accessibility — the carry's load and its cost readable from the instruments, never hidden-only

never-hidden-only discipline (`the-granary.md` §6e; `the-cart.md` §5e;

## 6. Feasibility verdict

**PHASE-1-ABLE — the pack, the corpus's inventory-primitive.**

The corpus designs the burden (the carried ε²-debt), the tool (a single held
object), the instrument family (worn small reads), and the granary (a
settlement's store) — but **nothing designs the physical pack: the honest weight a
body carries on the walk, and its field-cost**. The Carry is that missing
primitive: a pack of tools, meals, a lantern, a small store, held as **carried
matter** — distinct from the ε²-Burden (a field-debt), the tool (one held object),
and the worn-fields (garments) — and it is Phase-1-able because its whole design
is a **bounded consumer of the already-published channels + a bounded Q4 write**.

**The Phase-1 slice (the first pack):** on the living-terrain demo, **carry a
light and a heavy pack a real stride and read the load's cost and the heavier
cadence** — a pure consumer of the publish (the `q`/`ε²`/`(1−q)`/`∇(g·Φ)` at the
player's position, one-to-few samples inside the ≈ 1–6 ms/tick budget) plus the
held load's Q4 book, proving the load-bearing claim (*carried matter is a real,
legible, costed weight the field reads*) before any full inventory UI exists. It
de-risks the walk's stride-cost, the swim's stroke-cost, and the Life-Signal's
maintenance axis by measuring the same engine-real `∇(g·Φ)` and `(1−q)` with a real
carried weight on the body, first.

**The gates, all held (§5):** (a) the [design]/engine-real line — the stride-cost,
the stroke, the burden's debt, the tool, the granary's store, the maintenance axis,
and the trail-read are their source docs' landed pieces; **the carry's composition
— the pack's form, the load's feel, the honest economics — is this doc's [design]**
over them; (b) **PHASE-1-ABLE** — a carried-weight read bound to the published
channels as a load-cost and a heavier live-cadence is a consumer + Q4 write, with
the first-pack slice stated; (c) determinism — same load, same field state → same
carry, a hard gate, the weight's cost deterministic, never a seeded roll; (d) the
no-free-energy cap — a carry converts nothing, no pack that yields, the heavier
live-lock is a cost, never a gain; (e) accessibility — the carry's load and its
cost readable from the instruments, never hidden-only.

**Binding risks, in order:**
1. **The load-cost calibration (§3/§5b).** Does the pack's weight read *felt* and
   *distinguishable* — a heavy pack's stride measurably slower and its cadence
   measurably heavier than a light pack's, distinguishable from a flat, unloaded
   walk — or does the engineering reality wash the weight's spread out at
   foot-pace/stride scale? The §5b first-pack slice is the pre-registered probe
   that answers it; a flat read pushes the Carry to pure presentation (the load
   read, honestly, as the single channel it is), still a legitimate finding — the
   same honest outcome the walk, the swim, and the tool pre-register for their
   calibrations.
2. **The heavy-cadence read's separation from the Burden (§3d).** The Carry reads
   on the maintenance axis (a heavier live-lock); the Burden reads on the
   deliberate-vent axis (`life-signal.md` §3.3). The Life-Signal's §6 N2 probe must
   confirm the two registers separate at the noise floor — a heavy practitioner who
   also carries a heavy pack should read as heavier-maintained *and* heavier-loaded,
   two distinct registers, never a merged "loud." Inherits `life-signal.md` §6's
   probe discipline. **[probe]/[design]**
3. **The climb's landed arrival (§3b/§7 open-Q2).** The climb's specific cost-at-load
   now **has a landed source** (`the-climb.md` is on disk as of wave 41) — this
   doc's climb-tax composes over the walk's against-the-lean gradient logic **and**
   the climb's landed §1/§5d no-free-energy cap. Should a future climb revision
   alter its mechanics, this doc's §3b must re-read it; until then the two-way is
   landed, not flagged.
4. **The pack's book vs. the slot-inventory temptation (§4/§5e).** The design's
   whole point is that the carry is a field-read weight, never a hidden-cap slot
   system. The risk is an implementation that reaches for a familiar inventory
   grid and loses the field-read honesty; the guard is the accessibility floor
   (§5e) and the deterministic load-cost (§5c) — the load must read on the body and
   the field, never in a private counter.
5. **The no-free-energy cap held against "pack to earn" (§4b).** A player must not
   read a heavy pack as a way to *gain* (a load that charges a battery by walking,
   a laden body that reads as richer). The guard is the cap's plain register: the
   heavier live-lock is a cost, never a gain; carried matter converts nothing.

**None contradicts the async, dual-world, or regime-collapse architecture** — the
Carry adds no physics and no channel: it is a designed field-read and a bounded
write over the load's real `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)`, the booked held-item magnitude
through the settled op-record, and the maintenance/trail reads the Life-Signal and
the signature-predator already land — a consumer of the publish and a Q4 write
through the existing lane, the pack the corpus's carried-matter gap was always
missing.

> **The honest statement that makes this doc load-bearing: the corpus designs the
> burden (the carried ε²-debt), the tool (a single held object), the worn reads
> (the small instruments), and the granary (a settlement's store) — but nothing
> designs the physical pack, the honest weight a body carries on the walk, and its
> field-cost.** The Carry is that missing inventory-primitive: a pack of tools,
> meals, a lantern, a small store, held as *carried matter* — the corpus's first

> the walk's footing, taxes a climb, fights a swim, and reads on the Life-Signal as
> a heavier live-lock — a pack is legible to the field, a small extra signature a
> Coda could find. And its honesty is absolute: **the carry is carried matter,
> never free — the weight is real on every movement, the load is field-read never
> hidden, the choice of how much you carry is the corpus's first inventory

> bounded consumer of the already-published channels plus the held load's Q4 book —

> corpus's inventory-primitive, now designed.

   Inherits the trail-read's Phase-1 slice (`signature-predator.md` §8) and the
