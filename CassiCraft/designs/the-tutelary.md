## 2. The bond — the honest mechanics

### 2.1 What the bond IS — the family's Π-anchor made a watcher

The corpus's identity stack has already made two holdings that a line-attached
keeper needs. The **Family** is a shared persistent-Π anchor — a designed joint
hold where member-lines deliberately co-bind their carried patterns around a
common anchor (a hearth, a named structure, a bound pair of lines), held by the
members' joint maintenance (`the-family.md` §1/§2.1). The **Guardian** is the
same persistent-Π holding made a *living keeper of a place* — the name's holding
at the being's scale, bound to a named valley or cave's mouth (`the-guardian.md`
§1/§2). The Tutelary composes the two:

> **[design] The Tutelary's bond is the family's persistent-Π anchor made a
> watcher — the joint hold the family maintains, read as an attached, following
> presence.** Where a Guardian binds the name's holding to a *place* (`the-guardian.md`
> §1), the Tutelary binds the *family's* holding to the *line itself*: the same
> persistent-Π structure the family holds at the hearth (`the-family.md` §2.1)
> becomes a living keeper that shadows the members, not the hearth. The bond is
> not a new pattern the engine writes; it is the family's own joint hold — the
> anchor the members maintain together — read as a *watcher*. A family that
> already holds a hearth has, in the field's own terms, a persistent-Π pattern at
> N>1 over its members' carried patterns; the tutelary is that pattern given a
> keeper's following form.

The bond's *form* — the attachment, the following — is this doc's [design] over
the landed pieces, probe-calibrated (`§5`). The *holding* it rides is the family's

## Open questions

1. **The attachment's scope — which lines may seal a keeper (§2.2/§5b).** The two
   attachments resolved here are the birth-read (a fresh line's first lock,
   `the-child.md` §2) and the name's seal (a true name, `the-name.md` §1 — the
   family's anchor extended to a jointly-held thing, `the-family.md` §2.2). The
   honest rule mirrors `the-name.md` §5.1 and `the-family.md` §2: a tutelary seals
   where the field can be said to *hold a line as a persistent structure* — a family
   with a maintained anchor. Whether an *un-named pair-bond* (an apprenticeship's
   N=2 working, `the-apprenticeship.md` §2.1, with no hearth) or a *settlement-wide
   line* (a commons' continuous claim rather than a family) can seal a keeper is a
   [design] dial against the plausible Π-anchors. **[design]**
2. **The keeper's morphology — what the shadow *is* (§2/§7).** Is the tutelary an
   organism-class in the strict ecology sense (a run the field holds, that dissolves
   when the run ends — inherits `field-emergent-ecology.md` §1.4's P4) or a *bound
   structure* — the family's anchor made a following presence, more like the
   guardian's bound structure than a wild creature? The honest default leans with
   the guardian: the tutelary is the family's *anchored* keeper — its "life" is the
   line's held order, not a wandering morphology — but whether it persists and sheds
   only when its line ends (§3.2), or "lives" and "dissolves" like an organism, is
   this doc's [design] fork, probe-calibrated against the family's and guardian's
   P4 verdicts. **[design]/[probe]**
3. **The anchor's maintenance floor (§2.1/§4.1).** Is the tutelary's watch
   *absolute* — any line that holds its anchor truly and maintains it always keeps a
   following keeper — or *gated* on the anchor's maintained order exceeding some
   floor (a marginal family too strained to host a shadow's watch, so its steadiness
   is the members' own, tutelary-less)? The former is the clean bound keeper (the
   keeping is the family's own order held); the latter is a stronger, more honest
   read (a rearguard line too strained to keep a keeper). Needs the placed-order
   measurement (`the-family.md` §5b) to decide. **[design]/[probe]**
4. **The exile's shadow across the line's return (§4.3).** When an exiled line
   re-earns its place by the book (`the-exile.md` §4.2; `schema-that-settles.md`
   §2.1.1 — no amnesty), does the shadow that followed it out return *with it* — the
   bond re-sealed by the line's re-earning, the keeper re-admitted as the line is —
   or does the exile's shadow shed with the severed line, and a returned line starts
   keeper-less, re-earning a new seal by a *new* act? The no-amnesty rule says the
   drain is never erased; whether the shadow's bond also re-binds on return, or
   sheds with the severance and must be re-sealed, is the sharpest [design] fork.
   **[design]**
5. **The shadow's visibility and the danger layer (§4.4).** A moving personal-order
   band is a trail-read target (`signature-predator.md` §2) — but does the shadow's
   own legibility mean a Coda can accumulate on the *shadow's* clean band in the
   margins (a tutelary crossing a desert is itself a bright organized thing, drawing
   the predator toward its charge), or does the shadow's band read as the line's own
   held order, inseparable from the charge it shadows? The former is the honest,
   dangerous reading (the guard's presence is a cost, not a cloak); the latter
   the bodyguard-safety reading the no-free-energy cap (§5d) refuses. Needs the
   trail-legibility read (`signature-predator.md` §8) extended to the shadow. **[design]/[probe]**
