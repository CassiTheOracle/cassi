# The Climb: The Designed Ascent — the Body Gaining Height Deliberately Against ∇(g·Φ)

**Question under design:** the corpus designs the vertical's downward faces and its
ceiling, but never the *gaining-height body-act* that makes a height reachable. It
designs the **fall** ([`the-fall.md`](./the-fall.md) — the unguided descent, the one
movement with no skill-buff, where the ∇(g·Φ) gradient *owns* you), the **walk**
([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) — the un-roaded crossing *across* the ground, the
gradient at foot-pace), and the **zenith** ([`the-zenith.md`](./the-zenith.md) — the
vertical's ceiling, the *place* at the top that completes the window's vertical). But
nothing designs **going up guided**: the body deliberately gaining height against the
gradient each hand is the vertical's missing *upward primitive* — the act by which
the zenith, a tall house, a cliff, a mountain's height are ever *reached* by the body
rather than descended into or read from below. This document is that design: **the
Climb is the designed ascent — the body gaining height deliberately against ∇(g·Φ),
the fall's controlled-inverse, the vertical's missing upward primitive.**

The corpus's movement stack has the walk (ground), the swim (in-water), the fall
(unguided drop), the voyage (between windows) — and its vertical has a designed
*ceiling*. What it lacks is the one act the stack's "up" presupposes: every tall
house, cliff, or mountain the corpus sends the player toward is *reached*, and the
reaching is undesigned. The Climb is that designed reach — a move on the same
gradient the walk rides and the fall obeys bare, but *worked*, not ridden and not
obeyed. Distinct from the walk (which traverses *ground*), distinct from the zenith's
read (which is a *place*), the Climb is the *act* of gaining height.

## 1. The Climb, stated once

The corpus designs the vertical in its convenience and its honesty. The fall
([`the-fall.md`](./the-fall.md) §1) designs the descent with no craft — where the
gradient *owns* the body absolutely. The walk ([`the-walk.md`](./the-walk.md) §1)
designs the un-roaded crossing *across* the ground — the gradient at foot-pace, cheap
downhill, dear against the grain. The zenith ([`the-zenith.md`](./the-zenith.md) §1)
designs the vertical's *ceiling* — the place at the top that completes the window's
vertical. **None of them designs going *up* guided.** The walk traverses ground. The
fall descends unguided. The zenith is reached "the way any height is reached" — an
ellipsis the corpus has never filled with an act. The Climb is that missing act,
stated once and kept:

> **The Climb is the designed ascent — the body gaining height deliberately against
> ∇(g·Φ).** It is the fall's **controlled-inverse**: where the fall is the gradient
> *owning* you (the-fall §1), a climb is you *working* the gradient — each handhold a
> small re-lock against the up-slope, the body converting the gradient's pull into
> deliberate, held gains. It is the walk's **vertical-twin**: the same river-law haul
> the walk rides cheaply downhill (§2a), taken *against* the body at hand-pace in
> place of foot-pace. It is **the vertical's missing upward primitive** — the act by
> which the zenith, a tall house, a cliff, a mountain's height are ever *reached* by
> the body rather than only descended into or read from below.
>
> Its honest field-cost is that **climbing fights gravity**: a steep ∇(g·Φ) taxes the
> body's carried field more per stride than walking, and a climb with a pack taxes
> more (§4). And its load-bearing design is that **a climb's handholds are a read**: a
> coherent cliff-face gives good holds and a clean climb; a scarred or thin face gives
> bad holds and wastes `(1−q)` — you can read a cliff's height-feasibility before you
> commit to it (§3). A climb converts nothing — the height is the body's own work,
> never a gain (§4-d). It is the vertical's missing up: the designed reach that a
> fall's inverse and a walk's twin make the same law's third face.

**Why this is the vertical's missing upward primitive, honestly.** Every face of the
vertical now has a designed act or a designed place: the fall is the descent's
honesty (the-fall §1), the walk is the ground's crossing (the-walk §1), the sea the
middle's carrying surface, the zenith the sky's top (the-zenith §1), the below the
strata. **The upward face has neither**: nothing in the corpus moves the body *up*
against the gradient as a designed act. The zenith is a *place* — "the act by which
its height is reached" is exactly the sentence every doc that sends the player toward
a height writes around without designing. A tall house, a cliff, a mountain's summit:
each is *reached*, and the reaching is this doc's gap. **The Climb names the act the
vertical's "up" has been waiting for** — the fall's controlled-inverse, the walk's
vertical-twin, the vertical's missing upward primitive, now designed.

## 2. The ascent — the honest mechanics

### 2.1 The fall's controlled-inverse — you work the gradient

The fall's own mechanics establish the gradient's absolute ownership. The river law
`a = −G_N·(π/ρ)·∇(g·Φ)` is the corpus's canonical per-entity steer
(`corpus-reconciliation.md`; `chunk-field-quantization.md` §2.1, ≈ 40 ns/entity), and
a fall is that law "owned absolutely — with no body, line, or shell between the
gradient and the velocity" (the-fall §2.1). Where the walk *reads* the gradient and
steps with it, and the road *routes* it along a held line, a fall *obeys* it without
mediation.

**The Climb is the exact inverse, taken from the fall's own words:** where the fall
is *the gradient owning you*, the climb is *you working the gradient*.

> **A climb converts the gradient's pull into deliberate, held gains instead of an
> unmediated descent.** Each handhold is a **small re-lock against the up-slope**: the
> body catches a coherent feature of the face, holds its φ-lock against the gradient
> for a beat, and translates that held lock upward before the gradient re-asserts. The
> same `∇(g·Φ)` that would accelerate a fall bare is, in a climb, *resisted stride by
> stride* — not refused (there is no counter-term a climber owns mid-air, the-fall
> §4.2), but worked: the fall's absolute obedience is met by a series of small,
> deliberate, maintained re-locks. Where a fall spends no `(1−q)` mid-air (the-fall
> §2.2), a climb *spends* it — each re-lock sheds the waste the gradient's pull
> demands against the body, and that waste is the climb's living cost (§4).

**[design]** — the fall's *unresisted obedience* is landed (the-fall §2.1). That the
climb is the *designed reading* of the same law with a working counter-term — the
held re-lock that converts descent's pull into ascent's gain — is this doc's
composition, probe-calibrated (§5b). The law is untouched; the climb is the designed
inverse reading over it.

### 2.2 The walk's vertical-twin — the gradient at hand-pace in place of foot-pace

The walk establishes the stride-cost at foot-pace: walking a ridge is cheap going
downhill along `∇(g·Φ)` — the field doing the work — and dear crossing against it
(the-walk §2a/§2b). The walker *reads* where the field is cheap to cross; "a step
with the lean is cheap; a step against it labors" (the-walk §2a).

**The Climb is the same gradient at the same stride-discipline, taken *against* the
body and *upward*,** so the honest ground is the walk's own §2a inversion:

> **Where the walk cheap-rides a descent along ∇(g·Φ), the climb *labors* an ascent
> against it — the walk's stride-cost mapping, applied with the gradient reversed.**
> The walker's stride reads the gradient and the thin to weight each step (the-walk
> §4a, the stride-cost mapping); the climber reads the same `∇(g·Φ)`, `q`, and `ε²`
> to weight each *hold* — with the sign of the work flipped. A face aligned along the
> gradient's grain (a ridge the walk descends cheaply) is the *hardest* to climb, and
> a face that offers ledges against the steep pull is the *workable* one. The walk
> trades speed for freedom (the-walk §1.2); the climb trades the ground's freedom for
> the vertical's reach — same stride, hand-pace, inverted gradient.

This is the honest vertical-twin: **the climb is the walk's descent inverted upward** —
the same stride-cost read the walk builds its crossing on, applied at hand-pace
against the gradient. The *stride* here is the climb's form (its pace, its holds),
and that form is this doc's [design] over the landed gradient + the material read,
probe-calibrated (§5b).

### 2.3 Distinct from the zenith's read — the act, not the place

The zenith is the vertical's *ceiling* — a thin, structured edge, a *place* with a
readable drain and a boundary, "the window's own topmost read" (the-zenith §1/§2b).
The zenith is *reached* — the corpus never designs how. **The climb is the reaching.**

> **The zenith is a *place*; the climb is the *act* of reaching it.** The zenith names
> the top the sky ends at; the climb names the deliberate, costed, readable ascent
> that gets a body to it. A climb that tops out on a cliff reads the zenith's boundary
> from the near side; a climb that never reaches the ceiling is still a climb — a
> designed ascent against the gradient — while the zenith is defined without any
> body ever ascending to it. The place is landed (the-zenith §1); the act that reaches
> it is this doc's design.

The distinction is load-bearing for the corpus's movement stack: the fall, the walk,
the swim, the voyage are *acts*; the zenith is a *place*. The vertical had an act down
(the fall), a crossing across (the walk), but no act *up*. **The Climb is that act.**

## 3. The handholds as a read — a coherent face gives good holds, a scarred or thin face wastes `(1−q)`

### 3.1 Hardness-as-rung at the escarpment — a coherent face gives good holds

`material-regimes.md` §4 establishes hardness as rung depth: "a deeper-rung material
is more ordered (lower `ε²`) and holds its φ-lock against a wider range of
perturbation — that *is* hardness." A deep-`n` face (a coherent, high-`q`, low-`ε²`
cliff) resists the perturbation a body's grip applies, so its holds hold. **The
climb's handholds are a read of that rung structure, at the escarpment:**

> **A coherent cliff-face — high-`q`, deep-rung, low-`ε²` (material-regimes §4, a
> hardness-as-rung face) — gives good holds and a clean climb.** Each hold is a
> feature of the face whose φ-lock the body's grip can catch: the deep `n` holds its
> coherence against the applied perturbation, so the re-lock takes, the grip holds,
> and the body gains a clean, costed stride upward. The face's coherence *is* its
> climbability — the same `q`/`ε²`/rung the material regime publishes, read as holds.

**[design]** — that a face's rung structure presents as *grippable holds* (which
features read as holds, how deep a `n` holds a grip) is this doc's designed read over
the landed hardness-as-rung semantics, probe-calibrated (§5b). The *fact* that
deeper-`n` matter holds its lock against perturbation is engine-real / landed
(material-regimes §4).

### 3.2 A scarred or thin face wastes `(1−q)` — the elevated `ε²` read before the holds fail

`energy-harnessing.md` §2 gives the waste law: every working field system bleeds
`E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput`. `field-hazards.md` §3/§4 establishes the thin as a
region of collapsed `q` and elevated `ε²`. **A scarred or thin face is one whose holds
are bad — and the cost is the `(1−q)` waste, read before the holds fail:**

> **A scarred or thin face — low-`q`, high-`ε²`, shallow-rung (a decohered, scarred,
> or thin margin of the field) — gives bad holds and wastes `(1−q)`.** Each grip on so
> decohered a face sheds the waste law's fraction *with nothing held*: the shallow
> rung cannot hold the lock against the grip's perturbation, the hold does not take,
> and the `(1−q)` spent is lost — the body slides a little, spends a little, gains
> nothing. And the tell is *before* the failure: the thin face's elevated `ε²` read —
> the `q ~ 1e-3…1e-1` noise floor toward the attractor ≈ 0.947
> (`energy-harnessing.md` §7 Q1; `corpus-reconciliation.md`) — is the honest
> pre-warning that the holds will fail, the same `ε²` the reader and Weatherglass
> already render (field-hazards §5.1, readable-before-it-arrives).

**[design]** — *which* face reads as bad-holds, and how much each wasted hold costs,
is this doc's designed read over the landed `q`/`ε²`/rung and the `(1−q)` waste law,
probe-calibrated (§5b). The *fact* that a low-`q`, high-`ε²` region wastes `(1−q)` is
landed (energy-harnessing §2); the *climb's* reading of that waste as bad holds is the
design.

### 3.3 Readable height-feasibility — the same honesty, applied to the ascent

`field-hazards.md` §5.1's cardinal rule — hazards are **readable before they arrive** —
is the corpus's discipline for anything dangerous that comes for the body. The climb's
handholds-as-a-read apply that same honesty to *the height itself*:

> **You can read a cliff's height-feasibility.** Before committing to an ascent, the
> climber reads the face's `q`/`ε²`/rung structure off the published channels the way
> a walker reads a crossing's gradient and a reader reads an approaching storm: a
> coherent, deep-rung face reads *climbable* (good holds, a clean, costed ascent); a
> scarred or thin face reads *not* (bad holds, `(1−q)` wasted, an elevated `ε²` tell).
> **A climb begun on a readable face is a climb begun honestly** — the climber never
> commits to a height blind, any more than a player walks a thin crossing or waits out
> a storm it did not see coming. The read is the material's own, on the instruments'
> own channels — never a hidden skill meter (§5e).

The honest frame, stated once: **the climb's difficulty is not a hidden stat; it is
the face's own coherence read as holds — the same `q`/`ε²`/rung the reader, the
Weatherglass, and the sky show, at the vertical's scale.** A player who reads the
field reads the cliff.

## 4. The gradient's cost — the climb's honest price

### 4.1 Climbing fights gravity — a steep ∇(g·Φ) taxes the carried field

`energy-harnessing.md` §1.1 establishes the primary reservoir: a mass condenses, the
field around it carries `∇(g·Φ)`, and letting something move *down* that gradient is
the field doing work on it. The climb's inversion is that the body is moving *up*
against it — and against a gradient, the carried field pays:

> **Climbing fights gravity.** Every gain against a steep `∇(g·Φ)` is the body's own
> held work in place of the field's free downhill haul — each re-lock against the
> up-slope spends the carried field's coherence the way a stride into the walk's
> *dear* direction spends it (the-walk §2a/§2b), but against a steeper pull and per
> foot of vertical rather than per stride on the flat. **A steep ∇(g·Φ) taxes the
> body's carried field more per stride than walking.** Where the walk rides a descent
> for free, a climb pays the same gradient's steepness as a *cost* — the steeper the
> face's pull, the more each held re-lock sheds against it (the `(1−q)` floor,
> energy-harnessing §2), and the dearer the ascent becomes.

**[design]** — the *per-hold* cost over the published `∇(g·Φ)`/`q`/`ε²` at the climb
(the ascent's stride-cost mapping) is this doc's designed weighting, probe-calibrated
(§5b). The *fact* that moving a mass up a potential gradient is work the body's carried
field pays is the landed gradient + waste-law physics; the climb is its designed
reading at the vertical.

### 4.2 A climb with a pack taxes more — the carried field at the ascent

`the-burden.md` §1/§2 establishes the carried cost: the ε² a channeler sheds
accumulates on them as a carried debt, and a laden act is a dear act — a laden walk is
a dear walk, "the more carried `ε²`, the more each step sheds into the stride"
(the-walk §3; the-burden §2). **The climb takes that carried weight against gravity:**

> **A climb with a pack taxes more.** The carried ε² the body drags (the-burden §1)
> is weight the ascent must *lift against `∇(g·Φ)`*: where a laden walk sheds the debt
> into each stride, a laden climb sheds it into each **re-lock** — and because the
> climb is already the dear direction (against the gradient), the carried debt
> compounds the ascent's `(1−q)` waste harder than it compounds a flat stride. **The
> carried field at the ascent is heavy precisely because it is at the ascent** — the
> same burden that is a slow drag on the level is a real tax on the vertical.

**[design]** — the exact *carried-weight multiplier* on the climb (how much harder a
laden ascent's re-lock sheds than a laden stride) is this doc's designed read over the
landed carried-debt aggregation (the-burden §6a) + the climb's gradient cost,
probe-calibrated (§5b). The *fact* that carried ε² sheds harder in the dear direction
is the burden's own landed claim (the-walk §3's dear-stride); the climb is its
vertical instance. **The carry doc is absent on disk; the carried cost here is grounded
solely in the-burden's landed read, and the pack's specific load is noted open (§6).**

### 4.3 A failed hold is a fall — the honest risk at height, never hidden

`player-remains.md` §1 establishes that the player's body is a coherence store and
death is a field act — including, per §4, "the gradient's ownership at the moment of
release." The fall's own honesty is that the gradient owns a free descent absolutely
(the-fall §1). **The climb's most honest risk is that it is one held-altitude away
from a fall:**

> **A failed hold is a fall.** Every gain the climb makes is a height above the ground
> the body would descend if the re-lock did not take — and a re-lock that fails at
> height is exactly the fall: the body's held lock releases into the gradient's
> absolute ownership, and the descent begins as a the-fall free act (no `(1−q)` spent
> to soften it, the-fall §2.2) with the landing's `ε²` spike as its honest cost
> (the-fall §3; player-remains §1.2's dissolution). **The climb's honest risk at height
> is the fall's own honesty, reached the other way: the higher the climb, the farther
> a failed hold falls — and this risk is never hidden** (the fall's cost is the
> published channels' own, the-fall §5e).

The honest frame, stated once: **a climb is a series of re-locks each of which, on
failure, hands the body to the fall — and the corpus's honesty about the fall (its
cost is the impact's, never a hidden damage roll; a climb's held altitude is real
height) is the climb's honesty about its own risk.** A player who climbs reads the
height's risk the way they read a fall's: on the same channels, never a hidden roll.
