## 1. Naming as a field act — stated once

The corpus already holds that identity is a persistent-Π pattern
(`field-npc-ai.md` §2.1: "an NPC's identity IS a persistent Π pattern — a
coherence configuration the field is actively holding"); the Reason Field makes
a mind a *published* persistent-Π structure (`reason-field.md` §3.1), and the
schema attaches a stable member-id at the world-writer's single write
(`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1). Naming is the same machinery turned outward:

> **To name a thing is to bind a persistent-Π anchor to it.** A name — for a
> house, a road, a settlement, a companion — is a designed tag over the *same*
> structure the field uses to hold a mind, a member-identity, or a ruin's
> deep-rung core. The field re-quantizes around and holds *the named* the way it
> holds any persistent pattern: the way it holds an NPC's Π pattern
> (`field-npc-ai.md` §2.1), the way it re-quantizes around a dead residue
> (`qi-computation.md` §5.2; `field-archaeology.md` §2.1), the way it holds a
> member line across death (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1). A name is that
> holding, applied to a *place the settlement cares for* instead of a *person
> the field carries*.

**A name is the member-id's principle extended from players to things.** The
schema's `member` field is the seed of every name: the world-writer attaches an
id at its single, non-forgeable write (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1 —
"attribution is the read's spine"), and a name is that same principle — a stable
tag over a field-held identity — detached from a player and bound to an object.
The player who names a house is doing, for the house, what the schema already
does for the player: attaching an honest identity at the point where the world is
written. **[design]** The engine writes no "name" record; the *bind* is this
doc's designed application of the schema's own id-principle to things.

The honest flag, once: the *holding* a name invokes is the theory's own
persistence — `qi-computation.md` §5.2's persistent Π patterns, which
`reason-field.md` §6a flags as speculative-as-memory; the *binding* (which
structures can be named, how the anchor attaches) is this doc's design. The two
are never blurred (§4).

## 2. The honest bound — you cannot name falsely

### 2.1 The heart of the design

> **A bound-name can only name what is there.** If you name a house and it is not
> a house — if you call a scar a hearth, a pile of indifferent rubble a kept
> room, a stranger's trail your companion — the anchor does not hold. The field
> re-quantizes *around* what is actually in front of it, and the name that named
> what is not there **decays**: the lie erodes exactly where the truth would have
> held.

This is not a rule the game enforces. It is a **field consequence**: a name is a
persistent-Π anchor (§1), and the field only holds what is attractor-consistent
(`qi-computation.md` §5.2's "persists as long as the ambient q remains above the
threshold"). A name bound to a true structure sits at a real coherent well — the
field *prefers* to hold it, the way it re-quantizes around a residue
(`field-archaeology.md` §2.1). A name bound to a falsehood is a Π configuration
with no corresponding structure to stabilize it — nothing holds it up, so it
dissolves the way any shallow, unsupported coherence does. **Honesty is a field
consequence, not a rule**: the field does not punish you for lying; it simply
*does not hold the lie*. `[design]` The field has no moral judgment; it has
thermodynamics — the truth is the deep well that holds, the lie is the
unsupported bump that sheds.

### 2.2 The honest mechanics — what a true name does, what a false name does

| | A **true** name | A **false** name |
|---|---|---|
| The anchor | **holds** — sits in a real coherent well, the field keeps it (persistent-Π, `qi-computation.md` §5.2) | **decays** — nothing stabilizes it; the unsupported coherence sheds the way shallow rungs shed (`energy-harnessing.md` §2's `(1−q)` waste) |
| The named | **survives** — its record persists longer than a nameless one's (the ruin keeps its record, `house-that-steers.md` §3) | **erodes** — the lie un-binds, the "name" fades to nothing and the thing stands, named-falsely, becoming anonymous again |
| The record | **persists and reads** — archaeology reads a named stratum as *its* record; the Board reads a named contribution as attributable | fails and is **forgotten** — a false name leaves no stratum, no legible line; the field "forgets" the lie |
| The cost | holds for the price of the structure's own coherence — **never a mint** (§6d) | costs the attempt and returns nothing — a net loss, an honest one |

### 2.3 Naming is an act of truthfulness about what a structure IS

Because a name only holds on what the field actually holds, **naming is the
player's act of truthful reading** — the moment they must name a thing by what it
*is* in the field:

- **Its rung** — what cascade depth its coherence actually sits at (deep-rung
  ordered matter, `material-regimes.md` §4/§5; a deeper structure resists
  dissolution, so a "deep" name holds only on a genuinely deep-rung thing).
- **Its role** — what it does to the local field (a hearth anchors, a room
  holds, an open edge drains — `house-that-steers.md` §1.1; a name that calls a
  drain a hearth is a lie the field sheds).
- **Its place in the settlement's body** — where it sits in the gradient-stolen
  layout (`house-that-steers.md` §2: bath at the center, productive edges,
  deliberate drains). A name that calls the margin a core, or a dweller a
  steward, does not hold.

The player who names well must first *read well*. This is the design's deepest
mechanic-by-consequence: **the honesty of the world is enforced by its physics —
the field will not hold a lie, and the player learns to name by learning to see.**

### 2.4 The determinism tie — a name is a pure function of the state it binds

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `field-archaeology.md` §1.2; `field-npc-ai.md`
§2.3's hard gate). The name inherits it:

> **Same structure, same field state → same truth → same name's holding.** A
> name is a deterministic function of the field state it binds: a true name on a
> real structure holds every time, in every run; a false name on the same
> structure decays every time. There is no seeded-RNG judgment about whether a
> lie "sticks" — the field's determinism makes naming honest the same way it
> makes an NPC's mind honest (`field-npc-ai.md` §2.3): reproducible, learnable,
> a real relationship between the name, the structure, and the field.

## 3. What a name does

A name is not a cosmetic label. It is a persistence-and-legibility operation on
whatever it binds, and each binding composes with an existing corpus system:

### 3.1 The named ruin keeps its record

`house-that-steers.md` §3 establishes the ruin's three classes — the fallen
floor (a scar), the ghost-halo (residue, findable archaeology), the **skeleton**
(the still-holding low-rung structure, *re-authorable*). What a name does to a
ruin:

> **A named skeleton is a re-authorable Π the field holds longer.** The skeleton
> is already a persistent Π pattern the field keeps holding (`house-that-steers.md`
> §3.2 — via `qi-computation.md` §5.2). Binding a name raises what the field
> holds: the name tags the skeleton as *a structure with an identity*, the way
> the member-id tags a player, so the field re-quantizes around it as a kept
> thing rather than a fading one — **the ruin keeps its record because it has a
> name, and a nameless ruin's core has nothing to anchor the record to.**

The re-authoring act (`house-that-steers.md` §3.4 — re-locking the skeleton's
order) becomes *re-condensing a named thing*: the player restores, not just a
skeleton, but *the house that had this name* — the name is what connects the
present repair to the past structure it restores.

### 3.2 The named contribution is legible

`shared-ledger.md` builds the Coherence Board on the member-id: per-member
attribution is "the read's spine" (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1). What a name
does extends that:

> **The Board reads a named act as a bounded, attributable line** — and a named
> contribution is one whose provenance the society can carry. The member-id is
> the seed of every name (§1); when the *object* a player built or tended is
> named, its maintenance books not just to the player's `C(M)` but to a
> *legible, repeated subject the settlement can talk about* — "the Hearth at the
> corner is this tide's steward" is the name carrying the contribution, the way
> "X is this tide's steward" carries a player's line (`shared-ledger.md` §3.1).
> A named structure is a place the Board's book can point at; a nameless one is
> an anonymous drain or contribution the settlement cannot name as its own.

The honest caution: the misattribution guard (`shared-ledger.md` §3.3/§6e — "the
Board must not blame a player for a scar they didn't cut") extends to names — a
name must not let a structure's false attribution hide *whose* the lie is; the
name's decay (§2) is the guard's physical form.

### 3.3 The named place's strata read differently

`field-archaeology.md` §2.1 establishes the residue model: a dissolving
structure's deep-rung core persists as a q-locked residue; §3.1 reads it as a
dark core + ε² ghost halo. Archaeology is **residue-reading, not time-travel**
(`field-archaeology.md` §1.2). What a name does:

> **A named locality's residue is readable as *its* record, not anonymous.**
> The strata of a nameless place are history without a speaker — residue any
> region might have shed. The strata of a *named* place are, at the named core,
> **the record of that thing**: the field held a persistent-Π anchor there, so
> the deep-rung residue at that site is the named structure's own accumulated
> record — the way a fossil is "the record of that organism"
> (`field-archaeology.md` §2.2), a named site's residue is the history of that
> name. A player digs a named settlement's ruins and reads *its* arc, not a
> generic band. The name is the speaker that turns strata into a story.

### 3.4 A named companion is a Π anchor on a run

`field-npc-ai.md` §2.1: an NPC's identity IS a persistent Π pattern; §2.4 —
dissolution = death, and the deep-rung fraction persists as residue. What a name
does to an NPC:

> **Naming an NPC binds a Π anchor to their run.** The identity was already
> there — a persistent Π pattern the field holds (`field-npc-ai.md` §2.1). The
> name is the *label the settlement's book can carry* for that run: a named
> companion is one whose Π pattern has a legible identity the player can track,
> whose residue is a *known* fossil ("this was the animal you named") rather than
> an anonymous one, and whose run — like a member-line across death
> (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1) — the name holds continuity for. The name is
> the carried identity's *flag* — the running-scar's legible counterpart
> (`the-burden.md` §1: what the corpse's fossil and the trace carry, a name makes
> the *living run* legible).

## 6. Honest gates

### (a) The [design]/engine-real/theory line

### (b) GRAND/LATER mechanical — the Phase-1-legible principle

The *mechanical* naming layer — persistent-Π anchors on things, the true-name
holding / false-name decay as a live field effect — is **GRAND/LATER**, gated on
the same frontier every identity claim is gated on:

- **The reason-field / persistent-Π frontier** — the sites must actually carry
  readable, long-lived Π structure (`reason-field.md` §6a: the meshless/Π probe),
  and the engine's persistent-Π-as-memory must be verified before a name can be
  said to *hold* anything.
- **The member-id** — the name extends the id; the id is landed
  (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1) but the *named-thing* attach is this doc's
  design over it.

But the **Phase-1-legible principle is designable now**:

> **Naming as an honest Π-anchor, no-false-naming — the *principle* is designable
> now against the landed member-id and the house/ruin and archaeology pieces.**
> The scaffold does not need the meshless/Π probe to state the rule: a name
> holds only on the structure it truthfully names, a false name decays, a name
> is a pure function of the state it binds (§2.4), and naming composes with the
> tutor, the ledger, the fate, and the archaeology (§5). Every one of those
> compositions is a *designed statement* the corpus can hold now, whether or not
> the mechanical anchor is live.

**What Phase-1 gains from the scaffold:** (1) the **named-ruin record** reads
against the *already-designed* house/ruin pieces (`house-that-steers.md` §3) and
archaeology's residue model (`field-archaeology.md` §2.1) — the design says *how*
a name changes what a ruin is, before the anchor is mechanical; (2) the
**named-contribution legibility** reads against the *landed* member-id and the
Board's aggregation (`shared-ledger.md`; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1) — the
"name" is the member-id's principle applied to things, already settled for
people; (3) the **honesty principle** (no-false-naming) is itself a Phase-1-legible
truth about how the world *should* behave — a pre-registered design stance the
corpus can state and later verify mechanically. The scaffold is real: it is the
decision that naming is a Π-anchor, not a label — the decision the corpus's
identity machinery waits on, in the same way `reason-field.md` is the decision
the three forks wait on (`reason-field.md` §7).

### (c) Determinism

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `field-archaeology.md` §1.2; `field-npc-ai.md`
§2.3). Therefore:

> **A name's holding is a pure function of the field state it binds.** Same
> structure, same truth, same name's holding — every run (§2.4). A true name
> holds deterministically; a false name decays deterministically. There is no
> seeded-RNG judgment; naming is as reproducible as an NPC's mind
> (`field-npc-ai.md` §2.3) or a trace's record (`resonance-tutor.md` §6e).

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — a name holds, never mints

`energy-harnessing.md` §6's cap (`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`) applies to a name as a
hard bound:

> **A name holds the coherence the field already provides; it never mints it.**
> A name does not generate `q`, does not make a thing more than it is — it *holds*
> the structure that is already there, the way a house "holds and steers, never
> generates" (`house-that-steers.md` §5d). A name that appears to *make* a thing
> worth more is a lie the field sheds (the cap's honest consequence: you cannot
> mint worth with a label). The highest a name can hold a thing is what the thing
> already *is* in the field — the name is a persistence-and-legibility act over
> real structure, never a net generator.

**The attended name's re-bind (reverse pointer, two-way).** This §6d no-raise
bound is exactly the boundary the corpus's why-walk doc keeps for its *re-bind*:
[`the-pilgrim.md`](./the-pilgrim.md) §4 says a visited named place is "re-bound —
the anchor **refreshed by the keeping**," under the hard bound that "the refresh is
the name's own holding, at most a return to it, **never an increase**"
(`the-pilgrim.md` §4/§5d — it cites this §6d as the re-bind's no-raise boundary).
The pilgrimage's keeping is this §6d's "at most return to what the name already
does, touched again"; the two docs now cite each other as read-and-answered.

### (e) Accessibility — never a hidden-only effect

- The **reader / Sense** (`coherence-magic.md` §2) renders the *held* against the
  *named* — a true name's structure is a real coherent well the reader shows; a

## Open questions

1. **Which structures can be named — the binding's scope.** The moveable/static
   line: a *house, a road, a settlement, a companion* are named in §1, but what
   about a *region*, a *pocket-cosmos*, a *machine*, a *dormant seed*? The
   honest rule is that a name binds only what the field can be said to *hold as
   a persistent structure* (§1) — so an active machine (a held lock) probably can
   be named, an open drain (a non-holding edge, `house-that-steers.md` §1.1)
   probably cannot. The exact scope is a [design] dial against the plausible Π
   anchors. **[design]**
2. **The friend-or-false name boundary.** §2 makes a false name decay "the way
   shallow rungs shed" — how fast, and is it ever *permanent*, or does a decayed
   false name leave a scar the field eventually heals (`field-archaeology.md`
   §2.1: scars heal but leave residue)? A falsely "named" over-drained site might
   leave a q-locked *lie-core* residue — the archaeology of a lie. The honesty
   of that is attractive; the lifetime is a [design]/[probe] dial. **[design]**
3. **The naming authority.** Who can bind a name — a single player's act, or
   does a name *hold* because the settlement's book carries it (the Board reads a
   named contribution, §3.2), meaning a name is a *social* hold as much as a
   field one? The strongest reading (and the one this doc leans on) is that the
   name's hold is the field's (§2), and the settlement's *legibility* of it is
   the ledger's — the two are separate holds that can disagree. The rule for
   when a name is "claimed" (first namer, majority, maintenance) is [design].
   **[design]**
4. **The name's continuity across decay and rebuild.** If a named house falls to
   a fallen floor (a scar, `house-that-steers.md` §3.2 — unrecoverable) and a new
   structure is built on the site, does the name persist (the settlement names
   the site) or does the name die with the structure (only a *re-authorable
   skeleton* keeps its name, §3.1)? The design's default is toward the latter —
   a name holds the *structure*, not the *plot* — but the settlement's memory
   (§3.3, the strata as its record) pulls toward the former. Reconciles with
   `player-remains.md`'s conservation-of-"you" (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1.1)
   at the structure scale. **[design]**
