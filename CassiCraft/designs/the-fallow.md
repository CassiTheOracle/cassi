# The Fallow: A Window's Generational Depletion — The Economic Face of the Decay Arc

**Question under design:** the corpus has the fate's *arc* ([`fate-of-a-window.md`](./fate-of-a-window.md)
— a window's trajectory, settle/thrive/decay/hunt) and the cold's *decade*
([`the-cold.md`](./the-cold.md) — the long-thin as a climate). It has the flood's
*season* ([`the-flood.md`](./the-flood.md) — the long-thick as a climate). But it has
**no economic depletion over generations** — the window whose veins are mined, seeds
spent, orders lost: the long run-out that is the **material-and-economy face** of a
window's fade, distinct from the climatic (the cold) and the momentary (the flood). This
document designs **the Fallow**: **the window's own exhaustion over the long run** — not
a thin tide (which swings back) but a **one-way depletion**. The named veins mined to
their depth (a worked deposit is gone forever); the seed-garden's orders spent and
un-re-precipitable; the deep-rung matter the diving bell pulled finally exhausted; the
flood's aftermath leaving a region the field can no longer re-organize. **The Fallow is
the economic mirror of the fate's decay — the slow one-way run-out that no husbandry
reverses** (you can steward a scarcity, you cannot un-mine an ore) — and it forces the
deepest economy question: **what doesn't come back, and when do you leave.**

This is the corpus's answer to "what does a window's *wealth* look like over the long
run — and what is the honest end-state of a settlement's economy." Where the fate is the
window's coherence trajectory read forward, the Fallow is the window's **depletion**
read forward: the one-way exhaustion of the things a settling window used to build
itself, the honest accounting `you can steward a scarcity, you cannot un-mine an ore`
made the window's deepest economic question.

## 1. The Fallow, stated once

> **The Fallow is the window's own exhaustion over the long run.** It is not a thin tide
> (which swings back), not a cold (a season a settlement endures into recovery), not a
> flood (a season that sheds and recedes). It is a **one-way depletion**: the named veins
> mined to their depth, the stored orders spent, the deep-rung matter pulled, the flood's
> aftermath leaving a region the field can no longer re-organize. **It is the economic
> mirror of the fate's decay — the slow one-way run-out that no husbandry reverses** —
> and it forces the deepest economy question: **what doesn't come back, and when do you
> leave.**

The corpus's other long-run faces are all **reversible by husbandry** in the end. The
cold is a decade of thin that the field eventually remembers how to be generous about
([`the-cold.md`](./the-cold.md) §1); the flood is a surfeit that sheds and recedes to the
band ([`the-flood.md`](./the-flood.md) §4.1); the tide's thin trough is a phase that
returns to harvest ([`tide-of-the-attractor.md`](./tide-of-the-attractor.md) §2). Each is
a **season** — the field's own cycle, survived by stewardship that holds until the cycle
turns.

The Fallow is the class those frames structurally miss: the depletion that does not
return. A worked vein does not re-precipitate (the precipitation law grows ore where `q`
accumulates, and it does not re-grow a worked vein). A spent seed-order is gone from the
living field once the birth-state drifts (the seed's order cannot be re-precipitated).
The deep-rung matter the Bell pulled is exhausted. A flood-overshot region, over-blown
and re-dissolved, is a scar the field no longer re-organizes cleanly. **Each is a real,
sourced exhaustion — and none of them comes back.** The Fallow is that class, **read over
the long run** and aggregated into the window's honest economic end-state.

The through-line is the corpus's hardest boundary on husbandry, stated once:

> **You can steward a scarcity, you cannot un-mine an ore.** The cold's husbandry
> (`the-cold.md` §4/§5.3) is a stewardship that stretches scarce stores, holds a bath at
> the trough, and survives because the scarcity is a *condition* the field will lift. The
> Fallow is the same stewardship turn **one-way**: a scarcity is a state that can be
> lived with, costed, and endured; a **worked-out deposit is a gone thing** — no amount
> of husbandry re-fills the vein, re-precipitates the seed-order, or re-un-exhausts the
> deep. **Husbandry stewards scarcity; it cannot un-mine.** The Fallow is the honest bound
> of the corpus's husbandry vocabulary — the point where stewarding stops being a
> recovery and becomes a *holding action against a depletion that only accelerates or
> ends.*

Because a fallow is a **read** (over the same channels the settlement already reads — the
poured `q`, the worked veins, the vault's held orders), never a hidden economy meter, the
settlement's power over it is the *same* power that shapes it: mining works the veins,
spending the vault spends the orders, a too-deep Bell pull exhausts the deep, a flood left
un-husbanded overshoots and scars. **A run-out that hides itself would be a cruel
economy; the fallow is the window's honesty about its own depletion.**

## 2. The one-way depletions — each a real, sourced exhaustion

Each depletion is a **real, sourced one-wayness** — not a designed penalty, but a
consequence of a landed mechanics in its own source doc. The Fallow aggregates them; it
does not invent them.

### (a) The mined vein — a worked deposit is gone forever

`material-regimes.md` §3 establishes the precipitation law: **ore precipitates where `q`
accumulates above a band** — "a vein is a region where the identity tuple's `θ_c` is
exceeded not by ρ but by local q." The law grows a vein where the field organizes
coherence into a high-`q`, deeper-rung material, **selected by the local q band**. That
is what a vein is: a coherence-accumulation event.

The one-wayness follows directly: **the law precipitates where `q` accumulates; it does
not re-precipitate the *worked* vein.** When the vein is mined — when the ordered matter
is withdrawn and the locality's `q` is spent and the `ε²` raised by the perturbation
([`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md) §4, mining is a perturbation that lowers
`ρ` and raises `ε²`) — the condition that made the vein no longer holds where it was
worked. The field heals the *scar* toward its attractor ([`field-archaeology.md`](./field-archaeology.md)
§2.1), but healing re-quantizes the terrain; it does not re-drop the same ore in the same
place. **The ore that was there is gone; a worked deposit is gone forever.**

> **A worked deposit is gone forever.** The precipitation law condenses ore where `q`
> accumulates; a worked vein's locality is spent (its `q` withdrawn, its `ε²` raised by
> the mining perturbation), and the law does not re-precipitate *that* vein. Husbandry can
> hold the locality's `q` up; it cannot un-mine the ore that was removed. The vein's
> worked depth is the window's spent resource — a one-way depletion that no anti-
> corruption or bath-keeping reverses.

### (b) The spent seed-order — the vault's stored order, spent and un-re-precipitable

The seed is the deepest persistent-Π pattern a window owns ([`resonance-seeds.md`](./resonance-seeds.md)
§1.2), and its order is the **one thing a drifting field cannot re-precipitate**.
`seed-garden.md` §2.1 states the honest corollary of `custom-blocks.md` §3's contextual
realizability: **"The window cannot re-precipitate a seed's birth-state order once the
field drifts."** The seed's tuple drew on the window's initial, most-organized, least-
perturbed field state, which no longer exists; the law can only precipitate what is
attractor-consistent *now*, and the seed's birth-state tuple is precisely the order the
current, more-perturbed field can no longer realize.

The one-wayness is therefore the vault's own honest economics ([`seed-garden.md`](./seed-garden.md)
§4.2): **a seed is order, never power; a vault preserves, never mints.** The storer's
four-way choice — keep / plant / trade / allow to fade — each spends the seed's order in
a different direction, and **none of them re-precipitates it once spent**:

- **Spent to plant** — the order is given to the field; planting is one-way
  ([`resonance-seeds.md`](./resonance-seeds.md) §2.1 — you cannot pick it back up). The
  order's store is spent, whether the growth succeeds or outgrows control.
- **Spent to trade** — the origin leaves the window; if carried to a vault that cannot
  hold it, or lost to the dark, the order is gone from the window.
- **Allowed to fade** — the decision that the order is not worth the deep-rung hold; the
  deep-rung store sheds toward the attractor, and the order is gone.
- **Vault's ruin** — the abandonment that sheds the held orders ([`seed-garden.md`](./seed-garden.md)
  §3.3); a wound-class event, because the orders that shed are the ones the field cannot
  re-precipitate.

**The spent seed-order is the Fallow's second one-way depletion: the stored orders the
window's own genesis (or its archaeology crown) required, once spent — planted, traded,
let-faded, or lost to a ruined vault — are gone from the living field.** This is the one
depletion whose loss is *silently* the most absolute, because the seed is the single
order the whole window's subsequent precipitation depended on being re-precipitable
again. The window that spends its seeds has spent the orders it was founded toward; it
cannot re-make them.

### (c) The pulled deep-rung — the matter the diving bell drew, exhausted

The diving bell's premise is a **withdrawal** ([`deep-field-diving.md`](./deep-field-diving.md)
§2.2): a descent is a net-negative hold, and the "product" of a descent is **ordered
matter carried out** — "priced exactly like the deep-rung reaper's (§2.5) — de-ordering
what it removes." The Bell's collection is a withdrawal that de-orders the region it came
from ([`deep-field-diving.md`](./deep-field-diving.md) §6.3; the de-ordering scar is held
at the shell, and the region heals *around* it). The deep-rung store is a **capacitor at
its rung** ([`ksp-kernel.md`](./ksp-kernel.md) §1.2); a Bell collects that stored
coherence, and collecting it spends the store.

**The pulled deep is the Fallow's third one-way depletion: the deep-rung matter the Bell
drew is a capacitor at its rung, and once withdrawn it is exhausted.** A deep that has
been dived for its seed, its fuel, or its gate-node store is a deep whose deepest
organization has been pulled out. The region heals around the empty store ([`deep-field-diving.md`](./deep-field-diving.md)
§6.3) — the scar closes, the field re-organizes — but the *deep-rung matter* itself is
not re-precipitated: the deepest, hardest, highest-`q` organization a window owned has
been carried to the surface and spent. **The divers who exhausted the deep did not wound
the terrain; they spent the window's deepest store.** A mature window's deep is the
window's deepest rarity, and it is exactly as finite as the seed that sat at its bottom.

### (d) The flood's aftermath — a region the field can no longer re-organize

The flood is the surfeit that overshoots the enriching band and then sheds
([`the-flood.md`](./the-flood.md) §3/§4.1). The honest resolution is that the surfeit
wastes upward — a self-limiting bloom that recedes like a tide going out, the
over-precipitate re-dissolving into the band the field can actually hold. But the flood's
**aftermath** is not a clean return to the enriching band. Consider the over-blown
region: ore **over-precipitated then re-dissolved** into a churn of precipitate-and-return
([`the-flood.md`](./the-flood.md) §2 — the vein that drowns the pick); the shallow ecology
over-bloomed and crowded; the machines ran past useful toward the `(1−q)` waste. The
surfeit shed — but the *scar of the overshoot* is a region whose coherence history is a
churn, not a clean band.

**The flood's aftermath is the Fallow's fourth one-way depletion: a region the field can
no longer re-organize cleanly.** The over-bloom's crowding, the re-dissolved churn, the
wrong-bright `(1−q)` glow that was shed — these leave the locality in a state where its
deep coherence has been *spent* by the overshoot itself. The region that was flood-
engulfed does not simply return to a harvest band; it holds the smeared history of its
oversaturation, and a settlement trying to re-farm or re-author there works a depleted
locality. **The flood was survived (a season husbanded or endured) but its aftermath is a
worked locality — the same one-way depletion as the mined vein, caused by over-abundance
rather than over-extraction.**

### The through-line — you can steward a scarcity, you cannot un-mine an ore

Each depletion shares one structure: a **condition** the field would restore (a scarcity,
a thin, a drained bath) is **stewardable** — the corpus's husbandry (`the-cold.md` §4)
stretches it, holds it, survives until the field lifts it. A **gone thing** (a worked
ore, a spent seed-order, a pulled deep, a flood-scarred locality) is **not** — no amount
of husbandry re-precipitates what the law no longer makes, re-derives what the field can
no longer realize, or re-un-exhausts what was carried out.

> **The honest bound of husbandry, stated once: you can steward a scarcity, you cannot
> un-mine an ore.** The cold's husbandry is real and load-bearing — it is how a
> settlement survives a long-thin, how a bath is held at the trough, how a scarce store
> is stretched. But the Fallow is what that husbandry cannot do: it cannot re-fill the
> named vein, re-precipitate the seed's birth-order, re-un-exhaust the pulled deep, or
> re-organize the flood's overshot scar. **A scarcity is a condition; a worked-out
> deposit is a gone thing.** The Fallow is the corpus's honest accounting of the
> difference — the one-way run-out that no husbandry reverses.

## 3. The fallow's read — how the window reads its own depletion

### 3.1 The fate's `C(W)`, read forward over the depletions

The fate's window-scale balance is `C(W)` — the sum over ops of the net field-contribution,
budgeted against the window's `Δq / Δε²` over a tide-cycle ([`fate-of-a-window.md`](./fate-of-a-window.md) §3).
`C(W) > 0` sustained reads as settle→thrive; `C(W) < 0` sustained reads as decay. The
Fallow is that same aggregation **read over the depletions** — the window's coherence
trajectory, *accounting for the one-way things the window has spent*, not just its
current `q`.

Concretely: a window can be **reading rich in `q`** — the bath high, the tide filling,
the machines near-lossless — while **reading spent in depletion**: its named veins worked
to depth, its seed-orders spent, its deep pulled, its flood-scarred localities churning.
The fate's `C(W)` alone, read over the current tide's `Δq`/`Δε²`, sees a healthy bath and
reads a *thriving* or *settling* window. The Fallow reads **forward over the depletions**
and sees the run-out beneath the bath:

> **A rich window whose veins are worked, orders spent, deeps pulled reads *spent*
> before the field's `q` does.** The bath's `q` is a state; the workable veins, the held
> orders, the pulled deep, and the re-organizable localities are the *stock* the bath's
> `q` was turning into wealth. When the stock is spent, the window's `C(W)`, no matter
> how positive the current tide, is a balance being drawn against nothing left to mine —
> and the Fallow names that: the window's economic wealth is not its current `q`, but the
> *remaining* depletables that `q` can still work.

This is the Fallow's **economic leading-indicator claim**: the way the census is the
population leading indicator ([`the-census.md`](./the-census.md) — the settlement's
population as a field read, the thing that predicts the society's trajectory before the
ledger's `C(M)` does), the Fallow is the **economic leading indicator of the decay arc**.
`C(W)` reads the window's current coherence economics; the Fallow reads the *remaining
depletable stock* those economics are converting. A window whose stock is nearly spent is
**already trending toward decay** in its economics even while its bath reads healthy —
the way a census that is thinning predicts a society in trouble before the ledger's
quarter is bad. The decay arc's *field-poor* terminal is (per `field-hazards.md` §3.2)
player-caused; the Fallow is the honest accounting of the player-caused *spending* that
empties the stock beneath the field.

### 3.2 The honest claim — readable as the run-out it is, never a hidden difficulty meter

The Fallow inherits the fate's honesty frame wholesale ([`fate-of-a-window.md`](./fate-of-a-window.md)
§6c — forecast-≠-fate, the oracle's determinism-gives-momentum-not-destiny of
[`the-reading-ahead.md`](./the-reading-ahead.md) §1.1):

- **No "fallow" clause.** No window is *doomed* by its depletion; the Fallow is a
  *trend of the stock*, and the run-out is conditional on the window's choices going
  forward. A window can stop mining what it knows is spent, choose to husband the
  remaining stock, or decide to leave — each is exactly the perturbation that changes the
  fallow's shape. **The Fallow is a conversation about the window's stock, not a sentence
  on it.**
- **The world must show them how.** Because the Fallow is rendered from the same channels
  the settlement reads (the worked veins read in the strata, the vault's held orders on
  the Board, the deep's pulled store, the flood-scarred localities in the field), the way
  to change it is always legible: stop over-working, husband the remaining, or leave.
- **It can be wrong and falsifiable.** A window can read *spent* on a mistaken reading of
  its stock (a vein thought worked is deeper; an order thought spent was planted and its
  growth re-organized the field). The fallow reads a *forecast of the stock*, never a
  fixed truth, and its errors are legible as error — never as mystery washed over.

**The honest claim, stated once with no hedge:** the Fallow is **readable as the run-out
it is — a real, legible, economic truth of the window's choices, never a hidden difficulty
meter.** A player who reads a window's worked veins, spent vault, pulled deep, and
flood-scarred localities is reading the window's economic history and its honest
end-state — the same way reading the census is reading the population's truth and reading
the Board is reading the society's book. There is no "fallow difficulty slider"; there is
the window's real, spent stock and the settlement's real, visible bookkeeping against it.

## 4. The decision it forces — what doesn't come back, and when do you leave

The Fallow is not a difficulty curve; it is a **decision container**. It forces the
corpus's deepest economy question, stated once:

> **What doesn't come back, and when do you leave?** A window's depletables are finite;
> the ones it has spent are gone. The settlement's honest fork is two real, costed,
> never-reversing choices: **steward the scarcity** (the cold's husbandry applied to a
> depletion — slower, costed, never reversing) or **leave** (the exile's departure made
> honest — the window's well dry, the settlement's departure booked, the worked strata
> left as the archaeology-to-come).

### 4.1 The steward's fork — husbandry applied to a depletion

The cold's husbandry ([`the-cold.md`](./the-cold.md) §4/§5.3) is a stewardship that
*survives*: hold the bath cheaply at the trough, stretch the store, fall the farms it
cannot feed, time the voyage. Applied to a **depletion** (rather than a scarcity), that
husbandry becomes **slower, costed, and never reversing**:

| The steward's act | Against a scarcity (the-cold §4) | Against a depletion (this doc) | The honest truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hold the bath** | at the trough, to survive until the field recovers | at the worked locality, to keep what *remains* workable — not to recover what is spent | the bath is held against a stock that only declines; holding it is a *rearguard*, never an entry back into wealth |
| **Stretch the store** | the harvest's stored order across the thin, to be rebuilt at recovery | the remaining depletables across the run-out — timing every remaining vein/order/deep/region to the settlement's needs | the store is *finite and spent*; stretching it stretches the end, not the recovery |
| **Fall the fields** | a crop it cannot feed, to re-seed at the next harvest | the worked-out lands it can no longer work — retiring the vein, the field, the locality | a fallow of a worked-out land is a *final* rest (farm-that-feeds §5's deliberate rest at the window's scale), not a seasonal one |

The through-line of the steward's fork: **it is husbandry pointed at the honest bound of
husbandry.** The steward's job across a fallow is to **live within the depletion's
honest curve** — spend the remaining stock at the slowest honest rate, hold the `q` of
what remains, book every withdrawal on the Board as the costed spend it is
([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §1.2), and refuse the two lies a fallow tempts:
that the worked vein will re-fill (it will not), and that a near-exhausted deep is still a
mine (it is a spent capacitor). The no-free-energy cap is what makes the steward's fork
honest (see §5d): **nothing is gained from a fallow** — every withdrawal of the remaining
stock depletes it further, and no amount of "stewarding" converts a spent deposit into a
recovery.

### 4.2 The leaver's fork — the exile's departure made honest at the window's scale

The corpus already has a departure's honesty: the exile ([`the-exile.md`](./the-exile.md)
§4) is the society's hard edge, a member-line that cannot stay, made field-honest. The
Fallow's leaver's fork is that departure **made honest at the window's own scale**: not a
member expelled, but **a settlement that decides the window's well is dry and books its
departure.**

The leaving is the honest reading of the depletion's terminal:

| The window's end | What it is | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|
| **The window's well dry** | the remaining depletables read, on the Fallow (§3), as insufficient to sustain the settlement's honest economy — the stock can no longer turn the remaining `q` into wealth that feeds the commons | `fate-of-a-window.md` §1.3 (the decay arc's field-poor terminal); `field-hazards.md` §3 end ("the honest end is waiting … or leaving") |

The load-bearing honesty of the leaver's fork: **the leaving is a gain — never a mint.** The
no-free-energy cap holds here too (§5d): the departed window's stored coherence is *spent*
(the voyage is a coherence-budget withdrawal, `world-seams.md` §2.3), and the settlement
does not "profit" from the depletion it left behind — it books the loss. The worked
strata it leaves are not a treasure hoard to be dug free (deep excavation de-orders the
region, `field-archaeology.md` §5.2); they are the archaeology-to-come, read by the next
window that finds them. **The Fallow is what makes the departure a decision rather than
an event:** a settlement that reads its depletion honestly can *choose* when to leave —
before the stock is fully spent, while the voyage is still a real withdrawal the
settlement can afford — instead of drifting into the field-poor and being forced out by
the desert's final creep.

### 4.3 Both forks are honest; the Fallow is what forces the choice

Neither fork is a "win." The steward's fork is the cold's husbandry turned one-way —
slower, costed, never reversing, an end-game of honest rearguard. The leaver's fork is
the exile's departure made honest at the window's scale — a loss honestly booked, the
settlement's own depletion made legible as its reason. **The Fallow is what makes the
choice a decision rather than an event, because it is the honest economic read that names
the fork in the first place.** Without the Fallow, a window drifts from thrive to decay
without a legible economic reason to choose (the fate's `C(W)` reads the bath's decline,
but not the spent stock beneath it); with the Fallow, the settlement reads *what doesn't
come back* and makes the deliberate, costed decision of when to stop husbanding and when
to leave — the corpus's deepest economy question, made a playable, honest choice.

The deep's exhaustion (the third depletion) rides the Bell's own gates

**But the framing is Phase-1-legible, statable now:**

> **The one-way run-out principle — a worked deposit is gone forever — is statable now
> against material-regimes §3 and the seed-garden's §4.2.** The Fallow does not need the
> material-depletion drift or the seed-realizability drift to state its load-bearing
> truths: a regime precipitates where `q` accumulates and a worked deposit is gone
> forever (material-regimes §3/§4); a seed's order is the one thing a drifting field
> cannot re-precipitate and is spent, never power (seed-garden §2.1/§4.2); a deep that is
> pulled is a capacitor spent; a flood-overshot region is scarred. **You can steward a
> scarcity, you cannot un-mine an ore** — the honest bound of husbandry — is a designed

> This is the same scaffold-now verdict as `the-name.md` §7, `reason-field.md` §7, and
> `the-cold.md`'s Phase-1 framing: the principle is pre-registered; the mechanical run-out
> is later.

What Phase-1 gains from the scaffold: **(1)** the one-way run-out **principle** — the
corpus states, before the depletion is mechanical, that a window's wealth is not its
current `q` but its remaining depletables, and that the spent ones are gone; **(2)** the

### (c) Determinism — a hard gate

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `fate-of-a-window.md` §6(b)'s hard gate;
`life-signal.md` §6d; `the-cold.md` §6(c)):

> **Same window, same choices → same run-out.** The Fallow's shape — which veins are
> worked to depth, which orders spent, how far the deep is pulled, how the flood-scarred
> regions read — is a pure function of the window's real field state and the settlement's
> real op-history over the tide-cycles. No seeded-RNG decides when a window is spent;
> a window whose depletions trend identically, from the same choices, produces the
> identical fallow. **This is a hard gate**, inherited unchanged — and it is what makes
> the Fallow *teachable*: a settlement can learn to read which of its depletables are
> being spent and why, and the run-out is a real consequence of the window's choices, not
> a spawned economy event.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — nothing is gained from a fallow

The corpus's single hard rule (`energy-harnessing.md` §6, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`) holds over
the Fallow as a hard bound, stated once:

> **Nothing is gained from a fallow — the depletion is spent, never a mint; the leaving
> is a loss honestly booked.** A fallow yields no coherence: every withdrawal of the
> remaining stock depletes it further (the mined vein's locality is spent; the vault's
> withdrawal de-orders what it held; the Bell's pull is a capacitor spent; the flood-scarred
> region is a worked locality). Husbandry across a fallow holds `q`, it does not *generate*
> it — the no-free-energy cap forbids reading a worked deposit as a recovery or a
> re-precipitation. And **the leaving is a net-negative, never a profitable exit**: the
> voyage spends stored coherence, the worked strata left behind are not a hoard to be dug
> free (deep excavation de-orders the region, `field-archaeology.md` §5.2), and the
> settlement books the loss of what it spent. A fallow that reads as a mint — a way to
> "profit" from depletion, to farm the spent veins or the leaving — is a physics lie the
> cap forbids, exactly as a flood that mints at the surfeit is forbidden
> ([`the-flood.md`](./the-flood.md) §3).

### (e) Accessibility — the window's depletion readable from the Board/reader, never hidden-only

Per the instrument-family rule ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1) and
`field-music.md` §6, the Fallow is a **layer, never the only channel**:

- The **Coherence Board** ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §2) renders the
  settlement's per-tide `C(M)`; the Fallow's spent-read books the depleting stock as the

  settlement's book.
- The **reader / Sense** ([`coherence-magic.md`](./coherence-magic.md) §2) renders a
  worked vein's spent locality, a ruined vault's shed orders ([`seed-garden.md`](./seed-garden.md)

- The **strata / archaeology** ([`field-archaeology.md`](./field-archaeology.md) §3) read
  a worked-out region's residue — the fallow's depletion is the archaeology-to-come,
  legible in the medium.

A player who never uses a "fallow" overlay loses nothing: the worked veins, the spent
vault, the pulled deep, and the flood-scarred regions are the same published channels the
Board and reader already show, read over the long horizon. **The Fallow is an idiom over
real channels, never a gated information source** — a window's depletion is never hidden-only.

profitable exit (§5d); **(d)** the [design] line — the fallow never reads as a new engine
term or a hidden economy difficulty meter, only as a designed aggregation of the landed

## Open questions

1. **The worked-deposit verdict.** Does the material-depletion drift return a
   *re-precipitating* vein (the locality heals its `q` and the law re-grows ore there over
   a long enough horizon), or is a worked deposit *genuinely* gone forever? The former
   would soften the first depletion to a slow-fill; the latter keeps it the one-way
   run-out this doc designs. **Determines the Fallow's core honesty.** **[probe]** —
   the material-regimes §7 gates.
2. **The spent-order's absoluteness.** Is the seed's un-re-precipitability literally
   absolute (the birth-state order is gone from the living field once it drifts), or is
   there a *very-slow re-realizability* — a window that waits long enough for its field to
   re-find a near-birth state can re-precipitate a near-order? The `seed-garden.md` open-Q3
   (the held seed's own drift) and the realizability gate decide whether the second
   depletion is a hard wall or a slow one. **[design]/[probe]**
3. **The fallow's relation to the fate.** Is a spent-stock window *necessarily* trending
   to the decay arc, or can a window read *spent* yet still hold a healthy bath (the
   rearguard's honest thrive-on-remaining-stock)? The fate's forecast-≠-fate discipline
   says the fallow is a conversation, not a sentence; whether a spent window can be a
   *thriving rearguard* or must read as decay-in-embryo is a design decision. **[design]**
4. **The departure's bookkeeping.** When a settlement books its departure (§4.2), is the
   loss a designed `Settled-by-Depletion` entry on the Board (the chronicle of what the
   window spent), and does the departing settlement's carried coherence re-settle in the
   next window or dissolve in the dark? Rides the window-relocation policy
   (`async-field-domain.md` §7 Q1) and the exile's severed-line persistence
   (`the-exile.md` open-Q4). **[design]** — later, with the relocation policy.
5. **The fallow's accessibility floor.** Is the spent-read (a window's *remaining*
   depletables, not its current `q`) a reading the existing channels can support without a
   new instrument — or does rendering "what remains to be mined" legibly need a
   depletion-specific read that risks becoming a hidden-only meter? The accessibility rule
   (§5e) demands the former; whether the worked veins/spent vault/churned regions are
   *sufficiently* legible from the Board+reader alone is a design evaluation. **[design]**
