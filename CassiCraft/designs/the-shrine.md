# The Shrine: The Built Remembrance — The Funeral's Register Made a Place, the Votive's Leaving Given a Built Home

**Question under design:** the corpus designs the funeral as the letting-go — the
procession that settles a dead member's dissolved line and re-binds their named
structures into the commons, spent under the no-free-energy cap
([`the-funeral.md`](./the-funeral.md) §2/§2.3). And it designs the votive as the
giving-to-the-field — an offering left at a place for no human recipient
([`the-votive.md`](./the-votive.md) §1), whose three placings are the spring's
welling, the bell's voice, and the named land (§2.2). But **nothing gives the
letting-go and the leaving a built home.** A funeral is an *event* — a coordinated
op that settles the line, then the line is settled and the mourning moves on; the
register it closes is a book entry, the re-bind a held state, neither made a
place. A votive is a *leaving* — an object or held order left at a place the field
already holds legible, and none of the three placings is a *built* receiving-place
built for the purpose. **The corpus has the letting-go (the funeral, an event) and
the giving-to-the-field (the votive, a leaving), but not the built somewhere the
letting-go is held and the leaving is made — the standing, deliberate,
remembrance-made-a-place.**

This document is that design: **the Shrine — the built remembrance, the funeral's
register made a place, the votive's leaving given a built receiving-place.** It is
the deliberately built structure where a leaving is made and a letting-go is held:
the place a settlement *builds* to hold the record of its dead and to receive the
offerings no one collects. It is the funeral's register (the settled dissolved
line, the named re-bind) given a standing, legible home, and the votive's leaving
given a built place to be left at. And it is honest about what it is not: **it is
not a hidden religion, not a power, not a way to reach or move the field.** It
keeps no power; it holds remembrance.

## 2. The built remembrance — the honest mechanics

The Shrine is built from the corpus's already-landed pieces, in the built-place
vocabulary the settlement already uses. It adds no physics, no channel, no write
beyond the Q4 ops a settlement already books (a build, a record, a leaving all
ride the settled schema). What is new is only the *standing composition* — that a
settlement builds a place to hold the letting-go and receive the leaving, and that
the place reads legibly on the instruments.

### 2.1 What the shrine is — a deliberately built, legible, standing structure

Imagine the bell-pit (`the-bell.md` §3.1 — a coherence-architecture chamber in the
house-that-steers vocabulary: a wall concentrates, a chamber holds, a corner pins),
built not to ring a warning but to hold a record. That is the shrine's honest
form: **a deliberately architected chamber whose walls hold the settlement's read
at a place, and whose room holds the funeral's register and receives the votive's
leavings.** It is a real, costed, stewarded structure — built like any house
(`house-that-steers.md` §1), drawn and aged on the map like any kept place
(`the-map.md` §3), read through the instruments like any legible locality
(`field-instruments.md` §2.1). The shrine is not a new kind of thing; it is the
bell-pit's built-place vocabulary pointed at remembrance, and the map's maintained
place pointed at the settlement's dead.

**[design]** The shrine's *form* — its chambers, its registering surface, its
built receiving-place — is this doc's design over that built-place and
map-stewardship vocabulary, exactly as the bell-pit's own geometry is `the-bell.md`
§3.1's design over the house's. What is engine-real is everything it reads: the
published `q`/`ε²`/`∇(g·Φ)`/ρ at its place, the settled Q4 op-stream, and the

### 2.2 The registering surface — the funeral's register made a place

The funeral settles the dissolved line by a coordinated op (`the-funeral.md` §3.1)
and re-binds the named structures into the commons (§3.2). That register lives, as
data, in the Board's book and the named structures' held-locks. **The shrine's
registering surface is that same settled record, read at a building.** It is a
consumer of the publish with a presentation idiom (`field-instruments.md` §2.1),
the way the Still Room is a room-scale Weatherglass and the Observatory is a room

Concretely — and honestly: **the shrine's read is a deterministic presentation of

the member's settled line the way the Board's book reads it, moved to a place.
The re-bound names are the named structures' held-locks (`the-funeral.md` §3.2 /
`the-name.md` §6e); a shrine that names the dead presents the same held-lock read
at its own place. **Nothing is re-derived, re-booked, or newly written by the
shrine; it is the register's existing settled record, presented at a standing,
deliberately built place.** The letting-go a settlement holds at a shrine is the
same letting-go the funeral performed — the shrine makes it *kept*.

**[design]** The *registering surface's form* — how the settled lines and the

### 2.3 The receiving-place — the votive's leaving given a built home

The votive is the un-recipiented giving, a leaving at a place for no human
recipient (`the-votive.md` §1/§3). Its three placings are places the field already
holds legible — the spring's welling, the bell's voice, the named land (§2.2) —
none of which a settlement built for the purpose. **The shrine is the votive's
*built* receiving-place: a deliberately constructed place a leaving may be made
at**, the awaited place of `the-votive.md` §7 open-Q5.

The honest mechanics of a leaving at the shrine are the votive's own:
- **The leaving reads as present and absent-its-giver** — the votive's
  deterministic giving-read (`the-votive.md` §5c). An offering left at the shrine
  is a deterministic fact about the giver's held state and the place's published
  channels: it is read as *left* (present at the shrine, absent from the giver's
  held state) or it is not, with no seeded giving roll.
- **The leaving converts nothing** — the votive's no-free-energy law
  (`the-votive.md` §5d; `energy-harnessing.md` §6). The order left is gone from the
  giver's hand, spent by the leaving's own `(1−q)` loss, and nothing comes back.
  A leaving at a shrine is the one economic act with no return at all, the same as
  any votive.
- **The leaving is accessible** — the votive's never-hidden read (§5e). An
  offering at the shrine reads on the instruments a settlement already reads; the
  shrine's receiving-place is legible, never a hidden collection.
- **The leaving asks nothing back** — `the-votive.md` §4. The field does not
  answer; the shrine receives the leaving and the field does not reply. A leaving
  made to *buy* a shrine's favor, a protected dead, a turned-away hazard, would be
  a false giving the field's indifference sheds (§4.2 there).

**[design]** The *receiving-place's form* — where in the built chamber a leaving
is left, how its presence reads on the instruments — is this doc's [design] over
the votive's leaving mechanics. The *leaving itself* is the votive's landed,
deterministic, non-minting act, now given a built home.

### 2.4 The built place's honest cost — kept, never powered

The shrine is a maintained structure like any the corpus builds. It is a real,
costed build (the bell-pit's architecture, `the-bell.md` §3.1; a house's geometry,
`house-that-steers.md` §1), and it is *maintained* or it falls — the map's honest
stewardship rule (`the-map.md` §3: a well-kept place reads maintained, a neglected
one falls toward the attractor). Nothing about the shrine is free:

- **Building it** costs what any built structure costs (the house's geometry, the
  materials' pull from the field).
- **Keeping it** costs what any maintained place costs — the map's re-read-and-
  redraw stewardship (§3), the house's upkeep discipline.
- **It holds none of that as stored power.** The shrine's held order — the record,
  the leavings — is *spent and kept*, never converted. The no-free-energy cap
  (`energy-harnessing.md` §6) holds through every act at the shrine exactly as
  through the letting-go it houses (`the-funeral.md` §2.3) and the leaving it
  receives (`the-votive.md` §5d).

The shrine does not *do* anything to the field. It is a place where the settlement
*holds* the record of its dead and *receives* the offerings no one collects. Its
only honest output is the standing legibility of that remembrance — which is
information, never energy, and which it presents by reading the settled book and
the published channels it already holds.

## 3. The load-bearing design — why the shrine is remembrance, not power

The load-bearing design, stated once and never blurred:

> **The Shrine is the built receiving-place and the standing register: the votive's
> leaving given a built home and the funeral's register made a place.** Two landed
> acts — the letting-go (the funeral, `the-funeral.md` §2) and the giving-to-the-
> field (the votive, `the-votive.md` §3) — are both, in the corpus, *unhoused*: the
> funeral is an event that closes and moves on; the votive is a leaving at a place
> the field already holds, none of which was built for it. **The Shrine is the
> standing, deliberately built home of both** — the letting-go held as a legible
> record, the leaving made at a built place. **And the whole is remembrance, never
> power:** it holds the record of the dead and receives the offerings; it does not
> answer, reward, bless, protect, or resurrect. The field it stands in is
> indifferent, deterministic, and legible (`the-dispute.md` §2), so a shrine that
> reached or moved it would be a false shrine the indifference sheds.

Three honest reasons this is the design's shape, and not a power worship:

1. **The funeral's register is a record, and a record is a place it can be made.**
   The funeral settles a line and re-binds a name; both are data the corpus already
   books (`the-funeral.md` §3.1/§3.2; `the-census.md` §2.3). A register made a
   place is the honest reframing: the settlement *builds* to keep the letting-go
   standing, exactly as it builds a hearth to keep a room's `q`-core. There is no
   power in it — only a kept, legible record where a letting-go was held.

2. **The votive already asks for a place, and a built place is an honest one.**
   The votive leaves an offering at a place the field holds legible (`the-votive.md`
   §2.2/§3). A settlement may *build* such a place — a deliberate receiving-place —
   and a leaving made there is still the votive's own deterministic, non-minting
   act, distinct from the more-laborious leaving at the welling or the named land
   only by being *built for the purpose*. The votive's open-Q5 named this exact
   place; this doc resolves it.

3. **The indifference the field has toward a shrine is the same indifference it
   has toward everything.** The field does not notice the shrine, does not favor
   it, does not protect what is left in it. That is not a defect the design papers
   over; it is the load-bearing honesty. A shrine keeps nothing the field holds
   more than any other place, and answers nothing at all. **The shrine is the place
   the settlement built so its own letting-go could be held and its own leaving
   could be made — visible to the settlement and to any stranger, never to a
   power.**

> **The shrine keeps no power; it holds remembrance.** The field that a shrine
> stands in cannot be worshipped, prayed to, or bargained with — it is
> **indifferent, deterministic, legible** (`the-dispute.md` §2): a deterministic
> field has no will a prayer could reach, no favor a plea could move, no judgment a
> bargain could buy. **A "shrine that answered" — that returned favor, weather, or
> fortune to its offerings — would be a false shrine the field's indifference
> sheds.** The shrine keeps no power because the field is not a power to keep. It
> holds the record of a settlement's dead and receives the offerings no one
> collects, and neither act reaches, moves, or is answered by the field. **A shrine
> is not a hidden religion** — it is a built, legible, non-minting place, honest
> that nothing comes back from it.

The honest list of what the shrine does not do — stated plainly so no reader
misreads it as a withheld power:

- **It does not answer.** No offering, no prayer, no vigil is answered. The field
  does not reply to a leaving at a shrine any more than at a welling
  (`the-votive.md` §4.4 — a conversation with no reply). A shrine that seemed to
  answer would be reading its own ordinary legibility as a response.
- **It does not reward.** No leaving is returned as favor, protection, fortune, or
  a favorable read. The votive's no-return law holds absolutely: there is no
  offering that yields, no hidden investment wearing the leaving's shape
  (`the-votive.md` §4.3).
- **It does not bless, protect, or bar.** A shrine does not raise a boundary, turn
  away a Coda, or shelter what is left in it. The dead it holds are the field's own
  settled residue — static, dark, `ε²≈0` (a fossil), never a protection
  (`the-funeral.md` §2.3; `the-census.md` §2.3). The leavings it receives are
  spent order at a place, with no stored, recoverable power in them.
- **It does not resurrect or restore.** The no-free-energy cap
  (`energy-harnessing.md` §6) is the funeral's own hard bound (`the-funeral.md`
  §2.3 — a funeral heals the shared wound, never mints, never resurrects). The
  shrine holds no resurrection; it holds the letting-go's settled record.
- **It is not hidden.** The shrine is a built, legible point of remembrance — a
  point of fame the way the spring is a point of fame (`the-spring.md` §3):
  seen, known, charted, and readable from the instruments
  (`field-instruments.md` §2.1). A shrine that hid its remembering would be a
  secret rite the design's own honesty forbids (§5e).

The one honest pair the shrine refuses, mirroring the votive's own refusal
(`the-votive.md` §4.2/§4.3): **the shrine is not a bribe and not an investment.**
A settlement cannot leave an offering "so the shrine keeps its dead safe" or "so
the dead's name holds longer" — the name holds by the field's own holding
(`the-landform-name.md` §2's honest decay), and the dead's line is settled by the
census's booked close, never by an offering. **Nothing left at a shrine earns the
left thing a thing.** The shrine receives the leaving precisely because no
recipient collects it; the moment a leaving is left to *gain* something for it, it
is not a votive and not a remembrance — it is a failed bargain with a field that
has no side to bargain on.

## Open questions

1. **The register's persistence — how long does a shrine's held record read?**
   The shrine presents the settled funeral bookkeeping; like the map's drawn lines,
   does the register's legibility *age* as the field moves (a re-bound name that
   sheds, a settled line that fades from the place's read), or does the shrine keep
   the settled record indefinitely as a fixed ledger? The name's hold decays with the
   field's `q` (`the-name.md` §6e; `the-landform-name.md` §4) and the map's ink ages
   (`the-map.md` §3); the shrine's register should honestly inherit one of those —
   a standing record that holds while the field holds it, read as settled-but-kept
   rather than forever. **[design]**
2. **How a leaving at a shrine differs from a leaving at the welling/voice/named
   land (§2.3/§5).** The votive's three placings are places the field already holds
   legible; a built shrine is a fourth, deliberately built. Is a shrine-leaving
   *indistinguishable* from the votive's other placings (the same deterministic,
   non-minting leaving, at a built place — the honest default), or does the
   built-ness carry a *designed* difference (a marked register of what was left,
   a sanctified-absence read) the other placings lack? The honest default is that
   the leaving is the same votive act; whether the built receiving-place adds a
   *register-read* (the offerings a settlement keeps track of at its shrine) is the
   design's dial. **[design]**
3. **Who builds and keeps the shrine — the membership line (§5/§6).** Is a shrine
   a *settlement's* built place (kept by the commons, its register the departed
   class at that place, `the-census.md` §2.3), a *family's* or individual's built
   remembrance (a personal letting-go held, `the-funeral.md` §3.2's joint-anchor
   scale), or both at the honest scales? The upkeep is the map's stewardship rule
   (`the-map.md` §3) either way; who is responsible for a neglected shrine reads as
   a real, honest question about whose memory the place holds. Inherits the
   player-vs-NPC membership fork (`field-npc-ai.md` §7 open-Q3). **[design]**
4. **The shrine's place among the votive's placings (§2.3).** The votive's §2.2
   lists the spring, the bell, and the named land; this doc adds the built shrine as
   a fourth. Does the shrine *replace* the other placings (a settlement builds one
   shrine and that is where its leavings are made), *join* them (a shrine is one
   more legible place a leaving may be made at — the honest default), or *collect*
   them (a settlement's shrine is the place its welling/voice/named-land leavings
   are gathered)? The honest default is the join; the collection read would give
   the shrine a bookkeeping role the votive's own un-receiptedness
   (`the-votive.md` §3) must not blur. **[design]**
5. **What a shrine's emptiness reads** (§5b). A shrine with an empty register — no
   funeral settled at it — and empty receiving-place reads, on the instruments, as a
   *built but un-used* place. Does that read honestly as "a place waiting to hold
   remembrance" (a maintained structure, `the-map.md` §3, kept even un-used) or as
   a *fallow* emptiness — a built place with nothing in it, the way a neglected
   chart is a fallen floor of ink? The honest default is the maintained-empty (a
   shrine is a kept place whether or not it has yet held a letting-go); the
   fallow-read would import `the-fallow.md`'s depletion honesty into a place that
   has simply not yet been used. **[design]**
6. **The shrine's weather-and-state legibility (§5e).** As a built point of
   remembrance in the field, does the shrine's own read *track* the field's health
   the way any built place does (a well-kept shrine in a `q`-healthy settlement
   reads bright and maintained; a shrine in a thinned settlement reads
   `q`-depleted, `the-map.md` §2.1's maintained-vs-fallen)? The honest default is
   yes — a shrine is a built place, and its field legibility is the field's own
   health read at that place — never a shrine-specific power, but the ordinary
   `q`/`ε²` read the instruments already show, at a place the settlement chose to
   build. **[design]/[probe]**
