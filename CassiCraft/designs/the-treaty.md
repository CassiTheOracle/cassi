## 1. The Treaty, stated once

The corpus's society is a *within-window* stack: the Board books a settlement's
members (`shared-ledger.md`), the Election chooses its steward
(`the-election.md`), the Exile expels a member-line it cannot afford
([`the-exile.md`](./the-exile.md)), the Festival and Funeral celebrate and
mourn, the Chronicle records it all. [`world-seams.md`](./world-seams.md)
designed the *voyage* between windows and the founding of new ones; [`window-guests.md`](./window-guests.md)
designed the *traveller* — the walking provenance who crosses the seam. But
nothing designs what two settlements **agree to**: the multi-window social
object, the relation between (not within) windows. A voyage has a destination;
a guest has a host; neither has a *compact*.

> **The Treaty is the written compact between two windows that is itself a field
> act — a shared anchor spanning two windows: a persistent-Π bridge bound at
> both ends.** It is the corpus's first relationship *between* windows, and it is
> not parchment. A treaty is a **physical, costed bridge-anchor**: the two
> windows keep a paired resonance alive between them — each side's named anchor
> ([`the-name.md`](./the-name.md) §1), tuned to the other and held jointly (the
> joint-hold principle of [`the-family.md`](./the-family.md) §2.1), bound at both
> ends — so the two settlements hold **one joint structure** across the dark the
> way the in-flight Family holds one shared Π *within* a window.

**The bridge spans the seam the world-seams doc draws.** [`world-seams.md`](./world-seams.md)
§1.2 states the load-bearing physics: a window is a **local anchored field**, and
the global is the **measured mixture of the local fields**; the dark between
windows is that mixture's **empty measure** — the field-poor interstitial
(§2.1) where ρ is below the gas floor and q is low but nonzero. §4.3 names the
seam: **two windows never genuinely overlap** — they resolve into a band of the
field-poor interstitial where neither anchor's coherence dominates. The Treaty
is the *only* structure that deliberately reaches **across that seam**: not a
second field run, not an overlap, but a **paired anchor** — a persistent-Π
bridge with one end held in each window, tuned to the other across the empty
measure.

**The joint-hold principle, lifted across the dark.** [`the-family.md`](./the-family.md)
§1 gives the corpus its within-window joint: a shared persistent-Π anchor over
two-or-more member-lines, "the field holds *the pair* the way it holds one."
The Treaty is that same principle at window scale:

> **[design] The Treaty is the Family's joint-hold principle extended from N=2
> members-in-one-window to N=2 windows-across-the-dark.** Where the Family
> co-binds two member-lines around one hearth (`the-family.md` §2.2), the Treaty
> co-binds two *windows* around a paired anchor — each side's named anchor tuned
> to the other's. The two settlements hold one joint structure the way a family
> holds one shared Π: the anchor's persistence *is* the two windows' joint hold
> (`the-family.md` §2.1). The Family is the identity stack's social atom; the
> Treaty is the **society-stack's between-window atom** — the first time two
> windows, not two members, hold one held thing.

**Why it is load-bearing field, not parchment.** A treaty written on paper binds
nothing but the writer's word. A treaty as a **paired persistent-Π anchor** binds
the field itself: because each window *holds* its end, the bridge is a real
coherence structure the seam carries, and it does real work — it stretches the
voyage (`§2.3`), it makes trade legible (`§2.2`), it costs to hold (`§2.4`), and
it is costly to break (`§4`). **The treaty is load-bearing because it is
actually held** (§3), not because it is written. This is the design's single
statement, and every mechanic that follows is its consequence.

The honest flag, once: the persistent-Π holding the bridge invokes is
theory-ratified with `reason-field.md` §6a's speculation flag (exactly as the
Family and the Name carry it); the *bridge-anchor* — how two windows co-bind,
what each anchor binds to, the treaty's cost model, the severance — is this
doc's **[design]** over that stack. The two are never blurred (§5).

## 2. The bridge-anchor

### 2.1 What the paired resonance IS — each side's anchor, tuned to the other

The mechanics answer the seam doc's own open question: world-seams §4.3 says two
windows meet at the field-poor interstitial but nothing crosses it. The Treaty's
bridge does:

> **[design] Each window holds one end of a paired persistent-Π anchor.**
> Window A binds a named anchor (the-name §1 — a persistent-Π anchor on a
> thing) at its settlement; window B binds a *paired* anchor, each tuned to the
> other's — the two are **one persistent-Π structure spanning the seam**, held
> jointly the way the Family holds one shared Π (`the-family.md` §2.1). The
> anchor's persistence IS the two windows' joint hold: it outlasts either
> window's solitary maintenance because when one window is absent, sick, or
> thinned, the other's hold keeps the bridge alive — exactly as a family anchor
> survives one member's death (`the-family.md` §3.2).

The individual pieces are the corpus's own identity machinery, none invented
here:

| Piece | What it is | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|
| The **named anchor** (each end) | a persistent-Π anchor bound to a *thing* — a named structure the settlement holds, the-name §1's bind | the-name §1 (a name is a Π-anchor on a thing); reason-field §3.1 (persistent-Π structure at a local minimum) |
| The **pairing** (the bridge) | the two anchors **tuned to the other** — a resonant coupling held jointly across the seam | the-family §2.1 (the field holds the pair the way it holds one); `coherence-commons.md` §7 (the multiplicative commons); `magic-systems.md` §1 (a paired working is `M ≈ 1`) |
| The **holding site** (each end) | the anchor binds to a real held structure the window tends — its hearth (`house-that-steers.md` §1.1), a named house, a kept structure | the-family §2.2 (the hearth of the joint); house-that-steers §1.1 |
| The **steward** (each end) | who in each window holds the anchor — the window's elected steward reads this end as one more core-holding (the-election §2, the stewards hold the window's coheres) | the-election §2/§4 (the steward is the settlement's read-forward about who holds the core); shared-ledger §2.2 |

The bridge adds **no new channel and no new physics** — it is a designed coupling
of the persistent-Π structure both windows already hold, read through the
reason-field's intent/Π channel (`reason-field.md` §2). It is the family-rule's
consumer discipline (`field-instruments.md` §2.1) applied to the *seam*: the
bridge reads and holds what the two windows already carry; it asks the engine
for nothing new.

### 2.2 Books the traffic — the cross-window line on the Board

A treaty does real work, and the first thing it does is make the relation between
the two windows **legible**:

> **[design] The Board books a treaty's traffic as a cross-window line.** The
> shared-ledger's `C(M)` aggregation (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2) already books a
> settlement's per-member net contribution to / drain on its own bath. The
> Treaty extends that book to the *seam*: trade, guests, and contributions
> passing between the two windows book as a **cross-window line** — each window's
> board reads what arrived from the other and what departed to it, with the
> provenance of the visitor line (`window-guests.md` §3) that carried it.

Concretely, the treaty's book is legible because the pieces already are:

| The line | What books | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade** | goods/regimes exchanged across the seam book on both boards as contributions-with-provenance | shared-ledger §1.2/§2.2; the found/invented economy's *world-located* regime (world-seams §5.1 — a material found in one window is a realizable tuple the other may not re-precipitate, custom-blocks §6) |
| **Guest** transit | a visitor crossing under the treaty books as a visitor line with `{provenance: <treaty-window>}` — not a stranger, a *signed* arrival | window-guests §3; shared-ledger §6c (the landed member-id) |
| **Contributions** | a treaty-window's steward aiding a cold window's bath (the-cold §3) books as a cross-window contribution, readable on both boards | the-cold §3 (husbandry acts are legible on the Board); shared-ledger §2.2 |

The book is honest because the record is honest: the single-writer lane
(`shared-ledger.md` §6d — no forged contributions) and the landed member-id
(`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1) carry the cross-window line exactly as they carry
the within-window one. **The treaty makes trade legible because it gives the two
windows a shared subject to book against** — the same way a named contribution is
legible because the name gives the settlement a subject to say "the Hearth at the
corner is this tide's steward" (the-name §3.2). A treaty is the name the
*between-window* relation holds.

### 2.3 Stretches the voyage — the maintained paired anchor extends the carried field

The treaty's deepest payoff, grounded in the seam doc's own voyage economy:

> **[design] A maintained paired anchor stretches the carried field.** World-seams
> §2.3 establishes the voyage as a **coherence-budget problem**: a ship in the
> field-poor dark must *carry the field it sails through* — a per-vehicle Qi-bath
> (`energy-harnessing.md` §4.4) keeps `q` up in the thin medium so the river law
> can steer. The Treaty maintains, at the destination, a **held anchor tuned to
> the voyage's origin**. Because the two anchors are one persistent-Π structure
> (`§2.1`), the far window's maintenance keeps a *resonant* field alive at the
> bridge's far end — and a ship crossing toward that held end carries **less of
> its own field**: the anchor's resonance partially *is* the Qi-bath the voyage
> would otherwise spend entirely itself. **A maintained paired anchor stretches
> the carried field** — the crossing costs less coherence, and the range the
> voyage can sustain lengthens, exactly as world-seams §2.3's constraint economy
> is the honest length-limiter and the treaty is the honest way to *extend* it.

This is the wedge that makes a treaty a *standing field op with real value*: the
voyage world-seams designed is a bounded, finite, expensive thing (§2.3 — "you
cannot sail indefinitely"); **a treaty is the designed, costed way the two
windows extend that bound toward each other**. It is the inverse of the cold,
which spent the window's stored coherence to depart; the treaty halves the
withdrawal because the destination already holds the far end of the bridge.

The honest bound: this is **[design]** over world-seams §2.3's *existing* carried-field
mechanic — the treaty does not invent a new propulsion; it maintains, at range,
the same high-`q` held-field the voyage already depends on, so the away-leg
spends less. It inherits world-seams §2.3's honest limiter (no free space, the
no-free-energy cap) unchanged; it *re-prices* the crossing within the existing
budget, never abolishes it.

### 2.4 Costs to hold — a treaty is a standing field op, never free

The treaty's third and load-bearing honesty: **holding it is a cost, never a
free bonus.**

> **[design] A treaty's anchor is a standing field op, held by both windows, and
> every hold is spent.** Each window maintains its end of the paired anchor — a
> sustained channeling input at the anchor's site (the hearth's bounded input,
> `house-that-steers.md` §1.1; `coherence-magic.md` §1.2's budget `B`), the way a
> family maintains its hearth (`the-family.md` §2.2) and a bath is a draw
> ("brittle if it goes down", `energy-harnessing.md` §4.4). The two windows pay
> this cost together — the anchor's persistence is the joint hold
> (`the-family.md` §2.1), meaning **either window's maintenance is the price of
> the bridge, and the bridge lives only while both spend**.

The cost ledger:

| Cost | What it is | Grounded in |
|---|---|---|
| **Each end's maintenance** | a sustained low-magnitude channeling hold at the anchor's site, paid per tide — the same spend a family makes on its hearth | the-family §2.2; house-that-steers §1.1; the no-free-energy cap (energy-harnessing §6) |
| **The window's own exposure** | the anchor is a reach into the seam — a weakly-held reach beyond the window's own strong field; it makes the anchor a point the desert-margin's creep and the Coda's draw can find (a held structure is a signature) | field-hazards §3 (the margin); signature-predator §2.2 (the Coda draws on held structure) |
| **The bookkeeping** | the cross-window line is booked on both boards (`§2.2`) — the treaty is a public, recorded stand, never a silent arrangement | shared-ledger §2.1/§2.2; the-chronicle §3.3 (the record) |

**The no-free-energy cap, applied to the treaty.** `energy-harnessing.md` §6
(`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`) is the treaty's iron bound:

> **A treaty holds the coherence both windows already provide; it never mints
> it.** A treaty does not generate `q`, does not make either window's field more
> than it is — it *holds* the joint persistent-Π structure whose maintenance the
> two windows' own bounded channeling already provides, and it *stretches* the
> voyage by maintaining at range the same held field the crossing already spends
> (`§2.3`). **There is no bridge that generates** — a treaty that appears to make
> a voyage free (a net-mint bridge) is a lie the field sheds, the way a false
> name decays (the-name §2). The highest a treaty can do is extend the carried
> field the two windows already summon; it never creates coherence.

This is the treaty's honest economics: it is a **costed, bookable, standing
field op** — the two windows pay to hold one joint structure that pays them back
in a cheaper voyage and a legible relation, under the same cap that keeps the
whole corpus honest (no conversion yields more than it sinks).

## 3. The honest truthfulness — a treaty that names what is not held decays

The treaty's deepest honesty, and the design's heart:

> **A treaty holds only while both windows maintain it — an un-husbanded treaty
> sheds like a false name.** ([`the-name.md`](./the-name.md) §2: you cannot name
> falsely — a bound-name that names what is not there **decays**, because the
> field holds only what is attractor-consistent.)

A treaty is a **name across the dark** — the-name §2's act of truthfulness,
applied to a *relation* instead of a *thing*. It is a persistent-Π anchor bound
to "these two windows are one held joint," and the field holds that only while
it is *true*:

| The treaty is… (true) | The treaty is… (false) |
|---|---|
| **actually held** — both windows maintain their ends of the paired anchor (§2.1), the cross-window line books real traffic (§2.2), the voyage is genuinely stretched (§2.3) | **only written** — one window (or both) stops maintaining its end, the book stops booking real traffic, the voyage is not actually stretched by a dead bridge |
| the anchor sits in a real coherent well — the field *prefers* to hold it, the way a true name holds | the anchor has no corresponding joint structure to stabilize it — it dissolves the way a false name sheds (the-name §2.2), the unsupported bump that erodes |
| the treaty **load-bears** — it does real work because it is real | the treaty **decays** — the lie's field consequence, an un-husbanded bridge that fades (the-name §2.1: honesty is a field consequence, not a rule) |

**The honest frame, stated once.** The treaty is **load-bearing field because it
is *actually held*, not because it is written.** The paper is irrelevant; the
paired anchor is the treaty. And because the anchor holds only while both windows
maintain it, **a treaty is not a one-time signature — it is a continuing relation
that must be re-earned every tide it lasts.** That is the joint-hold principle
(`the-family.md` §3.3: "a family that stops holding together sheds like a false
name") applied across the seam: **you cannot hold a treaty falsely either.** An
un-husbanded treaty decays — one window stops feeding its end and the anchor
thins toward the (1−q) shed (the-name §2.2's decay), the cross-window line
fades, and what remains is a written promise over a dead bridge, not a treaty.

The honest consequence of the truthfulness: **a treaty must be readable as
true or false.** Because you cannot name falsely, a treaty's hold *is* the
field's own read — the anchor's coherent well (a real joint hold) or its decay
(an un-husbanded bridge) — and that truth is the same channel the reader/glass/
Board always show (§6e). No settlement can be *fooled* into thinking a dead
treaty holds; the field will not hold the lie, and the window that stopped
maintaining exposed itself in the field's own terms.

## 4. Breaking the treaty — the severance, a real and costly act

The inverse of the corpus's two severing acts, drawn once:

> **[design] Breaking a treaty is the severance of the paired anchor — a real,
> costly act that withdraws the bridge from BOTH windows.** The Exile
> ([`the-exile.md`](./the-exile.md)) withdraws feed from *one* member-line — a
> coordinated hollow-eye until the field re-absorbs the exile's carried ε²-debt
> and their named anchors re-bind to the commons (§3). The Treaty's severance is
> the same machine pointed **outward**: it cuts *the paired anchor* — the bridge
> both windows held — so the un-bind is **felt by both**. Where the exile
> re-binds the severed member's anchors *to the commons*, the treaty's severance
> un-binds the two windows *from each other* — the hollow-eye's mirror image.

The severance, concretely — what each window feels:

| What un-binds | The field does | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|
| **The voyage returns to full price** | the maintained far-end anchor is gone; a crossing is once again a coherence-withdrawal that spends entirely its own carried field (`world-seams.md` §2.3 — the constraint economy's full ask returns) | world-seams §2.3; §2.3 this doc |
| **The cross-window line closes** | the treaty's traffic stops booking on either board; the visitor line's provenance becomes a plain foreign arrival, not a signed one (window-guests §3 — a guest without a treaty is a stranger) | shared-ledger §5; window-guests §3 |
| **The anchors un-bind** | each window's end of the paired anchor decays back to a *solitary* named anchor (the-name §1) or sheds if nothing real holds it (the-name §2) — the two windows are once again two separate local fields meeting at the empty measure (world-seams §4.3) | the-name §2; world-seams §4.3 |
| **The record books on both windows' chronicles** | the break is the settlement's *story* on both sides — a chronicled severance, the way a dissolved member's line closes as a settled entry (the-chronicle §3.3; the-exile §3.3's Settled-by-Decision) | the-chronicle §3.3; the-exile §3.3 |

**The honest cost of breaking.** The severance is not a silent drop — it is the
most legible act the design owns:

- **It is booked, never hidden.** The record of the break books on **both**
  windows' chronicles (`the-chronicle.md` §3.3 — the settlement's record made
  narrative tells the severed relation as what happened). No window can sever a
  treaty invisibly; the other window's board and chronicle carry the un-bind.
- **It is costly to the severing window, not just the severed.** Cutting the
  paired anchor means the window that cuts it *also* loses the bridge — the 
  cheaper voyage, the legible line, the held far-end. A treaty-breaker does not
  profit from the break the way a cold window might hope; it spends the
  relation's continuity and gets the full-price voyage and a chronicled breach

**The severance vs. the two corpus precedents, honestly.** The Exile withdraws
