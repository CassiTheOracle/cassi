## 2. The steward's passing — the honest mechanics

The Hand-Over is not a new system. It is the **designed composition** of the
settlement's landed pieces into the office-to-office transition, in four acts
that are each a real, costed, legible thing. The four-act form is **[design]**;
every act composes the source docs' landed pieces.

### 2.1 The audit — the outgoing book read to resolution

The first act is the **honesty of the whole**. The outgoing steward's book — the
running `C(M)` line, the commitments held, the choices made while they held the
core — is not taken on faith and is never whitened. It is **read to resolution
against the single-writer ledger** ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §6d
— the ledger records exactly what was executed, no forged contribution). The
audit settles three things, each read from the book the corpus already writes:

| The audit's question | What the ledger reads | The honest resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **What was run** | the outgoing steward's `C(M)` line over the tides they held the core ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §1.2) | the stewardship that *was* — the anti-corruption that held the desert at the trough, the held core through a thin tide, the contributes and drains, told as the record's own ([`the-chronicle.md`](./the-chronicle.md) §3.1) |
| **What was committed** | the pledge-lines the old steward held ([`the-oath.md`](./the-oath.md) §2.2 — the promises whose keeping books on the Board), the will's claims ([`the-inheritance.md`](./the-inheritance.md)) | the commitments are **named**, never dropped — a commitment the ledger holds does not end because its original holder ended |
| **What was left** | the book's closing — the settled line, the dissolved entry ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §2.2; [`the-funeral.md`](./the-funeral.md) §2.2 if the steward died) | what was left is **settled honestly** — the margin-causer's negative `C(M)` is carried, no amnesty ([`schema-that-settles.md`](./schema-that-settles.md) §2.1.1, the same honest exclusion as the inheritance), never a ledger reset |

The audit is the book **read to resolution** — the same honest ground the
dispute reads ([`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2: a claim checked against
the single-writer book and the present state, the field delivering the answer).
Where the dispute adjudicates *between claimants*, the audit settles one
settlement's own *ending*: the outgoing book read to its truthful close,
deterministic (same book, same ledger, same field state → same audit), and
unaffected by who performs it. **[design]** The audit's *form* — which rows it
reads, how it presents the three settlements — is this doc's composition over
the landed book; the *rows it reads* are the ledger's own, un-curated through
the archivist's raw hold ([`the-archivist.md`](./the-archivist.md) §2.1).

### 2.2 The book's re-binding — the audited truth, transferred

The second act is the **transfer's cost**. The incoming steward does not inherit
a clean book and does not inherit a whitened one — they inherit **the audited
truth**, re-bound to their line. The re-binding is a real, costed act:

> **[design] The book is re-bound to the incoming steward — the settlement's
> running state is transferred whole, audited, onto the new steward's line.**
> The re-binding rides the same-member-line rule ([`schema-that-settles.md`](./schema-that-settles.md)
> §2.1.1 — the office's line is a continuous subject, no ledger amnesty), so the
> incoming steward's book is not a fresh page but the *same* running book, held
> by a new hand. The cost is the re-recognition's own — the audit's labor, the
> book's re-reading, the transfer's real work — bounded by the no-free-energy
> cap ([`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md) §6): the book transfers
> what the settlement already runs, never fabricates a balance, never mints a
> continuity.

The re-binding transfers **what the settlement was running** — and only that. It
does not transfer the outgoing steward's personal will (that is the
inheritance's claim over objects, [`the-inheritance.md`](./the-inheritance.md)
§2), and it does not transfer a "fresh start" (a steward's *personal* record —
their own `C(M)` arc — is closed or carried on their own line, while the
*office's* running book is re-bound to the next holder). This is the honest
distinction the transition must hold: **the settlement's running state belongs
to the settlement and is re-bound; the old steward's personal line is their
own, settled by the audit (§2.1) and, if they willed it, passed by the
inheritance.** The book's re-binding is the settlement's continuity, never a
person's inheritance.

### 2.3 The commitments' re-recognition — the running choices, not reset

The third act is the **continuity's substance**. A steward does not only hold
the core; they *carry the commitments* — the pledge-lines binding the
settlement's promises ([`the-oath.md`](./the-oath.md) §2.2), the running choices
that were the settlement's working state. When the steward ends, those
commitments do not end with them. They are **re-recognized onto the incoming
steward's line**:

> **[design] The commitments are re-recognized, never reset.** The pledge-lines
> the old steward held are read, named, and re-bound to the new steward's line
> — the settlement's running promises stay running, held by a new hand, read the
> way the pledge-line always reads ([`the-oath.md`](./the-oath.md) §2.2). A
> settlement does not lose its commitments when its steward ends; it re-binds
> them. And the honest bound is the oath's own: a re-recognized commitment holds
> only if it is *kept* — the incoming steward's re-recognition is a real,
> maintained act over the tide (the treaty's re-earned-every-tide honesty,
> [`the-oath.md`](./the-oath.md) §2.3, inherited whole), and a commitment the
> new steward does not keep decays the way a false name does. The re-recognition
> is the old steward's kept lines handed on; it is never a promise the new
> steward is granted by the office.

The re-recognition is the honest opposite of a reset: **a settlement that reset
its commitments at every hand-over would shed the very continuity the transfer
exists to buy.** The re-recognition is what makes the hand-over a *transfer of
the running state* rather than a *rebirth of the settlement* — the running
choices handed on, kept or shed honestly by the new steward, never wiped and
never minted.

### 2.4 The continuity's legibility — the audit, the transfer, the continuity on the instruments

The fourth act is the **accessibility of the whole**. The hand-over — the audit,
the transfer, the continuity — is not a private ceremony between two offices. It
reads legibly through the existing channels, per the instrument family rule
([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1):

| The hand-over's legible face | The existing read | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|
| **The audit's resolution** | the outgoing steward's settled line and the book's closing read on the Board's public rows ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §2.1) and the Chronicle's telling of what was run ([`the-chronicle.md`](./the-chronicle.md) §3.1) | the ledger's lines; the chronicle's record |
| **The re-binding** | the office's running book, now held by the new steward, reads on the member-line and the census's active class — whose line holds the book is the census's read ([`the-census.md`](./the-census.md) §2.1) | the census's classes |
| **The commitments' re-recognition** | the pledge-lines, re-bound and re-read, read on the Board and the keeping's maintenance the oath books ([`the-oath.md`](./the-oath.md) §2.2; [`the-mirror.md`](./the-mirror.md)) | the pledge-line's read |

A settlement member who never "attends a hand-over" loses nothing the field
hides — the audit's resolution, the re-binding, and the commitments' keeping are
the same lines, record, and classes the instruments already show. **A hand-over
that hid its audit, or kept its transfer a secret of the offices, would be a
hidden transition the design's own honesty forbids** — the same never-hidden-only
discipline the corpus holds everywhere ([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md)
§2.1; [`the-census.md`](./the-census.md) §6e). The hand-over's legibility is the
settlement's own book and record, read at the office's end.

**[design]** The whole four-act form — the audit's form, the book's re-binding,
the commitments' re-recognition, the continuity's legibility — is this doc's
composition over the landed book, member-line, record, classes, and pledge-lines.
Every act composes what the corpus already writes; no act asks the engine for a
"hand-over" or "audit" record.

## 3. The load-bearing design — why the office-to-office transition is honest to design

### 3.1 Why the transition needed designing

The corpus gave the settlement a *choosing* (the election), a *will* (the
inheritance), a *record* (the archivist + the chronicle), a *book* (the shared
ledger), a *promise* (the oath), a *becoming* (the apprenticeship), a *chain*
(the generations), a *threshold* (the rite), and a *census* (the population
read). But it never designed the **office-to-office moment itself** — what
happens when the current steward ends and the next begins, to the settlement's
running state. The choosing picks who; the will passes what a person owned; the
becoming honors a line. **What the steward was running** — the book they held,
the commitments they carried, the running choices that were the settlement's
working state — had no passage. That is the honest hole this design fills: not a
new office, not a new channel, but the designed transfer of what a settlement
already runs, at the one moment the corpus had left undesigned.

### 3.2 Why the auditing is the honesty

The hand-over is honest *because* its first act is the audit. The outgoing
steward's book is not accepted on faith, and the settlement does not take the
next steward's word that the book is clean. It is **read to resolution against
the single-writer ledger** ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §6d — the
no-false-booking, exactly what was executed). What was run, what was committed,
what was left is settled honestly:

> **The audit is the honesty of the whole — the outgoing book read to its
> truthful close, never whitened, never amnestied.** The margin-causer's
> negative `C(M)` is carried (the same honest exclusion as the inheritance,
> [`the-inheritance.md`](./the-inheritance.md) §2.5); a stewardship that drained
> the bath is settled as a drain, not polished into a legacy. A hand-over that
> "wiped the record," that amnestied the outgoing steward's honest drains, or
> that handed the next steward a whitened book, is a **false hand-over the field
> sheds** — the same refusal the corpus holds against a false name
> ([`the-name.md`](./the-name.md) §2) and a false compact ([`the-oath.md`](./the-oath.md)
> §3.1). The incoming steward inherits the **audited truth**, never a whitened
> record — because the settlement's honesty, like its book, is single-writer and
> cannot be lied to.

The audit is what keeps the hand-over from being a *cover*: a leadership change
that erased the outgoing steward's drains would be a settlement wincing at its
own record, and the field's no-free-energy cap ([`energy-harnessing.md`](./energy-harnessing.md)
§6) and the single-writer book's no-false-booking ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md)
§6d) together make such a whitening read as the lie it is.

### 3.3 Why the settlement runs on

The hand-over's deepest design property is that **the settlement's running
state survives the steward who ran it.** The book's lines are re-bound; the
commitments are re-recognized; the chronicle's record carries the ending; the
census's classes show whose line holds the book now. This is the honest answer
to the corpus's recurring question about continuity — the same question the
generations answers at people-scale ([`the-generations.md`](./the-generations.md)
§1: the succession made a people) and the hand-over answers at office-scale:

> **The settlement runs on because its running state was transferred, audited
> and legible — never because it was reset and never because one person was
> irreplaceable.** A steward ends; the settlement does not. The book they held
> is not lost to the hand-over, the commitments they carried are not shed, the
> record of what they ran is not wiped. The continuity is the settlement's own —
> the same held order the generations tend across deep time
> ([`the-generations.md`](./the-generations.md) §1), tended *within* one
> settlement's span when its office changes hands. The hand-over is the
> settlement's way of saying: **we are not our steward; we are what we run, and
> what we run is handed on.**

## 4. The load-bearing honesty — what the hand-over is and is not

### 4.1 Never a mint

A hand-over converts nothing. `energy-harnessing.md` §6's cap (`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`)
applies as a hard bound, inherited unchanged from the Board, the Chronicle, the
Election, and the Oath:

> **A hand-over transfers what the settlement already runs; it never mints — and
> there is no transfer that yields coherence, comfort, or a freer past.** The
> audit's labor is real work over the book the settlement already books; the
> book's re-binding is a real costed re-read; the commitments' re-recognition is
> a maintained act — none of them adds `q`, none of them writes into the field a
> balance the settlement did not earn, and none of them frees the settlement
> from the ledger's honest lines. The **highest a hand-over can hand on is the
> continuity it buys** — the settlement runs on, its state transferred audited
> and legible. A hand-over that appeared to *make* the settlement worth more —
> a "fresh start," a "forgiven past," a "clean book" the ledger does not hold —
> is a lie the field sheds, exactly as a false name decays and a false compact
> sheds. **The settlement's past is not for sale, not even at a leadership
> change.**

### 4.2 The audit's resolution — never whitened, never amnestied

The audit is the honesty, and the honesty has hard edges. The outgoing steward's
book is read to resolution **against the single-writer ledger**
([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §6d), and the resolution is carried,
not brushed away:

> **The margin-causer's negative `C(M)` is carried — the same honest exclusion
> the inheritance names ([`the-inheritance.md`](./the-inheritance.md) §2.5), the
> same no-amnesty the same-member-line rule holds ([`schema-that-settles.md`](./schema-that-settles.md)
> §2.1.1).** A hand-over does not amnesty an outgoing steward's drains, does not
> white out a stewardship that pushed the margin, and does not hand the next
> steward a clean book the ledger does not hold. What the audit settles is
> *the truth* — and the truth, unlike a sympathetic hand, does not forgive on
> its own. A hand-over that "wiped the record" is a **false hand-over the field
> sheds** — the one act this design names as its own forbidden opposite.

The misattribution guard (shared-ledger §6e) binds the audit the same way it
binds the Board: the audit may name a *pattern* over the outgoing steward's many
tides ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §3.3 — never a single event,
never a scar the steward didn't cut), never a person off a single reading. The
audit settles what the book *actually* holds; it never invents a legacy in the
outgoing steward's favor, and never invents a guilt the record does not support.

### 4.3 The transition's distinct lines

The hand-over is **distinct from the election, the will, and the rite**, and the
design draws these lines exactly:

| The corpus's act | It is | The hand-over against it |
|---|---|---|
| **The Election** ([`the-election.md`](./the-election.md) §1) | the **choosing** — a read that points *who* holds the core, made operative | the election picks **who**; the hand-over passes **how the settlement runs**. The election is the read before; the hand-over is the transition the read hands into |
| **The Inheritance** ([`the-inheritance.md`](./the-inheritance.md) §1) | the **will** — the deliberate bequest of *what a person owned* across the re-lock | the will passes **what a person owned** (their trace, name, holdings, Board line); the hand-over passes **how the settlement runs** (its book, its record, its commitments). The two compose — a steward's will is the personal claim; the hand-over is the settlement's continuity |
| **The Rite of Passage** ([`the-rite-of-passage.md`](./the-rite-of-passage.md) §1) | the **becoming** — the designed ceremony of a field-threshold | the rite honors a **becoming** (a line's threshold); the hand-over transfers the **running state** at an office's end. The rite invests the chosen; the hand-over re-binds the book and re-recognizes the commitments the transferred running state carries |
| **The Hand-Over (this doc)** | the **passing of how the settlement runs** — the running state, audited and transferred | the office-to-office transition the choosing, the will, and the becoming all presuppose |

The through-line: **the corpus chose who, willed what a person owned, and
honored a becoming — but never passed how the settlement runs.** The Hand-Over
is that passing, and it composes the landed choosing, will, record, book,
promise, chain, threshold, and census into the office-to-office transition.

### 4.4 Deterministic and legible

The hand-over is a **hard-gated deterministic act** and a **legible one**. Same
field state, same outgoing book, same audit → same resolved book, same
re-binding, same re-recognition, every run — inherited unchanged from the
corpus's determinism (one PDE; [`the-dispute.md`](./the-dispute.md) §2.1; the
book's own determinism, [`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §6d). **[design]**
The *audit's form* (which rows it reads, how it presents the resolution) is a
designed dial — but once set, the audit's resolution, the book's re-binding, and
the commitments' recognition are a pure function of the outgoing book × the
ledger × the field state. There is no seeded-RNG "successor luck" in an honest
hand-over — the incoming steward's book is exactly what the outgoing book,
audited, resolves to. And the whole is **legible through the instruments**
([`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1): the audit, the
re-binding, and the continuity read on the ledger's lines, the chronicle's
record, and the census's classes, never hidden in a private office-to-office
hand-off.

### 4.5 Honest about what it does and does not do

The hand-over is honest about its own scope. It does:
- pass **how the settlement runs** — the book, the record, the commitments, the
  running choices, audited and made legible at the office's end;
- cost the transfer **honestly** (the audit's labor, the re-binding's re-read,
  the re-recognition's maintained keeping), bounded by no-free-energy;

It does **not**:
- pass **what a person owned** (that is the inheritance's claim over objects,
  [`the-inheritance.md`](./the-inheritance.md) §2);
- choose **who** leads (that is the election's read, [`the-election.md`](./the-election.md));
- grant the incoming steward **a fresh book or a freer past** (the audited truth,
  carried, never whitened — §4.2);
- add **any channel, write, physics, or mint** (it is a composed transfer over
  the landed book and lines, a consumer with a presentation idiom,
  [`field-instruments.md`](./field-instruments.md) §2.1).
