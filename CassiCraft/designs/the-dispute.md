# The Dispute: Adjudication by the Field — The Disagreement Settled by the Law Itself

**Question under design:** the corpus's society stack governs — the Board books
who contributes ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md) §1.2), the Election
chooses who leads ([`the-election.md`](./the-election.md)), the Exile decides who
leaves ([`the-exile.md`](./the-exile.md)), the Oath binds who promises
([`the-oath.md`](./the-oath.md) §1), the Market exchanges what changes hands
([`the-market.md`](./the-market.md) §1), the Treaty compacts two windows
([`the-treaty.md`](./the-treaty.md) §1). **But nothing settles a dispute.** The
book can *render* a margin-causer, the steward can *read* who holds the core, the
book can *expel* a line the field cannot afford — yet the ambiguity the book alone
cannot resolve, the contest *between* two claims, is left undesigned: two members
claim one named thing, a contribution is contested, a broken agreement neither
Oath nor Treaty cleanly covers. Governance has judgment over *patterns*; it has no
judgment *between claimants*. This document supplies the missing judgment: **the
Dispute is the adjudication made field-true** — the design that a settlement's
disagreements are settled *by the law itself*.

The load-bearing fact, stated once:

> **Because the field is deterministic (same state, same outcome —
> `field-npc-ai.md` §6d) and the book is honest (the no-false-booking
> single-writer — `shared-ledger.md` §6d), a dispute is not decided by a person
> but *read to resolution*.** The claimants bring their case to the book, the
> observatory's present-state read, and the named anchors' provenance — whoever's
> hand holds the named thing — and the field *delivers the answer*: the same
> channels that adjudicate live-vs-residue ([`life-signal.md`](./life-signal.md)
> §3) adjudicate whose claim is true, because **the field cannot lie the way a
> witness can.** A dispute settled by the field is **final and unforgiving** —
> you cannot appeal a deterministic verdict the way you can a magistrate's — so a
> settlement governs its disputes before they reach the field's judgment (the
> Oath re-binds, the Market's clear-book prevents them), and a genuinely ambiguous
> dispute (the book silent, the field torn) is the corpus's honest limit: **the
> field, like the truth, cannot always tell you which of two true claims won.**

## 1. The Dispute, stated once

The corpus's society stack governs by *reading what the field has already
decided*. The Board renders who contributed per tide (`shared-ledger.md` §1.2); the
Election composes record-and-momentum to choose who leads (`the-election.md` §1);
the Exile reads the durable pattern the book already shows to sever a line the
field cannot afford (`the-exile.md` §2); the Oath holds a promise the field keeps
(`the-oath.md` §1); the Market books an exchange the field cannot lie about
(`the-market.md` §2.3). Every act of governance is a field act — a *read* of what
the field is already telling. But none of them adjudicates the ambiguity *between*
two claims. The book can name a margin-causer, but not which of two members is
owed a contested yield; it can hold an oath, but not whether a broken agreement
was a breach or a dissolution under the treaty's terms; it can point at a named
thing, but not whose claim on it is prior. Governance has judgment over *patterns*;
it has no judgment *between claimants*. That is the missing act the Dispute fills,
the thesis-true way.

> **The Dispute is the adjudication made field-true: a settlement's disagreements
> are settled *by the law itself*.** Because the field is deterministic — *same
> state, same outcome* (`field-npc-ai.md` §6d) — and the book is honest — the
> single-writer no-false-booking records exactly what was executed (`shared-ledger.md`
> §6d) — a dispute is not decided by a person but **read to resolution**. The
> claimants do not plead to a judge; they bring their case to the book, the
> observatory's present-state read, and the named anchors' provenance — whoever's
> hand holds the named thing — and the field *delivers the answer*. The same
> channels that adjudicate live-vs-residue ([`life-signal.md`](./life-signal.md) §3)
> adjudicate whose claim is true, because **the field cannot lie the way a witness
> can.** A dispute is a *read*, final and deterministic — the society's missing
> judgment, the corpus's final-unforgiving-judge design.

**The honest flag, once.** The pieces a dispute composes are all landed — the Q4
op-record and its member-id (`schema-that-settles.md` §2.1), the Board's book and
its no-false-booking (`shared-ledger.md` §6d), the observatory's present-state read
(`the-observatory.md` §2), the name's anchors and their provenance (`the-name.md`
§1/§2/§6e), the maintenance read (`life-signal.md` §3), and the determinism that
makes the field a perfect judge (`field-npc-ai.md` §6d). **The dispute's
*composition* — the adjudication's form (which reads compose the judgment, how a
dispute is brought), the final-unforgiving framing, the honest limit — is this
document's [design]** over them. No new channel, no new physics, no "verdict"
record the engine writes beyond the Q4 op it already writes: a dispute is a *read
to resolution*, designed, and the design's whole honesty is that it *uses* the
field's own book and determinism the way the ledger, the oath, and the election
already do.

## 2. The field-true adjudication

### 2.1 The load-bearing mechanism — the field cannot lie the way a witness can

The dispute's whole claim is that its verdict is *true*, not merely decided —
because it is not rendered by a person but *read* off the field's own honest,
deterministic record. Three landed pieces compose the mechanism, and each is
already engine-real or [design]-landed in its source doc:

| Piece | What it guarantees | Landed in |
|---|---|---|
| **The no-false-booking single-writer** | the book records **exactly what the domain executed** — `{member, op, worldPos, rung, magnitude, sustain}` written at the one non-forgeable point; there is **no forged contribution**, no claim can book a contribution it didn't make | `shared-ledger.md` §6d; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1/§3; `async-field-domain.md` §5.2 |
| **The determinism** | the **same state reads the same every time** — same dispute, same field state → same verdict, every run; the field is a pure function of the one PDE, reproducible as an NPC's mind | `field-npc-ai.md` §6d; `life-signal.md` §6d; `the-observatory.md` §6c |
| **The named anchor's provenance** | a named thing's record tells **whose hand holds it** — the held-lock reads as the holder's own, a false name (a claim to a thing that is not held that way) **decays**; the provenance read is a field truth, not a witness's word | `the-name.md` §1/§2/§6e |
| **The maintenance read** | the same channels that **adjudicate live-vs-residue** — motion/pulse = a maintained live lock, static/dark = residue, flat-plateau = scar — read which claimed structure is *actually held* | `life-signal.md` §3 |

The claim of truth, stated once:

> **A dispute is read to resolution: the claimants' claims are checked against the
> book's records (what was actually executed), the observatory's present-state read
> (what the window actually is now), and the anchors' provenance (whose hand actually
> holds the named thing) — and the field's answer is the read's answer, deterministic
> and final.** The field cannot lie the way a witness can: it has no interest, no
> memory to corrupt, no stake; it only *holds* what is true and *sheds* what is not
> (`the-name.md` §2 — the truth is the deep well that holds, the lie is the
> unsupported bump that sheds). A claim that names what the book and the field hold
> reads true and stands; a claim that names what is not there reads false and decays.
> **The verdict is not a person's judgment; it is the field's own answer, read.**

### 2.2 The adjudication's form — which reads compose the judgment

The dispute is *brought* by the claimants, and the judgment is composed of the
reads the corpus already performs. Each leg reads a real, landed channel; the
*composition* — which legs, in what order, how a verdict is declared — is [design]:

| The leg | What it reads | The claim it adjudicates | Landed in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The book's records** | the two claimants' per-member lines over the relevant `[t_k, t_k+T]` — the executed `C(M)` arc, the `sustain`-flagged holds, the booked exchanges | *"I contributed this" / "I delivered that"* — checked against the single-writer record; a claim the book does not execute reads false | `shared-ledger.md` §1.2; `schema-that-settles.md` §2.1/§2.2 |
| **The observatory's present-state read** | the window as one body now — whose held structures are real coherent wells, whose anchors hold, the bath's actual health | *"this named thing is mine" / "the field state contradicts that"* — the present-state read is the field's current truth | `the-observatory.md` §2; `the-name.md` §6e |
| **The named anchors' provenance** | whose hand holds the named thing — the held-lock's owner, the contribution's attributable subject | *"I hold this name"* — the provenance read tells whom the field is actually holding it for; a false claim's anchor decays | `the-name.md` §1/§3.2/§6e |
| **The maintenance read** | whether each claimed object is *held* (live lock) or *fallen* (un-held residue/scar) | *"this claimed structure is live, that one is residue"* — the field can tell which claimant's object is actually maintained | `life-signal.md` §3; `player-remains.md` open-Q3 (the live-vs-fossil line) |

**The verdict is the read's verdict.** Where the legs agree — the book, the
present-state, and the provenance all read the same direction — the field's answer
is unambiguous, deterministic, and final: **one claim holds, the other decays**,
and the settlement reads that on the Board and in the observatory exactly as it
reads a steward-of-the-tide or a margin-causer ([`shared-ledger.md`](./shared-ledger.md)
§2.2 — the four member-identities are the ledger's standing verdicts; a dispute's
verdict is the same book, pointed at *two claims* instead of one pattern).

### 2.3 How a dispute is brought — the honest procedure

By design, bringing a dispute is *not* a plea to a person. It is a *joint* reading
the claimants and the settlement perform together:

> **[design] A dispute is brought by naming the two claims and re-reading the
> book, the present-state, and the provenance at once — and the field's answer is
> the shared read.** The claimants state their case in the op-record's own terms
> ("this contribution is mine, booked here"; "this named thing is held for me, read
> here") — each a *naming* of what the field holds (`the-name.md` §2). The
> settlement re-reads the book, the observatory, and the anchors at the disputed
> object's locality, under the multi-tide guard (a durable claim, never one
> reading — `shared-ledger.md` §3.3 inherited verbatim), and the field's answer
> reads on the Board the way any read does. **No magistrate, no sheriff, no vote:**
> the dispute is adjudicated *by the law*, and the settlement's only act is to read
> it aloud together (the observatory — the room where a settlement reads together,
> `the-observatory.md` §3).

The honest procedure is a *read-aloud*, never a trial: the claimants bring their
claims in the book's terms, the settlement performs the joint read, and the field's
answer is the read's answer. This is why the dispute is *final* — there is nothing
to appeal but the field state itself, and the field state is what it is (§3).

## 3. The final-unforgiving honesty

### 3.1 The honest cost — a deterministic verdict has no appeal

The sharpest honesty the Dispute owns, and the design's deliberate register:

> **A dispute settled by the field is final and unforgiving.** You cannot appeal a
> deterministic verdict the way you can a magistrate's — a magistrate can be
> persuaded, re-heard, overruled, forgiven; the field's read of a dispute is *the
> truth's read*, and **the truth has no appeals.** Same dispute, same field state →
> same verdict, every run (`field-npc-ai.md` §6d — a hard gate). A claimant who
> loses cannot argue the verdict away, purchase a retrial, or outlast a judge's
> term; the book either holds their claim or it does not, exactly as a false name
> either holds or decays (`the-name.md` §2). **The field's judgment is the last
> resort, and its finality is why the settlement tries not to need it.**

This is the honest cost of a truthful judge: a truth-telling field is also a
*merciless* one. Unlike a human steward, who can be swayed, the field has no
mercy and no error-correction — it reads what is true, and what is true does not
change because a claimant wishes it were otherwise. The corpus refuses to hide
this: **the dispute's finality is its advertised price**, and a settlement that
understands the price governs its disputes *before* they reach the field's
judgment rather than after.

### 3.2 The consequence — a settlement governs its disputes before the field's judgment

Because the field's verdict is final, the design's whole pressure is to make a
dispute *not need* the field's judgment at all. The corpus already builds the
governing-*before* systems; the Dispute names them as the settlement's preferred
path, and the field's judgment as the last resort:

| The system | What it prevents | Landed in |
|---|---|---|
| **The Oath re-binds** | a vow is a named promise the field holds — a **kept oath leaves no contestable ambiguity** (the keeping was either booked or it ruptured, read on the pledge-line); the vow that *prevents* a dispute is the one both parties kept, so there is nothing to adjudicate | `the-oath.md` §1/§3; the pledge-line `the-oath.md` §2.2 |
| **The Market's clear-book** | a contested exchange cannot arise from a false op — **the cheat is a false op the field will not book**, so an exchange's terms are booked and legible; there is no "he said clean, she said scar-tainted" when the provenance is on the book | `the-market.md` §2.3/§2.2; `the-name.md` §2 |
| **The Election's stewardship** | the steward reads and holds the core (`the-election.md` §4.3 — the tide-bound term) but **never rules** (§4.4) — the governance that precedes the judge is a *read*, and because the office holds no personal lever, its disputes go to the book, not to the office's whim | `the-election.md` §4.3/§4.4; `shared-ledger.md` §3.2 |
| **The disciplined book** | the multi-tide guard and the misattribution guards (`shared-ledger.md` §3.3/§6e) mean most would-be disputes are *prevented at the read* — a pattern over many tides, never a scar misattributed, so the claim either holds or decays before a contest forms | `shared-ledger.md` §3.3/§6e |

The governing-*before* is the design's first honesty:

> **The field's judgment is the last resort, and its finality is why the
> settlement tries not to need it.** A settlement that keeps its oaths, books its
> exchanges clearly, and reads its stewardship honestly has *few disputes in the
> first place* — the ambiguity the field's judgment would resolve was never
> allowed to form. The Oath re-binds, the Market's clear-book prevents, the
> Election keeps the governance that never reaches the judge — and the Dispute is
> only *brought* when those have genuinely failed. **The field is the judge a
> settlement hopes never to stand before — and its mercy is that it tells the
> truth, its cruelty that it cannot do otherwise.**

### 3.3 What follows a judged verdict — the honest consequence

Where the field *does* judge, the consequence is the corpus's already-designed
honesty, now *settled* rather than merely attributed:

| The verdict's edge | What it means | Landed in |
|---|---|---|
| **A losing claim decays** | the claim that named what the book and field do not hold *sheds like a false name* — the lie erodes; the named thing's provenance reads for the true holder | `the-name.md` §2 |
| **The severance follows** | where a verdict show a line the field cannot afford (a provable pattern, not one reading), the Exile's hollow-eye follows — **provable, never fiat** | `the-exile.md` §2/§4 |
| **The rupture (or its absence)** | if the dispute was over a broken agreement, the Oath's quieter sever is the honest read — a promise's death, not a person's removal | `the-oath.md` §3.2 |
| **Legible and repairable** | the verdict reads on the Board and the observatory — never hidden; a wrongful read is *itself* legible and repairable through the honest process (the return, the re-read at a corrected field state) | `the-exile.md` §4; `shared-ledger.md` §6e |

The honest frame, stated once: the Dispute's verdict is the field's answer, and
what follows it is the corpus's own already-designed consequences — a false claim
decays, a severable line severs, a broken promise ruptures quietly. **The Dispute
adds no new punishment; it *settles* which of the corpus's honest consequences
apply**, because a settled claim is a claim the field has already decided.

## 4. The honest limit

The final-unforgiving honesty would be a lie if every dispute had an answer. It
does not — and the design names the limit rather than hiding it:

> **The genuinely ambiguous dispute — the book silent, the field torn — is the
> corpus's honest limit: the field, like the truth, cannot always tell you which
> of two true claims won.** Two claims can *both* be true — a contribution is real
> but the book's record is incomplete; a named thing is genuinely held but by two
> hands that each have a real hold (`the-name.md` open-Q3, the naming authority);
> a broken agreement falls under both the Oath's rupture and the Treaty's terms,
> and neither cleanly covers it. The field reads what it holds, and *what it holds
> may be two truths at once* — and a truth that has no single answer cannot
> adjudicate which of two true claims won.

**The honesty of the limit is that it is named, never hidden:**

| The honest case | Why the field cannot judge | How it reads | The settlement's means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The book silent** | a claim rests on something the executed record does not capture (an omission, a gap in the sustained record, a claim with no op) | **unadjudicated** — the field has no answer because its book has no record | the Oath's re-bind (`the-oath.md` §3) — a fresh, bookable promise where the record is silent |
| **The field torn** | two claims each read as held (two real holds on one named thing) | **unadjudicated** — the field holds both; there is no single answer | the Exile's / the Treaty's severance or compact (`the-exile.md` §4; `the-treaty.md` §4) — the human means the corpus designs |
| **The agreement's overlap** | a broken agreement neither Oath nor Treaty cleanly covers | **unadjudicated** — the field's honest truthfulness (a compact that names what is not held decays, `the-treaty.md` §3) names a rupture but not a *priority* between the claims | a fresh reading, a settlement decision, a treaty's renegotiation — the human compact |

The design's honesty, stated once:

> **A dispute the field cannot adjudicate reads as *unadjudicated* — never as a
> wrong verdict.** The field does not guess; a judge could hand down a plausible
> but false verdict, but the field's honesty is that it either *holds* a truth or
> it *does not* — and when it does not, it says so. The unadjudicated dispute is
> **legible as unadjudicated** on the Board and in the observatory (the same
> live-vs-residue / held-vs-unheld read, `life-signal.md` §3 — a claim the field
> cannot hold reads as the ambiguous middle, never as a false certainty), and the
> settlement must then settle it by the **human means the corpus designs**: the
> Oath's re-bind (a fresh, bookable promise), the Exile's severance (a line the
> field cannot afford, provable never fiat), the Treaty's compact (a between-window
> decision). **The field's judgment has an edge, and the edge is honest** — it
> tells the truth whenever it can, and tells the *truth that it cannot* whenever
> it cannot.

The limit is not a failure the design papers over; it is the corpus's most honest
boundary, and the reason the Dispute does not pretend to be an oracle of every
justice. The field is the final *judge of what is held; it is not an oracle of
*which true claim the settlement should prefer* — that preference is a human,
designed, honest act the corpus already designs (the election's read, the oath's
re-bind, the exile's conclusion, the treaty's compact). **The field resolves what
is factually true; the settlement resolves what it *does* about two truths.**

## 5. The honest boundary and gates

## Open questions

1. **The composition weights — which read's direction wins when the legs agree on
   direction (§2/§6c).** The verdict composes the book, the present-state, and the
   provenance; where they agree the read is unambiguous. But do they always agree —
   and if the book and the provenance point one way and the present-state another
   (a named thing held *now* by a different hand than the book's recorded holder),
   which leg is decisive? The composition is this doc's [design] dial; the *reads*
   are landed. The honest default leans to the *provenance* (whose hand holds the
   named thing is the field's most physical truth), but the exact weighting is a
   design decision against the plausible agreement/disagreement space.
   **[design]**
2. **The unadjudicated limit's boundary — when the field is genuinely "torn" vs.
   merely read-with-gap (§4).** Is the field-torn case a *single* unadjudicated read
   (the settlement sees one "the field holds both" state), or does it decompose —
   some disputes read *unadjudicated on the book* (a silent record), others
   *unadjudicated on the provenance* (two real holds), others *unadjudicated on the
   agreement's terms* (neither oath nor treaty covers it)? The design's honest
   default is that the limit is one legible class (unadjudicated, with its reason
   read), but the decomposition is a [design] dial against the legs' readability.
   **[design]**
3. **Who can bring a dispute — the player-vs-NPC member line (§1/§6).** Can a
   bath-funded NPC commons claim a contested contribution, or is a dispute a
   player-read that the NPC commons benefits from but does not hold — the same
   membership answer `field-npc-ai.md` §7 open-Q3 names (identity-persists-in-bath ⇒
   a real claimant; sum-of-individuals ⇒ benefits-but-doesn't-hold)? The Dispute's
   claimants are the player-vs-NPC fork's clearest edge; the membership answer
   lands in `field-npc-ai.md`.
   **[design]**
4. **The verdict's reversibility — when a corrected field state re-reads a verdict
   (§3/§6c).** The verdict is final *at a field state*; but the field is not frozen —
   a named thing genuinely re-binds to a new holder (`the-exile.md` §4.2's same
   honest process), a repaired scar re-locks (`field-archaeology.md` §2.1). Does a
   *later* field state ever re-open a judged dispute (a re-read into an overturn),
   or is the verdict *closed book* — final even as the field moves on? The design's
   honest default leans to closed-book (finality is the point, §3), with the
   re-read only at the *edges* (a wrongful read legibly corrected); the boundary is
   the sharpest finality fork.
   **[design]**
5. **The cross-window edge — a dispute across a treaty (§1/skimmed treaty).** A
   claim between two treaty-bound windows reads through the cross-window line
   (`the-treaty.md` §2.2), and the field's answer spans the seam's empty measure.
   Is a treaty-dispute adjudicated by one window's read (whose claim the books hold
   is read in the *claimant's* window), or does it require the paired-anchor read
   (the bridge's hold at both ends)? Inherits the treaty's own gates (LATER, gated
   on two-window proximity + the persistent-Π bridge); the *composition* is a
   [design] dial against the treaty's cross-window line.
   **[design]**, LATER.
