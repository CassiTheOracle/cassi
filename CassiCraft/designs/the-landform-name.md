# The Landform-Name: The Named Land — The-Name's Scope Dial Taken at Landscape Scale

**Question under design:** the corpus designs naming at structure scale — a named
house, a named anchor, a named companion (`the-name.md` §1) — and explicitly
leaves *what counts as a nameable thing* as a designed dial: **the-name §5.1 —
"the exact scope is a [design] dial against the plausible Π anchors."** Nothing
names the *land*: the named mountain, the named river, the named region — the
landscape-scale Π-anchor the corpus never designs. A named ruin, a named
hearth, a named road all read at arm's length; nothing is named at the scale
the observatory charts, the atlas marks, and the voyage steers by. **This
document is that design: the Landform-Name — the-name's scope dial taken at
landscape scale — the naming of a landform (a mountain's Π-anchor, a river's
course-anchor, a region's boundary-hold) — honestly framed as the *resolution*
the-name §5.1's dial calls for, not a new hole.** It is the corpus's *landscape
legibility* doc: the named land that makes a window knowable at a glance.

## 2. The landscape-scale anchor — the honest mechanics

A landform is named by what the field actually holds about it, exactly as a
structure is: *a name binds only what the field can be said to hold as a
persistent structure* (`the-name.md` §5.1). At landscape scale, that holding
has three forms, each grounded in a landed piece:

### 2.1 A mountain — a large coherent structure, named the way a house is

`the-name.md` §1 is explicit that a name is the member-id's principle extended
from players to *things* — a persistent-Π anchor bound to the object, the field
re-quantizing around and holding *the named* the way it holds any persistent
pattern. A mountain is a **large coherent structure**: a landscape-scale body of
condensed, deep-rung, field-held order whose coherence the field keeps above the
dissolution threshold the way it keeps a house's `q`-core, but at a scale the
sites themselves organize around (the site map clusters where the field is
coherent — `chunk-field-quantization.md` §5, the 8,192 moving-Voronoi sites).
**[design]** A named mountain's Π-anchor binds to the peak's own persistent
order — the same anchor `the-name` designs for a house, held across a much
larger coherent body.

The honest distinction from a house: the mountain's order is **the field's own
condensation, not the player's built structure**. Where a named house sits in a
real coherent well the field prefers to hold (`the-name.md` §2.1), a named
mountain *is* such a well — deep-rung ordered matter, `material-regimes.md`
§4/§5, that the local field has itself accumulated. The name does not mint the
mountain's coherence; it binds a name to an order the field already holds. **The
player who names a mountain names what the land already *is* in the field — the
peak's persistent order, given a legible identity.** `[design]` The *binding* —
that a landscape-scale condensation is a nameable persistent structure — is this
doc's resolution of the-name §5.1's dial; the *holding* — that deep-rung
landscape order persists — is the theory's own.

### 2.2 A river — the conduit's own flow, a course-anchor

`coherence-highway.md` §1.1 establishes the **conduit**: a manufactured high-`q`
filament that makes a path the field's preferred channel for routing coherence,
`energy-harnessing.md` §4.1. The highway doc explicitly anticipates its
nature-scale face: `the-sea.md` §2a already designs **a river as a
moving-conduit band** — a high-`q` filament carrying *flow* the way a
manufactured conduit carries *coherence*, held by the liquid regime rather than
by a player ("a river is a corridor the field itself maintains, the highway's
§1.1 conduit where the field is the roadman"; `the-sea.md` §2a cites the
highway's conduit as its idiom). A river rides `∇(g·Φ)` at nature's scale the
way a road rides it at infrastructure scale.

**[design]** **A named river's Π is the conduit's own flow.** The river is
already a kept high-`q` line — a corridor the field itself maintains, flowing
along `∇(g·Φ)`. A river's name binds a course-anchor to that conduit: the name
tags the *course* — the line the field's own flow holds — the way a road's name
tags a kept line a settlement maintains. **A named river's name holds what the
conduit's flow holds.** The honest consequence (see §4) is structural: because
the river's Π *is* its carried order, the moment the flow moves off the course,
the anchor has nothing left to hold but the dry conduit — and the name sheds
honestly.

### 2.3 A region — a boundary-hold, named by its current boundary

A region is the corpus's most boundary-formed landform: a stretch of land marked
off not by a single condensation (a mountain) or a single flow-line (a river) but
by a *boundary*. `the-threshold.md` §1 designs the boundary-mark as "a
deliberate, built boundary-structure the field respects" — held, quiet, and
named, never a draining wall. A region is that boundary at landscape scale.

**[design]** **A region's name is a boundary-hold over the region's *current*
boundary.** The region is named by the boundary-hold the land currently carries
— its edge marking, its coherent margins, its site-map ridges
(`chunk-field-quantization.md` §5). **A named region's name holds the region's
*current* bound: the Π-anchor binds to the present boundary-hold and its
contents, not to a plotted territory the field has wandered past.** This is the
honest, at-scale form of the-name §5.1's "a name binds only what the field can
be said to hold as a persistent structure" — a region is held *as a bounded
place*, and its name holds the bound the field currently respects, exactly as
`the-threshold.md`'s arch is a marked seam that field reads as a boundary.

### 2.4 The landform-name's form — the scope dial resolved, honestly

The three forms above each resolve the-name §5.1's dial at an honest point,
grounded in what the field can be said to hold:

## 4. The honest decay — the load-bearing honesty

`the-name.md` §6e states the anchor's no-false-holding bound: a bound-name can
only name what is there; a false name decays, because the field will not hold a
lie. At landscape scale that honesty has a third face beyond the true/false
distinction — the *decay of a true name that outlived its land*. This is the
load-bearing honesty of the Landform-Name:

> **The name holds what the land held, and when the land changes, the name
> changes honestly.** A landform-name is honest about what it cannot hold: it
> is the-name's own honesty at the largest scale — a name holds, never mints,
> and what it cannot hold it sheds.

Three honest decays, each grounded in a landed piece:

### 4.1 The river that changes course — the course-anchor breaks honestly

A named river's Π *is* the conduit's own flow (§2.2). The river is a
moving-conduit band the field maintains along `∇(g·Φ)` (`the-sea.md` §2a; the
conduit, `coherence-highway.md` §1.1). The honest consequence is structural:

> **A river that changes course breaks its course-anchor honestly — the
> renamed landform is the old name's honest decay, never a lie.** When the
> field's flow moves off the conduit (a flood re-dissolves and re-channels the
> band, `the-flood.md` §2; the channel migrates), the named course's Π has
> nothing left to hold but the *dry conduit* — the old line that once carried
> the flow and now carries only its `q`-residue. The name does not lie and say
> the river is still there; it **sheds** — the old course-anchor decays the way
> any unsupported coherence sheds (`the-name.md` §6e, the false-name decay;
> `energy-harnessing.md` §2's `(1−q)` shed), and what was "the Smithwater" is
> honestly no longer that river. A settlement may name the *new* course a new
> river-name, or keep the old name as the honest archaeology of a dry channel;
> but the old name's hold on the *river* is spent. **The old course's decay is
> the name's no-false-holding at the largest scale — the name's honesty about
> what the conduit no longer carries.**

### 4.2 The region that grows its boundary — the boundary-hold re-binds or sheds

A named region's name holds the region's *current* boundary (§2.3, the
boundary-hold). The honest consequence:

> **A window that grows its boundary re-binds the region's name or sheds it —
> the boundary-hold's honest re-bind.** The region is named by the boundary the
> field currently respects. When the settlement grows — the boundary-mark
> extends, a new coherent margin is claimed, the site-map ridges shift
> (`chunk-field-quantization.md` §5) — the boundary-hold has moved. **The name
> honestly follows the bound it holds:** the region's name is re-bound to the
> current boundary (the settlement re-names what its held land now is — the
> same act as the map's redraw, `the-map.md` §3.1), or — if the old bound was
> the name's true object and the land has grown past it — the old name sheds
> that margin honestly. A region-name that insisted on a plotted territory the
> field has wandered past would be a false name; the boundary-hold's honest
> re-bind keeps the name true to the *current* land. **The re-bind is at most a
> return to what the bound already holds, never a raise** — the-name §6d and
> `the-pilgrim.md` §4's no-raise boundary, applied to the boundary-hold.

### 4.3 The mountain mined away — the spent named land

A mountain is a landscape-scale deep-rung condensation (§2.1). `the-fallow.md`
§1–§3 designs the window's one-way depletion: **"you can steward a scarcity, you
cannot un-mine an ore"** (`the-fallow.md` §1) — a worked vein is gone forever;
the precipitation law does not re-precipitate a worked deposit
(`material-regimes.md` §3). The honest consequence for a named mountain:

> **A mountain that is mined away reads as a named thing that was spent — the
> fallow's worked vein at landscape scale.** A named mountain is a deep-rung
> body that *is* the field's stored coherence. When it is worked — the veins
> mined to their depth, the deep pulled (`the-fallow.md` §1) — the mountain is
> a **worked deposit**, a gone thing no husbandry un-mines. **The name holds
> what the land held; it reads what is spent honestly.** The named mountain's
> name does not mint the ore back; it does not pretend the peak still holds
> what a worked vein no longer does. It either **sheds** (the name cannot hold
> an order the land no longer carries — the honest decay of a spent named
> thing) or becomes **the name of the spent place** — a worked land that keeps
> its name as the honest record of what was taken, the worked vein's strata read
> as the name's own history (`field-archaeology.md` §2, a named locality's
> residue reads as *its* record, `the-name.md` §3.3). **Either is honest; what a
> landform-name never does is hold a peak that is no longer there.**

### 4.4 The through-line — the name's no-false-holding at the largest scale

| Landform | What cannot be held | The honest decay | The landed grounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **River** | the conduit's flow when it moves off the course | the course-anchor **breaks** — the old name sheds honestly (a renamed landform, never a lie) | `the-sea.md` §2a; `the-flood.md` §2; `the-name.md` §6e |
| **Region** | a plotted territory past the current bound | the **boundary-hold re-binds** to the current boundary, or sheds that margin | `the-threshold.md` §1; `the-map.md` §3.1; `chunk-field-quantization.md` §5 |
| **Mountain** | the deep-rung order a worked vein has spent | the name **reads the spent**: sheds, or becomes the honest name of the spent land | `the-fallow.md` §1–§3; `material-regimes.md` §3; `field-archaeology.md` §2 |

**The honest frame, stated once and kept:** the landform-name is the-name's own
honesty at the largest scale. **A name holds, never mints, and what it cannot
