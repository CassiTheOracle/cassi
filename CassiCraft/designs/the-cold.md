# The Cold: A Window's Winter — The Long Thin as a Season, Not an Event

**Question under design:** the danger layer has *events* — the storm, the
desert's collapse, BH accretion, the Coda ([`field-hazards.md`](./field-hazards.md),
[`signature-predator.md`](./signature-predator.md)) — and the fate of a window has
the *arc* ([`fate-of-a-window.md`](./fate-of-a-window.md)), the trajectory a
window's coherence economics follow. But the **long exposure** — the sustained
thin that is not an event but a *climate* — is undesigned. A window does not lose
itself to one storm; it is *worn thin by a decade the storms measure their worst
against.* This document designs **the Cold**: the window whose tide-times the
thin-tide decade into a lived season — a sustained period where harvest never
fully returns, every op wastes more, the vent backs up, the Coda accumulates
quicker, and what a settlement does *across the whole cold* is the real
difficulty. **The danger the event-mechanics never caught: not what kills you,
but what wears a settlement thin.**

**The Cold is the long-thin as a lived season, not a threshold event.** It is
the threat layer that the corpus's event-mechanics — each hazard a front with an
onset and a verdict — structurally miss. A storm ends; a Cold *is the year that
keeps raining.* The cold's difficulty is not surviving *one* thin trough; it is
*enduring a decade without ever fully thriving* — and that difficulty is
**decision-shaped**: hold the bath or stretch the pantry, fall the farm or starve
the table, endure or voyage. Every husbandry act is a real, bounded, legible
choice.

This is the corpus's **climate doc**: where the tide gives "when" a season
([`tide-of-the-attractor.md`](./tide-of-the-attractor.md)) and the fate gives a
window's trajectory ([`fate-of-a-window.md`](./fate-of-a-window.md)), the Cold

## 1. The Cold, stated once

> **The Cold is the long-thin as a lived season, not a threshold event.** A
> window that tide-times the thin-tide decade into a climate — a sustained period
> where the regional operating coherence `q` holds near the floor, harvest never
> fully returns, and the settlement's real difficulty is **not surviving one
> storm but enduring a decade without ever fully thriving.** It is the danger
> the event-mechanics never caught: **not what kills you, but what wears a
> settlement thin.**

The corpus's danger layer designs *events* — each with a front, a readable-onset,
and a verdict. The storm's `ε²` front arrives and passes
([`field-hazards.md`](./field-hazards.md) §2); the desert's collapse is a slow
consequence with a
terminal state (§3); the Coda precipitates toward a trail it can resolve
([`signature-predator.md`](./signature-predator.md) §2). Each is a *happening* —
you see it come, you respond, it ends (or it doesn't, but the frame is the
event).

The Cold is the class the event frame structurally misses. It is not a happening
with an onset; it is the **long exposure** — the year-shaped, decade-shaped
condition that *is* the thin tide's lived texture. It has no front to read and no
verdict to survive; its only "event" is that it *keeps being.* And because it has
no single moment, the game's event-mechanics — the readable-before-it-arrives
rule, the pre-registered probes, the hazard thresholds — can describe a thin
trough but cannot *hold* a cold.

**Two load-bearing anchors ground this. First, the tide's own honesty.**
`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §1.2 already insists that if the field's relaxation is
monotone — only heals toward the attractor, no oscillation — the tide collapses
to a **drift**, and the design "should prefer the honest drift reading over
forcing a cycle." The Cold is the same discipline applied to the *long* direction:
the field has real long periods where it is de-organized, and those periods are
not a single thin trough but the tide's *season at its longest.* **[design]** —
the decade-long aggregation is this doc's reading of the thin state held.

**Second, the fate's strain phase.** `fate-of-a-window.md` §1.3 names decay as
the arc where the bath drains and the desert-margin creeps — a *trajectory*, read
forward over tide-cycles (§3). The Cold is that arc's **climate**: not the moment
the window tips toward the field-poor state, but the *long season of weakness* in
which the decay's causes (over-reaping, BH draw, sustained overdraw, drift —
`field-hazards.md` §3.2) each become more dangerous because the field that would
heal them is weak (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §3.1). A window is largely decided
in its colds: **the storms that matter are the ones that find it thin, the
harvests that fail are the meager ones, and the exodus that ends it is the one a
cold finally justifies.**

The honest scope of the claim: **the thin-tide states are engine-real; the cold
as a climate — the decade's aggregation, the husbandry, the pacing — is [design]
over them.** That is the line §4 draws and never blurs.

## 5. The danger's honest shape

### 5.1 Not an event — a sustained exposure

The danger layer's rule is *readable before it arrives* (`field-hazards.md`
§5.1); every hazard has a front and a verdict. The cold **has neither.** It is not
announced by a readable onset and it does not resolve in a moment — it *wears.*
A player cannot "beat" a cold the way they ride out a storm; they can only *endure
it* — husband the bath, stretch the store, keep the margin's creep at bay, decide
not to leave — while the thin field slowly depletes the settlement's reserves. **A
storm is a test; a cold is a budget.** That is the honest shape: the cold's
adversity is not a discrete threat to dodge but a standing drain to manage,
and the difficulty *is* the sustained, unspectacular, decade-long task of not
letting it land as decay.

### 5.2 Decision-shaped difficulty

Because the cold has no front, its difficulty lives in its **choices** — and
every choice is real, bounded, and legible:

- **Hold the bath or stretch the pantry?** The bath's standing draw vs. the
  stored harvest's finite spend are two honest uses of the same limited coherence.
- **Fall the farm or starve the table?** A fallowed bed yields nothing but heals
  for the next harvest; a held bed yields a meager crop but spends the field's
  scar. The settlement cannot afford both forever.
- **Endure or voyage?** A window in a long cold is a window that leaves (spends
  its stored coherence on the departure), endures (holds the draw until the field
  returns), or decays (lets the drain win). The voyage out is a real withdrawal,
  not a free escape.

Each is a **bounded Q4 write** or a **read-only consumer** of the publish (the
instrument-family rule, `field-instruments.md` §2.1) — the settlement's choices
are legible on the Board, exactly as any field operator's are (`shared-ledger.md`
§4.3). There is no hidden "cold difficulty slider"; there is the field's real
thinness and the settlement's real, visible bookkeeping against it.

### 5.3 The no-free-energy cap holds — nothing in a cold mints

The corpus's single hard rule (`energy-harnessing.md` §6, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`) is
the cold's iron bound, inherited unchanged:

1. **A cold is not a reason to mint.** The thin field's meagerness cannot be
   "farmed" — the auroral collector's thin-tide brightness is amplitude-capped and
   net-negative (it taps only the `(1−q)` fraction that would be wasted), and the
   tide-farming gate closes the cold exactly as it closes the thin trough
   (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §5d; `field-hazards.md` §5.3).
2. **Husbandry is a cost, never a free win.** Holding the bath, stretching the
   pantry, falling the farm, timing the voyage — every one is a net-negative
   spend (or a withdrawal of stored coherence), never a source. **A cold
   settlement does not gain coherence by surviving; it *spends* less of what it
   had.**
3. **The settlement's survival is its stored and stewarded coherence, spent
   honestly.** The cold's difficulty is that the stores are finite and the draw
   is continuous. The no-free-energy cap is what makes the husbandry *honest*: a
   settlement that survives a cold does so by husbanding — not by inventing
   coherence.

**The mirror (reverse pointer).** This deficiency-half of the cap has its
abundance twin: [`the-flood.md`](./the-flood.md) is the cold's **opposite and
equal danger** — the same "not an event, a season" frame pointed at the too-much
instead of the too-little (`the-flood.md` §1/§4 — the long-thick as the cold's
long-thin; its anti-bloom husbandry is this §5.3's anti-corruption *inverted*).
The flood stress-tests the identical `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` cap from the other side
— nothing overflowing can be minted either (`the-flood.md` §3) — so the two docs
stand as the cap's both-sides proof, and each now cites the other as its mirror
(§5.3 there names this §5.3 back). Both ride the same `tide-of-the-attractor.md`
§5a probe, one as the thin-trough climate, the other as the harvest-tide's
surfeit.
