# The Cistern: The Held Water — the Built Store of the Spring, Water Kept Against the Dry

**Question under design:** the corpus designs the **spring** as the *living well*
— the fixed, bounded place where the field's ambient `q` rises of itself, the
point of abundance (`the-spring.md` §2 — the welling, the field's own
conservative re-precipitation at a stationarity point). It designs the **rain**
as the *fall* — the nourishing-but-wet weather that gives order back rather than
drowning it (`the-rain.md` §1/§3 — the flood's gentle twin, the harvest-weather
that feeds). It designs the **desert** as the *dry* — the thin regime, a window
living at the attractor's thin edge (`the-desert.md` §2 — the tide at its
thinnest, sustained). And it designs the **granary** as the *order-store* — the
settlement's everyday order-vault that holds the harvest against the lean
(`the-granary.md` §1/§2 — a store, never a mint). But it has **no built
water-hold**: no design for a settlement's *deliberately stored water* — the
rain caught and the spring-drawn kept against a drought's lean. The corpus *maps*
the dry and it *endures* it (`the-desert.md` §2 — the thin regime to read); it
never designs the built answer that turns a dry season from a mapped threat into
a *survivable* one. This document designs it: **The Cistern is the held water —
the built store of the spring, water kept against the dry**: a settlement's
deliberately built water-hold — rain caught (the-rain's wet fall directed into
the built hold), spring-drawn (the-spring's welling drawn into the built store) —
kept against a drought's lean (the desert's dry and the dry season's thin), the
**water-twin of the granary's order-vault**, the store that makes a dry season
**survivable rather than merely mapped**.

## 2. The built hold — the honest mechanics

The cistern is a **deliberately built water-hold** — the rain caught, the
spring-drawn, kept against the dry. Each half of the hold is grounded in its
source doc's real quantity; **the cistern's form — its hold's bound, its water's
draw — is this doc's [design]** over the built-hold landed mechanics,
probe-calibrated exactly as the granary's store and the spring's welling are
their docs' [design] over the landed channels.

### 2.1 The rain caught — the wet fall directed into the built hold

**The landed piece.** The rain is the nourishing weather — the regular,
sustaining precipitation that *gives order back* rather than drowning it: the
flood's gentle twin, the same q-surfeit source at a yield the field can take
(`the-rain.md` §1/§2.1). The rain's *wet cost* is real too: it *feeds* a plot but
*muddies* a signature — the fall's own mild high-`q` texture is a small extra
order that the held water and the walked wake must stand out against
(`the-rain.md` §3.1, the marsh's blur on a smaller scale). And the rain gives
back at the field's own yield, never a mint (`the-rain.md` §3.2 — the fall's
feed is the water cycle's own return, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` held).

**The [design] over it — the cistern catches the fall:** a settlement's built
hold **directs the rain's wet fall into itself** — the same high-`q` fall that
feeds the beds, caught in an enclosure rather than run off, its water's order
held against the dry. The rain does not *become* a store by falling; it becomes a
store when a *built hold* catches it. That is the cistern's first water:

> **The cistern is the rain caught — the wet fall directed into the built hold.**
> The fall that feeds the beds and waters the fallow's season (`the-rain.md`
> §2.2) is the same fall a settlement can *hold*: an enclosure set in the fall's
> path gathers the mild high-`q` water and keeps it, so the nourishing water of
> the wet season is the drinking-and-holding water of the dry. [design] The
> *catch — how much of a fall a hold directs into itself, over what bound — is
> this doc's dial over the rain's published `q`/`(1−q)`/`∇(g·Φ)`, probe-calibrated
> against `the-rain.md` §5b's nourishing-precipitation read.*

The honest bound: the catch is the rain's own fall, at the field's own yield. A
cistern catches what a fall *gives* to a bounded place — never more, never a
fall the settlement forces, and never a rain-farm into a water-mint (the guards
of `the-rain.md` §3.2 all inherited: no rain-farm into a water-mint, the feed is
the field's own round-trip, the wet's blur is information-loss never a gain).

### 2.2 The spring-drawn — the welling drawn into the built store

**The landed pieces.** The spring is the wellspring — a fixed, bounded place
where the field's order *rises of itself*: the welling, the field's own
conservative re-precipitation at a stationarity point, drawn-and-stored distinct
from the harvested-and-stored (`the-spring.md` §1/§2.1/§4). A spring is a point
of abundance *and* fame — the known, legible source, the settlement's lifeline
and its liability (`the-spring.md` §3). And the drawn well's honesty is hard: a
settlement that draws a spring *out beyond its replenishment* spends the very
stationarity that made it a spring, falling toward the fallow's read
(`the-spring.md` §4).

**The [design] over it — the cistern draws the welling into the built store:**
the settlement's built hold **draws the spring's water into itself** — the same
well that feeds the settlement and the carry, its water's order drawn into the
hold and kept against the dry. The cistern is the spring's water *built-held*:

> **The cistern is the spring-drawn — the welling drawn into the built store.**
> The fixed well the field keeps full (`the-spring.md` §2) is the source the
> hold draws from: the settlement's built store collects the welling's water
> against a drought's lean, the drawn water's order held at rest the way the
> granary holds the harvest (`the-granary.md` §2). [design] The *draw — how much
> of the welling a hold takes, at what cadence, over what bound — is this doc's
> dial over the spring's published `q`/`ε²`, probe-calibrated, and it must hold
> `the-spring.md` §4's over-draw honesty: the cistern shares the well's draw, it
> never compounds it.*

The honest counter, inherited: a settlement that *over-draws* the cistern from
the spring draws the stationarity itself into the fallow's read — the drained
well falls one-way (`the-fallow.md` §2a). The cistern draws the well's water at
the well's own replenishment; it is the *store of the water the well provides*,
never a demand that drains the source it draws from.
