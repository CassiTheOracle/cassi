# The Compost: The Turning — Deliberate Decomposition, Spent Order Fed Back to the Field

**Question under design:** the corpus *takes* order — the tool bites the vein
([`the-tool.md`](./the-tool.md)), the farm harvests the crop
([`farm-that-feeds.md`](./farm-that-feeds.md)), the granary holds the yield
([`the-granary.md`](./the-granary.md)). It *depletes* it — the fallow's one-way
run-out ([`the-fallow.md`](./the-fallow.md)), the worked deposit gone forever.
And it *gleans* it wild — the scavenger fits the spent's residual
([`the-scavenger.md`](./the-scavenger.md)). But the corpus **never deliberately
returns it**: no designed act of *turning spent order back* — a compost heap, a
deliberate decomposition that feeds a depleted plot. The corpus has no "give
back what you used." This document designs it: **the Compost is the deliberate
re-turn of spent order — anti-corruption as literal decomposition** — a
designed heap/place where a settlement turns its spent matter (tool-scarred
steel, fallow-stripped ore, a granary's gone-bad grain) *back into the field* as
a slow-release feed for a depleted plot (a farm's starving bed, a marsh-ravaged
bank). It is the corpus's **first giving-back**, and the economic face of the
field's own conservation ([`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md) §3 —
the field re-precipitates; the Compost is the deliberate return of what the
fallow cannot).

## 1. The Compost, stated once

The corpus's relation to the field's order is, throughout, a *taking* with honest
accounting. The tool bites the vein and wears against its own `(1−q)` bleed
(`the-tool.md` §2). The farm harvests the crop at tide-high, bounded by the bed's
`q` and the tide (`farm-that-feeds.md` §4). The granary holds the yield against
the lean — a *store*, never a mint, bleeding as it holds (`the-granary.md`
§6/§5d). The fallow reads the whole window's one-way run-out: the mined veins,
the spent orders, the pulled deep — spent, gone, **what no husbandry un-mines**
(`the-fallow.md` §1/§2). And in the margins the field *itself* gleans the spent —
the scavenger, a residual-band run that fits what a window discards, "the honest
answer to what eats the spent" (`the-scavenger.md` §2.2).

**None of it deliberately gives back.** The corpus takes order, depletes it, and
— in the wild — lets the field fit its own waste. But there is no designed act of
*returning*: no heap where a settlement consciously turns what it used back into
the field it depleted. The scavenger is the field's own wild gleaning; nothing
is the settlement's *built* giving. This document designs that missing act:

> **The Compost, stated once.** The deliberate re-turn of spent order —
> anti-corruption as literal decomposition. A designed heap/place where a
> settlement turns its spent matter — tool-scarred steel, fallow-stripped ore, a
> granary's gone-bad grain — *back into the field* as a slow-release feed for a
> depleted plot: a farm's starving bed, a marsh-ravaged bank. It is the corpus's
> **first giving-back** — the economic face of the field's own conservation
> ([`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md) §3 — the field re-precipitates;
> the Compost is the player's way of helping it, the deliberate return of what
> the fallow cannot).

The one-line distinctions that place it against what the corpus already builds:

## 2. The heap — the honest mechanics

The Compost is a designed place where spent matter is decomposed and released as
feed. Each spent matter is grounded in its source doc's real quantity; **the
heap's form — its structure, its feed's release — is this doc's [design]** over
the matter's residue + the plot's depletion, probe-calibrated (§5).

### 2.1 The tool's worn steel — the `(1−q)`-bleeding alloy, turned back

`the-tool.md` §2b designs the tool's wear as the `(1−q)` waste law read as the
alloy's life: a high-`q` low-waste alloy bites clean and lasts; a **scar-tainted
alloy dulls fast** — the edge sheds its organization as it bites, carrying the
ε² of where it was mined. §3 lands the held-regime framing: a tool is the
`(ξ, ω₀², θ_c, n)` tuple realized as an alloy, and **"the tool does not hide its
lineage"** — a dull high-ε² blade *is* the reading of where the metal came from.

**The compost's first spent matter is that worn steel.** A tool that has dulled
to uselessness — its edge bleached of order, its alloy's `q` dragged down by the
scarred steel it is made of — is not re-forged (a scar-tainted alloy re-forges
dull) and not left to rust into the field's own slow residue. It is **turned
back**: the exhausted alloy, laid on the heap, decomposes — the worn steel's
residual order, the last coherence the `(1−q)` bleed has not shed, released back
toward the locality the tool worked.

**[design]** The *turning-back mapping* — that an exhausted alloy's residual
`(1−q)`-un-shed order is releasable as feed at all (that a tool's worn edge can
*return* rather than merely rust) — is this doc's [design] over the tool's real
wear physics (`the-tool.md` §2b), probe-calibrated on the Phase-1 slice. What is
landed is that a tool's wear is the `(1−q)` bleed and that the alloy carries its
provenance; that the *residual* order of a spent blade can be deliberately
decomposed into feed is the Compost's composition. The heap does not mint steel;
it returns what the steel no longer holds, at a loss.

### 2.2 The fallow's worked vein's residue — the spent locality, re-turned

`the-fallow.md` §2a lands the first and hardest one-way depletion: **a worked
deposit is gone forever.** The precipitation law grows ore where `q` accumulates
(`material-regimes.md` §3); when the vein is mined, the locality's `q` is
withdrawn and its `ε²` raised by the mining perturbation — **the condition that
made the vein no longer holds where it was worked, and the law does not
re-precipitate *that* vein.**

The fallow's honesty is that this is a *gone thing*: *you can steward a scarcity,
you cannot un-mine an ore* (`the-fallow.md` §1/§3). The Compost does not
contradict that — it cannot un-mine the ore, and never claims to. **But the
worked vein leaves a residue**: the spent locality — the `q` drawn, the `ε²`
raised — is precisely the residual band the field keeps and the scavenger fits
(`the-scavenger.md` §2.1). **The compost's second spent matter is that residue,
deliberately re-turned** — not the vein re-precipitated (the fallow's hard
one-wayness holds), but the *locality's* spent order laid back into itself: the
raised `ε²` of the worked ground, decomposed toward the scar's healing; the
small residual order of the stripped deposit, released toward the depleted plot.

**[design]** The *residue-re-turn mapping* — that a worked locality's spent order
can be gathered (as the scavenger gathers it wild) and deliberately returned as
feed at a loss — is this doc's [design] over the fallow's spent state
(`the-fallow.md` §2a) and the scavenger's residual band (`the-scavenger.md`
§2.1), probe-calibrated. The one-wayness of the *vein* is never violated: the ore
is gone forever; the Compost turns back the *residue*, never the deposit. This is
the honest line that keeps the Compost a giving-back and not a quiet un-mine.

### 2.3 The granary's gone-bad grain — the store's spoiled surplus, composted

`the-granary.md` lands the store that holds the ordinary harvest against the
lean — and its **bleed**: every held structure wastes `E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput`
as glow (`the-granary.md` §6, `energy-harnessing.md` §2), so **a full store does
not stay full forever unless it is kept.** The bleed is the honest cost of
holding plenty — and it means a granary, over the lean it guards against, sheds
order it cannot keep: grain that turns, stores that spoil, the surplus that
outlives its usefulness.

**The compost's third spent matter is that spoiled surplus.** Where the granary
holds the *good* yield against the lean (`the-granary.md` §2.1), the Compost takes
what the store lets go bad — the gone-bad grain, the spoiled store, the kept
steel that proved not worth holding. This is not a waste-mint (the granary's
no-free-energy cap holds — the spoiled surplus is a loss honestly booked,
`the-granary.md` §5d); it is the **deliberate re-turn** of what the store's bleed
already spent: the gone-bad order, composted instead of discarded, its residual
released back to a depleted plot.

**[design]** The *spoiled-store mapping* — that a granary's gone-bad surplus is
spent order *re-turnable* (that the bleed's residue can feed, rather than merely
waste) — is this doc's [design] over the granary's bleed (`the-granary.md` §6),
probe-calibrated against the same shallow-rung storage physics the bleed dials.
The Compost does not rescue the granary's loss; it **gives that loss back** at a
further loss — the honest floor of the whole design (§4).

### The feed — a slow-release re-turn to a depleted plot

The heap's purpose is the feed: **the decomposed spent matter, released slowly
back into a depleted plot.** Two plots, both sourced:

- **A farm's starving bed.** `farm-that-feeds.md` §5 lands the thin trough as
  the farmer's season — the bed whose `q` falls toward the crop-band's floor, the
  run dissolving unless stewarded. The feed is a **re-turn of the bed's `q`
  toward its crop-band** (`farm-that-feeds.md` §2) — the spent order, decomposed,
  raised the starving bed's coherence so the crop's run can hold through the
  thin. *Yield law held:* the feed moves the bed's `q` *within* the band's bound —
  it never lifts `yield ≤ bed's q + the tide` (`farm-that-feeds.md` §4); it
  restores a depleted bed toward the yield its soil could already give.
- **A marsh-ravaged bank.** `the-marsh.md` §3 lands the marsh as the shallow,
  textured water where the field is patchy — and the damaged margin a settlement
  may need to re-turn. The feed re-turns the ravaged bank's band — the patchy
  `q`-map nudged toward coherence where the flood or the scavenger's spent
  margin thin it. **[design]** — the marsh as a *depleted plot the Compost
  feeds* is this doc's composition over the marsh's patchy `q` (`the-marsh.md`
  §2/§3), probe-calibrated.

**[design]** The *release form* — how the heap's feed actually reaches the plot's
band: the decomposed spent matter as a slow-release `q`-feed, a measured rate
spread over the heap's patience (below, §4), the release's magnitude bounded by
the no-free-energy cap — is this doc's [design] over the precipitation law's
residue lever (`material-regimes.md` §3), probe-calibrated on the Phase-1 slice.
The *precipitation-residue leverage* (that the field re-precipitates where `q`
accumulates) is real ([`material-regimes.md`](./material-regimes.md) §3); the
*built release* — that a settlement can deliberately direct spent order into a
plot's band — is the Compost's composition.

## 3. The built giving-back — the corpus's first 'give back what you used'

This is the load-bearing design, stated once and never blurred:

> **The corpus has taken order (the tool, the farm, the granary), depleted it
> (the fallow's one-way run-out), and gleaned it wild (the scavenger) — the
> Compost is the first 'give back what you used': the deliberate re-turn of spent
> order to a depleted plot, at a loss, over patience.**

Three distinctions make it honest.

**Distinct from the scavenger's wild gleaning.** The scavenger is the corpus's
wild twin: a residual-band run the field holds in the spent locality's low-`q`,
high-`ε²` residue, "the honest answer to what eats the spent" (`the-scavenger.md`
§2.2). **The field does this itself in the margins** — a residual-band organism
that fits the waste, converts nothing, is lawfully harmless. The Compost is the
**built, deliberate version**: where the scavenger's gleaning is a field event
the settlement *reads*, the Compost is an anti-corruption act the settlement
*performs* — a designed heap, a conscious turning, a booked giving. The field
fits its waste in the margins by itself; the Compost is a settlement choosing to
fit its own spent matter deliberately, at a designed place, as an act.

**Distinct from the husbander's wild-care.** The husbander keeps the wild —
a costed care of a patch nobody owns, ending in nothing but the patch staying
wild (`the-husbander.md` §1). The Compost is the husbander's **built sibling**:
the husbander keeps the wild (an anti-owner, the field's care without claim);
the Compost **feeds the spent** (a deliberate turning of the field's own used
matter back to a depleted plot). They are the same conservation principle
(`material-regimes.md` §3 — the field re-precipitates) at two edges: the
husbander holds the ordered margin so it *stays*; the Compost re-turns the spent
locality so it *recovers*, at a loss.

**The honest frame — helping the field's own conservation.** `material-regimes.md`
§3 lands the precipitation law: the field *itself* re-precipitates where `q`
accumulates. The fallow reads the one-way run-out — the window's depletions that
no husbandry reverses (`the-fallow.md` §2/§3). **The Compost is the player's way
of helping the field's own conservation: the deliberate return of what the
fallow cannot.** Where the fallow says *you cannot un-mine an ore*, the Compost
answers — honestly — *but you can turn the spent matter's residue back, at a
loss, to a depleted plot.* It does not un-mine; it gives the field back what the
settlement used, so the field's own re-precipitation has something to work with.
This is the first time the corpus's taking-and-depleting arc turns into a
designed returning act — the economic face of conservation, made deliberate.

## 4. The time-and-lock — the patience, and the loss

This is the load-bearing honesty, stated once and never blurred:

> **A compost is a slow hold — turning spent order into re-usable feed takes
> patience, and a rushed compost just sheds waste.** And the no-free-energy
> guard holds it: **the compost returns order at a loss — it recovers *less*
> than was spent — it is giving back, never a mint.**

**The patience.** `patient-field.md` §3.3 lands the slow hold: the field's
coherence economics play out over tides and strata-lifetimes; the field is
patient, and the player is the only thing in it that is not. A compost is the
spent world's own patience made a practice: **decomposition is a slow re-turn** —
the spent matter's residual order releases over the heap's patience, the length
of the fallow's own horizon, never an instant. **A compost rushed is a compost
wasted**: tear the heap before its turning is done — pull the feed early, break
the decomposition before the spent order has released — and the heap sheds the
still-un-returned order as waste, exactly the feral shed the field's own law
describes (`energy-harnessing.md` §2 — the `(1−q)` waste never stops). The
patience is the cost: the slow hold is what separates a true re-turn from a
hurried shedding, and it is deliberately the time-and-lock that gives the
compost its honest economics.

**The loss.** `energy-harnessing.md` §6's hard rule — **no conversion yields more
than it sinks, `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`** — holds the compost exactly, stated once:

> **The compost returns order at a loss — it recovers *less* than was spent.**
> The spent matter that went in (a tool's worn steel, a worked vein's residue, a
> granary's gone-bad grain) releases back a *smaller* residual order than it
> held — the `(1−q)` waste law, `E_waste = (1−q)·E_throughput`
> (`energy-harnessing.md` §2), bleeds through the whole turning, and the feed
> the depleted plot receives is `output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` of the spent matter's order.
> **The compost is giving back, never a mint** — a compost that appears to *make*
> the spent matter worth more — that returns a feed richer than the waste it was
> made from, that turns a heap into a profit — is a **false heap the field sheds**,
> exactly as a house that "generates" `q` is a lie (`house-that-steers.md` §5d)
> and a granary that "makes" the harvest worth more is a false store
> (`the-granary.md` §5d).

The honest consequence, concrete: **a compost is a net-negative act by design.**
It takes spent matter and returns *less* of it, over *longer* patience, to a
depleted plot — the value is that the *plot recovers toward the yield it could
already give* (`farm-that-feeds.md` §4), not that the compost mints anything.
A settlement feeds a compost because it wants the bed back, not because the heap
pays. The `(1−q)` bleed through the turning, the patience's duration, and the
`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input` bound are the three honest costs that keep the compost a
giving-back and never a false economy.

Three concrete guards, all inherited: **(1)** no compost that yields — the feed is
