## 1. The Tide-Staff, stated once

The corpus reads the tide four ways ([`tide-of-the-attractor.md`](./tide-of-the-attractor.md) §4):
the **reader** (a handheld scan of the regional-`q` trend), the **sky** (the
aurora's atmospheric read), a **Weatherglass-style ambient read** (a mounted
trend monitor — the tide doc's named "[design] extension of the reader into a
dedicated season instrument"), and the **`(1−q)` glow floor** (the world burning
bright-and-wasteful at thin). Four channels — and every one is either *carried*
(the reader, the Weatherglass worn) or *ambient at range* (the sky) or *mounted
inside a room* (the Observatory's composed display). **Nothing is planted at a
place to read the phase across seasons.** The Tide-Staff is that planted thing.

> **The Tide-Staff, stated once.** The planted standing gauge — a physical
> cast-`q` staff rooted at a settlement's place (the observatory's yard, the
> farm's edge) that reads the tide's phase at *that place* over the long run. A
> cast-`q` band marks the current season (harvest / thin — the tide's two
> states); the staff's rise/fall shows the drift toward harvest or thin (the
> regional-`q` trend — the accumulation the probe measures). It is the tide doc's
> observability made a **physical, shared object**: the standing, passive,
> communal twin of the carried glass ([`the-hourglass.md`](./the-hourglass.md)).
> It is a **planted sample-at-position** of the published `q` — a standing
> consumer, never a new channel — and because it is planted it **ages**,
> carrying the cold's deep run and the fate's drift as marks a settlement walks
> past and reads.

The one-line distinction from the carried glass, its direct sibling — the same
temporal axis, one worn and one rooted:

| Instrument | It is | It reads | Its scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Hourglass ([`the-hourglass.md`](./the-hourglass.md) §1) | a **carried** device | the local tempo *accumulated over a chosen interval* (how long the moment takes) | the player's position, worn |
| **The Tide-Staff (this doc)** | a **planted standing** gauge | the tide's phase *at one place, across seasons* (which season, and the drift between them) | the place it is rooted in — the settlement's yard, the farm's edge |

The Hourglass is the carried glass that tells a player *how long the current
moment takes* at the local tempo; the Tide-Staff is the planted staff that tells
a settlement *where in its year it is* (which season the tide is in, and which
way its drift points). They are the family's **two temporal instruments** — the
carried duration read and the standing phase read — each a consumer of the
published `q`/`(1−q)`, never a channel.

The load-bearing honesty, stated once: the staff **introduces no new engine
term, no new channel, no new physics.** It reads the published `q` at its
position (the ≈ 6 MiB publish, `corpus-reconciliation.md`) — the same `q` the
reader scans, the Weatherglass lumes, and the Observatory composes — and
presents it as a *planted, standing, aging gauge*. What this doc adds is only the
**physical instrument and its idiom**: the band-mapping (which `q` values mark
harvest vs thin, the rise/fall's scale), the aging (the marks a planted staff
accumulates), and the design surface (a consumer with a presentation idiom). That
line is drawn in §5 and never blurred.

## 2. The staff's read — the gauge mechanics

The staff presents the published `q` at its position through three family-rule
forms (a consumer of the publish with a presentation idiom, `field-instruments.md`
§2.1). All three are **[design]** over the engine-real `q` and the tide's
[design]-landed states; the *mapping* is faithful to the channels the tide doc
and the observability already read.

### 2.1 The band — the current season

The **cast-`q` band** marks which of the tide's two states the staff's position
is in. The tide's two states are the naming the tide doc already lands
([`tide-of-the-attractor.md`](./tide-of-the-attractor.md) §2):

| State | Regional field | The band at the staff |
|---|---|---|
| **Harvest** | `q` high, `ε²` low, near the attractor (`q ≈ 0.947`, `coherence-magic.md` §1) | the band **reads full / high** — the staff's gauge sits in the organized phase (machines near-lossless, ore precipitating, the biosphere's basins at full strength) |
| **Thin** | `q` toward the noise floor (`q ~ 1e-3…1e-1`, `energy-harnessing.md` §7 Q1) | the band **reads low / empty** — the gauge sits in the wasteful phase (machines glow bright off `(1−q)`, basins shed, the desert-margin creeps) |

**[design]** The *band mapping* — which `q`-interval at the staff's position reads
as "harvest" vs "thin," and where the season's threshold (the moment the regional
operating coherence tips from one to the other) sits — is this doc's choice over
the tide's two states, **probe-calibrated** against the tide probe's measured
trend (§5a) and the thin-floor discipline of `tide-of-the-attractor.md` §5b (the
trough must read *wasteful*, never *deserted*). This is exactly how `farm-that-feeds.md`
§2.2 probes its crop-band constants: the *states* are engine-real, the *mapped
interval* is a designed threshold the probe tunes.

### 2.2 The rise/fall — the regional-`q` trend (the drift)

The staff's **rise/fall shows the regional-`q` trend** — the direction the field
at that place is moving, which is the tide's observability's *first* channel (the
reader's regional-`q` trend, `tide-of-the-attractor.md` §4). Where the band names
*which* season the staff is in, the rise/fall names *which way it is drifting*:

| The staff's rise/fall | What it reads | The field truth |
|---|---|---|
| **Rising** | the drift **toward harvest** | `q` accumulating — the field organizing; vein material, cheaper channeling, a farm's stored coherence filling (`material-regimes.md` §3; `farm-that-feeds.md` §5) |
| **Falling** | the drift **toward thin** | `q` de-organizing — machines wasting more, the vent backing up, the bath's draw biting, the desert-margin's creep approaching (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2) |

The rise/fall is the **accumulation the probe measures** — the trend's slope over
the tide's measured series (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §5a, T1–T4), presented at
a standing place. **[design]** The *scale* of the rise/fall — how much `q`-trend
moves the marker, how fast the drift reads as "approaching the peak" or
"approaching the trough" — is this doc's designed mapping over the probe's
measured amplitude, probe-calibrated (§5a).

### 2.3 The readers — the three consults

The staff is a *shared* instrument: three of the corpus's reader-roles consult
the same planted gauge, each for their own act's timing. Each is a designed use
of the same read, grounded in its source doc:

| Who reads it | Where the staff stands | What they read | The honest frame | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The husbander** | at the **farm's edge** | the drift **toward the harvest peak** — is the bed's `q` rising toward the tide-high the crop peaks at? | the staff's rising marker tells the farmer when to harvest the harvest; a falling one tells them to husband the band through, or fallow (`farm-that-feeds.md` §5) | `farm-that-feeds.md` §5; `tide-of-the-attractor.md` §3.2 |
| **The voyager** | at the settlement (the departure read) | the **home window's trend** — is the regional `q` rising toward harvest (a long carried field to stretch across the dark) or falling toward thin (burns the reserve harder)? | the staff's trend is the coherence-budget timing read: launch from a harvest-ward trend, or accept the thin's cost (`world-seams.md` §2.3) | `world-seams.md` §1/§2.3; `tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2 (voyage row) |
| **The steward** | at the **observatory's yard** | the settlement's **own seasonal phase and its drift** — the yard's planted read beside the room's composed display | the staff is the yard's read: the seasonal phase at the settlement's own position, the same tide's calendar the steward reads in the room, made a standing object | `the-observatory.md` §2/§3.1 |

The through-line: the staff is a **place's read of the tide**, consulted by the
three roles who plan *around* the season. It is not four sub-instruments; it is
one planted gauge, read by whoever plans a harvest, a voyage, or a season.

### 2.4 The planted sample — the family's §1.4 pattern

Concretely, the staff is the Weatherglass's own sample-at-position pattern
(`field-instruments.md` §1.4) made **standing and planted**:

- **Sample point:** a single trilinear sample of the published `q` at the staff's

- **Render:** a cast-`q` staff whose band and rise/fall marker read the four

  aesthetic; the *channel* it reads (the published `q`, the probe's trend) is
  honest.

This is the whole instrument. It is **nothing but the tide's already-designed
observability made physical and planted** — a standing consumer of the same
published `q` the reader, the Weatherglass, and the Observatory already read.

## 3. The staff's age — because it is planted, it ages

The load-bearing move of a *planted* instrument, distinct from every carried one:
**it is rooted, so it accumulates.** A carried glass or reader travels with the
player and holds no place's memory; the staff stands at one place across seasons,
and its long marks are that place's record of the tide. That record is the tide's
own aggregation **made visible at a place** — the same [design]-landed
aggregation the cold and the fate read over the long horizon, planted in the
yard.

### 3.1 A staff that has stood a decade shows the cold's deep run

The cold is the window's long-thin decade — the sustained period where `q` holds
near the floor and harvest never fully returns ([`the-cold.md`](./the-cold.md)
§1). A staff that has stood through it **carries that decade as its drift**:

> **A staff that has stood a decade shows the cold's deep run.** Across a cold,
> the staff's band sits low and its rise/fall rarely climbs — each thin-trough
> carved into the gauge's drift as a long, shallow trough the planted staff
> records. The settlement walks past a decade-staff and reads not "one bad
> season" but *the cold's whole run*: the period when `q` held near the floor,
> every op wasted more, the bath had to be held not grown (`the-cold.md` §2).

**[design]** The *aging* is this doc's design over the tide's [design]-landed
aggregation: the staff's long marks are the sum of its position's `q`/trend over
its standing (the tide's measured series accumulated), rendered as physical wear
— the troughs that deepened, the crests that never returned during a cold. It is
the cold's climate read *at one place*, the way the cold's severity already reads
from the `(1−q)` glow across the region, the bath's draw, and the Board's summed
`C(M)` (`the-cold.md` §5(e)) — made a standing staff. Gated on the same tide
probe the cold rides: if the regional `q` does not oscillate, the staff's decade
reads as a *long drift toward the floor* — a legitimate, weaker, stated finding,
not a failure (`the-cold.md` §5(b)).

### 3.2 A staff that drifts from its marks shows a window whose tide is changing

The fate is the window's trajectory — the arc its coherence economics are
*already moving toward* ([`fate-of-a-window.md`](./fate-of-a-window.md) §1). A
staff that **drifts from its own marks** shows that arc at a place:

> **A staff that drifts from its marks shows a window whose tide is changing.**
> When the staff's gauge no longer returns to the heights it once marked — when
> the harvest band it used to reach is now a low crest, or the depth it used to
> fall into is shallower — the planted staff reads the window's drift: the
> decreasing reach of each harvest, or the slow creep toward the floor. It is the
> fate's arc (`fate-of-a-window.md` §1.3 — a decaying window's regional `q`
> trending down), made a standing mark the settlement can compare, year to year.

**[design]** The *drift-from-marks* is this doc's design over the fate's
[design]-landed arc: the staff's recorded crest of each season, compared to its
historic marks, renders the window's `C(W)` trend at a place (`fate-of-a-window.md`
§3, the window-scale aggregation read as physical registers the staff keeps).

### 3.3 Honest framing — the long memory, never a forecast

The staff's honesty is the corpus's own forecast-≠-fate discipline
([`fate-of-a-window.md`](./fate-of-a-window.md) §6(c), inherited from
`the-reading-ahead.md` §7c):

> **The staff's long memory is the tide's [design]-landed aggregation made
> visible at a place — never a forecast, always the honest read of where the
> window has been.** The decade-staff shows what a cold *was*; the drifted staff
> shows where the tide *has been* over the years it stood. Neither is a sentence
> about where the window is going — the fate is a conversation, not a sentence
> (`fate-of-a-window.md` §6(c)), and the staff is the *record* half of that
> conversation, not the prophecy. It reads the window's past at a place; how the
> settlement responds to that read is the same husbandry that changes the fate.

This is important because the staff is a *standing, easily-trusted* object — the
danger is that it reads as a verdict when it is only a record. The staff **never
forecasts**: it shows where the tide has been at its place, and the settlement's
response (harvest at the rising band, husband through the falling, endure the
cold's drift, change the hand against the fate's arc) is the same legible,
bounded, non-minting act the tide, the cold, and the fate all demand.

   staff reads flat — is that presentation *genuinely felt* (a planted gauge

   Phase-1.5 design judgment. **[probe]**

   (a decade's troughs and crests recorded literally), or a designed register
   that reads the cold's run and the fate's drift (a curated set of crests
   compared against marks)? The honest default is the raw record — the staff is
   the honest measure, not a shaped mood — but the register's shape is a [design]
   choice the probe's spread informs. **[design]**

   composition already renders the tide on the map wall and the glass's core
   (`the-observatory.md` §2), does a *planted* staff risk redundancy (a pretty
   yard pole that shows the same `q` the room already composes), or is the

   Hourglass-open-Q5's trip-meter-vs-speedometer question runs? The design commits
   to the latter; whether the settlement feels the staff as a separate instrument
   is a Phase-1.5 design judgment. **[design]**
5. **The staff's propagation.** A staff that drifts from its marks reads a
   changing window — but *how many seasons* of drift, by how much, before the
   staff reads "the tide is changing" rather than "ordinary season-to-season
   variation"? This is the staff's instance of the cold's boundary

   (`fate-of-a-window.md` §7 open-Q1), applied to the planted gauge's marks.
