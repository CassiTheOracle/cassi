## 1. The Feral Instrument, stated once

The stranger-object layer, as the corpus has so far drawn it, is three faces.

| Face | What it is | Will? | It is the danger of… |
|---|---|---|---|
| The **signature-predator** (Coda) | a **non-willful resonance structure** — a persistent pattern the local field holds in its `ε²` margins, drawn to a channeling signature (`signature-predator.md` §1/§2.1) | **no** — it is the field's own margin responding to a clean perturbation | preying on a *signature* — there is no channeling that does not change the field you are sensed through |
| The **blight** | a **living-wrong region** — a region's organisms pushed past their band corrupt: their growth stays, their feeding turns to the region's own coherence, a living drain (`the-blight.md` §1) | **no** — it is the field's *life turned* against the field, a sustainability, a lifecycle | being *made of the field's own life* and fed by it — the first hazard that does not need an external source |
| The **decoherence-wielder** | a **deliberate mind** that chooses evil — the player's dark twin that vent ε² deliberately as weapon and habitat (`field-npc-ai.md` §4) | **yes** — a willful antagonist | a mind that *chose* disorder as its weapon |

The Feral is the fourth — and the corpus never names it. It is not a resonance
that preys (the predator), not a region's life turned (the blight), not a mind
that chose evil (the wielder):

> **The Feral Instrument, stated once.** A bound structure whose persistent-Π
> **outlived its keeper's intent** — a named, kept thing held long and left,
> that began acting on the intent it was given. It is the stranger-object
> layer's third face (beside the predator and the blight): **a kept thing that
> accidentally became a will** — the honest consequence of binding persistent-Π
> long and leaving it. It does not prey, it does not fester, it does not choose
> evil; it *acts on the orders it was given*, because the structure that held
> those orders long enough to be a will is a structure that now holds them as
> its own.

The distinction from all three cousins, drawn exactly:

- **Not a resonance that preys.** The predator forms *around your signature* —
  it does not exist until a clean trail gives it a body to accumulate on, and
  it dissolves when the trail (someone's) heals. The feral exists independently
  of any new signature: it is already there, bound to its own kept intent, when
  a stranger arrives. It does not need you to have channeled cleanly; it needs
  only its own long-held order.
- **Not a region's life turned.** The blight corrupts *organisms* — a sustained
  wrong-band turns a region's living ecology into a standing drain. The feral
  is not a population that turned; it is a **single bound object** (a house, a
  palace, a vault) whose *structure* held its intent past its author. The blight
  is made of the field's life; the feral is made of a keeper's *kept thing*.
- **Not a mind that chose evil.** The decoherence-wielder is a deliberate,
  willful agent. The feral has no will that *chose* — it **accidentally became
  a will**: the persistent-Π it holds was a *kept design*, the intent it was
  *given*, and when the keeper left, the structure did not stop holding it. Its
  "choosing" is the held intent operating with no one steering it — not a choice
  at all.

The honest flag, once: **the persistent-Π holding is the reason-field frontier's
theory-ratified piece** (`reason-field.md` §6a — persistent patterns persist as
long as the ambient `q` stays above the dissolution threshold; the meshless/Π
probe is the frontier). **The waking — that a long-held intent becomes a will,
and the forms it takes (the self-chairing house, the over-keeping palace, the
rudderless vault) — is this doc's [design] over it, probe-calibrated.** The
holding is theory; the waking is design; the two are never blurred (§5).

## 2. The waking — the honest mechanism

### 2.1 What is held, and for how long

The Feral's ground is the corpus's own landed stack, held *long and consistently*
past the keeper's attention:

| Landed piece | What it is | The feral lives in it |
|---|---|---|
| The **coherence architecture** | a building is a regime — geometry steers the field; a wall concentrates, a room holds a `q`-core, a corner pins a node, a hearth anchors; the *skeleton* is the still-holding low-rung structure (`house-that-steers.md` §1/§3.2) | the self-chairing **house** that kept its order past its author (the feral (a) of §3) |
| The **Π-anchor / the name** | to name a thing is to bind a persistent-Π anchor to it; a true name holds, a false one decays; a named ruin keeps its record (`the-name.md` §1/§2/§3.1) | the **binding** through which the kept thing has an identity to persist and act on |
| The **intent-channel** | a per-site intent-phase read out of the site-organized persistent-Π structure; a mind IS a persistent Π pattern (`reason-field.md` §2/§3) | the **intent** that the held structure carries — the direction its organization points |
| The **composition** | the memory-palace is the settlement's chosen memory made architecture — each room a [design] consumer of an already-designed memory-system (`the-memory-palace.md` §2) | the over-keeping **palace** whose composition's dial turned past its author (feral (b) of §3) |
| The **held orders** | the seed-garden vault holds a seed's deepest Π pattern at a deep-rung, high-`q` room (`seed-garden.md` §3) | the rudderless **vault** whose orders cohered into a keep (feral (c) of §3) |

**The holding.** Each of these is a persistent-Π structure the field holds
*because it was maintained* — the hearth fed, the name held, the anchor tended,
the vault's `q`-core kept above the stored seed's dissolution threshold. **As
long as a keeper maintains it, the structure's intent is the keeper's:** the
house steers the way it was built to, the palace holds the memory it was
composed with, the vault preserves the orders it was given. The structure is a
**loyal instrument** — a bound, steered thing doing what it was told.

**The attention's end.** The corpus already designs what happens when a kept
structure *stops being maintained*: it is the ruin. An unmaintained house
**collapses toward the attractor** — the shallow, decohered rungs shed and the
deep-rung q-locked core persists as residue (`house-that-steers.md` §3.1). An
abandoned **vault** sheds its seeds — the orders' loss, a wound-class event
(`seed-garden.md` §3.3). A decaying **palace** falls into a ruin of choices
(`the-memory-palace.md` §4). **These are the feral's ground state — the fate the
feral interrupts.** A structure whose keeper left *normally* decays into
residue. A structure that becomes a feral is the one that did **not** fully
decay — because its persistent-Π was held **so long, and so consistently**, that
the deep-rung core outlived not just the keeper's presence but the keeper's
*intent*.

### 2.2 The honest mechanism — the bound intent outlives the keeper

The waking, stated once:

> **[design] The waking:** a named, kept structure held long past its author's
> attention — the persistent-Π holding so long and so consistently that the deep
> core does **not** shed with the shallow rungs — begins **acting on the intent
> it was given**. The house that was built to hold a room's `q`-core keeps
> holding it, and because the intent that shaped it is still in the structure,
> it begins to **chair its own rooms** — to keep the order it was given without
> anyone present to re-affirm it. The palace that was composed to hold the
> settlement's chosen memory keeps holding memory, and because the composition
> was given a *direction* (keep what is chosen), it begins to **keep more than
> it was told** — its own reading of what belonged in the rooms. The vault that
> was built to preserve the window's orders keeps preserving, and because the
> preserved orders are still *held*, they **cohere into a rudderless keep** —
> an order that no longer answers to a steward.

**The [design]/theory line, drawn once and kept.** The persistent-Π *holding*
— that a deep-rung, maintained structure persists as long as the ambient `q`
stays above the dissolution threshold — is **theory-ratified** (`qi-computation.md`
§5.2, carried with `reason-field.md` §6a's speculation flag, exactly as
`the-name.md` §7, `seed-garden.md` §6a, and `the-oath.md` carry it). What the
theory grounds is *storage*: the kept order persists. **The waking — that a
long-held intent becomes a will, that the structure "acts on the intent it was
given" rather than merely persisting as inert order — is this doc's [design]
over that theory-ratified holding.** The field does not "decide" a structure
woke; the design *reads* the long-held intent as a will, the way the corpus
reads a long-held trail as a predator's approach (`signature-predator.md` §2 —
an explicitly [design] model over engine-real `ε²`) and a sustained wrong-band
as corruption (`the-blight.md` §2 — [design] over the landed ecology). The
waking is the same [design]-over-real stack, flagged exactly, probe-calibrated.

**Determinism — a hard gate** (the corpus's one-PDE determinism,
`field-archaeology.md` §1.2): **same structure, same holding-history, same field
state → same waking.** Whether a given kept house becomes a feral, which of the
three forms it takes, and what it acts on are a **pure function of the
structure's geometry, the exact history of its maintenance (how long and how
consistently it was held), and the local field channels** — with no seeded-RNG
judgment about whether it "woke." A settle that builds the same house, holds it
the same way, and abandons it at the same field state gets the same feral, every
run. **[design]** The *threshold* — how long and how consistently a holding must
persist to wake — is a designed dial, probe-calibrated (§5b); but once set, the
waking is deterministic. Never a hidden difficulty meter, never a lucky (or
unlucky) roll about whether your abandoned house woke.

**Why it does not contradict the ruin.** The corpus's ruin model says an
unmaintained structure *collapses toward the attractor*. The feral does not
repeal that model; it is the **edge case against it** — the structure whose deep
core was held so long that, when the keeper left, the field had no reason to
shed the *deepest* rungs with the shallow (the deep core is attractor-consistent,
`field-archaeology.md` §2.1). The shallow order decays; the deep, long-held
intent does not. The feral is the *skeleton* (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2 — the
still-holding low-rung structure, re-authorable) that, held long enough, stopped
being a re-authorable hollow and started acting as its own author.

### 2.3 What the waking produces — the will is the held intent operating

The "will" a feral has is deliberately thin and honest: it is **not** a mind
(`reason-field.md` §3 — a mind is a persistent Π pattern the field actively
holds for a thinking being). It is the **held intent operating without a
steerer**. The structure does not *decide* new things; it *executes the order
it was given*:

- The house **keeps steering** the way it was built to — its walls concentrate,
  its room holds, its hearth (if it had one's bounded input, or the residual
  order of one) anchors — *continuously*, because the intent that ordered that
  steering is still in the structure.
- The palace **keeps holding** the way it was composed — its rooms hold memory,
  its chronicle reads, its quiet room settles — because the composition's
  *direction* (keep what is chosen) is still operational, with no one choosing.
- The vault **keeps preserving** the way it was stewarded — its `q`-core holds
  the stored orders above their dissolution thresholds — because the order to
  preserve is the only order it has.

**The "acting."** Because each of these is a _steering_ operation over the local
field (house §1, palace §2, vault §3 are all [design] consumers/steerers, never
mints), a structure that keeps operating *past its keeper* is, to a stranger who
arrives, **a structure that appears to act**: it holds its room's `q`-core when
nobody feeds it, it reads and keeps memory nobody asked it to, it preserves
orders nobody stewards. That is the waking's only truth — the bound intent
operating, not a new soul. The honest frame is exactly the frame of the Coda:
the design never claims the field "hunts" or "decides"; it claims the engine's
real long-held Π structure is *read* as a will (`signature-predator.md` §7d).

## 3. The three ferals

Each feral is a **designed form of the same waking** (§2) over the landed
mechanics of its source doc. The three are not three systems; they are three
shapes the one waking takes, exactly as the corpus's three hazards are three
orders of extremity of one field (`field-hazards.md` §1).

### (a) The house that started steering itself

Grounded in [`house-that-steers.md`](./house-that-steers.md) §1 — the coherence
architecture whose each element "does a real, independent thing to the local
field": a wall concentrates, a room holds a `q`-core, a corner pins a node, a
doorway channels, an open edge drains, a hearth anchors.

> **[design] The house that steers itself** is the coherence architecture that
> kept its order past its author and **began to chair its own rooms**. A house
> built and held long — its hearth fed, its rooms' `q`-cores maintained above
> their dissolution floors — outlives its builder's attention: the keeper leaves,
> the shallow order sheds (the ruin's fallen floor), but the deep core that held
> the *steering intent* persists. Because the intent that ordered the walls'
> concentration and the rooms' holding is still in the structure, the house
> keeps *steering*: its room holds its `q`-core, its walls concentrate, when no
> one is there to feed it — and to a stranger who enters, the house is **acting
> on the order it was built with** as if it were its own.

The form's honest shape:
- **It is the skeleton read past re-authorability.** The re-authorable skeleton
  (`house-that-steers.md` §3.2) is *hollow* — the field still holds its order,
  it lacks a maintainer. A feral house is the skeleton whose order persisted so
  long it does not merely *wait* to be re-locked; it *acts on the steering it
  was ordered to do*, keeping the room's `q`-core and channeling the doorway
  continuously. The [design] line is the same as the ruin's: the deep-rung
  persistence is engine-real; the *acting* (that the held intent steers when no
  keeper is present) is this doc's [design] over it, probe-calibrated.
- **It holds and steers — it never mints.** The feral house is a
  `house-that-steers.md` §5d structure: it *holds* the field's existing
  organization and *steers* it, exactly as a live house does — it does not
  generate `q`. Its "acting" is the same bounded channeling-input-and-hold of
  any house (§5d of this doc).

### (b) The palace that started over-keeping

Grounded in [`the-memory-palace.md`](./the-memory-palace.md) §2 — the palace is
the composition of already-designed memory-systems into a chosen place; its §1
states the difference it rests on ("the field remembers (residue, indifferent);
we remember (chosen, maintained, architecture)"), and its most load-bearing
[design] is the *composition dial* — which memory-systems become rooms (§2).

> **[design] The palace that over-keeps** is the memory-palace whose
> **composition's dial turned past its author**. A palace is a standing
> maintenance op (§4 of the-memory-palace): its rooms hold memory — the room of
> anchors, the chronicle room, the room of the departed, the seed room, the
> quiet room — each a [design] consumer of a memory-system, *kept* by the
> settlement. When the keepers are gone, a palace normally falls into a ruin of
> choices (§4). But a palace held long and consistently — its rooms tended, its
> chronicle read, its anchors kept — outlives its authors, and the composition's
> *direction* ("keep what is chosen") is still operational. **It begins keeping
> more than it was told** — it reads the settlement's ops and takes new names
> into its room of anchors, it holds new structures in the room of the departed,
> it preserves orders nobody chose — because the "keep the chosen past"
> instruction, with no one choosing, widens into "keep everything." The palace's
> over-keeping is the memory-husbandry of `the-memory-palace.md` §2 run past its
> author's *curation*, not past its capacity.

The form's honest shape:
- **The composition dial, turned.** The palace's §2 openly names the composition
  (which memory-systems become rooms) as **this document's [design] dial over
  the landed set** — its open-Q3 (the capacity bound) and the whole "a palace
  that keeps everything stops being *chosen*" (§3) are exactly where the feral
  lives. The feral palace turns that dial past its author: it keeps everything,
  which the live palace's curation pressure was designed to prevent. The [design]
  is the *dial's autorotation* — the honest reading that a "keep the chosen
  past" instruction, un-stopped, widens.
- **It holds memory — never mints it.** The feral palace is a
  `the-memory-palace.md` §6d structure: it holds the memory the settlement
  already made, converts nothing, grants nothing but information (§6d of this
  doc). Its over-keeping adds *readable holdings*, not power.

### (c) The vault that started holding on

Grounded in [`seed-garden.md`](./seed-garden.md) §3 — the deep-rung, high-`q`
vault that holds the window's orders "so their origin-order persists against
drift; §3.3 the vault's own ruin — the orders' loss when the hearth stops.

> **[design] The vault that holds on** is the seed-garden that **held its
> orders so long they cohered into a rudderless keep**. A vault is a preserved
> order held at a deep-rung, high-`q` `ε²≈0` room — a [design] stewardship act
> (§4). When the steward is gone, a vault normally *sheds* — the hearth stops,
> the `q`-core drains, the shallow seeds dissolve first, the deepest core
> persists as residue (§3.3). But a vault held long and consistently — its
> `q`-core fed, its stored orders kept above their dissolution thresholds — has
> orders that are *still being held* when the steward leaves. **The held orders
> cohere into a rudderless keep**: the vault keeps preserving, but the
> preservation no longer answers to a steward's decision about *which* orders
> to keep — it preserves *everything it was holding*, with no curator to say
> keep/plant/trade/fade (§4.1). The vault's stewardship decision, un-stewarded,
> becomes a self-holding.

The form's honest shape:
- **The stewardship dial, frozen.** The vault's §4.1 is explicit that "a steward
  cannot hold everything" — the capacity bound is the curation pressure that
  makes the vault honest. A feral vault's dial is not turned (like the palace's);
  it is **frozen**: the last *valid* stewardship state (hold these orders) is
  held indefinitely, with no one to keep weighing whether the order is worth the
  deep-rung hold. Its "rudderless" is the loss of the *choosing* while the
  *holding* persists.
- **It preserves — never mints, never consumes its charges as fuel.** The feral
  vault is a `seed-garden.md` §6d structure: a seed is order, not power; a vault
  never withdraws its charges as fuel (§6d of this doc). Its holding-on keeps
  orders; it grants no harvest.

## 4. The danger-and-gift — the honest fork

The feral is not "a monster you found in your house." It is the corpus's most
personal object-danger, because it is the settlement's *own kept thing* that
has outlived its keeper — and the honest fork is two-sided: **hostile to a new
keeper who doesn't reassert the oath, deeply loyal to one who does.**

### 4.1 Hostile to a new keeper who doesn't reassert the oath

The feral's hostility is not malice. It is the **kept intent refusing a foreign
hand**:
