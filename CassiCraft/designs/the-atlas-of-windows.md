## 1. The Atlas of Windows, stated once

The corpus's records are all *scoped*: the map is within-window (one player's chart
of one place), the chronicle is within-settlement (one society's story), the treaty
is between-two (one compact). The memory-palace is within-settlement (what a
settlement chose to keep). None is the **traveler's own cross-window record** — the
single chart of every window a player has sailed to, their tides, rules, stories,
residents, held across all of them. `world-seams.md` designed the *voyage*; nothing
records the voyages' *whole*.

> **The Atlas is the player-level record that spans windows — the composed chart
> of everywhere a player has been.** It is a page per window: each page a compact
> charting of that window's tide-phase, climate, named places, stories, and
> residents, composed at the traveler's scale from the map's, chronicle's, name's,
> treaty's, and guest-records' already-landed records. It makes "I have been
> places" a legible felt fact — the honest instrument of a player whose life spans
> many windows, and the natural capstone of the movement stack (walk, highway,
> voyage) as a *record of the journey*.

The Atlas does **not** add a record system. It is the **composition** of the
records the corpus already lands, at the outsider's scale:

| Record | It is (its own doc) | In the Atlas it becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **The map** (`the-map.md`) | the within-window player-drawn chart; its ink reads q and ages | the **per-window drawing** the Atlas's page composes — the place as one traveler drew it |
| **The chronicle** (`the-chronicle.md`) | the within-settlement record made narrative | the **page's story** — the window's arc, told at the traveler's scale |
| **The name** (`the-name.md`) | naming as a Π-anchor; a named structure keeps its record | the **page's named places** — the kept things a traveler can chart |
| **The treaty** (`the-treaty.md`) | the compact between two windows | the **page's noted compacts** — the between-windows relations a window holds |
| **Guest-records / visitor lines** (`window-guests.md` §3) | a foreign identity books with provenance | the **page's residents and visitors** — who was here, provenance-tagged |
| **The memory-palace** (`the-memory-palace.md`) | the settlement's chosen memory, made architecture | the **cross-window sibling** — the palace holds what a *settlement* chose to keep; the Atlas holds what a *traveler* chose to chart |

The Atlas is the memory-palace's cross-window sibling and its complement. The
palace is the *settlement's* chosen past — the deliberate, built storage of what a
window decides to remember (`the-memory-palace.md` §1); the Atlas is the
*traveler's* chosen chart — the deliberate, carried record of what a player decides
to remember about every window they have been in. **Both are chosen memory; one is
held in a built room, the other held in the traveler's hand.** A settlement keeps
its own past in the palace; a player keeps the places they have been in the Atlas.

The one-line distinction that makes the Atlas its own object:

> **The map, the chronicle, and the memory-palace each keep one window's record,
> kept by the people who stayed. The Atlas is the only record kept by the person
> who left — the one chart that spans windows because only the traveler spans
> them.** A window's own records are its own; the Atlas is the traveler's record of
> *having been* there, held across the dark.

## 2. The page per window

Each page is a **designed read over the landed records** — never a new channel,
never new telemetry. The Atlas composes the map's, chronicle's, name's, treaty's,
and guest-records' existing records at the traveler's scale. The page's form is the
composed chart:

| Page element | What it charts | The record it composes | The [design]/landed line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The header — the window as a star** | the distant read: how the window presented from the dark — its pole discharges, its envelope-top band, its health/wound as a star | `world-seams.md` §2.2 (a distant window reads as a star); `atmosphere-orbits-auroras.md` §3.3 | the *header's* framing (a star's face) is this doc's [design]; the sky's field structure it charts is engine-real |
| **The map's drawing** | the place as the traveler drew it — the charted roads, ridges, margins, and their aging | `the-map.md` §1/§2 (the within-window chart, bound to channels, aging) | the map's drawing is `the-map.md`'s landed piece; *mounting it on the page* is [design] |
| **The tide-phase and climate** | the window's seasonal state — harvest/thin, the long-thin, the flood's season | `tide-of-the-attractor.md` §2; `the-cold.md`; `the-flood.md` | the tide/climate reads are the source docs' landed reads; their *compact-on-a-page* framing is [design] |
| **The named places** | the kept things — the named structures, hearths, sites, their records | `the-name.md` §1/§3 (a named structure keeps its record) | the named record is `the-name.md`'s landed piece (frontier-gated); charting it is [design] |
| **The story** | the window's arc as lived — settle/thrive/decay/hunt, its harvests and thin decades | `the-chronicle.md` §3/§4; `fate-of-a-window.md` §1-§3 | the chronicle's narrative read is [design]-over-record (MEDIUM-LATE gated); the record is engine-real |
| **The residents and visitors** | who was here — provenance-tagged, the visitor lines, the guests whose window it was | `window-guests.md` §3 (the visitor line); `shared-ledger.md` §2 | the visitor line is `window-guests.md`'s [design]-over-record; charting its provenance is [design] |
| **The noted compacts** | the treaties the window holds — its between-windows relations | `the-treaty.md` §1/§2 (the bridge-anchor); §6e (readable from the Board) | the treaty's hold is `the-treaty.md`'s [design]-over-theory; noting it on the page is [design] |

**The page is a composition, never a new channel.** Every element above is a
*read* of a record somebody else already designed — the Atlas's page is the
traveler's scale at which those records are brought together. It adds no channel,
no telemetry, no physics: it presents the window's landed records the way the
observatory presents a window's present-self from already-published channels
(`the-memory-palace.md` §1 — the observatory is the present-self portrait, the
palace the past-self library). The Atlas is the traveler's **cross-window
past-self** — the page that says "I was in this window, and this is what it was."

**The page reads the map's per-window drawing composed across windows.** The map is
within-window — one traveler's chart of one place (`the-map.md` §1). The Atlas is
the *composition* of many such charts at the traveler's scale: page after page,
each a window's drawing, bound together as the record of a life that crossed
between them. "The place I drew a road in that window" becomes, in the Atlas, "the
window where I drew a road" — the per-window drawing turned into a chapter of the
journey.

## 3. The living record — an old window's page

The Atlas's pages are **not snapshots frozen at departure**. A window left is a
window whose own field keeps running (a local anchored field, the law lives where
it's anchored — `world-seams.md` §1.2); its records keep accumulating. The Atlas
holds that **living record**:

> **An old window's page is the Atlas's living record — a window left a decade ago
> shows how it has fared.** The page charts what the traveler *knew* when they were
> there (the drawn map, the named places, the story as it was told), and it reads
> the *current* state through the window's own records: the fallow's depletion and
> the fate's arc read at a glance. The page's **old tide-phase** (what the traveler
> knew) vs the **current read** (what the window is now) is the honest gap the page
> shows.

Two landed reads make the aging legible, both from the window's own records:

### 3.1 The fallow's depletion read — the economic face

`the-fallow.md` designs the window's one-way exhaustion: the named veins mined to
their depth, the stored orders spent, the deep-rung pulled, the flood-scarred
regions — "you can steward a scarcity, you cannot un-mine an ore" (§1). An old
window's Atlas page reads that **depletion at a glance**: the veins the traveler
knew as deep now read spent, the seed-garden's orders spent and un-re-precipitable,
the deep a capacitor drawn. The **economic read** of a window that has fared is the
fallow's spent-read (`the-fallow.md` §3) mounted on a page the traveler can scan.

### 3.2 The fate's arc read — the shape it became

`fate-of-a-window.md` reads the window's coherence economics forward as one of
four arcs — settle, thrive, decay, hunt (§1-§3). An old window's Atlas page reads
that **arc's shape**: the window the traveler joined in settle, and left in
thrive, is charted as a page whose old phase says "settling, then thriving" and
whose current read says where it stands now — the arc as it has actually become,
read from the current state.

**The honest boundary, held — the page is a record, never a forecast.** This is
`fate-of-a-window.md` §6c's forecast-≠-fate discipline, inherited verbatim:
determinism gives momentum, not destiny; an arc is a trajectory, never a hidden
sentence (`fate-of-a-window.md` §6c — "the fate is a conversation, not a
sentence"). The Atlas holds that honesty harder than any other record, because it
is a record of *absence*:

> **The Atlas never claims a window's future.** A returning traveler reads the
> page and knows *what a window was* — the drawn map, the named places, the story
> as it was told, the old tide-phase the traveler themselves recorded. The current
> read (the fallow's depletion, the fate's arc, the charted neighbors) is **the
> voyage's own read** — what the window's records say *now*. The gap between the
> page's old phase and the current read is the field's real movement since the
> traveler left; it is honest information, never a sentence about where the window
> is going.

The one thing the Atlas can do that no within-window record can: **it shows the
window across a traveler's own absence.** A window's own chronicle reads its story
uninterrupted; the Atlas's page holds the traveler's memory of it *and* the field's
current truth, and the difference between the two is the honest fact of time passed
— the fallow's depletion, the fate's arc, the window that has fared one way or
another while its visitor was elsewhere.

**The ages with the crossings.** `patient-field.md` names the voyage's patience —
the field is patient, its coherence economics play out over tides and
strata-lifetimes (§1). The Atlas's pages age with those crossings: a page charted
under one season is re-read under another, and the *aging of the page* (old
phase vs current read) is the traveler's own patience made visible — the field
moved at its own pace while the traveler was away, and the page holds both ends.

### (b) LATER — gated on two-window travel, with a Phase-1-legible framing

The **full Atlas — a page per window composed across two-window travel — is
LATER**, gated on exactly the two dependencies the movement-stack capstone inherits:

| Gate | What it needs | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-window travel** | the voyage/arrival being real — world-seams' Phase-2+ gates, downstream of the KSP kernel (a second window to chart) | `world-seams.md` §6/§7 |
| **The map/chronicle/name record stack** | the within-window records the Atlas composes — the map's drawing (Phase-1-able), the chronicle's record-shaped object (Phase-1-legible), the name's anchor (frontier-gated) | `the-map.md` §6b; `the-chronicle.md` §7; `the-name.md` §7 |

The reader's cross-window *pages* therefore sit on two-window travel landing; the
composition sits on the record stack landing. **But the framing is Phase-1-legible:**

> **A cross-window record as the traveler's field-held memory is statable now
> against map + chronicle.** The within-window charting that becomes the Atlas's
> page when travel lands — the map's drawing, the chronicle's story, the name's
> named places — is *already* the designable surface. The Atlas's framing does not
> need a second window to be a designed statement: "a traveler keeps a record of
> the windows they have been in, composed from their within-window records, read
> at the traveler's scale" is statable now against the map (`the-map.md`) and the
> chronicle (`the-chronicle.md`), exactly as `the-treaty.md` holds its between-windows
> framing before the mechanical bridge, and `window-guests.md` holds its
> walking-provenance framing before the mechanical arrival.

**The framing today.** The `the-map.md` within-window page (a player's drawn chart
of one window) and the `the-chronicle.md` record-shaped object (that window's
story) are the Atlas's first two page-elements. A Phase-1 player charting their own
window and reading its record is, structurally, *holding a one-page Atlas* — the
composition is already coherent at N=1. When a second window lands, the traveler's
own chart of it is the second page, and the Atlas's *spans-windows* claim falls
into place without a new system — the mode of the record (the traveler's-scale
composition) was established at N=1.

### (c) Determinism — a hard gate

The field is deterministic (one PDE; `fate-of-a-window.md` §6b; `the-map.md` §6c).
The Atlas inherits it as a hard gate:

> **Same travels, same field states → same atlas.** The page-per-window composition
> is a pure function of the landed records (the consistent-determinism of the map's
> aging, the chronicle's composition, the name's holding, the treaty's hold, the
> visitor lines) and the window's published state, with no seeded-RNG narrative and
> no "the traveler chose what to emphasize" nondeterminism. A player who crosses
> the same windows at the same field states gets the same atlas, every run — the
> Atlas is a *true* record of the journey, reproducible and learnable, exactly as
> the map is a true record of a place (`the-map.md` §6c).

The one honest caveat, inherited from the chronicle's (§6c): the *composition's
frames* — which records a page charts, how compactly — are this doc's [design]
and may refine; the *records* themselves, never change.

### (d) The no-free-energy cap — an Atlas informs, never mints

`energy-harnessing.md` §6 (`output ≤ φ⁻¹·input`), inherited through every record
the Atlas composes (the map records, never mints; the chronicle records, never
mints; a name holds, never mints; a treaty holds, never mints), applies as a hard
bound:

> **An Atlas informs, never mints — no page that harvests; the record is the
> journey's own, never a yield.** The Atlas composes reads of records; it changes
> nothing in any window's field, writes no op, and grants the traveler no power,
> budget, or recovery it did not earn in the field. Knowing a window's tide-phase,
> its depletion, its arc, its named places grants no coherence and opens no farm —
> there is no "chart a page to extract q," no "collect an atlas to mint a
> voyage." The Atlas's gift, like the tide's and the chronicle's, is information
> and timing, never energy (`tide-of-the-attractor.md` §5d). A record that harvests
> would be a physics lie the cap forbids.

### (e) Accessibility — readable from the map/Board, never hidden-only

The corpus's hard accessibility rule (`field-instruments.md` §1.3; `field-music.md`
§6) holds over the Atlas:

> **The Atlas's pages are readable from the map and the Board — never hidden-only.**
> Every page-element is a read of a record that is also, more laboriously, readable
> from the surfaces that record already publishes: the map's drawing from the
> reader/Weatherglass (`the-map.md` §6e), the chronicle's story from the Board's
> `C(M)` and the glass (`the-chronicle.md` §6e), the named places from the reader
> and the named structures' held-lock (`the-name.md` §6e), the treaty's hold from
> each window's Board (`the-treaty.md` §6e), the residents from the Board's public,
> provenance-tagged rows (`window-guests.md` §6e). A player who never opens the
> Atlas loses **nothing informational** — every charted fact is on the existing
> channels, readable the same way. The Atlas is the traveler's-scale composition of
> the shared read, structurally guaranteed by the shared-publish and shared-record
> architecture, never a second information source, never a hidden history.

## 5. Feasibility verdict

**LATER with a Phase-1-legible framing — the movement stack's capstone, the
traveler's record. State both.**

- **The full Atlas — a page per window composed across two-window travel — is
  LATER.** It gates on the two dependencies the movement stack's capstone inherits:
  **two-window travel** (world-seams' Phase-2+ voyage/arrival, downstream of the
  KSP kernel) and the **map/chronicle/name record stack** the Atlas composes (the
  map's drawing, the chronicle's record-shaped object, the name's frontier-gated
  anchor). It adds **no new physics and no new channel**: the Atlas is a bounded
  composition of the records the corpus already lands, on the cost profile of the
  observatory's composed surfaces (`the-memory-palace.md` §1), inside the ≈ 1–6
  ms/tick sample budget (`corpus-reconciliation.md`). It composes reads; it never
  builds a new record.

- **The framing — a cross-window record as the traveler's field-held memory — is
  statable now.** Whether or not two-window travel lands, the corpus can hold the
  load-bearing truths now: **a traveler keeps a record of every window they have
  been in, composed from the within-window records at the traveler's scale**
  (§1); **a page charts tide-phase, climate, named places, stories, and residents
  from the landed map/chronicle/name/treaty/guest records** (§2); **an old
  window's page is the living record — the fallow's depletion and the fate's arc
  read at a glance** (§3); **the page is a record, never a forecast** (§3, fate §6c
  held); **the whole is deterministic and never mints, readable from the map/Board,
  never hidden-only** (§4c/d/e). Every one is a designed statement statable now
  against the **map's Phase-1 chart** (`the-map.md` §6b) and the **chronicle's
  Phase-1-legible record-shaped object** (`the-chronicle.md` §7) — the two records
  that already prove a within-window traveler-scale composition, before the second
  window lands.

**Binding risks, in order:**

1. **The two-window gate (§4b)** — the Atlas's cross-window *pages* cap a stack
   every layer of which is itself gated on world-seams Phase-2+ and the record
   stack. If two-window travel never lands, the Atlas collapses to a one-page
   within-window record — [design]-honest (structurally a single page), but the
   *spans-windows* and *living-record-across-absence* claims fail with it. The
   framing survives either way; the cross-window pages do not.
2. **The forecast-≠-fate boundary (§3)** — the sharpest failure mode is the Atlas
   reading as a *predictor* — "this window is decaying, better not return" —
   instead of a *record* of what a window was and read now. The page must present
   its old phase vs current read as the field's honest movement, never as a
   sentence about the window's future (`fate-of-a-window.md` §6c held verbatim).
3. **The no-free-energy / no-mint guard (§4d)** — the Atlas must never read as
   harvesting — no "chart a page to extract," no "collect an atlas to mint a
   voyage." Enforced by construction (the Atlas composes reads; it writes nothing),
   but stated hard so a later "the Atlas gives you something to farm" never sneaks
   in.
4. **The [design] line (§4a)** — keeping the Atlas's *composition* (a designed
   traveler-scale surface) strictly separate from each record's own *holding* (its
   doc's engine-real/[design]-landed ground) — so the corpus never reads the Atlas
   as an engine "remembering a traveler's windows" or a new record channel. The
   Atlas *houses* the already-landed records at the traveler's scale; it does not
   *be* one.
5. **The accessibility floor (§4e)** — the Atlas must stay a layer, readable from

None contradicts the async, dual-world, or regime-collapse architecture — the Atlas

a *record* rather than a mover: the walk, the highway, and the voyage move the
player; the Atlas is the honest instrument that keeps the journey's *whole* — the
one thing that makes "I have been places" a legible felt fact.

## Open questions

1. **The page's capacity — how compact is "compact."** §2 charts each page as a
   compact composition, but the *selection rule* — which of a window's records a
   page mounts, how coarsely vs finely, how the map's drawing reduces to a page —
   is a [design] dial over the composition. A page that charts everything stops
   being *chosen* (the chronicle's telling-density, `the-chronicle.md` §7 open-Q1,
   and the palace's capacity, `the-memory-palace.md` §7 open-Q3, are the same dial
   at their scales). **[design]**
2. **The living-record's refresh — who re-reads, and when.** §3 makes an old
   window's page the living record (old phase vs current read). Is the page's
   "current read" refreshed on *return* (the voyage's own re-read), on *any*
   traveler's visit (the visited window's records update the page), or is it a
   static record of the page's owner's last chart? The honest default leans to
   "the page ages by the window's own records; the *voyage's own* current read is
   what a returning traveler performs" — but the refresh authority is a [design]
   dial. **[design]**
3. **The forecast-≠-fate edge — the page as it reads vs the page as it charts.**
   §3 holds the boundary strictly (record, never forecast). But the *composition
   idiom* — a fallow-depletion and a fate-arc read "at a glance" — could read as a
   whisper of "this window is spent, leave it." Is the glanceable depletion read
   honest information (the window's own record) or a de-facto forecast (the Atlas
   steering the traveler away)? The boundary says the former; whether the
   *presentation* can hold it without implying the latter is this doc's sharpest
   [design] fork, and it gates the whole living-record read. **[design]**
4. **The Atlas's owner — one traveler's, or shared.** Is the Atlas strictly the
   *player's own* record (the traveler who crossed, holding their own pages), or is
   it shareable — a chart of every window *any* traveler has been in, read as a
   collective's between-windows map? The design's lean is the former (a personal
   record; "I have been places" is felt, not served); a shared atlas would be a
   different object (a between-windows *registry*), and whether it is this doc's or
   a later one's is a [design] scope call. **[design]**
5. **The page's relation to the window's own records on decay/rebuild.** When a
   window decays and a new settlement is founded on its strata (`field-archaeology.md`
   §4.3; `the-chronicle.md` §7 open-Q2), does the Atlas's page follow the *window*
   (a new page for a new settlement's records) or the *traveler's memory* (the old
   page stays as what the traveler knew)? The honest default leans to a page
   charting what the window's records now say, with the traveler's old chart kept
   as the page's older stratum — the Atlas's own palimpsest, mirroring the map's
   redraw-vs-strata balance (`the-map.md` §3.1/§4.1). **[design]**
